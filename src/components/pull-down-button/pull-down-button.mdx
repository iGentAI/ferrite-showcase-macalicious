<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSPopUpButton` with `pullsDown` on. It is built on
[Base UI's Menu](https://base-ui.com/react/components/menu): the button is the menu's trigger,
drawn with the bezel of the [pop-up button](/components/pop-up-button), and the menu is the
library's [menu](/components/menu) with [menu items](/components/menu-item) as children. Unlike
a pop-up button its title never changes: it names the menu.

## Usage

```tsx
import { MenuItem, MenuSeparator } from "macalicious/menu-item";
import { PullDownButton } from "macalicious/pull-down-button";
import "macalicious/menu.css";
import "macalicious/menu-item.css";
import "macalicious/pop-up-button.css";
import "macalicious/pull-down-button.css";

<PullDownButton title="PDF">
  <MenuItem onClick={openInPreview}>Open in Preview</MenuItem>
  <MenuItem onClick={saveAsPdf}>Save as PDF</MenuItem>
  <MenuSeparator />
  <MenuItem onClick={sendInMail}>Send in Mail</MenuItem>
</PullDownButton>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add pull-down-button`.

The children are the menu's items: `MenuItem`, `MenuCheckboxItem`, `MenuRadioGroup`,
`MenuSeparator`, `MenuSection`, and submenus. The menu opens below the button, left-aligned
with it, in the font of the button's size. A pull-down button is as wide as its title unless it
is given a `width`.

For an Action button pass an `image` (a template icon drawn with `currentColor`, such as a
gear) instead of a title, and name the button with `aria-label`.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines,
[Pull-Down Buttons](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503154252/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/buttons/pull-down-buttons/):

- Use a pull-down button for commands, or for items that are toggled independently; use a
  pop-up button for one choice out of several.
- The title names the menu and stays the same when an item is chosen, so the button needs no
  label beside it. Give the items titles in title case.
- An Action button shows a gear instead of a title and lists commands for the selected item;
  those commands should also be available in the menu bar.

## Variants and states

Pressed and keyboard-focused are transient, so this gallery pins them.

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | – | The pop-up button's bezel and accent cap, with one down arrow | Measured (PDF in the Print sheet) |
| Pressed or open | `:active`, or while the menu is open | The cap deepens to 88%; the menu hangs below the button | Provisional |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring around the bezel | Ring measured on a text field |
| Disabled | `disabled` | Bezel at half opacity, no accent on the cap, title at 25%, arrow black at 40% (white at 25% in Dark Aqua) | Measured (Sidecar pane) |
| No bezel | `bordered={false}` | The title followed by a small chevron with round ends, 3 pt after it, both black at 65% (white at 85% in Dark Aqua) | Measured (Trackpad pane; the chevron also on Books' Sort By button; disabled in the Mouse pane) |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | No accent on the cap, arrow in the title's colour, no focus ring | Provisional, from the disabled rendering |

An application can draw a button without a bezel in colours of its own. Because the button is
drawn by the pop-up button's bezel, it takes that component's two colour hooks, set on the
button or on an ancestor:

| Custom property | Colours | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-pop-up-button-borderless-color` | the title | black at 65%, white at 85% in Dark Aqua |
| `--mac-pop-up-button-borderless-arrow-color` | the chevron | the title's colour |

Books' "Sort By" button is such a button: its title is in the label colour
(`var(--mac-color-label)`, black and white at 85%), its chevron black at 77% and white at
76.5%, and its title is 12 pt, set with `style`. The chevron itself and its distance
from the title are the ones of the Trackpad pane. A disabled button keeps the disabled colours,
and a button with a bezel ignores both hooks.

## API

`PullDownButton` takes the props below; every other prop, `disabled` included, goes to the
button, which is Base UI's Menu.Trigger rendered as a `<button>`.

<PropsTable name="PullDownButtonProps" />

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-size` | `regular`, `small`, `mini` | Reflects `size`; the menu carries it too. |
| `data-bordered` | `false`, or absent | Reflects `bordered`. |
| `data-arrows` | `down` | The single arrow; a pop-up button has `double`. |
| `data-popup-open` | present or absent | Set by Base UI while the menu is open. |
| `data-disabled` | present or absent | The button is disabled. |
| `data-force` | `pressed`, `focus` | Documentation and visual tests only: pins a transient state. |

The button carries two classes, `.mac-pop-up-button` and `.mac-pull-down-button`: the bezel,
the cap and the states are the pop-up button's styles.

## Accessibility

- The button is a native `<button>` with `aria-haspopup="menu"` and `aria-expanded`; the popup
  has `role="menu"` (the menu button pattern).
- Tab reaches the button. Down Arrow, Return or Space opens the menu and highlights the first
  item; the arrow keys move, typing the start of a title selects it, Return or Space chooses,
  and Escape closes the menu and returns focus to the button.
- A button without a title, such as an Action button, needs an `aria-label`.
- A disabled button uses the real `disabled` attribute.
- The arrow is decoration and hidden from assistive technology. In forced-colours mode the
  bezel and the arrow are drawn with system colours.

## Visual comparison

`npm run visual -- pull-down-button/` compares four examples with the reference screenshots in
both appearances (`pull-down-button.visual.json`): the PDF button at the foot of the Print
sheet, the disabled No Devices button of the Sidecar pane, the enabled small button without
a bezel under "Secondary click" in the Trackpad pane, and Books' "Sort By" button, a regular
button without a bezel tinted with the two colour hooks. Share of pixels that differ at
2x, in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Crop | Aqua, whole crop | Aqua, drawing only | Dark Aqua, whole crop | Dark Aqua, drawing only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PDF, Print sheet | 5.19% | 0.29% | 2.99% | 0.38% |
| Disabled, Sidecar | 7.42% | 0.15% | 2.30% | 0.03% |
| No bezel, Trackpad | not scored | 0.55% | not scored | 0.55% |
| No bezel, Books' Sort By | not scored | 1.14% | not scored | 1.14% |

"Drawing only" masks the title and counts a pixel as differing at a colour distance of 0.05,
half the default. The Print sheet is a material over the desktop picture and is noisy by a few
levels in the capture; the example paints a flat colour behind the button, which is most of
that crop's mean difference. The Trackpad and Books crops are a line of text and a chevron, so
they are scored with the title masked only: their score is the chevron. The title has
the chevron's colour in the Trackpad pane: `#535353` on `#f0f0f0` and `#e1e1e1` on `#363636`
in the screenshots.

The chevron of a button without a bezel is one drawing in every screenshot that shows it: the
Trackpad and Mouse panes, Books' Sort By button and the Action segment of Finder's Connect to
Server show the same shape, row for row. It is fitted to the Books capture, where it is dark
on white: arms of 3 pt at 45 degrees, a stroke of 1.43 pt, round ends and a round corner,
7.4 x 4.4 pt of ink. Before the fit it was drawn with a stroke of 1.75 pt and flat ends, which
scored 1.32% and 1.36% on the Trackpad crop. In the Books captures the chevron sits half a
device pixel off the pixel grid; the comparison example moves the button by that much with a
transform, which gives its chevron the same phase against the pixel grid, so that it is
rasterised as the capture's is. Without the
shift the same drawing scores 3.9% there, all of it along the chevron's edges.

States no screenshot shows are checked in a real browser against values from the spec (the
`expect` comparisons): the cap at rest, the deepened cap while the mouse is held down, the open
menu below the button with its first item highlighted, the focus ring after Tab, and the cap
without accent and no ring in an inactive window.
