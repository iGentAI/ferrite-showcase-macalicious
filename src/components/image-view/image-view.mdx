---
title: Image View
description: Shows one image, scaled to its view, optionally cut to a circle or tinted as a template.
---

<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSImageView` without a frame: the view adds no border, background or shadow. An
editable image view is an image well, and a clickable image is an image button; this one is
neither.

The library contains no artwork. App icons, device pictures and glyphs in the Catalina
screenshots are Apple's and are not reproduced: supply your own images. The pictures on this
page are stand-ins drawn for the examples.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ImageView } from "macalicious/image-view";
import "macalicious/image-view.css";

<ImageView src="/printer.png" alt="Office printer" width={93} height={68} />
<ImageView src={user.picture} alt="" mask="circle" width={24} height={24} />
<ImageView alt="Stephen Hackett" mask="circle" width={62} height={62}>SH</ImageView>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add image-view`.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for image views:

- Use an image view to show a picture, not to start an action. If people can click it, use a
  button with an image.
- Give the view the size the image should have, and let the image scale down to it. Do not
  enlarge small artwork.
- Use a template image for a glyph that should follow the text colour of its surroundings and
  both appearances.
- Centre a pane's hero image in its column; put its caption 10 pt below it, in the emphasised
  body font.

Sizes measured in System Preferences: a pane's hero image is 117 pt square, a user picture is
62 pt in the detail area and 24 pt in a list row, a monogram avatar 98 pt, a list icon 24 to
32 pt.

## Variants and states

<Example name="variants" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scaling | `scaling` | Shrink to fit (default), always fit, stretch, or own size |
| Circle mask | `mask="circle"` | The view is cut to a circle |
| Template | `template` | The image's alpha channel, filled with the surrounding text colour |
| Monogram | no `src`, initials as children | Initials on a grey disc; use with `mask="circle"` |
| Disabled | `disabled` | Grey and at half opacity, as the Guest User's picture in a disabled row |

A template image scales like a bitmap: by default it shrinks to fit the view and is never
enlarged. Its `<img>` sits inside a `<span>` the size of the view, which is the element that
takes `className`, `style` and `ref`.

Measured: the monogram's disc in both appearances, and its initials in Aqua: their colour and
the height of their capitals, a quarter of the view (24.5 pt in the 98 pt avatar). Inferred:
the light weight of the initials, from the 2.5 pt stems of the capture. Provisional: the
disabled look (the screenshot shows a grey picture; the exact treatment of a coloured one is
not shown), and the monogram's initials in Dark Aqua, which the capture draws in the disc's
own colour and which are white here so that they can be read.

An image view is not interactive and is drawn the same in a key and in an inactive window.

## Sizes

An image view has the size you give it: `width` and `height`, or CSS. A bitmap without a size
is shown at its own size, limited to the width of its container. A template without a size is
as large as its image; a monogram has no size of its own and needs one.

## API

<PropsTable name="ImageViewProps" />

A bitmap also takes every attribute of `<img>` (`srcSet`, `sizes`, `loading`, `decoding`); an
image given by `srcSet` alone, without `src`, is a bitmap too.

`onLoad` and `onError` (and their capture forms) are called with the `<img>` as
`currentTarget` in a bitmap and in a template; a monogram has no image and never calls them.
Every other event handler goes to the rendered element: the `<img>` of a bitmap, the `<span>`
of a template or a monogram.

A template image is read as a CSS mask, and the browser fetches a mask with CORS. An image on
the page's own origin, or a `data:` address, always works; an image from another origin must
be served with an `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header, and without one the template draws
nothing even though the same address works as a bitmap. The browser fetches the mask as soon
as the view is styled, whatever the `<img>` inside does: `loading="lazy"` defers a bitmap and
does not defer a template.

`aria-label` and `aria-labelledby` name the image in every form. One that is empty or only
white space names nothing and is left out, so the image is then decorative unless it has an
`alt`.

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-scaling` | `proportionally-down`, `proportionally-up-or-down`, `axes-independently`, `none` | Reflects `scaling`. |
| `data-mask` | `none`, `circle` | Reflects `mask`. |
| `data-kind` | `template`, `monogram` | On the `<span>` forms. |
| `data-disabled` | present or absent | Reflects `disabled`. |

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-image-view-position` | `center` | Where the image sits in a view of another shape (NSImageView.imageAlignment), as an `object-position` value. A template image that is scaled down or shown at its own size does not use it: set the two below. |
| `--mac-image-view-align` | `center` | Template image that is scaled down or shown at its own size: `start`, `center` or `end` on the block axis (top to bottom). |
| `--mac-image-view-justify` | `center` | The same on the inline axis; `start` is the leading side, so it follows the text direction. |

## Accessibility

- Give a meaningful image an `alt` that says what it shows. Leave `alt` empty for a decorative
  image: it is then hidden from assistive technology.
- A bitmap is a native `<img>`. A template image is a native `<img>` too, inside a `<span>`;
  only its colour is replaced. A monogram is a `<span>` with `role="img"` and the `alt` as its
  name; its initials are not read separately.
- The view is not focusable.
- Use `loading="lazy"` for bitmaps below the fold (it does not defer a template image, see
  above), and always give `width` and `height` so the page does not shift when the image
  arrives.
- In forced-colours mode a template image is drawn in the system text colour.

## Visual comparison

Only the monogram can be compared: every other image view in the screenshots shows Apple's
artwork. `visual-monogram` reproduces the avatar of the Screen Time pane. Results in Chromium
on Linux with the fallback font:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monogram, Aqua | 1.35% of pixels differ | 1.19% |
| Monogram, Aqua, initials masked, strict | 0.24% | 0.08% |
| Monogram, Dark Aqua | 2.81% | 3.17% |
| Monogram, Dark Aqua, initials masked, strict | 0.07% | 0.02% |

The masked comparisons count a pixel as different at half the usual colour distance
(`threshold` 0.05); what they find is antialiasing on the rim of the disc.

The disc matches; what differs in the first row is the initials, set in the fallback font
instead of San Francisco: the fallback's letters are wider and their strokes are cut
differently. On an Apple device the initials are San Francisco and this figure differs; it has
not been measured there. The unmasked Dark Aqua comparison scores the initials too: the
capture draws them in the disc's own colour, so they barely show, and the component
deliberately draws them in white; that is most of its difference.

The scaling of a template image is checked without a screenshot: `visual-template-scaling`
puts a 16 pt and a 128 by 64 pt template in 64 pt views and reads pixels to confirm that the
first stays 16 pt and the second shrinks to 64 by 32 pt, in both appearances.
`visual-template-alignment` does the same for `--mac-image-view-align` and
`--mac-image-view-justify`: a 16 pt template in the top leading corner of a 64 pt view, one in
the bottom trailing corner, a 128 by 64 pt template scaled down and placed at the bottom of
its view, and one shown at its own size, larger than its view, aligned to its leading edge.
