<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSRulerView` as TextEdit shows it above a rich-text document: a 32 pt strip that
measures the text container and holds a marker for each tab stop and indent of the selected
paragraphs. Base UI has no ruler; the strip is drawn with CSS gradients, the numbers are text
and the markers are clipped boxes.

The row of controls above the ruler in TextEdit (paragraph style, font, size, colours,
alignment, spacing, lists) is the text view's format bar. It is not part of this component: it
is made of small pop-up buttons,
[segmented controls](/components/segmented-control) and colour wells, laid out by the
application.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Ruler } from "macalicious/ruler";
import "macalicious/ruler.css";

<Ruler
  markers={[
    { type: "first-line-indent", position: 0 },
    { type: "head-indent", position: 0 },
    { type: "left", position: 72 },
    { type: "tail-indent", position: 468 },
  ]}
/>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add ruler`.

The ruler fills the width of its container; put it directly above the text it measures and
give both the same width. Positions are in points from the origin, whatever the units: an inch
is 72 pt. `origin` is where 0 is, by default 5 pt from the left edge, the left padding of a
text container.

With `onMarkersChange` the markers can be dragged and moved with the keyboard. The ruler does
not keep the markers itself: store the array it calls back with.

```tsx
const [markers, setMarkers] = useState<RulerMarker[]>([{ type: "left", position: 72 }]);

<Ruler aria-label="Paragraph ruler" markers={markers} onMarkersChange={setMarkers} />
```

To scroll the ruler with wide text, put it inside the scrolling content, as wide as the text.

## Guidelines

The macOS Human Interface Guidelines have no page for rulers. What follows describes AppKit's
ruler as the measured specification records it; it was not checked against an Apple document.

- Show a ruler only above text whose paragraphs have tab stops and indents to edit, and let
  people hide it (TextEdit: Format, Text, Show Ruler).
- Measure in the units of the user's locale.
- The markers show the paragraphs that are selected. When the selection changes, change the
  markers.
- A marker snaps to the ruler's ticks while it is dragged.

## Variants and states

<Example name="markers" both />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decorative | no `onMarkersChange` | The strip and its markers; hidden from assistive technology |
| Editable | `onMarkersChange` | The same look; each marker is a slider that can be dragged and focused |
| Left tab stop | marker `type: "left"` | A 6 x 9 pt triangle pointing right, standing on the rule, its left edge on the position |
| First-line indent | `"first-line-indent"` | A 9 x 2 pt bar on a 1 pt stem that reaches the rule |
| Head indent, tail indent | `"head-indent"`, `"tail-indent"` | A 9 x 6 pt triangle pointing down at the rule |
| Centre, right and decimal tab stop | `"center"`, `"right"`, `"decimal"` | A diamond, a triangle pointing left, a disc |
| Keyboard focus on a marker | Tab | Focus ring around the marker's box, in a key window only |
| Dragging | pointer held on a marker | No change of look; the marker follows the pointer from tick to tick |

Markers are translucent (black at 50%, white at 55% in Dark Aqua), so two markers at one
position darken where they overlap, as the first-line and head indent do at 0 in TextEdit. A
ruler looks the same in a key, main and inactive window.

Measured, in both appearances: the height, the rule, the four tick lengths and their
spacing, the position and colour of the numbers, every colour, and the left tab stop, the
first-line indent, the head indent and the tail indent. Provisional (no screenshot shows
them): the centre, right and decimal tab stops, the units other than inches, the focus ring
(AppKit's markers cannot be focused), snapping to ticks, and the look in an inactive window.
Not implemented: adding a tab stop by clicking the ruler, removing one by dragging it off, the
guide line that drops through the text while a marker is dragged, and a vertical ruler.

## Sizes

A ruler has one size: 32 pt high, which is 31 pt of fill and a 1 pt bottom border, with the
rule 15 pt under the top. Its width is its container's.

<Example name="units" both hideCode />

| Units | Numbered tick | Shorter ticks | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `inches` | every inch (72 pt), 11 pt long | half 4.5 pt, quarter 3.5 pt, eighth 2.5 pt long, 9 pt apart | Measured |
| `centimeters` | every centimetre (28.35 pt) | half | Inferred from the step cycles AppKit registers for the unit |
| `points` | every 100 pt | 50, 25 and 12.5 pt | Inferred |
| `picas` | every 10 picas (120 pt) | 5, 2.5 and 1.25 picas | Inferred |

## API

<PropsTable name="RulerProps" />

The root is a `div` and takes its attributes, `ref`, `className` and `style`. The ruler's own
`onPointerDown` and `onKeyDown` run after yours and do nothing when yours call
`preventDefault()`.

A marker (`RulerMarker`, exported as a type) is an object:

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `type` | `"left"`, `"center"`, `"right"`, `"decimal"`, `"first-line-indent"`, `"head-indent"`, `"tail-indent"` | A tab stop, named by its alignment, or one of the three indents. |
| `position` | `number` | Points from the origin. |
| `label` | `string`, optional | Accessible name of an editable marker. By default it is made from the type: “left tab stop”, “head indent”. |

| Attribute | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `data-units` | root | `inches`, `centimeters`, `points`, `picas` | Reflects `units`. |
| `data-type` | marker | the marker's `type` | Selects its shape. |
| `data-index` | marker | 0 and up | Its place in `markers`. |
| `data-force` | marker | `focus` | Pins the focus ring for documentation. |

| Class | Part |
| --- | --- |
| `.mac-ruler` | The strip; rule and ticks are its background |
| `.mac-ruler-numbers`, `.mac-ruler-number` | The layer of numbers, and a number |
| `.mac-ruler-marker` | A marker |

## Accessibility

- Without `onMarkersChange` the ruler is decoration and is `aria-hidden`: it repeats what the
  text shows. Offer the indents and tab stops through other controls as well.
- With it the ruler is a `group` (name it with `aria-label`) and every marker is a `slider`
  in the tab order, with its position in points as `aria-valuenow`, the position in the
  ruler's units as `aria-valuetext` (“1.5 in”), and a name made from its type or given by
  `label`. The numbers stay hidden from assistive technology.
- Keys on a focused marker: Right and Up Arrow move it one tick further from 0, Left and Down
  Arrow one tick nearer, Home to 0, End to the last tick inside the ruler. A tick is the
  smallest division of the units: an eighth of an inch.
- AppKit's markers are not in the tab order; here they are, because the web has no other way
  to move them with the keyboard. That is a deliberate difference.
- A marker is a small target: a 6 x 9 pt box, or 9 x 6 pt. Do not make the ruler the only
  way to set an indent.
- The focus ring is drawn only in a key window. In forced-colours mode the ruler keeps its
  rule and ticks and draws them, the markers and the numbers in the system text colour on
  the system background.
- The ruler's geometry is physical: 0 is on the left in every writing direction. A ruler
  whose origin is on the right, for right-to-left text, is not implemented.

markers differ from the averaged screenshot. The crops leave out the first and last point of

What no screenshot shows is checked with real input and pixel colours from the spec
(`visual-states`, both appearances): the rule, the border and three tick lengths at their
positions in a short ruler; the centre, right and decimal tab stops, whose shapes are
inferred; the focus ring around a marker after Tab; the marker one tick further after Right
Arrow; and the same marker focused in an inactive window, without a ring. Dragging with a
real pointer, the limits at both ends and the numbers of a resized ruler were exercised in
Chromium by the script `visual/output/b7/browser/ruler.mjs` when the component was finished;
that script is not part of `npm run check`. No comparison covers the units other than inches.
