<Example name="basic" both />

One notification in the Notifications view of a
[Notification Center](/components/notification-center). The banner that slides in at the top
right of the screen when a notification arrives is another component, the notification alert.

## Usage

```tsx
import { NotificationBanner } from "macalicious/notification-banner";
import "macalicious/notification-banner.css";

<NotificationBanner
  app="Music"
  icon={<MusicIcon />}
  time="now"
  title="Every Breaking Wave"
  onOpen={showInMusic}
  onClear={remove}
>
  U2 — Songs of Innocence
</NotificationBanner>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add notification-banner`.

The children are the message. The card has no blur of its own: it is an overlay on the
material of the panel behind it, so put it on a Notification Center or on a backdrop of your
own.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[Notifications](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503160812/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/notifications/):

- Write a short title that says what happened and a message that adds the detail; do not
  repeat the app's name, which the header shows.
- Make a click open the item the notification is about, not just the app.
- Do not put anything in a notification that people should not see on a locked or shared
  screen.

## Variants and states

<Example name="actions" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header | `app`, `icon`, `time` | 26 pt band: a 14 pt icon, the app's name in upper case, the time at the trailing edge in 11 pt |
| Title, subtitle, message | `title`, `subtitle`, children | Bold title, subtitle in the title's colour, message in the secondary colour on up to four lines |
| Thumbnail | `thumbnail` | 32 pt box at the trailing edge of the text |
| Can be opened | `onOpen` | The text is a button |
| Pressed | pointer down on the text | The text's area darkens by a tenth |
| Clear button | `onClear`, with the pointer over the card or the focus in it | A 14 pt disc with a cross takes the icon's place |
| Keyboard focus | Tab | Focus ring inside the text's area, or around the clear button |

Catalina blends the card with vibrancy, so one tint cannot reproduce every backdrop: the fill
is fitted to the card as captured over the sky of the default picture.

Measured: the card, its edge, corner, header band and the text colours and positions, in both
appearances. The corner is drawn with 7 pt, which matches the captured corner; the spec's
figure of 4 pt was read from a smaller crop. Provisional: the pressed look, the clear button
on a card (its colour is the one measured on a day heading's clear button), the thumbnail's
size and the subtitle; no capture shows them.

## Sizes

One size: 322 pt wide; a title and one line of message make 77 pt.
`--mac-notification-banner-width` changes the width.

## Accessibility

- The root is an `<article>`. Give it an `aria-label` when a list of notifications needs each
  one named.
- With `onOpen` the title, subtitle and message are one `<button>`, named by their text;
  Return and Space open the notification.
- The clear button comes before the text in the tab order and is named by `clearLabel`. It is
  shown under the pointer and whenever the focus is inside the card, so it is never focused
  while invisible.
- The icon is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. A thumbnail that carries
  information needs a text alternative of its own.
- Focus rings show only in the key window. In forced-colours mode the card has a border.

`visual-banner` reproduces the notification of the Notifications view over the panel colours
measured around it. Text is masked (the fallback font off Apple platforms) together with the
app icon, which is Apple artwork. Results in Chromium on Linux, at a colour distance of 0.05:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notification, Aqua | 0.17% of pixels differ | 0.16% |
| Notification, Dark Aqua | 0.06% | 0.00% |

What differs is the glow of the desktop picture under the card, which the backdrop follows
along one row only. A state check moves the pointer over the card and reads the colour of the
clear button's disc.
