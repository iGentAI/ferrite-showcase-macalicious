An alert (`NSAlert`) interrupts: nothing else in the application can be used until it is
answered. The parts are built on Base UI's
[Alert Dialog](https://base-ui.com/react/components/alert-dialog) and keep its anatomy;
`AlertPopup` combines Base UI's portal and popup with the library's
[Window](/components/window), and lays out the icon and the text column, because an alert is
always assembled the same way.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Alert,
  AlertButtons,
  AlertClose,
  AlertInformativeText,
  AlertMessage,
  AlertPopup,
  AlertTrigger,
} from "macalicious/alert";
import { DefaultButton } from "macalicious/default-button";
import { PushButton } from "macalicious/push-button";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<Alert>
  <AlertTrigger render={<PushButton />}>Empty Trash…</AlertTrigger>
  <AlertPopup icon={<TrashIcon />}>
    <AlertMessage>Are you sure you want to permanently erase the items in the Trash?</AlertMessage>
    <AlertInformativeText>You can’t undo this action.</AlertInformativeText>
    <AlertButtons>
      <AlertClose>Cancel</AlertClose>
      <AlertClose render={<DefaultButton />} onClick={emptyTrash}>Empty Trash</AlertClose>
    </AlertButtons>
  </AlertPopup>
</Alert>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add alert`.

- Open it with `AlertTrigger`, or control it with `open` and `onOpenChange` on `Alert`.
- `icon` is the application's icon, or the icon of what the alert is about, 64 pt (32 pt in the
  compact kind). The library ships no artwork; pass an image or a drawing of your own.
- `AlertMessage` is NSAlert's message text and names the alert; `AlertInformativeText` is the
  smaller explanation and describes it. `AlertAccessory` holds the suppression checkbox or a
  view of your own under the text.
- `AlertButtons` sets its children at the trailing end, the default button last. `AlertClose`
  is a [Push Button](/components/push-button) that answers and closes the alert;
  `render={<DefaultButton />}` makes it the [Default Button](/components/default-button).
  `leading` takes a third button or a [Help Button](/components/help-button) for the other end
  of the row.
- To attach an alert to one window instead, put the same content in a
  [Sheet](/components/sheet).
- The alert does not move by itself. A window manager moves it with
  `onDragRegionPointerDown` and `onDragRegionDoubleClick` on `AlertPopup`: they report a press
  and a double click on the title bar of a regular alert, and on the body of the compact kind,
  which has no title bar, but never one on a button or another control.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Alerts](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/alerts/):

- Use alerts sparingly, for something people must know or decide now. An alert that appears
  often is dismissed unread.
- Write a message that is a short, complete statement or question, and informative text that
  adds what the message does not say. Do not repeat the message.
- Give buttons titles that say what they do (“Erase”, “Empty Trash”) instead of “Yes” and
  “No”, and include Cancel whenever the action can be avoided.
- Put the button people are most likely to choose at the trailing end and make it the default.
  Do not make a destructive button the default: make Cancel the default, or leave the alert
  without one.
- Use the caution badge (`alertStyle="critical"`) only for an action that may destroy data.
- Offer the suppression checkbox for an alert people may see repeatedly and no longer need.

## Sizes

A regular alert is 420 pt wide: the icon 24 pt from the left edge, the text column 106 pt from
it and 20 pt from the right edge, 20 pt between the text and the buttons, 18 pt below them. The
buttons are 14 pt apart (15 pt between their bezels) and at least 84 pt wide
(`--mac-alert-button-min-width`). The compact kind is 399 pt wide with a 32 pt icon. `width`
changes the frame's width; the height follows the text.

## Accessibility

- The alert is an `alertdialog`, named by its `AlertMessage` and described by its
  `AlertInformativeText`, so both are read when it opens. It is modal: the rest of the page is
  inert and hidden from assistive technology, and a click outside does not close it.
- When it opens the focus goes to the window itself, as in AppKit without full keyboard access,
  so that Space cannot press a button by accident; Tab then reaches the buttons. Mark an
  element `data-autofocus`, or pass `initialFocus`, to start elsewhere; a marked element that
  cannot take the focus (disabled, inert, hidden or not focusable) is passed over. When it
  closes the focus returns to the control that opened it. An alert opened without an
  `AlertTrigger`, from a menu item that is gone by then or from your own state, has no such
  control: name where the focus goes with `finalFocus` on `AlertPopup`.
- Return activates the default button from anywhere in the alert. Escape closes the alert
  without running any button's action, which is what Cancel means; `onOpenChange` reports it.
- The icon and its badge are decoration and hidden from assistive technology: say in the
  message what the badge implies.
- In forced colours the badge uses the system's mark colours.
