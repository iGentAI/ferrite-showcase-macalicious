<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit draws the title bar and an attached toolbar as one continuous surface. `TitleBar` is
that surface: window controls at the leading edge, the title centred on the window, and its
children, usually a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar), below or beside them. Base UI has no title
bar, so the element is written here.

## Usage

```tsx
import { TitleBar } from "macalicious/title-bar";
import { Toolbar } from "macalicious/toolbar";
import { WindowControls } from "macalicious/window-controls";
import "macalicious/title-bar.css";

<TitleBar variant="unified" title="Network" controls={<WindowControls />}>
  <Toolbar aria-label="Finder">{/* toolbar items */}</Toolbar>
</TitleBar>
```

Inside a [Window](/components/window) the title names the window for assistive technology with
nothing to wire up.

## Guidelines

Not verified: this section paraphrases Apple's Human Interface Guidelines and was not checked
against Apple's text.

- Title a window with the display name of what it shows: the document, the folder, the pane.
  Never a file path.
- A document window may show a 16 pt proxy icon before its title: pass `icon`.
- Hide the title only when the window's content makes it obvious, as Safari and System
  Preferences do, and keep the toolbar on the controls' row (`variant="title-hidden"`).
- The whole bar moves the window. Do not put controls in it that need a drag gesture of their
  own.

## Variants and states

<Example name="variants" both />

| Variant | Height | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `standard` | 22 pt | Plain title bar: dialogs, Get Info, Go to Folder |
| `unified` | 22 pt strip plus the toolbar: 55 pt with an icon-only toolbar, 68 pt with labels | Finder, Preview, Console |
| `title-hidden` | 38 pt | Controls and toolbar on one row; a title, if given, is centred over it: Safari, System Preferences |
| `utility` | 16 pt strip, plus a toolbar if there are children | Utility panels such as Help; use small window controls |

| State | How it is set | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Key or main window | no attribute, `key` or `main` on the window | Vertical gradient under two highlight rows; a shadow row and a separator at the bottom |
| Inactive window | `data-window-state="inactive"` on an ancestor | Flat fill, dimmed title, lighter bottom rows |
| Followed by another bar | `divider="bar"` | The lighter pair of bottom rows Catalina draws above a tab bar or a scope bar |
| Followed by a bar, Dictionary's way | `divider="line"` | Both bottom rows in one colour: a single 1 pt line; the fill is the upper part of the gradient that the bar shares with the scope bar |
| Full screen | `data-full-screen` on the window | Square top corners |

The two top rows and the two bottom rows are each one device pixel and are part of the bar's
height. The top rows are measured under a window's inner rim, so a bar outside a
[Window](/components/window) is a little dimmer along its top edge. A title that does not fit
beside the controls starts right after them and is cut at the end.

## Translucent

<Example name="translucent" both />

A movie window has no opaque bar: QuickTime Player lays its title bar over the top of the
picture, and the picture shows through. `material="translucent"` gives the bar that fill, a dark
tint over a blur of whatever is behind it, in any of the four layouts. It is dark in Aqua and
in Dark Aqua alike: the bar carries `data-appearance="dark"`, so its window controls, its title
and a toolbar inside it are drawn in Dark Aqua. The material only fills the bar. Laying the bar
over the content is the window's layout: position the bar yourself, as the example does.

| Material | Look | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `opaque` | The gradient of the table above | Every ordinary window |
| `translucent` | A dark tint, 79% to 82% opaque, over a blur of what is behind; a flatter tint in an inactive window (provisional); opaque greys with Reduce transparency (provisional) | A bar laid over a picture or a movie |

## Drag region

The bar reports, and does nothing else. `onDragRegionPointerDown` runs when the primary button
goes down on the bar itself; `onDragRegionDoubleClick` when the bar itself is double-clicked.
Neither runs for a press on a control inside the bar: a button, link, field, checkbox, switch,
radio button, tab, slider, combo box, list, menu, a label (a press on it goes to its control),
editable text, or anything marked `data-window-no-drag`. Neither runs either when the bar's own
`onPointerDown` or `onDoubleClick`, or a handler that saw the event first, cancels it with
`preventDefault()`, so a window can veto a press on its bar. The bar carries
`data-window-drag-region`, so a manager can also find it from an event on the window. A window
without a title bar makes other surfaces drag regions with `windowDragRegion()` from
`macalicious/lib/window-drag-region` ([Window](/components/window)), which applies the same test.

## Sizes

A title bar has no `size` prop; its height follows from `variant` (the table above): 22 pt,
38 pt, a 22 pt strip plus the toolbar, or the 16 pt utility strip. Its width is the window's.

## API

<PropsTable name="TitleBarProps" />

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-variant` | `standard`, `unified`, `title-hidden`, `utility` | Reflects `variant`. |
| `data-title-bar` | as `data-variant` | The same value under a name that toolbar items inside select on. |
| `data-divider` | `content`, `bar`, `line` | Reflects `divider`. |
| `data-material` | `opaque`, `translucent` | Reflects `material`. |
| `data-appearance` | `dark`, or the caller's | A translucent bar always carries `dark`: it is dark in both appearances, whatever the caller passes. An opaque bar sets nothing and keeps a `data-appearance` its caller gives it. |
| `data-window-drag-region` | present | Marks the bar as the window's drag region. |

Parts: `.mac-title-bar-controls`, `.mac-title-bar-title`, `.mac-title-bar-icon`,
`.mac-title-bar-title-text` and `.mac-title-bar-content` (the children).

## Accessibility

- The title element gets the id that its `Window` points `aria-labelledby` at. Outside a
  `Window` the title is plain text.
- The proxy icon is decorative and hidden from assistive technology.
- The drag region is pointer-only by design: moving, zooming and minimizing must also be
  available from the window controls or a menu command. That is why the bar has pointer
  handlers without a role.
- The window controls and toolbar items inside keep their own keyboard behaviour.
