<Example name="basic" both />

Finder's icon view: items laid out left to right and top to bottom as icons with a name of up
to two lines under each. The grid fills the width with as many columns as fit, scrolls in a
[scroll view](/components/scroll-view) and renders only the rows in view, so it can hold
thousands of items. The artwork is yours: the view draws the grid, the labels and the
selection.

## Usage

```tsx
import { IconView } from "macalicious/icon-view";
import "macalicious/scroll-view.css";
import "macalicious/icon-view.css";

const renderIcon = (index: number) => <img src={files[index].icon} width={64} height={64} alt="" />;

<IconView
  aria-label="Documents"
  count={files.length}
  renderIcon={renderIcon}
  getItemLabel={(index) => files[index].name}
  getItemId={(index) => files[index].id}
  value={selected}
  onValueChange={setSelected}
  onItemAction={(index) => open(files[index])}
  style={{ height: 400 }}
/>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add icon-view`.

The view does not hold your data. It asks for `count` items and calls `renderIcon(index)` and
`getItemLabel(index)` for the ones in view.

- Give the root a height (or a flex or grid cell that has one). The width decides the number
  of columns: as many `cellWidth` cells as fit with at least 8 pt between them, then the same
  whole number of points between the columns and to both edges (an odd point goes to the
  trailing edge). That is Finder's layout: a
  915 pt wide Applications window has eight columns 113 pt apart, a 950 pt wide one eight
  columns 117 pt apart.
- `renderIcon` returns artwork of at most `iconSize` on each side; it is centred in the icon
  box and hidden from assistive technology, because the label names the item. It is artwork
  only: do not put a button, a field or another control in it. Define the
  function outside the component, or with `useCallback`: items are memoised, and with a stable
  function a selection change re-renders only the items it touches.
- The selection holds item ids. `getItemId` maps an index to an id (default: the index); use
  real ids when items can be added, removed or sorted.
- `renderTag` puts something before the name on the label's line, such as a
  [tag dot](/components/tag-dot) at its small size.
- `onItemAction` is "open": a double click, Return, or Command-Down Arrow.

### Large collections

<Example name="large" hideCode />

Measured in Chromium with this example (5,000 items in a 600 x 360 pt view, React's
development build): five columns; 35 item elements in 7 rows and 249 elements in all under
the view at rest, at most 55 items while scrolling. Twenty scroll steps from the top to the
bottom, waiting one animation frame after each, took 322 ms in all, 16 ms per step: one frame
each, with the rows rendered before the frame is painted. Selecting an item changes the DOM of
two items (the old and the new selection). The listbox carries all event handlers; items have
none. The width is observed through the library's one shared `ResizeObserver`.

## Guidelines

Catalina's Human Interface Guidelines have no page for icon or collection views (the Finder
and Window Anatomy pages, which the specification cites, do not describe them). What follows
is Finder's own behaviour, as the specification records it:

- Use an icon view when people recognise items by their picture: applications, folders,
  images, documents with previews. Use a [list view](/components/list-view) or a table when
  they compare names, dates or sizes.
- Keep names short. A name takes at most two lines; Finder truncates a longer one in the
  middle, this view at the end.
- A click selects, a double click opens, and a click on the background deselects. Do not put
  buttons inside an item; offer further actions in a toolbar or contextual menu.
- Let people choose the icon size where it matters. Finder offers 16 to 512 pt and shows
  64 pt by default.

## Variants and states

<Example name="variants" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | default | Icon in its box, name centred under it in 12 pt text, up to two lines |
| Selected, view without focus | click an item, focus elsewhere | A rounded backdrop 4 pt around the icon box (black at 10%, white at 15% in Dark Aqua) and a grey rounded highlight behind the name (`#dcdcdc`, `#464646`) |
| Selected, view with focus | click an item or Tab to the view, in a key window | The same backdrop; the name's highlight is the accent colour and its text white |
| Inactive or main window | `data-window-state` on an ancestor | The highlight stays grey also while the view has focus; no focus ring |
| Keyboard focus | Tab | Focus ring inside the view's edge, never around an item |
| Tag | `renderTag` | What it returns, 6 pt before the name |
| Icon size | `iconSize` | The icon box; cells and rows grow with it |
| Single or no selection | `selectionMode` | `"single"` selects one item at a time, `"none"` nothing |

Measured: the grid of 64 pt icons (cell width 103 pt, rows 116 pt apart, icon box 8 pt below
the row's top, label position and its 16 pt line pitch) and of 160 pt icons (cell width
177 pt, rows 226 pt apart), the whole-point gaps and the centring in four windows of different
widths, the background and label colours, and the tag dot's place. Provisional: everything
about the selection. No Finder window in the reference screenshots has a selected icon, so the
backdrop, the label highlight, its radius and padding are the specification's inferred values,
with the measured selection colours of lists. Also provisional: the least gap of 8 pt that
decides when a column is dropped (the screenshots bound it between 4 and 9 pt), and rows
taller than the default for sizes other than 160 pt.

Not implemented: selection by dragging a rectangle, drag and drop, renaming in place,
labels on the right, middle truncation, and Finder's free placement of icons that are not
arranged on the grid. Page Up and Page Down scroll and do not move the selection.

## Sizes

An icon view has no control sizes. Its cells follow `iconSize`:

| `iconSize` | Default `cellWidth` | Default `rowHeight` | Seen in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 (default) | 103 | 116 | Applications, Utilities |
| 160 | 177 | 212; the Home window has 226 | Home folder |
| other | `iconSize + 17`, at least 103 | `iconSize + 52` | not in the screenshots |

A row holds 4 pt, the backdrop (the icon box and 4 pt around it), 3 pt, two label lines of
16 pt and 5 pt. A taller `rowHeight` keeps that content in the row's middle, a point up.

## Accessibility

- The scrolling element is a `listbox` with `aria-multiselectable`, and each item an `option`
  with `aria-selected`, `aria-setsize` and `aria-posinset`. A listbox rather than a `grid`:
  the items are one ordered set, and its rows are an effect of the width that changes when the
  view is resized. A grid would have to announce row and column positions that mean nothing,
  and needs row elements that this layout does not have. Arrow keys in both dimensions work
  in a listbox all the same.
- Give the view a name with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. An item's name is its label;
  the icon is hidden from assistive technology.
- The view is one tab stop. Focus stays on the listbox, which names the current item with
  `aria-activedescendant`. An item out of view is not in the document and cannot be named,
  so the current item is scrolled into view when the keyboard focus enters the view, and
  when the selection is changed from outside while the view has the focus. Scrolling the
  current item out of view with the pointer leaves it unnamed until the next key.
- Keys: Left and Right Arrow move to the previous and next item (reversed in a right-to-left
  layout) and continue on the neighbouring row; Up and Down Arrow move by one row, and Down
  Arrow above a short last row goes to the last item; Home and End go to the first and last
  item; typing the start of a name selects it, and repeating a letter steps through the names
  that start with it; Return and Command-Down Arrow call `onItemAction`. In
  `selectionMode="multiple"` Shift with a movement key extends the selection over every item
  between, Command-click (Control-click) toggles an item, Shift-click extends, and Command-A
  selects all. Control with a movement key moves the current item without changing the
  selection, and Control-Space adds the current item to the selection or takes it out;
  Finder has no such keys, they are here so that any set of items can be selected without a
  pointer.
- Differences from Finder: there Return renames and Space shows Quick Look; here Return opens,
  as Command-Down Arrow does, and Space scrolls by a page, as do Page Up and Page Down. Shift
  extends over the items in reading order, not over a rectangle.
- Only the icon and the name take the pointer, as in Finder; the rest of a cell is background.
  Make the artwork fill the icon box if a larger target is wanted.
- The item moved to is scrolled into view. The emphasised highlight and the focus ring are
  drawn only in a key window. In forced-colours mode a selected item has an outline round its
  icon box and its name uses the system highlight colours.

The selection is in no screenshot. It is checked with `expect` comparisons on a three-item
view with real input: at rest (backdrop and grey highlight), after a click on another icon
(backdrop and accent highlight there, nothing on the first), after Tab and Right Arrow (focus
ring inside the edge, selection moved), and after a click and Tab in an inactive window (grey
highlight, no ring), in Aqua and Dark Aqua. The at-rest check and the Tab and Right Arrow
check now run in Dark Aqua too, and the accent highlight after a click is also checked with
the purple accent in both appearances. Those colours are the specification's inferred
values, so the checks prove that the view draws what the specification says, not that Finder
looked exactly so. The thumbnail grid of Photos, which the specification lists with this
view, has cells without labels and its own spacing; it is not reproduced or compared.
