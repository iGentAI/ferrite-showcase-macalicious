title: Level Indicator
description: Shows a value within a range as a capacity bar, a row of segments, a rating or a relevance.

<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSLevelIndicator`. It is built on
[Base UI's Meter](https://base-ui.com/react/components/meter), which supplies the `meter`
role and the value attributes. A level indicator shows a quantity, such as free space or a
signal strength; for the progress of a task use a [progress bar](/components/progress-bar).

## Usage

```tsx
import { LevelIndicator } from "macalicious/level-indicator";
import "macalicious/level-indicator.css";

<LevelIndicator value={62} aria-label="Input level" />
<LevelIndicator variant="discrete" value={3} max={5} aria-label="Signal strength" />
<LevelIndicator variant="rating" value={4} max={5} aria-label="Rating" />
<LevelIndicator variant="rating" editable value={stars} onValueChange={setStars} max={5} aria-label="Rating" />
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add level-indicator`.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for level indicators:

- Use the continuous style for a large range and the discrete style for a small one. A
  discrete segment is full or empty, never partly filled.
- Use tick marks only with the continuous style, to give the level a scale.
- The fill is green by default. Change it at a warning and at a critical level only when
  those levels mean something to people (a disk that is nearly full, a battery that is nearly
  empty).
- Introduce the indicator with a label that ends in a colon, to its leading side and centred
  on it.
- Use level indicators in views, not in window frames.

## Variants and states

<Example name="styles" both />

| Style | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous capacity | default | 12 pt track with 2 pt corners that fills from the leading end; optional tick marks below |
| Discrete capacity | `variant="discrete"` | `max - min` equal segments, 2 pt apart; whole segments fill |
| Rating | `variant="rating"` | `max - min` stars, 13 pt at a 16 pt pitch |
| Relevancy | `variant="relevancy"` | Thin vertical bars; the filled part is darker |
| Storage | `variant="storage"` | Disk Utility's capacity bar: 24 pt, bordered, with an outlined blue used part |

<Example name="levels" both />

| Level | When | Fill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | – | System green |
| Warning | the value has reached `warningValue` | System yellow |
| Critical | the value has reached `criticalValue` | System red |

When `criticalValue` is lower than `warningValue`, low values are the problem and the fill
changes at or below each limit; otherwise it changes at or above each. Levels apply to the
continuous and discrete styles.

With `tiered` (NSLevelIndicator's `drawsTieredCapacityLevels`) the fill is not one colour:
each part of a continuous fill, and each filled segment, keeps the colour of the level it lies
in, so a nearly full disk shows green, then yellow, then red. No screenshot shows a tiered
indicator; where the colours change follows from the two values, and the colours are the same
three.

Measured: the storage style, from Disk Utility, in both appearances. Everything else is
provisional: no screenshot shows a standard `NSLevelIndicator` (the Sound pane is captured on
its Sound Effects tab, not on Input). The standard styles follow AppKit's documented metrics;
their track reuses the measured track of the progress bar, their fills are the system colours,
and the star is original artwork, not Apple's glyph.

A level indicator is drawn the same in a key and in an inactive window.

### Editable

<Example name="editable" both />

With `editable` (`NSLevelIndicator.isEditable`) people set the value themselves, as with the
star rating of a song. The indicator is then built on
[Base UI's Slider](https://base-ui.com/react/components/slider) instead of the Meter and
looks the same.

| Input | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Click | Sets the value to the point clicked. A segment or a star fills when any part of it is clicked |
| Drag | The value follows the pointer; dragging past the leading end sets `min` |
| Left or Down Arrow, Right or Up Arrow | One step down or up: one segment or star, or `step` |
| Page Down, Page Up, or Shift with an arrow key | Ten steps |
| Home, End | `min`, `max` |

Use it controlled (`value` with `onValueChange`) or uncontrolled (`defaultValue`).
`onValueCommitted` fires once when a drag or a key press ends, for work that should not run on
every step. `name` submits the value with a form. `disabled` dims the indicator and takes it
out of the tab order. Keyboard focus shows the focus ring around the whole indicator, in the
key window only.

Everything about the editable form is provisional: no screenshot shows one.

## Sizes

One size. The capacity styles are as wide as their container and 12 pt high (24 pt for
storage); a rating is as wide as its stars.

## API

<PropsTable name="LevelIndicatorProps" />

A read-only indicator passes `format`, `locale`, `getAriaValueText` and `aria-valuetext` to
Base UI's `Meter.Root`; an editable one passes them, with `step`, `name` and `disabled`, to
Base UI's `Slider`. `max` defaults to 100: for the discrete and rating styles set it to the
number of segments or stars.

The root takes the attributes of Base UI's `Meter.Root`: `className` and `style` may be
functions of the component's state, and `render` an element or a function. A read-only
indicator renders `Meter.Root` and those functions receive the meter's state; an editable one
renders `Slider.Root` and they receive the slider's state (`disabled`, `dragging` and so on).
The three props are typed accordingly: with `editable` the functions are typed with the
slider's state, without it with the meter's.

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-variant` | `continuous`, `discrete`, `rating`, `relevancy`, `storage` | Reflects `variant`. |
| `data-level` | `normal`, `warning`, `critical` | The level reached; only on the continuous and discrete styles. With `tiered`, also on each filled segment: that segment's own level. |
| `data-tiered` | `rising`, `falling` | Present with `tiered` and a warning or critical value; `falling` when low values are the problem. |
| `data-editable` | present or absent | Reflects `editable`. |
| `data-disabled` | present or absent | Reflects `disabled`. |
| `data-dragging` | present or absent | While an editable indicator is being dragged; set by Base UI. |
| `data-filled` | present or absent | On a filled segment or star. |

The storage colours are the tokens `--mac-level-indicator-storage-*`, declared in
`level-indicator.tokens.css`.

## Accessibility

- A read-only indicator has `role="meter"` with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and
  `aria-valuemax`. It is not focusable.
- An editable indicator is a slider: a visually hidden `<input type="range">` holds the value,
  takes keyboard focus and is what assistive technology operates. It is one tab stop.
- It needs a name: `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby` pointing at its label.
- `aria-describedby` and `aria-details` go to the element that has the role. On an editable
  indicator that is the range input, and Base UI has no prop that puts `aria-details` on it:
  the component sets it when the input mounts, so markup rendered on a server carries
  `aria-details` only after hydration. A read-only indicator has it in its server markup.
- Use `getAriaValueText` when the number alone is not the natural reading (“3 of 5 stars”).
- A warning or critical level is shown by colour only. Say it in text as well, for example in
  `aria-valuetext` (“95% used, almost full”).
- In forced-colours mode the track is outlined and the fill uses the system highlight colour,
  in every style and with `tiered` too: the levels are then not told apart by colour.

## Visual comparison

`visual-disk-utility-start-light`, `visual-disk-utility-start-dark` and
`visual-disk-utility-end` reproduce the two ends of the capacity bar in Disk Utility (the
volume is fuller in the Aqua capture than in the Dark Aqua one). They count a pixel as
different at a colour distance of 0.05, half the default, so that the corners are judged.
Results in Chromium on Linux:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leading end, Aqua | 0.10% of pixels differ | 0.05% |
| Leading end, Dark Aqua | 0.15% | 0.15% |
| Trailing end, Aqua | 0.09% | 0.00% |
| Trailing end, Dark Aqua | 0.21% | 0.59% |

The pixels that differ are in the antialiasing of the corners. The corner radius is 4.5 pt.
The measured spec first read 3.5 pt from the capture's pixel grid and, after a least-squares
fit over eight corners, now gives 5 pt (4.75 to 5.125 pt on the outer edge of the border).
Rendered against the same four crops, 4.5 pt differs least overall: the four 2x figures above
add up to 0.55%, against 0.65% at 5 pt (0.17%, 0.16%, 0.00% and 0.32%) and 1.84% at 3.5 pt.
It is within half a point of the fit; 5 pt is better for the trailing end in Aqua alone.

The standard styles have no comparison: no screenshot shows them. The editable form is checked
with real input instead: `visual-editable` is clicked on its fourth star and on its seventh
segment, and stepped with the Right Arrow key, and the colours of the stars and segments on
either side of the new value, and of the focus ring, are read back, in both appearances. Those
clicks land in the middle of a star or a segment. Which one a click at its edge fills depends
on layout, so a browser test clicks the first and the last pixel column of stars and segments
in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and reads the value and the number filled, also for an
indicator with more steps than segments and for a rating that is an item of a grid.

Give a discrete indicator room for its segments, which are at least 3 pt wide and 2 pt apart:
in less, the row is wider than the indicator and a click is no longer read as the segment
under it.
