Split View is what Catalina offers from the green button's menu: two applications share the
screen, each in a full-screen window of its own, and the one used last is active. This is
the system's tiling of two windows, not the [Split View](/components/split-view) that divides
one window into panes. Base UI has no such primitive.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  SplitViewFullScreen,
  SplitViewFullScreenDivider,
  SplitViewFullScreenTile,
} from "macalicious/split-view-full-screen";
import { TitleBar } from "macalicious/title-bar";
import { WindowBody } from "macalicious/window";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<SplitViewFullScreen defaultSplit={0.44} defaultActive="trailing" style={{ height: "100vh" }}>
  <SplitViewFullScreenTile side="leading">
    <TitleBar variant="title-hidden" title="Notes" />
    <WindowBody>…</WindowBody>
  </SplitViewFullScreenTile>
  <SplitViewFullScreenDivider />
  <SplitViewFullScreenTile side="trailing">
    <TitleBar variant="title-hidden" title="Browser" />
    <WindowBody>…</WindowBody>
  </SplitViewFullScreenTile>
</SplitViewFullScreen>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add split-view-full-screen`.

- The children are a leading tile, the divider and a trailing tile, in that order. Give the
  root the size of the screen.
- A tile is a full-screen [Window](/components/window): its children are a
  [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) and a body. The active tile's window is key and the
  other one inactive, so the bars and controls inside draw themselves accordingly. In the
  captures the tiles' bars have no window controls.
- `split` is the leading tile's share of the width the divider leaves, from 0 to 1. It
  stays between `min` and `max`, which stand for the two applications' minimum window widths.
- While the divider is dragged nothing renders: `onSplitChange` is called once, on release,
  and once for every key that moves the divider.

## Guidelines

The macOS Human Interface Guidelines have no page for the Split View of two applications
(their Split Views page is about panes in one window). A tile is a full-screen window, and
what they say about
[Full-Screen Mode](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503161459/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/app-architecture/fullscreen-mode/)
holds for it:

- Keep the toolbar visible when it is needed for the task, and hide it when the focus is on
  content.
- Keep every essential feature available without leaving full screen.
- Let people choose when to leave full screen: do not leave it for them.

## Variants and states

| State | How it is set | Look | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active tile | `active`, `defaultActive`; a press or keyboard focus in the other tile | Its window is key; the other is inactive, with the flat bar and grey controls | Measured: Safari active, Notes inactive |
| Split | `split`, `defaultSplit`; the divider | The leading tile's share | Measured once, 740 to 928 points; any other share follows from it |
| Divider dragged | pointer | The tiles follow the pointer; `data-dragging` on the root | Provisional: Catalina dims the tiles while they are resized, which is not drawn |
| Divider focused | Tab | Focus ring around the handle; none while the page is not key | Provisional: Catalina's divider takes no keyboard focus |
| Reset | double click on the divider | Equal tiles | Catalog (macOS User Guide, as the spec records it) |

## Sizes

The divider is 12 points wide and its handle 4 by 64, centred on the height of the screen,
with round ends; both are measured and fixed. The tiles share the rest: the leading one takes
`split` of it and the trailing one what is left. There is no `size` prop.

## Accessibility

- Each tile is the labelled `region` of [Window](/components/window), named by its title
  bar. Without a title in the bar give the tile `aria-label`.
- The divider is a focusable `separator` with `aria-orientation="vertical"`, the leading
  tile's share in percent as its value, and the leading tile in `aria-controls` (an `id`
  given to that tile is the one named, in server-rendered markup too when the tile is a
  direct child): the window
  splitter pattern of the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices. Its name is "Resize tiles"; pass
  `aria-label` to name the applications.
- Left and Right Arrow move the divider by `step` (1% of the width), Home and End take it to
  `min` and `max`. In a right-to-left page the arrows and the drag are mirrored. Catalina has
  no keyboard resizing in Split View: the keys are this library's addition.
- Keyboard focus moving into a tile makes it the active one, as a press does, so the key
  window is always the one being typed in.
- A touch on the divider resizes and does not pan the page (`touch-action: none`).
- In forced-colours mode the gutter is a band between two edges with the handle in the
  system's text colour.

State checks cover what no screenshot shows: the focus ring around the handle after Tab, and
the gutter 1% of the width further left or right after an arrow key.

Measured: the gutter's width and colour, the handle's size, place and colour, the tiles'
square frames and their bars. Provisional: the focus ring and the keyboard resizing, the
default limits of a quarter and three quarters of the width, and the tiles not dimming while
the divider is dragged.
