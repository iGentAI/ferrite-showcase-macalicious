A sheet (`NSWindow.beginSheet`) asks something about one window, usually a document: save,
export, print, confirm. It hangs from the bottom edge of the window's title bar or toolbar, has
no title bar of its own and square corners, and may be wider or narrower than its window. The
parts are built on Base UI's [Popover](https://base-ui.com/react/components/popover), whose
positioner keeps the sheet attached to its window wherever the window is, and keep its anatomy;
`SheetPopup` combines Base UI's portal, positioner and popup, because a sheet is always
assembled the same way.

## Usage

```tsx
import { useState } from "react";
import { DefaultButton } from "macalicious/default-button";
import { Sheet, SheetActions, SheetClose, SheetDescription, SheetPopup, SheetTitle } from "macalicious/sheet";
import { TitleBar } from "macalicious/title-bar";
import { Window, WindowBody } from "macalicious/window";
import { WindowControls } from "macalicious/window-controls";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
const [titleBar, setTitleBar] = useState<HTMLDivElement | null>(null);

<Window state={open ? "main" : "key"}>
  <TitleBar ref={setTitleBar} title="Untitled" controls={<WindowControls closeDisabled={open} />} />
  <WindowBody inert={open}>…</WindowBody>
</Window>
<Sheet open={open} onOpenChange={setOpen}>
  <SheetPopup anchor={titleBar} width={360}>
    <SheetTitle>Do you want to keep this new document “Untitled”?</SheetTitle>
    <SheetDescription>You can choose to save your changes, or delete this document.</SheetDescription>
    <SheetActions leading={<SheetClose onClick={discard}>Delete</SheetClose>}>
      <SheetClose>Cancel</SheetClose>
      <SheetClose render={<DefaultButton />} onClick={save}>Save</SheetClose>
    </SheetActions>
  </SheetPopup>
</Sheet>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add sheet`.

- `anchor` is what the sheet hangs from: the window's `TitleBar` (which holds the toolbar of a
  unified window), as an element or a ref. The sheet's top edge follows that element's bottom
  edge and the sheet is centred on it, also while the window moves.
- The window is yours to hold: while the sheet is open give it `state="main"`, make its body
  `inert` and disable its close button, as the example does. The sheet itself is a window of
  its own and is key. Other windows on the page stay usable.
- Open it with `open` and `onOpenChange`, or put a `SheetTrigger` inside `Sheet`.
- `SheetClose` is a [Push Button](/components/push-button) that closes the sheet;
  `render={<DefaultButton />}` makes it the [Default Button](/components/default-button), which
  Return activates. Give it an `onClick` for what the button does.
- A sheet's content is yours: `SheetTitle` and `SheetDescription` are the message and the
  informative text of an alert-like sheet, and anything else, such as a form, goes beside or
  instead of them.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Sheets](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/sheets/):

- Use a sheet for a request that concerns one window, so that people can still work in other
  windows while it waits. A request that concerns the whole application is a dialog or an alert.
- Attach sheets only to windows that have a frame, and do not open a sheet from a sheet: close
  the first one before the second opens.
- Keep a sheet small enough to leave its window recognisable behind it, and include a way to
  dismiss it without doing anything: a Cancel button, which Escape presses.
- In a tabbed window a sheet belongs to the tab that was active when it opened.

## Variants and states

| State | Look | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | The sheet material, a 0.5 pt edge on the sides and the bottom (Dark Aqua: darker, with a 1 pt lighter rim inside it), a shadow on the sides and below, nothing above the line it hangs from | Measured on the Print sheet in both appearances |
| Opening, closing | Slides down from under the line in 0.25 s and back up | Provisional: the screenshots are stills; AppKit's duration is about 0.2 to 0.25 s |
| Reduced motion | Fades in under the line instead of sliding | Provisional |
| Page in the background | The controls inside lose their accent, as in any window that is not key | Provisional |

`modal` is `"trap-focus"` by default: the keyboard stays in the sheet while the pointer still
reaches everything else, which is what document-modal means; the window underneath is held by
its own `inert` body. `modal={true}` also blocks the pointer everywhere else. A sheet is never
non-modal, so `false` is not accepted. `dismissible`
lets a click outside close the sheet, which Catalina never does.

## Sizes

A sheet takes the width its content needs, or `width`; its height follows its content, up to
the room between the line it hangs from and the bottom of the viewport, beyond which it
scrolls. The
margins are the window's 20 pt on all four sides, the button row sits 20 pt below the content
with the buttons 12 pt apart, and each dismissing button is at least 84 pt wide
(`--mac-sheet-button-min-width`). The Print sheet in the reference is 756 pt wide on a 460 pt
window.

## Accessibility

- The sheet is a `dialog` with `aria-modal="true"`, named by its `SheetTitle` and described by
  its `SheetDescription`. A sheet without a title needs an `aria-label`.
- When it opens, the focus goes to the element marked `data-autofocus`, else to the first text
  field, else to the sheet itself, as in AppKit without full keyboard access; `initialFocus`
  overrides that. Checkboxes, buttons and disabled controls are passed over. Tab stays inside
  the sheet, and when it closes the focus returns to the control that opened it.
- Escape closes it, which is what Cancel does; Return activates the default button. A click
  outside does not close it.
- While it is open, the rest of the page is hidden from assistive technology. The window it
  belongs to must also be unusable with the pointer: set `inert` on its body, as the examples
  do. `aria-modal` promises that.
- With reduced motion the sheet fades instead of sliding. In forced colours it has a border on
  the sides and the bottom, since its shadow and material are gone.

## Visual comparison

The reference shows one sheet, the Print dialog on a TextEdit window, in both appearances. Its
content is the print panel, a component of its own; `sheet.visual.json` compares what the sheet
draws: its bottom edge where it lies over the page of the window behind, with the material, the
edge, the rim and the shadow. No text is in the crop, so there is no masked twin. The sheet's
sides lie over the desktop picture in the captures and cannot be compared.

more than the threshold (0.05), and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| `print-bottom-edge-dark` | Dark-Print (700, 982, 200, 80) | 0.00% | 3.62 | 0.00% | 3.54 |

The mean difference is in two shared tokens that the sheet uses as they are: the fitted sheet
shadow is darker close to the edge than the capture's (`#d1d1d1` against `#dcdcdc` 2.5 pt below
it), and the dark sheet material comes out as `#262626` over the dark page where the capture has
`#2d2d2d`.

`attachment-light` and `attachment-dark` check pixels of a whole sheet instead: the page above
the line it hangs from is untouched (a sheet casts no shadow there), the fill is the material
over the page, and the edge and the dark rim have the measured colours. `tall-light` and
`tall-dark` give a sheet more content than fits below its line and check that its bottom edge
stays inside the page, so the sheet scrolls. All pass at 1x and 2x.

Measured: the square corners, the edge and its colours, the dark rim, the material over a known
background, the shadow below, the margins. Provisional: the slide and its duration, the
reduced-motion and forced-colours looks, the minimum button width (taken from Finder's
dialogs), and a sheet while the page is in the background.
