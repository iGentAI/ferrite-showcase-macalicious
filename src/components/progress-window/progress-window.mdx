<Example name="basic" both />

Finder opens this window when a copy, a move or an emptying of the Trash takes long enough to
be noticed. It is a [Window](/components/window) without a title whose title bar is transparent,
holding one `ProgressWindowItem` per operation; each item is built on the library's
[Progress Bar](/components/progress-bar).

## Usage

```tsx
import { ProgressWindow, ProgressWindowItem, ProgressWindowName } from "macalicious/progress-window";
import { WindowControls } from "macalicious/window-controls";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<ProgressWindow controls={<WindowControls closeDisabled zoomDisabled />}>
  <ProgressWindowItem
    icon={<DocumentIcon />}
    title={
      <>
        Copying “<ProgressWindowName>Holiday in the mountains, final cut.mov</ProgressWindowName>”
        to “Desktop”
      </>
    }
    status="192.9 MB of 6 GB - About 3 minutes"
    value={3}
    onStop={cancelCopy}
  />
</ProgressWindow>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add progress-window`.

- `value` runs from 0 to 100. Leave it out, or pass `null`, while the length of the work is
  not known yet: the bar is then indeterminate.
- `title` is one line. A plain string truncates at its end; wrap a file name in
  `ProgressWindowName` and that name truncates in the middle instead, keeping its last
  characters (`tail`, 12 by default) so that the extension stays readable.
- `status` is the smaller line under the bar: how much is done and how long it will take.
- The stop button appears when `onStop` is given.
- The window is presentational, like `Window`: it does not open, move or close itself. Show it
  when the work starts taking time and remove it when the work ends; a window manager gives it
  `state` and a position, and can move it from `onDragRegionPointerDown`.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Progress Indicators](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/indicators/progress-indicators/):

- Use a determinate bar whenever the duration can be estimated, and switch from indeterminate
  to determinate as soon as it can.
- Say what is happening, and report progress accurately and at an even pace; a bar that jumps
  to 90% and waits there misleads.
- Give people a way to stop the work, and stop it at once when they ask.
- Do not show a progress window for work that ends within a second or two.

## Sizes

The window is 399 pt wide unless `width` says otherwise, and 69 pt tall with one item. In
points from the window's top-left corner, as measured: the icon's 32 pt canvas at 12, 27.5;
title and bar from 57 to 364; the title's baseline at 33 (11 pt text); the bar from 40.5 to
46.5; the 14 pt stop button 9 pt after the bar; the status line's baseline at 62 (9 pt text).

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region`. It shows no title, so it is named by `aria-label`, “Progress”
  unless you give another name or an `aria-labelledby`.
- Each item is a `group` named by its title. Its bar is a `progressbar` with the same name; a
  status that is plain text is the bar's value text, so a screen reader says “192.9 MB of 6 GB”
  instead of a bare percentage, and any other status describes the bar.
- The stop button is a `button` named “Stop” (`stopLabel`) and described by the item's title,
  so that several stop buttons in one window can be told apart. Space and Return activate it.
- A name in `ProgressWindowName` stays in the document in full: only its drawing is truncated,
  so it is read and copied whole.
- Progress is not announced as it changes; announce the end of the work yourself if people
  need to know.

## Visual comparison

`progress-window.visual.json` compares Finder's Copy window, with 8 pt of its shadow on every
side, in both appearances. The `-drawing` twins mask the title, the status line and the
document icon, which is Apple's artwork (the examples draw a plain page in its place), and
score the frame, the window controls, the bar and the stop button at the tighter threshold
(0.05).

Scores from `npm run visual -- progress-window` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:
the share of pixels that differ by more than the comparison's threshold, and the mean colour
difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Reference (crop x, y, width, height) | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `finder-copy-light` | Light-Finder-Copy (96, 60, 830, 170) | 8.00% | 7.78 | 5.56% | 6.73 |
| `finder-copy-light-drawing` (masked) | Light-Finder-Copy (96, 60, 830, 170) | 3.42% | 1.15 | 0.13% | 0.56 |
| `finder-copy-dark` | Dark-Finder-Copy (96, 60, 830, 170) | 7.39% | 7.71 | 5.84% | 7.00 |
| `finder-copy-dark-drawing` (masked) | Dark-Finder-Copy (96, 60, 830, 170) | 2.44% | 0.72 | 0.16% | 0.56 |

The unmasked scores are mostly the text, set in the fallback font, and the stand-in icon.
`stop-pressed-light` and `stop-pressed-dark` hold the mouse button on the stop button and check
the disc's colour against the inferred pressed values; they pass at 1x and 2x.

Measured: the frame and its size, the transparent title bar's two highlight rows, the
positions and sizes of the icon, title, bar, stop button and status line, the stop button's
colour. Provisional: several items in one window, the pressed and focused stop button, the
window when it is not key, and the forced-colours look.
