<Example name="basic" both />

Finder's fourth view: the selected item shown large, the folder's other items as a strip of
thumbnails under it, and optionally the Preview pane with the item's details beside both.
AppKit has no public class for it; the strip is a horizontal list and scrolls in a
[scroll view](/components/scroll-view).

## Usage

```tsx
import { GalleryView } from "macalicious/gallery-view";
import "macalicious/scroll-view.css";
import "macalicious/gallery-view.css";

const [value, setValue] = useState<(string | number)[]>([files[0].id]);

<GalleryView
  aria-label="Downloads"
  count={files.length}
  getItemId={(index) => files[index].id}
  getItemLabel={(index) => files[index].name}
  renderPreview={(index) => <img src={files[index].url} alt="" style={{ maxWidth: "100%", maxHeight: "100%" }} />}
  renderThumbnail={(index) => <img src={files[index].thumbnail} alt="" width={48} />}
  value={value}
  onValueChange={setValue}
  onItemAction={(index) => open(files[index])}
  style={{ height: 480 }}
/>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add gallery-view`.

The view asks for its items by index. `renderThumbnail` is called only for the thumbnails in
view and `renderPreview` only for the selected item, so neither needs the whole list in the
document. Keep `renderThumbnail`, `renderPreview` and `getItemLabel` stable (module-level
functions or `useCallback`): then a selection change re-renders two thumbnails and scrolling
the strip does not render the preview again.

A thumbnail is at most 48 x 48 pt and is centred in its square: 48 x 36 for a 4:3 picture,
48 x 27 for 16:9. The preview is centred in the stage, 32 pt from its sides; size it with
`max-width: 100%` and `max-height: 100%` (or a width and an `aspect-ratio`) so that it fits.

The selection is `value`, an array holding the selected item's id, as in the
[list view](/components/list-view) and the other data views, so that one state can serve
several views of the same items. A gallery selects one item: the array has one id, or none.
Ids are the indexes unless `getItemId` gives others.

### The inspector pane

<Example name="inspector" />

`inspector` puts your content in a 250 pt pane after a 1 pt divider, where Finder shows the
Preview pane with the file's details and Quick Actions. The pane is a column flex container
without padding; Finder insets its content by 20 pt. What is in it is yours: the component
provides the pane, not the details.

### Many items

<Example name="large" />

The strip renders the thumbnails in view and eight more on each side. Measured in Chromium
with 5,000 items in a strip 620 pt wide (`examples/large`):

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Thumbnail elements in the document | 18 at rest, at most 27 while scrolling |
| Elements under the view, scroll bar included | 44 at rest, 60 after navigating |
| 20 jumps of 15,469 pt through the strip, a frame after each | 321 to 322 ms in three runs, which is the 20 frames |
| 20 Right Arrow presses in the middle of the items, a frame after each | 331 ms |
| Event handlers | the view's own are on the root only; none on a thumbnail (0 listeners per thumbnail). The scroll view adds its own to the strip and its scroll bar |

Scrolling by up to eight thumbnails a frame never shows an empty place. After a jump of
thousands of items (End, or dragging the scroll bar's knob) the thumbnails there are painted
one frame later. The arrow keys, Home, End and type-select scroll the selected thumbnail into
view, also when it was not rendered; scrolling the strip away from the selection keeps the
preview. With ids of your own, finding the selected item's index calls `getItemId` for up to
every item when the selection changes.

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines have no page for the gallery view. Their
[Finder page](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503170015/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/finder/)
describes what Finder shows beside it:

- The Preview pane shows information about the selected item and offers Quick Actions on it,
  such as rotating a picture or marking up a document, without opening an application. Put
  such details and actions in the `inspector`, and only those that apply to the selected item.
- Use a gallery for items people recognise by looking at them (pictures, documents with a
  meaningful first page). For items told apart by name or date, a list or a column view
  serves better. This point is this library's advice, not Apple's text.

## Variants and states

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thumbnail | default | Centred in a 54 pt square at a 62 pt pitch; the first square is 8 pt from the strip's edge |
| Selected thumbnail | `value` | A grey backdrop behind it (`#c0c0c0`, Dark Aqua `#565759`): the 54 pt square with corners rounded by 4 pt |
| Strip with the keyboard | Tab into the strip | The same grey backdrop; a focus ring inside the edge of the gallery (stage and strip), in a key window only |
| Window that is not key | `data-window-state` on an ancestor | The same grey backdrop, no focus ring |
| Nothing selected | `value` empty | An empty stage |
| Inspector | `inspector` | A 250 pt pane after a 1 pt divider (`#d5d5d5`, Dark Aqua black), on the same background |

Catalina does not tint the backdrop with the accent colour: it is grey in the key window of
both screenshots and in the inactive window of a third.

Measured in Finder's Downloads window, in both appearances: the strip's height and position,
the thumbnail pitch and inset, the backdrop's size, radius and colour, the preview's 32 pt
side margins and its vertical centring, the pane's width, the divider and the background.
Provisional (no screenshot shows them): the focus ring, which Finder does not draw; the 16 pt
above and under a preview that is limited by the stage's height; the backdrop in an inactive
Dark Aqua window. The backdrop is 54 x 54 pt with a 4 pt radius in the screenshots; the spec's
table says 54 x 52 and about 3.

Not done: selecting several items (Finder then shows a stack in the stage), Space for Quick
Look, and file names under the stage. The preview does not animate when the selection changes.

## Sizes

A gallery view has no control sizes. It is as large as its root element: give it a height, or
place it in a flex or grid cell that has one. The strip is 62 pt high and the stage takes the
rest.

## API

<PropsTable name="GalleryViewProps" />

The remaining props are those of a `div` and go to the root element with `ref`, `className`
and `style`; `aria-label` and `aria-labelledby` name the root and the strip. Your `onKeyDown`,
`onMouseDown` and `onDoubleClick` run before the view's own; `preventDefault()` in `onKeyDown`
cancels the view's handling of that key.

| Attribute | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `aria-selected` | thumbnail | `true`, `false` | The item is the selected one. |
| `data-index` | thumbnail | number | The item's index. |

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-gallery-view-background` | `var(--mac-color-control-background)` | Background of the gallery and the inspector. |

| Class | Part |
| --- | --- |
| `.mac-gallery-view` | Root |
| `.mac-gallery-view-main` | The gallery: stage above strip |
| `.mac-gallery-view-stage` | The preview's area, a `figure` |
| `.mac-gallery-view-strip`, `.mac-gallery-view-list` | The strip (a scroll view) and its scrolling element, the listbox |
| `.mac-gallery-view-items`, `.mac-gallery-view-item` | The row of thumbnails and one thumbnail's square |
| `.mac-gallery-view-inspector` | The pane |

## Accessibility

- The root is a `group` and the strip a `listbox` with `aria-orientation="horizontal"`; both
  take the name you give with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`, which is required. Each
  thumbnail is an `option` named by `getItemLabel`, with its position among all items
  (`aria-setsize`, `aria-posinset`), since most are not rendered.
- The stage is a `figure` named after the selected item, and the strip points to it with
  `aria-controls`. Give a picture in the preview an empty `alt` when the figure's name says
  what it is, or a description of its own.
- The strip is the view's one tab stop. It keeps focus and names the selected thumbnail with
  `aria-activedescendant`. A thumbnail out of view is not in the document and cannot be
  named, so the selected one is scrolled into view when it changes and when the keyboard
  focus enters the strip.
- Keys: Left and Right Arrow select the previous and the next item, Home and End the first
  and the last, typing selects the next item whose name starts with what was typed. Return
  calls `onItemAction`, as a double click does. In a right-to-left layout the strip runs
  leftwards and the arrows change roles.
- Keyboard focus draws one ring inside the edge of the gallery, in a key window only. The
  selection looks the same with and without focus, as in Finder, so the ring is the only
  sign of focus.
- A thumbnail takes content that is not interactive: it is an `option`, and assistive
  technology does not expose a button, checkbox, field or link inside one. Put controls in
  the inspector, or use a [table view](/components/table-view) for rows that need them. As a
  safeguard only, a press on such an element inside a thumbnail is left to it.
- Content of the inspector is yours and keeps its own semantics and keys.
- In forced-colours mode the backdrop is the system highlight colour.
