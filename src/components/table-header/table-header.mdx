<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSTableHeaderView` and its `NSTableHeaderCell`s. A [table view](/components/table-view)
and an [outline view](/components/outline-view) draw their headings with it; use it directly
only for a table you lay out yourself, or for a pane title that looks like a header.

## Usage

```tsx
import { TableHeader, TableHeaderCell } from "macalicious/table-header";
import "macalicious/table-header.css";

<div role="table" aria-label="Processes">
  <TableHeader>
    <TableHeaderCell width={240}>Process Name</TableHeaderCell>
    <TableHeaderCell align="center" sortDirection="descending" onSort={reverse}>
      % CPU
    </TableHeaderCell>
    <TableHeaderCell width={90} onResize={setWidth}>Threads</TableHeaderCell>
  </TableHeader>
</div>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add table-header`.

The header is a `row` and needs a `table`, `grid` or `treegrid` around it. It holds no state:
you say which column is sorted and how wide each column is, and it tells you when a heading
is clicked (`onSort`) or a column edge is dragged (`onResize`). A heading without a `width`
shares the remaining width; give the rows below the same widths.

A header can also draw its headings from a list, as a table view's header does: give it
`columns` in place of children, the `sort` to show, and `onSortChange` and `onColumnResize`
to hear of a click on a heading and of a dragged column edge.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[table views](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503153039/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/table-views/):

- Write headings as nouns or short noun phrases in title case, without a trailing colon.
- Let people click a heading to sort by that column, and click it again to reverse the
  order. Only the sorted column shows an indicator.
- Let people resize columns. A heading that is too narrow truncates its title; keep the
  title and the indicator visible at the minimum width.
- Do not centre headings out of habit: text columns read best left-aligned, and a centred
  heading belongs over a column of short, centred or numeric values.

## Variants and states

<Example name="variants" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | default | 23 pt grey fill and a 1 pt bottom border; 1 pt column separators inset 3 pt from the top and bottom; 11 pt regular titles |
| Plain | `variant="plain"` | The same header on the content background, with lighter lines |
| Translucent | `variant="translucent"` | The plain header, 95% opaque: what is behind it, such as the rows a table scrolls under it, shows through faintly, as in Console |
| Top border | `topBorder` | A 1 pt line above the fill, for a header that is not directly under a toolbar (25 pt in all) |
| Alignment | `align` on a cell | Title left-aligned 5 pt from the column's edge, centred, or right-aligned |
| Title inset | `--mac-table-header-title-inset` | The space before and after every title, `5px 6px` by default: Console's titles start 2 pt from the column's edge (`2px 6px`), Keychain Access's 3.5 pt (`3.5px 2px`) |
| Title offset | `--mac-table-header-title-offset` | Moves every title down, or up when it is negative; a heading that sorts and a plain one move alike |
| Sorted | `sortDirection` on a cell | A 7 x 4 pt chevron 4 pt before the column's separator; the title keeps its weight and colour |
| Pressed | mouse button held on a sortable heading | The fill darkens (Aqua) or lightens (Dark Aqua) by about 8% |
| Resizing | drag on a column's trailing edge | Resize pointer; no other change |
| Keyboard focus | Tab to a heading | Focus ring inside the cell, in a key window only |

A header looks the same in a key, main and inactive window, without the focus ring. The last
heading has no separator after it.

The title of a heading that sorts is a button and a plain title is a `span`; both set their
capitals 7 pt under the fill's top, as measured on plain headings (Activity Monitor) and on
headings that sort (Console, Keychain Access).

Measured: the fill, borders, separators, title metrics and both indicators, in both
appearances, for the standard header (Activity Monitor, Keychain Access) and the plain one
(Console); the alpha of the translucent fill, 0.95, from the text of the row that Console's
header is over in both captures; the title insets of Console and Keychain Access.
Provisional (no screenshot shows them): the pressed fill, the focus ring, and the
look while a column is resized.

## Sizes

A header has one size: 24 pt with its bottom border, 25 pt with a top border as well. It is
as wide as its container. Columns take the `width` you give them, in points.

## API

<PropsTable name="TableHeaderProps" />

<PropsTable name="TableHeaderCellProps" />

Both parts are `div`s and take their attributes, `ref`, `className` and `style`.

A column of `columns` (`TableHeaderColumn`) has an `id`, a `title`, and optionally `width`,
`minWidth` (20), `maxWidth`, `align`, `headerAlign` (the heading alone, when it differs) and
`sortable`. A sort (`TableHeaderSort`) is `{ column, direction }`. They are the
[table view](/components/table-view)'s `TableViewColumn` and `TableViewSort`.

| Attribute | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `data-variant` | header | `standard`, `plain`, `translucent` | Reflects `variant`. |
| `data-top-border` | header | present or absent | Reflects `topBorder`. |
| `data-align` | cell | `left`, `center`, `right` | Reflects `align`. |
| `aria-sort` | cell | `ascending`, `descending`, `none` | The sort direction; `none` on a sortable column that is not sorted. |
| `data-direction` | indicator | `ascending`, `descending` | Which way the chevron points. |
| `data-dragging` | resize handle | present or absent | The column edge is being dragged. |
| `data-moving` | cell | present or absent | The heading is being dragged to another place. |

| Class | Part |
| --- | --- |
| `.mac-table-header` | The row |
| `.mac-table-header-cell` | A column heading |
| `.mac-table-header-title` | Its title: a `span`, or a `button` when the column sorts |
| `.mac-table-header-indicator` | The sort chevron |
| `.mac-table-header-resizer` | The resize handle over the trailing edge |

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-table-header-title-inset` | `5px 6px` | The space before and after every title: the padding of the title along the line, one value for both or the leading and then the trailing one. A sorted column keeps 16 pt after its title for the indicator whatever the second value. |
| `--mac-table-header-title-offset` | `0px` | Moves every title down by this length, or up when it is negative. |

Set them on the header, or on any ancestor: a table view's `style` reaches the header it
renders.

## Accessibility

- The header is a `row` of `columnheader`s. A sortable heading holds a real `button`, so it
  is in the tab order and is activated with Return or Space, and its `columnheader` carries
  `aria-sort`. The chevron is hidden from assistive technology.
- AppKit's headings are not in the tab order; here the sortable ones are, because the web
  has no other way to sort with the keyboard. That is a deliberate difference.
- Columns are resized from the keyboard with Option-Left and Option-Right Arrow on the
  focused heading, 10 pt at a time within `minWidth` and `maxWidth`. A heading that can be
  resized but not sorted is itself a tab stop for this. In a right-to-left layout the arrows
  follow the direction the edge moves on screen.
- The resize handle is for the pointer only; it is 7 pt wide over the 1 pt separator.
- With `onColumnMove`, a heading is dragged to another place or moved with Option-Shift-Left and
  Option-Shift-Right Arrow. The heading follows the pointer and the rows move when it is let go.
  A heading that neither sorts nor resizes is itself a tab stop for this.
- In forced-colours mode the border, separators and indicator use the system text colour.
