<Example name="basic" both />

Mission Control covers the desktop it is rendered into. It is a modal overlay built on Base
UI's [Dialog](https://base-ui.com/react/components/dialog): Escape, a click on the desktop, or
a chosen window or space closes it. `MissionControl` is the overlay, `MissionControlSpace` a
desktop in the Spaces bar and `MissionControlWindow` a window's thumbnail.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  MissionControl,
  MissionControlSpace,
  MissionControlWindow,
} from "macalicious/mission-control";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

const [desktop, setDesktop] = useState<HTMLDivElement | null>(null);
const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);

<div ref={setDesktop} style={{ position: "relative", height: "100vh" }}>
  …the desktop picture, the windows, the Dock…
  <MissionControl
    open={open}
    onOpenChange={setOpen}
    container={desktop}
    onAddSpace={addDesktop}
    spaces={
      <>
        <MissionControlSpace label="Desktop 1" current>
          <img src={desktopOne} alt="" />
        </MissionControlSpace>
        <MissionControlSpace label="Desktop 2" onClick={() => switchTo(2)}>
          <img src={desktopTwo} alt="" />
        </MissionControlSpace>
      </>
    }
  >
    <MissionControlWindow
      title="Notes"
      icon={<img src={notesIcon} alt="" />}
      frame={{ x: 80, y: 60, width: 640, height: 420 }}
      onClick={() => bringForward("notes")}
    >
      <NotesPicture />
    </MissionControlWindow>
  </MissionControl>
</div>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add mission-control`.

- **Render it into the desktop.** `container` is the element Mission Control covers, which
  must be positioned. Without it the overview covers the page.
- **It paints the desktop again.** The real windows are still on the desktop, so the overlay
  is opaque: set `--mac-mission-control-desktop` to the desktop picture (any CSS background),
  on the desktop element or on `MissionControl`. The Spaces bar is a material over it: it
  blurs what the overlay paints, in the same page ([Fidelity limits](/fidelity)).
- **Windows are data.** Each `MissionControlWindow` takes the window's `frame` on the
  desktop. Mission Control lays the thumbnails out from the frames (see
  [Sizes](/components/mission-control#sizes)); you do not position them.
- **What a thumbnail shows is yours.** The children are laid out at the frame's size and
  scaled down with the thumbnail: a picture of the window, or its elements rendered a second
  time. A browser cannot take a snapshot of part of a page, so there is no live thumbnail
  for free. The content is decoration: it is `inert` and hidden from assistive technology,
  and the button over it carries the window's title.
- **The Dock stays.** The overlay's `z-index` is `--mac-mission-control-layer` (890), above
  the windows of a [Window Manager](/components/window-manager) and below its Dock (900), and
  the thumbnails keep `--mac-mission-control-inset-bottom` (80 points) clear at the bottom.
  Hide a menu bar while Mission Control is open: the Spaces bar takes its place. A managed
  window in full screen is stacked above 1000 while it is key, over the overview: do not
  offer Mission Control while the manager's `fullScreenWindowId` is set, or take the window
  out of full screen first.

### With a window manager

<Example name="window-manager" />

`useWindowManager()` gives the open windows and `focus(id)`; each window's `title` and
`frame` are what a thumbnail needs. The hook has to be called below `<WindowManager>`, so the
overview is a component of its own inside the manager, and it is rendered into the manager's
element:

```tsx
import { MissionControl, MissionControlWindow } from "macalicious/mission-control";
import { useWindowManager } from "macalicious/window-manager";

function Overview({ open, onOpenChange, container }: OverviewProps) {
  const { windows, focus } = useWindowManager();
  return (
    <MissionControl open={open} onOpenChange={onOpenChange} container={container}>
      {windows
        .filter((window) => !window.minimized)
        .map((window) => (
          <MissionControlWindow
            key={window.id}
            title={window.title}
            frame={window.frame}
            onClick={() => focus(window.id)}
          >
            <Picture of={window.id} />
          </MissionControlWindow>
        ))}
    </MissionControl>
  );
}
```

`focus(id)` brings the window to the front, makes it key and moves the keyboard focus into
it; Mission Control then closes and leaves the focus there. A window's `frame` is where it
is when it is neither zoomed nor in full screen, so a zoomed window's thumbnail has the
window's own size.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Mission Control](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503153858/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/mission-control/):

- Mission Control lets people preview and move between open windows, full-screen apps,
  desktops and spaces. It is entered with a gesture or a keyboard shortcut (a swipe up with
  three fingers, Control-Up Arrow) that the user can change.
- Respect the ways of entering it: they have to work all the time, whatever the application
  is doing.
- Be prepared for a change of context: when the user leaves Mission Control, a different
  window may be active, or a different application.

A browser keeps Control-Up Arrow and the trackpad gestures for the operating system: offer a
button, a Dock item or a shortcut of your own to open the overview.

## Variants and states

<Example name="rows" />

| State | How it is set | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open, closed | `open` / `defaultOpen`; Escape, the desktop, a chosen window or space | The overlay fades over 0.25 s while the bar slides in from the top and the thumbnails settle from 108%; with reduced motion it only fades. Closed, nothing is rendered. |
| Current space | `current` on a `MissionControlSpace` | A 3 point ring, 1 point away from the thumbnail. Measured. |
| Window under the pointer or the focus | hover, arrow keys, Tab | The same ring around the thumbnail, and the window's icon and title on a dark plate at its bottom. Provisional. |
| Space focused | Tab, arrow keys in the bar | The focus ring around its thumbnail. Provisional. |
| Add button | `onAddSpace` | A 20 point plus at the right of the bar; dimmed while pressed. |
| No bar | neither `spaces` nor `onAddSpace` | Only the thumbnails. |

Not implemented: the collapsed bar that shows names only, the close button of a space under
the pointer, dragging windows onto spaces, and grouping windows by application.

## Sizes

There is no `size` prop. The Spaces bar is 146 points high; a space's thumbnail is 144 by 90
points, a 16 by 10 screen at 3/35, and narrower when the bar has no room for all of them.

The window thumbnails are laid out from the `frame` of every window:

- Windows are put side by side in rows, 12 points apart, so that none covers another. From
  top to bottom of the desktop they are dealt into the rows, each row runs from left to
  right as the windows are on the desktop, and within a row every window keeps its height on
  the desktop relative to its neighbours.
- The number of rows is the one that shows the windows largest in an area twice as wide as
  it is high, about what a 16 by 10 desktop leaves between the bar and the Dock.
- The whole layout is scaled by one factor to fit the area, which is 18 points away from the
  bar and the sides and from `--mac-mission-control-inset-bottom` above the bottom, and it
  is centred there. It is never shown larger than life.

The scale is computed in CSS from the size of the area (a size container), so the overview
follows a desktop that is resized without script and without measuring anything.

## Accessibility

- The overlay is a `dialog` named "Mission Control" (pass `aria-label` to rename it). It
  keeps the keyboard focus inside while it is open. After Escape the focus returns to where
  it was; after a chosen window it stays where the choice put it.
- The Spaces bar is a `group` named by `spacesLabel`, with one `button` per space, named by
  its `label`; the current space has `aria-current`. The add button is named by `addLabel`.
- The windows are a `group` named by `windowsLabel`, with one `button` per window, named by
  its `title`, in reading order: row by row, left to right. Pictures, thumbnail content and
  icons are hidden from assistive technology.
- When the overview opens, the overlay itself has the focus, so no window looks chosen. Any
  arrow key then goes to the first window. The arrow keys move along the windows (Right and
  Down to the next, Left and Up to the previous) or, in the bar, along its buttons; Arrow
  Down in the bar goes to the first window. Tab goes through every button. Return or Space
  chooses, Escape closes.
- The desktop behind the thumbnails is a button named by `closeLabel`, outside the Tab order
  because Escape does the same.
- A window's title is shown only under the pointer or the focus; it is always the button's
  accessible name.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves; with `prefers-reduced-transparency` the bar is
  an opaque colour; in forced colours the bar, the thumbnails and the windows have borders.

## Visual comparison

`mission-control.visual.json` compares the Spaces bar, the top 1680 by 148 points of the
screen, with the Mission Control screenshots in both appearances. The two captures have a
different space current, so each has an example of its own.

The bar in the screenshots is a material over Apple's desktop picture, which the library
does not have. The examples paint the bar's fill as captured (its top and bottom rows,
blended down the bar) and the strip of picture under it, and switch the bar's own tint and
blur off; thumbnails, ring, names, plus and the edge under the bar are Mission Control's.
Masked: the four space thumbnails, which in the captures are pictures of Apple's desktops
and in the examples plain stand-ins (21% of the crop); the `-drawing` comparisons also mask
the four names, which are text in the fallback font (26% in all), and use threshold 0.05.

Scores from `npm run visual -- mission-control/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback
font: the share of pixels that differ by more than the threshold, and the mean colour
difference out of 255.

| `bar-light` | Light-Mission-Control (0, 0, 3360, 296) | 0.73% | 1.89 | 0.60% | 1.77 |
| `bar-light-drawing` (names masked) | Light-Mission-Control (0, 0, 3360, 296) | 0.93% | 1.36 | 0.03% | 1.17 |

The window thumbnails are not compared with the screenshots pixel for pixel: there they are
Apple's applications over Apple's desktop picture. State checks hold them to the measured
layout instead, in a real browser, on a 1680 by 1050 point desktop painted a flat grey. Two
windows of the captures' sizes, which overlap on the desktop, come out side by side at
97.9%: from 18 to 996 points and from 1008 to 1662, as measured in the captures. Further
checks: the bar's material over the grey, the ring of the current space, the ring and the
title plate of a window under the keyboard focus and under the pointer, and that Return on a
window closes the overview.

Measured: the bar's height, the thumbnails' size, position and spacing, their shadow, the
ring and its colour, the names' position and colour, the plus, the edge under the bar in
Aqua, the 18 point margins and 12 point gaps of the window thumbnails and that they are
scaled as one. Provisional:

- the bar's material (25% white in Aqua, 15% black in Dark Aqua, blur 60, saturation 180%):
  the specification's estimate, because no screenshot shows that desktop picture bare;
- where the layout sits vertically: it is centred above the Dock inset, which puts the two
  windows of the captures 10 points higher than they are there;
- how windows are dealt into several rows, since the captures show a single row;
- everything about a window under the pointer or the focus (ring, plate, where the title is),
  the focus rings, the pressed plus, and the opening and closing;
- the ring's colour does not follow the accent colour: both captures use blue;
- the names in Aqua are a dark navy that Catalina computes from the picture behind the bar;
  here it is the captured colour.
