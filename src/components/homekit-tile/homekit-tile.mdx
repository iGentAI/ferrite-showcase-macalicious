---

<Example name="basic" both />

Home shows every accessory as a square tile and every scene as a wide one, in grids over a
wallpaper. A tile that is on is opaque white with black text and a glyph in colour; a tile that
is off lets the wallpaper through, blurred and tinted, with its text and glyph in a tone of
what is behind them. Home is an iPad app running through Mac Catalyst, so the tile's metrics
are the iPad's scaled to 77%. AppKit has no class for it. `HomekitTile` is Base UI's Toggle
for an accessory and its Button for a scene.

## Usage

```tsx
import { HomekitTile, HomekitTileGrid } from "macalicious/homekit-tile";
import "macalicious/homekit-tile.css";

<h2 id="accessories">Favorite Accessories</h2>
<HomekitTileGrid aria-labelledby="accessories">
  <HomekitTile
    room="Master"
    name="Corner Light"
    status={on ? "On" : "Off"}
    icon={<BulbGlyph />}
    active={on}
    onActiveChange={setOn}
  />
</HomekitTileGrid>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add homekit-tile`.

An accessory tile toggles: `active` is its state and `onActiveChange` hears a click. The
`status` line is text the application writes ("On", "72%", "Paused"); the tile does not derive
it. Draw the glyph with `currentColor`: it takes `tint` on an active tile (yellow by default,
the colour of a light that is on) and the text tone on an inactive one. The library ships no
glyphs: Home's are Apple's artwork.

A scene tile does not toggle by itself. Its `onClick` runs the scene, and the application sets
`active` on the scene that is in effect, which the tile also reports as `aria-pressed`:

<Example name="scenes" both />

Put the tiles over a picture or a colour. The inactive tile blurs its backdrop with
`backdrop-filter`; over a flat colour the blur does nothing and the tint alone remains.

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines for macOS have no entry for this tile. Related are those on
[Mac Catalyst](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ios/overview/mac-catalyst/)
and
[translucency](https://web.archive.org/web/20200504055746/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/visual-design/translucency/);
the rest is how Home uses the tile in the screenshots (not verified against a published
guideline):

- One tile is one accessory or one scene. Its lines are room, name and state, in that order,
  each on one line.
- Say the state in words on the last line. The white fill alone must not be the only sign
  that something is on.
- Keep tiles at their size and let the grid wrap; do not stretch them to fill a row.
- Translucency needs something behind it. Do not put inactive tiles on a plain window
  background.

## Sizes

Two sizes, set by `kind`. An accessory tile is 101.5 pt square, a scene tile 211 x 54 pt, the
width of two accessory tiles and the gap between them; corners are 13 pt. Tiles in a
`HomekitTileGrid` stand 7.5 pt apart both ways. The glyph has a 24.5 pt slot, 12 pt from an
accessory tile's top and leading edges, 9.5 pt from a scene tile's leading edge. Accessory text
is 12 pt bold on a 14.5 pt line, 12 pt from the leading edge, with the last baseline 11 pt above
the bottom edge; a scene's title is 14 pt semibold, 44 pt from the leading edge.

## API

<PropsTable name="HomekitTileProps" />

The remaining props are those of Base UI's Button (`disabled`, `focusableWhenDisabled`,
`onClick` and the attributes of a `<button>`) and go to the tile.

<PropsTable name="HomekitTileGridProps" />

| Attribute or variable | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `data-kind` | tile | `accessory`, `scene` | Reflects `kind`. |
| `data-active` | tile | present or absent | The accessory is on or the scene is active. |
| `aria-pressed` | tile | `true`, `false` | The same, for assistive technology. |
| `data-disabled` | tile | present or absent | Reflects `disabled`. |
| `data-contrast` | tile | `more` or absent | Reflects `contrast`. |
| `--mac-homekit-tile-tint` | tile or an ancestor | a colour | The glyph's colour on an active tile; `tint` sets it on one tile. |
| `--mac-homekit-tile-fill` | any ancestor | a colour | Tint of an inactive tile. |
| `--mac-homekit-tile-blur` | any ancestor | a length | Blur of the backdrop under an inactive tile. |
| `--mac-homekit-tile-text`, `-status` | any ancestor | a colour | Text and glyph, and the state line, of an inactive tile. |
| `--mac-homekit-tile-text-contrast`, `-status-contrast` | any ancestor | a colour | The same two when more contrast is asked for, by `contrast="more"` or by the reader's system. |

Classes: `.mac-homekit-tile`, `.mac-homekit-tile-icon`, `.mac-homekit-tile-badge`,
`.mac-homekit-tile-text`, `.mac-homekit-tile-room`, `.mac-homekit-tile-name`,
`.mac-homekit-tile-status` and `.mac-homekit-tile-grid`.

## Accessibility

- An accessory tile is a toggle button: `aria-pressed` says whether the accessory is on, and
  Space or Return toggles it. A scene tile is a button that does not toggle by itself, and
  `aria-pressed` says whether its owner has set it active, so the white fill is not the only
  sign of the scene in effect. Pass `aria-pressed` yourself to say otherwise.
- The tile's accessible name is its lines in order, "Master Corner Light On". The glyph and
  the badge are hidden from assistive technology, so put what a badge means into `status`.
- A line that is cut with an ellipsis is still read in full.
- `HomekitTileGrid` is a `group`; name it with `aria-labelledby` and the heading above it, or
  with `aria-label`.
- The text of an inactive tile is tone on tone by design: about 1.7:1 in Aqua and 4.8:1 in
  Dark Aqua over the capture's wallpaper, and in Aqua far below the 4.5:1 that text of this
  size needs. `contrast="more"` gives an inactive tile white text and glyph (6:1 and 12:1
  there), and a reader whose system asks for more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`) gets
  that without the prop. Use it wherever the tiles must be read at a glance. For other
  colours set `--mac-homekit-tile-text` and `--mac-homekit-tile-status`; while more contrast
  is asked for the tile uses `--mac-homekit-tile-text-contrast` and
  `--mac-homekit-tile-status-contrast` in their place, so set those for that case.
- The pressed tile's change of size is not animated when the user asks for reduced motion.
- In forced-colours mode a tile is a bordered button and an active tile uses the system's
  highlight colours.

## Visual comparison

`npm run visual -- homekit-tile` compares four tiles of the Home screenshots in both
appearances: an accessory and a scene, each on and off. The glyphs are masked everywhere,
because the examples draw stand-ins. The wallpaper is a photograph: under the tiles that are
off the examples draw a gradient between the colours measured at the tile's two sides, and
behind the corners of the tiles that are on a flat colour. Share of pixels that differ at 2x,
in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Crop | Aqua | Dark Aqua |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accessory, on, 102 x 102 pt (`580,1044,204,204`) | 6.71% | 6.69% |
| Accessory, on, text masked | 3.39% | 3.39% |
| Accessory, off (`143,1044,204,204`) | 0.67% | 1.65% |
| Accessory, off, text masked | 2.12% | 1.09% |
| Scene, on, 212 x 55 pt (`1017,728,424,110`) | 5.02% | 5.02% |
| Scene, on, text masked | 4.21% | 4.21% |
| Scene, off (`143,728,424,110`) | 2.35% | 3.54% |
| Scene, off, text masked | 1.45% | 1.20% |

The masked rows use a stricter threshold (0.05 in place of 0.1), which is why one scores
higher than the row above it. Measured: the tiles' sizes, corner radius, fills and tints, the
glyph slot, the text's size, weight, colours and position. What remains with the text masked
is the tile's outline: Home's corners have continuous curvature, a CSS radius of 13 pt is a
circular arc within a pixel of it, and the wallpaper behind the corners is not flat.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows them: the blur radius, the pressed tile, the tile
that does not respond and the focus ring. Turning a tile on with a click and from the keyboard
is checked in a real browser (`expect` comparisons). The badge is not compared.
