<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSPrintPanel`, as TextEdit shows it in Catalina: a white preview page with page
buttons under it; a form with Printer, Presets, Copies and Pages, and in the detailed form
Paper Size, Orientation and the option panes; and a bottom row with Help, PDF, Show or Hide
Details, Cancel and Print. The panel is built from the library's own controls
([Pop-Up Button](/components/pop-up-button), [Pull-Down Button](/components/pull-down-button),
[Text Field](/components/text-field), [Checkbox](/components/checkbox),
[Radio Button](/components/radio-button), [Bevel Button](/components/bevel-button),
[Segmented Control](/components/segmented-control), [Help Button](/components/help-button),
[Push Button](/components/push-button), [Default Button](/components/default-button) and
[Separator](/components/separator)), so each of them looks and behaves as it does on its own.

It is the dialog's content, not the dialog: it has no background, frame or margin. Nothing is
printed either. The lists come in through props, the state of the form is one `settings`
object, and Print and Cancel are reported.

## Usage

```tsx
import { MenuItem } from "macalicious/menu-item";
import { PrintPanel } from "macalicious/print-panel";
import { Sheet, SheetPopup } from "macalicious/sheet";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<Sheet open={open} onOpenChange={setOpen}>
  <SheetPopup anchor={titleBar} width={756} aria-label="Print">
    <PrintPanel
      printers={[{ value: "office", label: "Office Laser" }]}
      presets={[{ value: "default", label: "Default Settings" }]}
      paperSizes={[{ value: "letter", label: "US Letter", description: "8.50 by 11.00 inches" }]}
      panes={[{ value: "app", label: "Notes", content: <NotesOptions /> }]}
      preview={<PageThumbnail page={page} />}
      pageCount={12}
      page={page}
      onPageChange={setPage}
      pdfMenu={<MenuItem onClick={saveAsPdf}>Save as PDF…</MenuItem>}
      onCancel={() => setOpen(false)}
      onPrint={(settings) => send(settings)}
    />
  </SheetPopup>
</Sheet>
```

- **Where it goes.** In a [Sheet](/components/sheet) attached to the document's window, 756 pt
  wide: the sheet supplies the material and the 20 pt margins. Or in the body of a
  [Window](/components/window) with `padding: 20px`, for the application-modal presentation.
- **Lists.** `printers`, `presets`, `paperSizes` and `panes` are arrays of `value` and `label`;
  `separatorBefore` draws a line above an item in the menu. A paper size also has the
  `description` shown after the pop-up button and an `aspectRatio` (width divided by height of
  the upright sheet; US Letter when left out). A pane has the `content` shown under the pop-up
  button in the separator line: your own controls. List your application's pane first. Every
  pane stays mounted, hidden while another is chosen or the simple form is shown, so its
  controls keep their state.
- **Settings.** One object: `printer`, `preset`, `copies`, `twoSided`, `pages` (`"all"` or
  `"range"`), `from`, `to`, `paperSize`, `orientation` and `pane`. Leave it to the panel
  (`defaultSettings`, any part of it) or control it (`settings` with `onSettingsChange`, which
  always receives the complete object). The three number fields report whole numbers of at
  least 1; an empty or mistyped entry is dropped when the focus leaves the field. Typing a
  page chooses the range.
- **Preview.** `preview` is drawn on the page: render a small copy of the document's current
  page, laid out for a page 301 pt long. The page takes the proportions of the chosen paper,
  turns with the orientation, and is white in both appearances; what is on it is clipped and
  drawn in the light appearance. `pageCount`, and `page` with `onPageChange`, drive the text
  and the four buttons under it. When `pageCount` falls below the page shown, the last page
  is shown and reported.
- **Simple and detailed.** As in AppKit the panel starts with the simple form. The details
  button switches; `defaultDetailed` or `detailed` with `onDetailedChange` set it.
- **PDF.** `pdfMenu` holds the items of the PDF pull-down button, from
  `macalicious/menu-item`. Without it the button is left out.
- **Print and Cancel.** `onPrint` receives the settings; `onCancel` nothing. Return and Escape
  are the two buttons' key equivalents in the enclosing sheet, dialog or window (see
  [Default Button](/components/default-button)). In a sheet, Escape is the sheet's own: it
  closes through `onOpenChange` and `onCancel` is not called, so close on both. `printDisabled`
  disables Print and the Return key with it.
- **Translation.** `labels` replaces any of the panel's own texts, by their English wording:
  `labels={{ "Printer:": "Drucker:", Print: "Drucken" }}`.
- **Printing.** The panel collects settings. A browser prints with its own dialog
  (`window.print()`), which a page cannot fill in.

### In a window

<Example name="window" />

The application-modal presentation: the panel in the body of a window of its own. Here the
settings are controlled, the preview follows the page buttons, and Print is disabled while the
range ends before it begins.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Printing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/printing/):

- People expect the printing system's options in every application. Offer Print in the File
  menu, and enable it only when there is something to print and a printer to print it on.
- Use the standard Print dialog: it is familiar. It opens in a collapsed form with the settings
  people change most, and expands to show the rest.
- Enable the options that suit your content: a page range, several copies, and both sides of
  the paper when it makes sense and the printer can do it.
- Put application-specific options in a category of their own in the expanded dialog, named so
  that it does not conflict with the system's categories (Layout, Paper Handling): in general
  your application's name. Offer there only what the system does not already provide.
- Make dependencies between options clear, keep advanced options apart from frequently used
  ones, and preview the effect of a change where you can.
- Settings that are rarely changed and belong to one document (page size, orientation,
  scaling) go in a Page Setup dialog and are stored with the document; settings that change
  with every printing go in the Print dialog.

## Variants and states

| Variant or state | Look | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detailed form, in a sheet | Preview 233 x 301 pt, six rows, the pane pop-up button in its line, the pane | Measured (Print, light and dark) |
| Copies and Pages rows | A number field with Two-Sided; All, then From and to on a second line | Provisional: an open menu covers them in both captures, only the two labels are measured |
| Simple form (`detailed` false) | No Paper Size, Orientation or panes; the page at half its size; 150 pt lower | Provisional: no screenshot; the spec infers the height |
| Landscape | The page on its side, 301 x 233 pt, centred where the upright page is | Provisional: no screenshot |
| Page buttons | Disabled at the first and at the last page | Measured disabled, on a document of one page; enabled is the segmented control's look |
| Print disabled | The default button's disabled look | Provisional: no screenshot |
| In a window | The same content on the window background | Provisional: no screenshot |
| Window not key | Each control's own look in a window that is not key | Provisional: no screenshot |

The two orientation pictograms and the page chevrons are drawn for this library with
`currentColor`; Apple's artwork is not reproduced.

## Sizes

One size. The detailed panel is 716 x 379 pt (a 756 x 419 pt sheet with its 20 pt margins)
with a pane of two checkboxes; a taller pane makes it taller. The simple panel is 716 x 229 pt.
The preview column is 310 pt wide; the form takes the rest and grows with the panel. Labels
end 90.5 pt from the form's left edge and the controls start 9 pt after them. Printer and
Presets are 239.5 pt wide, Paper Size 178 pt, the pane pop-up button 175 pt, the orientation
buttons 38 pt, Cancel and Print at least 70 pt.

## Accessibility

- The panel is a `group` named "Print" (or by `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`). It is not a
  dialog: the sheet or window around it is, and names itself.
- Every control has a name. Each label names the controls of its row; the two fields of the
  range are named by "From:" and "to:"; the orientation buttons are "Portrait" and
  "Landscape", the pane pop-up button "Options", the page buttons "First page", "Previous
  page", "Next page" and "Last page". The page number is a polite live region.
- The preview page is one `img` named "Preview". Put nothing on it that must be operated or
  read out; describe the document elsewhere.
- Tab moves through the panel in reading order: the page buttons, the form from Printer down,
  then Help, PDF, the details button, Cancel and Print. The radio buttons and the orientation
  buttons are one stop each, moved through with the arrow keys.
- Return activates Print and Escape Cancel from anywhere in the enclosing sheet, dialog or
  window, except where the focused control uses the key itself: a button, an open menu, a
  multi-line text view in a pane. Both keys are left alone when a `keydown` handler prevents
  their default.
- A sheet gives the focus to its first text field, which is Copies, as the Print dialog
  does. Pass the sheet's `initialFocus` to start elsewhere.
- In forced-colours mode the page has an edge in place of its shadow.

## Visual comparison

`print-panel.visual.json` compares one example, the whole sheet of the reference screenshots,
with both captures of TextEdit's Print sheet. In the captures the Presets menu is open over the
middle of the form and its shadow falls on the Paper Size row, so five regions that the menu
leaves free are compared: the preview column, the Printer row, the Orientation row with the
pane pop-up button and the pane, and the two ends of the bottom row. The sheet's material
shows the desktop picture, blurred: each region paints the colours measured behind the sheet
at that spot, so the comparison scores the panel and not the material, which is the sheet's.
The dark sheet was found one pixel to the left of where the spec puts it (x 55, not 56): its
crops start there.

Every region is compared twice: whole, and with the text masked at a threshold of 0.05, which
gates the drawing. Text is masked because it is set in a fallback font off Apple platforms.
The two orientation pictograms are masked in both, because they are stand-ins. The widths of
the PDF and Hide Details buttons are set in the example, because their titles' widths place
the buttons after them.

At 1x the masked scores are higher than the whole ones in places: their threshold is half,
and the reference is the 2x capture averaged down, which a half-point hairline cannot match.
What is left at 2x is, in the preview, one device pixel along the page's left and right edges
(the captured page is about 233.6 pt wide) and, elsewhere, edges of the controls the panel is
built from. The scores of the regions with a pop-up or pull-down button follow those two
components: they were measured with the versions current on 5 October 2026.

Pixel checks without a reference: `simple-*` render the simple form and hold its height to
269 pt with the margins, the page to half its size and the bottom separator to its place;
`landscape-*` click the Landscape button and read the paper where only a page on its side is.

Measured: the page's size, place and shadow; the page controls and the page number's place;
the label column and every control's place and width in the Printer, Paper Size and
Orientation rows, the pane pop-up button, its line and the pane's place; the bottom separator
and the bottom row. Provisional: the controls of the Copies and Pages rows and the width of the
number fields, the simple form, the landscape page, and everything a window that is not key
changes. Not compared: the Presets row and the Paper Size row, which the open menu and its
shadow cover (their places are measured), and the open menu itself, which is the pop-up
button's. Not built: the printer status line, the system's own panes (Layout, Paper Handling
and the others, which an application can supply as `panes`), and the Page Setup dialog.
