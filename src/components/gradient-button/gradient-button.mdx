AppKit calls this control a *gradient button* (`NSButton` with the small-square bezel style).
The name is historical: in Catalina the fill is almost flat. It is built on
[Base UI's Button](https://base-ui.com/react/components/button). Three parts:

| Part | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `GradientButton` | One cell with a glyph. |
| `GradientButtonGroup` | A row of cells that share their borders. |
| `GradientButtonFiller` | The empty cell that continues the bar to the edge of the list above. |

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  GradientButton,
  GradientButtonFiller,
  GradientButtonGroup,
} from "macalicious/gradient-button";
import "macalicious/gradient-button.css";

<GradientButtonGroup aria-label="Edit accounts">
  <GradientButton glyph="add" aria-label="Add an account" onClick={add} />
  <GradientButton glyph="remove" aria-label="Remove the account" disabled={!selection} />
  <GradientButtonFiller />
</GradientButtonGroup>
```

`glyph` draws one of the three standard images: `add`, `remove` or `action` (a gear with a
chevron). For any other image pass an 11 to 14 pt icon that uses `currentColor` as children.
Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add gradient-button`. See
[Installation](/installation).

### Under a list

The bar is attached to the bottom of its list with no gap: its top border is the list's bottom
border. Give the group `display: flex` and the list's width, and end it with a filler.

<Example name="under-a-list" />

### Toggle and menu buttons

A gradient button can stay on, or open a menu. For a toggle, hold the state yourself and pass
it as `aria-pressed`. For a menu, pass the button to the trigger's `render` prop:
`<Menu.Trigger render={<GradientButton glyph="action" aria-label="Actions" />} />`; it is drawn
pressed while the menu is open.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for gradient buttons:

- A gradient button starts an immediate action that belongs to a view, typically adding or
  removing rows of the table directly above it.
- It contains an icon, not text, and needs no introducing label.
- Put it in or directly under its view, never in a toolbar or status bar. Order the buttons
  add, remove, then action.

A pair of add and remove buttons that is not attached to a list uses a rounded segmented
control in Catalina instead.

## Variants and states

Pressed and keyboard-focused are transient, so this gallery pins them. In an application they
appear on their own.

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | – | Near-flat light fill (flat dark grey in Dark Aqua), 1 pt border, glyph at 85% |
| Pressed | `:active` | Darker flat fill in Aqua, lighter in Dark Aqua |
| On | `aria-pressed="true"`, or its menu is open | As pressed |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring over the cell's border |
| Disabled | `disabled` | The glyph at 25%; the fill does not change |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | Unchanged, but no focus ring |
| Network bar | `variant="network"` on the group | The bar as the Network pane draws it: in Dark Aqua a fill of `#4e4f50` to `#48494a`, a `#808080` top line, `#636465` bottom and outer borders and `#8a8b8c` between cells; in Aqua within two levels of the standard bar |

## Sizes

One size: 21 pt high (19 pt of fill between two 1 pt borders), and at least 25 pt wide, which
leaves the 23 pt of fill measured in the Language & Region pane. The cells of Internet
Accounts have 24 pt of fill: give each button `width: 26px` (the fill plus its two borders).
An 11 pt glyph cannot be centred in an even width on whole points. The browser centres it on
the half point; AppKit keeps to whole points, and in Internet Accounts the spare point is
after the plus and before the minus. One point more than the default 4 pt of padding on that
side (`padding-inline-end: 5px` or `padding-inline-start: 5px`) reproduces it. The example
`visual-wide-cells` does so and is compared with that pane.

## Accessibility

- Each button is a native `<button>`. Space and Return activate it; Tab moves from one button
  to the next.
- A button has no title, so it needs an `aria-label` that says what it does to what ("Remove
  the selected account"), not the name of its glyph.
- The group has `role="group"`; name it with `aria-label` when the buttons need a common
  description. The filler is hidden from assistive technology.
- Deleting with the Delete key in the list above should do what the remove button does.
- In forced-colours mode the borders are drawn with system colours.

artwork**: drawn for this library on the measured ink boxes (a 13 pt gear, a 7 x 4.5 pt
chevron, 2 pt from the left of a 28 pt fill), so the last row scores that drawing against
Apple's, and the row above it shows that the cell around the glyph matches.

No screenshot shows a gradient button pressed, on or focused; those looks are the spec's
inference and provisional. They are checked in a real browser against the spec's values (the
`expect` comparisons): the borders, fill and glyph colours at rest, the pressed fill under a
real mouse press, the fill of a button that is on, the focus ring after a real Tab, and that
the ring is absent in an inactive window. The pull-down behaviour is checked the same way: the
example `visual-pull-down` makes the action button the trigger of a Base UI menu, a real click
opens the menu, and the trigger must then be drawn as pressed. That an open pull-down looks
pressed is this library's reading of AppKit; no screenshot shows one open.
