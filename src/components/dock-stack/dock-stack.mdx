A stack is a folder's tile in the [Dock](/components/dock). Activating it springs the
folder's contents out above the tile; while they are out, the tile shows a rounded square
with a down triangle in place of its icon. `DockStack` is the tile and its popup, a
`DockStackItem` is one file or folder, and `DockStackMore` is the element that opens the
folder itself ("7 More in Finder").

## Usage

```tsx
import { Dock, DockItem, DockSeparator } from "macalicious/dock";
import { DockStack, DockStackItem, DockStackMore } from "macalicious/dock-stack";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<Dock>
  <DockItem label="Files" icon={<FilesIcon />} running />
  <DockSeparator />
  <DockStack label="Downloads" icon={<NewestThumbnail />}>
    <DockStackMore onClick={openFolder}>3 More in Finder</DockStackMore>
    <DockStackItem icon={<Thumbnail file={dunes} />} onClick={() => open(dunes)}>
      Dunes.jpg
    </DockStackItem>
    <DockStackItem icon={<Thumbnail file={harbour} />} onClick={() => open(harbour)}>
      Harbour.jpg
    </DockStackItem>
  </DockStack>
</Dock>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add dock-stack`.

- `DockStack` takes the props of a `DockItem` (`label`, `icon`, `running`, `disabled`); they
  and the `ref` go to the tile. Catalina shows the newest file's thumbnail as the tile's
  icon, or the folder's icon: pass the one you want.
- The children are the contents **in the order they are shown, from the top**. In a fan the
  last child sits nearest the Dock, and the `DockStackMore` is the first child, because it
  is the fan's far end. In a grid or a list the `DockStackMore` is the last child.
- An item's children are its name, the text of its label. `icon` is its thumbnail, centred
  in a box of 60 points in a fan, 64 in a grid and 16 in a list; a photograph gets its white
  border and shadow from you. `onClick` opens the item, and the stack closes.
- A fan holds about ten items in Catalina; show the newest ten and let the
  `DockStackMore` name the rest. The measured curve ends at the eleventh place, and items
  beyond it continue straight up.
- `open` with `onOpenChange` controls the stack; `defaultOpen` starts it open.
- The popup is rendered in a portal, only while the stack is open.

## Guidelines

What the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS and the Dock's own options say about stacks,
as the measured specification records it (`reference/spec/components/dock-stack.md`); the
Apple page itself was not consulted for this text:

- A stack is a folder kept in the Dock, after the separator; Downloads is there by default.
- Its contents are shown as a fan, a grid or a list. "Automatic" shows a fan for a few
  items and a grid for many.
- The last element of a fan opens the folder in the Finder and says how many more items it
  holds.
- A fan rises from a Dock at the bottom of the screen only. With the Dock on a side, use a
  grid or a list.

## Variants and states

<Example name="views" />

| Prop or state | Values | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `view` | `fan` (default) | Measured on the Stacks screenshots: the pitch, the curve, the rotation, the labels, the round button |
| `view` | `grid`, `list` | Provisional: no screenshot shows them. A panel in the popover material with four columns of icons over their names, and a menu of names with small icons, each closed by the `DockStackMore` as a plain row |
| open | `open`, `defaultOpen`; `[data-popup-open]` on the tile | Measured: the tile shows a rounded square outline with a down triangle, an original drawing of Catalina's placeholder |
| item under the pointer or the keyboard | `[data-highlighted]` | Provisional: the label takes the selection colour, and in a list the whole row |
| disabled item | `disabled` on a `DockStackItem` | Provisional: half opacity |

Not implemented: the "Automatic" view (choose `fan` or `grid` from the number of items
yourself), the pointer of the grid's panel towards its tile, and a fan from a Dock on the
left or right, which macOS does not offer either. The spec infers the Dock's own material
for the grid; the popover material is used instead, so that names stay readable over any
desktop.

The fan springs out of the tile and its parts fade in; a grid or a list fades in. Only
`transform` and `opacity` are animated, and with reduced motion nothing moves: the popup
appears with the fade alone.

## Sizes

A stack has no `size` prop. The tile follows the Dock's `--mac-dock-tile-size`, and the
placeholder of the open tile scales with it (52 points in a 64 point tile). The popup's
measures are fixed, as in Catalina, whatever the tile size:

| Part | Size |
| --- | --- |
| Fan | Items 64 pt apart; a 60 pt box for each thumbnail, the lowest 15 pt above the shelf; each item 1.25 degrees further round than the one below it, from 1.6 degrees, and further right by 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 13 and 15 pt |
| Fan label | 26 pt tall, 14 pt beside its text, 4 pt corners, ending 7 pt before the thumbnail's box; 13 pt text |
| Round button | 56 pt across, with a 22 pt arrow |
| Grid | Four columns of 96 pt, 64 pt icons, 13 pt names |
| List | 19 pt rows, 16 pt icons, 14 pt names |

## Accessibility

- Built on Base UI's Menu. The tile is a `button` with `aria-haspopup="menu"` and
  `aria-expanded`; the popup is a `menu` and every item a `menuitem` named by its text. The
  thumbnails and the arrow are hidden from assistive technology.
- With focus on the tile, Up Arrow opens the stack on the item nearest the Dock, as on a
  Mac; Return, Space and Down Arrow open it on the first item.
- In the open stack Up and Down Arrow move between the items in the order they are shown,
  Home and End go to the first and the last, typing the first letters of a name goes to it,
  Return opens the item and closes the stack, and Escape or Tab closes it and gives the
  focus back to the tile.
- The stack is not modal: a press anywhere else closes it, and the rest of the page stays
  available.
- In a Dock on the left or right, Up and Down Arrow move between the Dock's tiles, a
  stack's included; Return or Space opens the stack there.
- A grid is the same menu laid out in rows: Up and Down Arrow go through it in reading
  order, and Left and Right Arrow do nothing.
- In forced colours the labels, the round button, the placeholder and the panels keep an
  outline; with `prefers-reduced-transparency` the labels are opaque.

## Visual comparison

`dock-stack.visual.json` compares three crops of the Stacks screenshots in both
appearances: the far end of the fan over the sky (the label of the last element, turned 14
degrees, and the round button), the label of the item nearest the Dock over the sea (turned
1.6 degrees: the same material over a dark backdrop), and the open tile in the Dock with
the nearest thumbnail's border above it.

Masked, and why: the round button's arrow, which is Apple's glyph in the reference and an
original drawing here; the thumbnails' pictures, which are photographs (their white border
is compared, and holds the fan's lowest place); the corner of the first crop where the next
item and the coast show; and, in Aqua, the sea around the label and the thumbnail, a
photograph of waves that the examples replace with its mean colour (in Dark Aqua it is flat
enough to compare). Each crop with text is also compared with the text masked, at threshold
0.05, to score the drawing alone; in the examples the text is given the width it has in San
Francisco, so that the labels are as wide as in the screenshots in the fallback font too.

The two `label` comparisons with text are above the tight tier at 2x, where the text is
most of what is left of a small crop; their `-drawing` twins are within it. The label
crops score high at 1x because a turned edge and a 1 pt line do not survive the averaging
of the reference to half its size. In `label-light-drawing` what remains is the sliver of
sea between the masks' steps and the label's sloping edge.

What no screenshot shows is checked on a flat grey desktop, with real input and colours
computed from the tokens: nothing above the Dock while the stack is closed, the label's
material and the down triangle after a press on the tile, the selection colour on the item
under the pointer, and the panel, the menu and their highlighted item in the grid and the
list.

Measured: the fan's pitch, curve and rotation, the labels' size, material, inner line and
outline, the round button and its soft edge in Aqua, the open tile's outline and triangle,
the gap to the Dock. Provisional: the grid and the list, the highlighted and disabled
items, the blur radius behind a label, the spring of the opening, the thumbnails' shadow
(yours to draw), and the padding beside a label's text, which is fitted to the ink of the
screenshots' text (14 pt; the spec gives 16 pt to the first glyph's stem).
