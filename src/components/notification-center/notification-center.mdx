<Example name="basic" both />

The panel that slides in under the menu bar when its menu bar extra is clicked. It holds
[Today widgets](/components/today-widget) in one view and delivered
[notifications](/components/notification-banner) in the other.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  NotificationCenter,
  NotificationCenterGroup,
  NotificationCenterView,
} from "macalicious/notification-center";
import "macalicious/notification-center.css";

<NotificationCenter open={open} editLabel="Edit" onEdit={edit} onSettings={openPreferences}>
  <NotificationCenterView value="today">…widgets…</NotificationCenterView>
  <NotificationCenterView value="notifications">
    <NotificationCenterGroup heading="Today" onClear={clearToday}>
      …notifications…
    </NotificationCenterGroup>
  </NotificationCenterView>
</NotificationCenter>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add notification-center`.

The panel is 344 pt wide and as tall as its container: place it in a layer that runs from the
menu bar's bottom edge to the bottom of the screen, at the trailing edge, in the same page as
the desktop it should blur. `open` moves it in and out; closing it on a click elsewhere is the
application's.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[Notifications](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503160812/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/notifications/):

- Notification Center lists what was delivered, newest first, grouped by day; people clear a
  day's notifications together or one at a time.
- Keep a notification short and specific, and make a click on it open the item it is about.
- Do not send a notification for something people are already looking at.

## Variants and states

<Example name="empty" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today view | `value="today"` (the default) | First segment selected; the footer can have its button |
| Notifications view | `value="notifications"` | Second segment selected; no footer button |
| Footer button | `editLabel` | Dark translucent button, centred: "Edit", or "2 New" |
| Gear | `onSettings` | 18 pt gear at the trailing end of the footer |
| Day group | `NotificationCenterGroup` | Large light heading with a 14 pt clear button |
| Empty view | `NotificationCenterEmpty` | Centred text in the heading colour |
| Closed | `open={false}` | Moved out past the trailing edge, hidden and inert |

The panel is a vibrant material: it blurs and tints the desktop behind it, so its colours
follow the picture. With `prefers-reduced-transparency`, or where `backdrop-filter` is not
available, it is an opaque colour.

Measured: the panel, header, segments, dividers, day heading, clear button, footer button and
gear, in both appearances, over the default desktop picture. Provisional: the pressed looks,
the empty view and the slide (a quarter of a second, by eye); no capture shows them.

## Sizes

One size. `--mac-notification-center-width` changes the width; the cards inside are 322 pt
wide and have variables of their own.

## Accessibility

- The root is an `<aside>`, a `complementary` landmark, named "Notification Center"; pass
  `aria-label` for another language.
- The header is a `tablist` with two `tab`s and each view a `tabpanel`, from Base UI: the arrow
  keys move between the segments, Return or Space selects, and a view without a focusable
  element is itself a tab stop.
- A day group is a `region` named by its heading, an `<h2>`. Its clear button is named by
  `clearLabel` and described by the heading, so two groups' buttons can be told apart.
- The gear has no text: it is named by `settingsLabel`.
- A closed panel is `inert` and hidden, so nothing in it can be focused or read.
- Focus rings show only in the key window. In forced-colours mode the panel and the segments
  have borders and the selected segment uses the system highlight.
- The slide is not played with `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Visual comparison

The captures show the panel over the default desktop picture, which is not reproduced: each
example paints, behind the panel, gradients that under the panel's material give the colours
measured along the header and under it. Text is masked (the fallback font off Apple platforms),
and so are the glyphs of the switch rows and the app icon, which are Apple artwork. Results in
Chromium on Linux, at a colour distance of 0.05:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today view: header and switches card, Aqua | 0.24% of pixels differ | 1.24% |
| Today view, Dark Aqua | 5.49% | 6.36% |
| Notifications view: header, day heading, start of a notification, Aqua | 0.04% | 0.90% |
| Notifications view, Dark Aqua | 5.73% | 6.69% |
| Footer with the gear, Aqua (button masked) | 3.42% | 3.55% |
| Footer with Edit and the gear, Dark Aqua | 3.23% | 3.54% |

In Dark Aqua nearly all of the difference is the glow of the sun in the desktop picture at the
trailing side of the panel, which the stand-in gradients follow along one row only; the
header, segments, card and clear button match. In the footer it is the gear, an original
drawing with the measured size, weight and colour, and the blurred end of the Dock behind the
panel. The Aqua capture's footer button reads "2 New" with a blue dot and is masked whole. A
state check clicks the second segment and reads the fills of both segments.

Not compared: the leading edge lines, which lie on the desktop picture.
