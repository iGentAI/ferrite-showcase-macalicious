The screen that `loginwindow` shows at start-up and when fast user switching moves to another
account. It looks the same in Aqua and Dark Aqua: white text and translucent controls over the
desktop picture. Nothing is authenticated by the component. It hands the typed password to
`onSubmit` and shows the answer you give it through `invalid`, `busy` and `hint`.

The round buttons, the arrow in the field and the password input are built on
[Base UI's Button](https://base-ui.com/react/components/button),
[Field](https://base-ui.com/react/components/field) and
[Input](https://base-ui.com/react/components/input).

## Usage

```tsx
import { LoginWindow, LoginWindowButton } from "macalicious/login-window";
import "macalicious/progress-spinner.css"; // the spinner shown while busy; its stylesheet comes first
import "macalicious/login-window.css";

<LoginWindow
  users={[{ value: "mh", name: "mhackett", picture: <img src={avatar} alt="" /> }]}
  invalid={rejected}
  busy={checking}
  hint={rejected ? "Hint: your first pet" : undefined}
  onSubmit={(password, user) => check(user, password)}
  onCancel={leave}
  actions={<LoginWindowButton onClick={leave}>Cancel</LoginWindowButton>}
  style={{ height: "100vh", backgroundImage: `url(${desktopPicture})` }}
>
  <span>3:37 PM</span>
</LoginWindow>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add login-window`.

- **`users`** has one entry for a password prompt and several for a list to choose from.
  A picture is any element; make it fill its 93 pt box (`width: 100%; height: 100%;
  object-fit: cover`). Without one a neutral silhouette is drawn.
- **The children** are the status items at the top right: a clock, and whatever glyphs you
  draw for battery and Wi-Fi.
- **`actions`** is the row of `LoginWindowButton`s at the bottom: Cancel when switching users;
  Sleep, Restart and Shut Down after a start.
- **The desktop picture** is yours: the window paints a neutral dark placeholder. Give the
  root a `background`, as above.

## Guidelines

Paraphrased from the measured spec and from how macOS behaves; not checked against a published
Human Interface Guidelines page.

- Ask for one thing at a time: first the account, then its password. Do not show a name field
  and a password field together unless the list of accounts is hidden on purpose.
- Keep the keyboard in the password field. Return submits; Escape goes back to the list of
  accounts, or cancels.
- Reject a wrong password with the shake and an empty field, not with an alert. Show the
  password hint under the field only after a few failures.
- Never put the password back into the field, and do not say whether the name or the password
  was wrong.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One user | `users` with one entry | Picture, name and password field; the field is centred on the screen |
| List of users | `users` with several entries and no selection | Pictures side by side, each a button; choosing one shows its prompt |
| Empty field | – | Placeholder at 40% white; no arrow |
| Typing | text in the field | An arrow in a ring appears at the end of the field and submits |
| Rejected | `invalid` | The field shakes once (0.4 s) and is emptied; `aria-invalid` is set. With reduced motion it does not move |
| Checking | `busy` | A spinner takes the arrow's place; the field is read-only and Return does nothing |
| Hint | `hint` | 13 pt text under the field, which it describes |
| Button pressed | `:active` | The disc behind the ring brightens |
| Button disabled | `disabled` on a `LoginWindowButton` | The whole button at the disabled opacity |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring round a button's disc, a user's picture or the arrow; the field shows only its insertion point, as in macOS |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | No focus ring |

Measured: the single-user prompt with an empty field and the Cancel button, the only state the
two captures show. Provisional: the list of users (its spacing), the arrow, the spinner's
place, the hint, the shake, the pressed and disabled buttons, the glyphs of Sleep, Restart and
Shut Down and the distance between two buttons (40 pt), the text shadow, and the placeholder
backdrop and silhouette.

## Sizes

The window fills the element it is in: give that element, or the window itself, a height. The
field stays in the middle, the status items at the top right and the buttons 104 pt above the
bottom (to the middle of their rings). The parts do not scale: the picture is 93 pt, the field
160 by 28 pt, a ring 30 pt on a 39 pt disc. The prompt needs about 380 pt of height.

## API

`className`, `style`, `ref` and other attributes go to the root element. A user is
`{ value: string; name: string; picture?: ReactNode }` (the type `LoginWindowUser`).

| Attribute | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `data-appearance` | root | `dark` | Always set: the login window is light on dark in both appearances, and so is everything put inside it. |

| `data-glyph` | `LoginWindowButton` | `cancel`, `sleep`, `restart`, `shut-down` | Reflects `glyph`. |

| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-login-window-backdrop` | `#3c2e2c` | What is painted behind everything when the root has no background of its own. |
| `--mac-login-window-field` | white at 38% | Tint of the password field over the blurred picture. |
| `--mac-login-window-disc` | white at 13.5% | Tint of the disc behind a round button. |

## Accessibility

- The prompt is a `form` named by the user's name. The password field is a real
  `<input type="password">`; its accessible name is the placeholder, and the hint is its
  description. A rejected password sets `aria-invalid`.
- Return in the field submits, and so does the arrow button, which is in the tab order once
  there is text and is named by `submitLabel`. While `busy` the spinner is a `progressbar`
  named by `busyLabel`.
- Escape returns from a prompt to the list of users, or calls `onCancel` when there is no list.
  It is handled on the root, so it works from the field and from every button inside.
- The list of users is a `group` named by `usersLabel`; each user is a button. Tab reaches
  every one, and Left and Right move between them. Choosing a user moves the keyboard to the
  password field; Escape moves it back to that user.
- A `LoginWindowButton` is named by its title, the children. Give one without a title an
  `aria-label`. Pictures and glyphs are hidden from assistive technology.
- The window does not trap the keyboard and does not move it when it appears: focus the field
  through `inputRef` when the window opens.
- The shake is an animation of `transform` only and is switched off by
  `prefers-reduced-motion`. The blur behind the field and the discs is switched off by
  `prefers-reduced-transparency`, with the shared material tokens. In forced-colours mode the
  field, the discs and the pictures get a border.

## Visual comparison

The reference is the fast user switching screen, captured once in a light and once in a dark
session; the two show the same screen. Two parts of it can be compared, because the rest is the
desktop picture and the account picture, which are not reproduced: `visual-field` is the empty
password field, cropped to its own box, and `visual-cancel` the Cancel button on the dark water
below it. Results in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field, light session | 3.22% of pixels differ | 2.75% |
| Field, dark session | 2.86% | 2.39% |
| Field with the placeholder masked, both sessions | 1.53% | 1.94% |
| Cancel, both sessions | 3.99% | 4.79% |
| Cancel with the title masked, both sessions | 3.88% | 5.23% |

The masked comparisons count a pixel as different at a colour distance of 0.05, half the
default. In the field what differs is its four corners, where the capture has rock and the
example a flat colour; the backdrop of that example is solved from the colours at the field's
two ends, so the comparison gates the field's size, corner radius and placeholder and not its
tint. In the button it is the antialiasing round the disc and the ring and the ripples of the
water. The placeholder and the title are set in the fallback font. Keyboard focus on the
button is checked by pixel (`cancel-focus`), since no screenshot shows it.

Not compared, because no flat backdrop exists for them: the position of the picture and the
name above the field (laid out from the measured distances), and the status items.
