<Example name="basic" both />

Voice Memos shows a recording twice. The detailed view draws its amplitude as red bars mirrored
around a centre axis, in a dark panel with a ruler of seconds under it; the playhead stands
still at the middle and the waveform moves beneath it. The overview shows the whole recording
in a rounded strip with a red handle at the current position. The app is an iPad app running
through Mac Catalyst and this content is dark in both appearances. AppKit has no waveform
class. `WaveformView` draws both views from an amplitude envelope; the overview is Base UI's
Slider.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  WaveformView,
  WaveformViewDetail,
  WaveformViewOverview,
} from "macalicious/waveform-view";
import "macalicious/waveform-view.css";

const [position, setPosition] = useState(0);

<WaveformView
  samples={envelope}
  duration={audio.duration}
  value={position}
  onValueChange={(seconds) => {
    audio.currentTime = seconds;
    setPosition(seconds);
  }}
  aria-label="New Recording"
>
  <WaveformViewDetail />
  <WaveformViewOverview style={{ margin: "66px 33px 0" }} />
</WaveformView>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add waveform-view`.

`WaveformView` holds the recording and the position and lays nothing out; the two views inside
it show the same position and can stand in any arrangement, or alone. `samples` is the
amplitude envelope: numbers from 0 to 1, evenly spaced over `duration`. Each bar shows the
loudest sample of its stretch of time, so any resolution works. The component does not decode
audio: compute the envelope from an `AudioBuffer` (the peak of each fiftieth of a second, say)
or load it with the recording.

During playback, set `value` from the audio element's time. The detailed view rebuilds its
bars only when the position crosses a four-second boundary, and holds no more than the bars
and labels near the playhead, so an hour of audio costs no more than a minute.

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines have no entry for a waveform. The nearest are those on
[sliders](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/selectors/sliders/)
and the
[keyboard](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/user-interaction/keyboard/),
and what follows also reads the two Voice Memos screenshots (not verified against a published
guideline for this view):

- A position in time is one value. Expose it once, as a slider with a readable value, not as
  hundreds of bars.
- Everything the pointer can do, the keyboard can do: the arrow keys seek by a small step.
- The area after the end of the recording is future time and stays empty. Do not draw grey
  bars there.
- The audio red belongs to the app. It does not follow the system accent colour.
- Put the transport controls (play, record, skip) beside the views; they are not part of them.

## Sizes

One size. The detailed view is 280 pt tall and as wide as it is given, up to 1848 pt (it
draws 924 pt of audio either side of the playhead): 7.6 pt for the
playhead's upper dot, the 243.5 pt panel, then the ruler. A second is 77 pt wide (`scale`) and
a bar stands every 4.15 pt, 1.5 pt thick. The overview is 68 pt tall: the 44.1 pt strip, which
the handle overlaps by 2.15 pt above and below, and the labels 9 pt under it. Its 310 bars are
placed by share of the width, 2.33 pt apart in the 723 pt strip of Voice Memos. Text is
13.5 pt with tabular digits.

## Accessibility

- The root is a `group`; name it with `aria-label`, usually the recording's title.
- The overview is one `slider` named "Recording position" (`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`
  renames it), from 0 to the duration in seconds, with the position as its value text, "00:03"
  (`formatTime` writes it; by default minutes and seconds). Left and Right move by `step`, Page Up, Page Down and the
  arrows with Shift by `largeStep`, Home and End go to the ends. A recording of no length is
  a disabled slider at 0, from 0 to 1: Base UI's slider needs a range.
- The detailed view is an image named "Waveform". It can be dragged with a pointer but takes
  no keyboard focus and is announced once, so the position is not read twice. A page that
  shows the detailed view alone must offer another way to seek from the keyboard.
- The ruler is part of the image and the overview's two labels are hidden from assistive
  technology: the slider's value text carries the time.
- The labels' grey on black is about 3:1, as in Voice Memos: below the 4.5:1 that small text
  needs. Raise `--mac-waveform-view-label` where the times must be read.
- In forced-colours mode the bars and the ruler's ticks use the system's text colours and
  the playhead and handle the highlight colour.

## Visual comparison

`npm run visual -- waveform-view` compares the detailed view and the overview with the Voice
Memos screenshots in both appearances; the recordings differ between the two (3.095 s and
3.065 s), so each has its own example. The bars are masked in every comparison: the
screenshots do not hold the recordings' samples, and a made-up envelope cannot match them.
Share of pixels that differ at 2x, in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Crop | Aqua | Dark Aqua |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detailed view, 789 x 280 pt (`112,210,1578,560`), with ruler labels | 0.93% | 1.25% |
| Detailed view, labels masked | 0.33% | 0.51% |
| Playhead alone, 10 x 259 pt (`892,210,20,518`), nothing masked | 5.62% | 5.63% |
| Overview, 723 x 68 pt (`178,860,1446,136`), with labels | 0.89% | 0.93% |
| Overview, labels masked | 0.24% | 0.23% |

Measured: the panel, the playhead's line and dots, the ticks' size, pitch and colours, the
labels' size, colour and position, the strip and its corners, the handle, and the bars' width,
pitch and colours (not compared, but read from the screenshots). What remains with the labels
masked is the ticks, which fall on fractions of a pixel in the screenshots, and the soft edges
of the playhead. The playhead's own crop is ten points wide, so that half a point of offset
would show as a tenth of it: what differs there is the line's leading edge, which is soft in
the captures and sharp in a browser, and the rims of the dots. At 1x that crop scores 20%,
because the line lies across two pixels of the scaled capture and on one in the browser.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows them: the height of a bar for a given amplitude; bars
after the playhead; the focus ring; the disabled view; and seeking, which is checked in a real
browser (`expect` comparison: Tab, then Home, moves the handle to the strip's start). The time
the overview's strip stands for (`span`, about 20 s in both captures) is fitted to the two
handle positions.
