<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSTabView` with top tabs and a bezel border, as in most System Preferences panes. The
tab strip is drawn like a segmented control; the content area is a [box](/components/box). It
is built on [Base UI's Tabs](https://base-ui.com/react/components/tabs), which supplies the
tab semantics, the keyboard handling and the controlled and uncontrolled value.

## Usage

```tsx
import { TabView, TabViewList, TabViewPanel, TabViewTab } from "macalicious/tab-view";
import "macalicious/tab-view.css";

<TabView defaultValue="output">
  <TabViewList aria-label="Sound">
    <TabViewTab value="effects">Sound Effects</TabViewTab>
    <TabViewTab value="output">Output</TabViewTab>
  </TabViewList>
  <TabViewPanel value="effects">…</TabViewPanel>
  <TabViewPanel value="output">…</TabViewPanel>
</TabView>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add tab-view`.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for tab views:

- Use a tab view for two to six closely related panes of the same level. For more, use a
  pop-up button or a sidebar.
- Title tabs with nouns or short noun phrases in title case (“Sound Effects”).
- Controls in a pane affect only that pane. Controls that apply to the whole window (help,
  lock) go outside the tab view, below it.
- Switching tabs must not change the size of the tab view or of the window: give the tab view
  a height that fits its tallest pane.
- Inset the tab view 20 pt from the window's edges.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selected tab | `value` / `defaultValue` | Accent gradient bezel, white title (white at 85% in Dark Aqua) |
| Unselected tab | – | Standard bezel; a 1 pt divider between two unselected neighbours |
| Pressed | `:active` | An unselected tab darkens; the selected one deepens. A press selects the tab when the button is released over it |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` on a tab | Focus ring around the whole strip |
| Disabled tab | `disabled` on a `TabViewTab` | Title at 25%; a disabled selected tab is grey |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | The selected tab is grey with a label-colour title; no focus ring |

The selected tab follows the accent colour (`data-accent`), with the same bezel as a
[push button](/components/push-button)'s primary variant.

Measured: the selected and unselected tabs, the divider, the end caps, the shadow under the
strip and the box, in both appearances with the blue accent. Provisional, because no
screenshot shows them: pressed, disabled, the focus ring, the look in a window that is not key,
and everything in the next section except the regular size with top tabs.

## Sizes

<Example name="sizes" both hideCode />

| Prop | Values | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `size` | `regular` (20 pt strip, 13 pt titles), `small`, `mini` | Regular is measured; small and mini follow the control-size tokens |
| `tabPosition` | `top`, `bottom`, `left`, `right`, `none` | Side tabs turn the strip on its side, as AppKit does: titles read top to bottom on the right and bottom to top on the left, and the bezel turns with them, so the accent gradient, the highlight and the shadow run from the strip's outer edge to the edge that faces the content. Bottom tabs are the upright strip on the bottom border: its bezel is not turned, light still falls from above (inferred from AppKit drawing the same strip there; no screenshot shows bottom tabs) |
| `border` | `bezel`, `line`, `none` | `line` is a 1 pt line in the separator colour |

The strip is centred on the border of the box: half of it is above the box. The tab view's own
box includes the strip, so its top edge is 10 pt above the border.

`TabViewTab` takes the props of Base UI's `Tabs.Tab`: `value` (required) and `disabled`.

<PropsTable name="TabViewPanelProps" />

`TabViewPanel` takes the props of Base UI's `Tabs.Panel`: `value` (required) and
`keepMounted`.

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-tab-position` | `top`, `bottom`, `left`, `right`, `none` | On the root. Reflects `tabPosition`. |
| `data-border` | `bezel`, `line`, `none` | On the root. Reflects `border`. |
| `data-size` | `regular`, `small`, `mini` | On the root. Reflects `size`. |
| `data-active` | present or absent | On the selected tab; set by Base UI. |
| `data-disabled` | present or absent | On a disabled tab; set by Base UI. |
| `data-force` | `pressed` (tab), `focus` (list) | Documentation and visual tests only: pins a transient state. |

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-tab-view-panel-padding` | `18px 20px 20px` | Padding of a pane. The default puts the first row of controls 29 pt below the box's top border, as in the Sound pane. |

`TabViewList` and `TabViewPanel` are items of the tab view's grid: put them directly inside
their `TabView`. A tab view in a pane of another keeps its own `tabPosition`, `border` and
`size`, whatever the outer one's are. The tabs of a strip are siblings: put them directly in
the `TabViewList`, or all together in one wrapper that has no box of its own
(`display: contents`).

`onValueChange` is called once for each change of tab. Control-Tab moves keyboard focus to the
new tab and selects it, and reports that as one change. The keys belong to the innermost tab
view around the element they come from: a tab view in a pane keeps them even when it has no
enabled tab to go to, and so does a menu or popover rendered outside the tab view.

## Accessibility

- The strip is a `tablist` of `tab` buttons and each pane a `tabpanel` named by its tab. Give
  the list an `aria-label`.
- The strip is one tab stop. Left and Right Arrow (Up and Down for side tabs) move to the
  neighbouring tab and select it, as in AppKit with full keyboard access; Home and End go to
  the first and last tab. Set `activateOnFocus={false}` on the list when showing a pane is
  slow: the arrow keys then only move focus, and Space or Return selects.
- Control-Tab and Control-Shift-Tab select the next and the previous tab from anywhere in the
  tab view, wrapping round and skipping disabled tabs, as in AppKit. Keyboard focus that is in
  the strip, or in the pane being hidden, moves to the newly selected tab. A browser keeps
  these two keys for its own tabs, so in an ordinary browser window the page never receives
  them: they work where the page has the window to itself (an installed web app, an embedded
  web view). Do not rely on them alone; the arrow keys always work.
- Tab moves from the strip to the pane, which is focusable so that a pane without controls can
  still be reached and read.
- A disabled tab cannot be selected.
- In forced-colours mode the selected tab is filled with the system highlight colour.

## Visual comparison

`visual-sound-tabs` reproduces the tab strip of the Sound pane on the top border of its box
(first tab selected) and `visual-energy-saver-tabs` the strip of the Energy Saver pane (last
tab selected). The segments are given their measured widths, because the titles are set in the
fallback font. Results in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Comparison | 2x, whole crop | 2x, titles masked, strict | 1x, whole crop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sound, Aqua | 9.08% of pixels differ | 0.01% | 15.30% |
| Sound, Dark Aqua | 8.61% | 0.06% | 14.87% |
| Energy Saver, Aqua | 10.96% | 0.02% | 17.26% |
| Security & Privacy (four tabs), Aqua | 7.52% | 0.02% | 12.78% |
| Security & Privacy (four tabs), Dark Aqua | 7.06% | 0.05% | 11.78% |
| Network Utility (eight tabs), Aqua | 6.66% | 0.02% | 11.26% |

`visual-security-tabs` and `visual-network-utility-tabs` reproduce two further strips, the
second across a whole window on a content box that runs from edge to edge. `visual-pane-corner`
reproduces the top-left corner of the content box in the Sound pane, counted at a colour
distance of 0.02 because the box's border is faint: 0.06% of the 2x pixels differ in Aqua and
0.00% in Dark Aqua, with the shared token `--mac-radius-group` at 4 pt (see the Box page). The
small and mini strips are in no screenshot and have no comparison.

The masked comparisons count a pixel as different at half the usual colour distance
(`threshold` 0.05). At 2x the bezel, the divider, the end caps, the shadow and the box match
the screenshots; what remains is the titles, set in the fallback font instead of San
Francisco. The strict comparison is what showed that the content box must not be painted
behind the strip: in Dark Aqua the strip's bezel is translucent, and with the box behind it
the box's top border showed through the unselected tabs (0.42% of the pixels); the capture
shows the window there. At 1x with the titles masked 9 to 19% of the pixels differ at the
strict distance: there is no 1x screenshot, the 1x reference is the 2x one averaged in 2 by 2
blocks, and the strip's half-point hairlines cannot match their averaged counterparts
([Fidelity limits](/fidelity)). The Energy Saver pane is not captured with this tab selected in
Dark Aqua.

Tabs on the left and right are in no screenshot. `visual-side-tabs` checks that their bezel is
turned: it reads the selected tab's fill a quarter and three quarters of the way across each
strip and expects the second and fourth colour stops of the accent gradient there, in both
appearances.

Pressed, keyboard focus, a window that is not key and another accent are in no screenshot
either. `visual-states` puts three top tabs into each of them with real input and checks
pixels, in both appearances: an unselected tab held down takes the pressed bezel fill
(`#f0f0f0`, and white at 36% in Dark Aqua) while the first tab stays selected, the selected
tab held down deepens, the focus ring surrounds the whole strip, the selected tab is grey
(`#d5d5d5` and `#6a6a6a`) with no ring in a main and in an inactive window, and with the
purple accent it takes the purple bezel. All of these values are inferred. Three more checks
follow real input to its result: a click on the third tab selects it and deselects the first,
in both appearances, and Tab followed by Right Arrow selects the second tab with the ring
still round the strip. The pressed check
is what showed that a mouse press used to select the tab at once, so that the pressed look of
an unselected tab could never be seen; a tab is now selected when the button is released.
