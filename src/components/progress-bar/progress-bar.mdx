title: Progress Bar
description: A capsule that fills as a task advances, or carries a sweeping band while the length of the task is unknown.

<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `NSProgressIndicator` with the bar style. It is built on
[Base UI's Progress](https://base-ui.com/react/components/progress), which supplies the
`progressbar` role and the value attributes. For work in a small space, use a
[progress spinner](/components/progress-spinner).

## Usage

```tsx
import { ProgressBar } from "macalicious/progress-bar";
import "macalicious/progress-bar.css";

<ProgressBar value={64} aria-label="Backing up" />
<ProgressBar aria-label="Connecting" />
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add progress-bar`.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for progress indicators:

- Use a determinate bar when the length of the task is known, and report progress
  accurately: do not let the bar stall at 90%. Use an indeterminate bar only while the length
  cannot be known, and switch to determinate as soon as it can.
- Keep a progress indicator in the same place for the whole task, in a view, not in the
  window frame. Do not swap between a bar and a spinner for one task.
- Say what is happening in words above or below the bar (“Copying 12 items…”, “About 4
  minutes remaining”), and offer Cancel, or a stop button at the bar's trailing end when
  interrupting has side effects.
- People do not interact with the bar itself.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Determinate | `value` | Accent fill from the leading end, square at its trailing edge; in Aqua the track and the fill each have a darker hairline |
| Complete | `value` equal to `max` | The fill is a full capsule |
| Indeterminate | `value={null}`, the default | A band 30% of the track wide sweeps along it every 1.5 s |
| Stopped | `animating={false}` | An indeterminate bar shows an empty track |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | Grey fill |

<Example name="live" />

The fill follows the accent colour (`data-accent`). A change of value is animated over a
quarter of a second, as in AppKit.

Measured: the determinate bar in both appearances with the blue accent. Provisional, because
no screenshot shows them: the indeterminate sweep (Catalina's replacement for the barber pole;
a still image cannot show it), the small size, the look in a window that is not key, and the
fill in the seven other accents, which is derived from their bezel colours the way the blue
one relates to the blue bezel.

### Animation cost

- The sweep animates `transform` only, so it runs on the compositor.
- While the bar is outside the viewport its animation is paused. One `IntersectionObserver`,
  shared by every indicator on the page (`macalicious/lib/offscreen`), sets `data-offscreen`
  on the bar; nothing re-renders. Measured in Chromium over two seconds: an off-screen bar
  produces no frames at all, on the compositor or on the main thread.
- Only a bar that is animating is watched. A determinate or stopped bar has no animation and
  no observer entry.
- `animating={false}` removes the animation altogether.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` nothing moves: the indeterminate band covers the
  track and fades in and out, and a change of value is not animated.

## API

<PropsTable name="ProgressBarProps" />

It also takes the props of Base UI's `Progress.Root`: `min`, `max`, `format`, `locale`,
`getAriaValueText` and `aria-valuetext`.

| Attribute | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-indeterminate`, `data-progressing`, `data-complete` | present or absent | The status, set by Base UI on the root and on the fill. |
| `data-size` | `regular`, `small` | Reflects `size`. |
| `data-animating` | present or absent | Reflects `animating`. |
| `data-offscreen` | present or absent | Set while an animating bar is outside the viewport; pauses the sweep. |

## Accessibility

- The bar has `role="progressbar"` with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax`;
  an indeterminate bar has no `aria-valuenow`. It is not focusable.
- It needs a name: `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby` pointing at the text that says what is
  in progress.
- Use `getAriaValueText` when a percentage is not the natural reading (“3 of 12 items”).
- Screen readers do not announce every change. Put the status line in a polite live region if
  progress must be spoken.
- In forced-colours mode the track is outlined and the fill uses the system highlight colour,
  with reduced motion too.

## Visual comparison

`visual-finder-copy-light` and `visual-finder-copy-dark` reproduce the bar of Finder's Copy
window on the window background, and `visual-time-machine` the backup bar of the Time Machine
pane, which lies on a box. Results in Chromium on Linux:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finder Copy, Aqua | 0.03% of pixels differ | 6.60% |
| Finder Copy, Dark Aqua | 0.00% | 1.07% |
| Time Machine, Aqua | 0.13% | 10.53% |
| Trailing end of the track alone, Aqua (strict) | 0.67% | 11.11% |
| Trailing end of the track alone, Dark Aqua (strict) | 0.00% | 6.67% |
| Leading end of the fill alone, Aqua (strict) | 1.22% | 14.22% |
| Leading end of the fill alone, Dark Aqua (strict) | 0.89% | 9.33% |

The last four (`visual-track-end`, `visual-fill-start`, `visual-fill-start-dark`) are 15 pt
squares around one rounded
end, counted at half the usual colour distance (`threshold` 0.05), so that the shape of the end
is most of what is scored: in the whole-bar comparisons a change of 1 pt in the radius moves
the figure by 0.03 points at most. The ends are half circles of 3 pt, as the measured spec
gives them. They do not match exactly: drawn with a radius of 2.5 pt the three strict figures
would be 0.22%, 0.00% and 0.33%. The radius is not changed on that evidence alone.

The budgets sit just above the figures in the table. What they catch was found by changing
the radius in the stylesheet and running the comparisons again:

- A track radius of 2 pt fails both `track-end` comparisons at 2x (1.78% and 0.67%).
- A track radius of 2.5 pt lowers every 2x figure, so no 2x budget catches it. The run still
  fails, but only at 1x and by one pixel: `track-end-light` scores 12.44% (28 of 225 pixels)
  against a budget of 12.23%.
- A larger radius cannot be drawn. The ends are already half circles, and a browser reduces a
  radius of more than half the height to exactly that; with 3.5 pt every figure is unchanged.
- The fill's own radius is not checked by a comparison. The track clips the fill, and with
  the fill's radius at 2 pt or 2.5 pt every comparison still passes.

Because the screenshots hold the radius so loosely, it is also checked directly:
`progress-bar.browser.test.tsx` reads the computed radii in a real browser and requires 3 pt
for the track and for the leading end of the fill (2 pt for the small bar). A change of the
radius in either direction fails that test, whatever the comparisons score.

At 2x what differs is the one column where the fill ends, which the screenshot draws half
covered. The 1x figures are not a fault of the bar. In both captures the bar lies on a half
point, and there is no 1x screenshot: the 1x reference is the 2x one averaged in 2 by 2
blocks, which spreads each of the bar's hairlines over two rows, while a browser at 1x draws
the bar on whole pixels. The Aqua Finder Copy figure is exactly one row of the bar (its top
hairline against the reference's plain track), and the Time Machine figure two rows over the
filled part and one over the rest; Dark Aqua has no hairline and scores 1%. The dark capture
of Time Machine shows no backup in progress, so the leading end of the dark fill is compared
with the bar of Finder's Copy window instead.

No screenshot shows a bar in a window that is not key or in another accent. `visual-states`
checks those with pixels in both appearances: the grey fill in a main and in an inactive
window (`#9b9b9b` in Aqua, `#6a6a6a` in Dark Aqua, both inferred), that the graphite accent
gets the same grey there in both appearances, and the purple fill (`#be63b3` and `#a541a3`,
the stops of the purple bezel that correspond to the measured blue fill).
