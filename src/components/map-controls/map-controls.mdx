Maps and Find My draw a few small controls over the map itself: a minus and a plus button that
zoom, a compass whose middle toggles 3D, a button that shows the current location, a "Show"
pull-down and, in Find My, a segmented switch between Map, Hybrid and Satellite. They stay in
the map's bottom corners while it pans. In AppKit they belong to `MKMapView`
(`showsZoomControls`, `showsCompass`); here they are separate components that an application
places over its own map with `MapControls`. The buttons are Base UI's Button and Toggle, the
map-type switch is its Radio Group.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  MapButton,
  MapCompass,
  MapControls,
  MapTypeControl,
  MapTypeOption,
  MapZoomControl,
} from "macalicious/map-controls";
import "macalicious/map-controls.css";

<div style={{ position: "relative" }}>
  <MyMap zoom={zoom} heading={heading} type={type} />

  <MapControls placement="bottom-leading" aria-label="Map type">
    <MapTypeControl value={type} onValueChange={setType} aria-label="Map type">
      <MapTypeOption value="map">Map</MapTypeOption>
      <MapTypeOption value="hybrid">Hybrid</MapTypeOption>
      <MapTypeOption value="satellite">Satellite</MapTypeOption>
    </MapTypeControl>
  </MapControls>

  <MapControls orientation="vertical" aria-label="Map controls">
    <MapCompass heading={heading} onNorthUp={() => setHeading(0)} />
    <MapZoomControl
      onZoomIn={() => setZoom(zoom + 1)}
      onZoomOut={() => setZoom(zoom - 1)}
      zoomInDisabled={zoom === MAX}
    />
  </MapControls>
</div>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add map-controls`.

The library has no map: the controls report what the user asked for and the application moves
its own map (a tile provider, an image). `MapControls` pins a group to a corner of the nearest
positioned ancestor, 15 pt from its edges (`--mac-map-controls-margin`). A vertical group
stacks the compass over the zoom pair; nest it in a horizontal one, with `placement="none"`,
to put a button beside the stack as Find My does.

`MapButton` is a single button: `bordered` is the small button of Maps and `rounded` the
borderless one of Find My. With `chevron` it is the "Show" pull-down; pass it as the `render`
of a menu's trigger to open a menu.

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines for macOS 10.15 have no entry for map controls. What follows is
how Maps and Find My use them in the screenshots (not verified against a published guideline):

- Keep the controls in the map's bottom corners and do not scale them with the window.
- The zoom pair and the compass belong together at the trailing edge; choices about what the
  map shows go at the leading edge.
- Every control has a menu command or a gesture as well: Command-plus and Command-minus zoom.
- Where 3D is not offered, show the 3D button dimmed, as Find My does; do not remove it.
- The glyph of a button is the application's own drawing. The library ships none of Apple's.

## Sizes

One size. A bordered button and each half of the zoom pair are 20 pt tall; the pair is two
28 pt buttons side by side. The rounded button is 34.5 x 21 pt. The compass is 53 pt across
with a 14 pt title. The map-type control is 22.25 pt tall and at least 231 pt wide; its
segments share the width, the pill stands 2 pt inside its segment, and the titles are 10.5 pt,
the iPad's size scaled to 77% as in every Mac Catalyst app, tracked 0.35 pt tighter, as the
scaled 13 pt type is (Find My's People, Devices, Map, Hybrid and Satellite). Controls in a row stand 12 pt
apart, in a column 6 pt.

## Accessibility

- `MapControls` is a `group`; name each one with `aria-label`.
- The zoom pair is a group named "Zoom" with two buttons, "Zoom out" and "Zoom in". A half
  that is disabled because the map reached a limit stays focusable (`aria-disabled`), so
  keyboard focus is not thrown away while zooming.
- The compass is a group named "Compass": its ring is a button named "Face north" and its
  middle a toggle button named "3D" whose `aria-pressed` says whether 3D is on. The rose is
  decoration; announce the heading elsewhere if it matters.
- The map-type control is a `radiogroup`; give it an `aria-label`. It is one Tab stop, and the
  arrow keys move the selection among its radios.
- A button that holds only a glyph needs an `aria-label`.
- The map under the controls is the application's: give it its own keyboard commands for
  panning and zooming, and do not rely on the controls alone.
- In forced-colours mode every control is outlined in the system's button text colour and the
  selected map type uses the highlight colours.

The masked rows use a stricter threshold (0.05 in place of 0.1), which is why some score
higher than the rows above them. Measured: the faces, edges, corners and shadows of the
bordered controls, the glyph bars, the compass's disc, gap, marks and 3D button, the rounded
button's size and face, and the map-type control's track, pill, hairline and title size.

What the remaining differences are: the map around and behind the controls; the translucent
dark faces, which take a little of the map's colour in the screenshots and are flat here; the
edge of the map-type control's track, which is a tone darker than its middle in the capture;
text in the fallback font; and the location glyph, a stand-in drawing.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows them: the pressed and disabled looks, 3D on, a turned
compass and the focus ring. Selecting another map type with a click, a pressed zoom half and
keyboard focus are checked in a real browser against the token values (`expect` comparisons).
The Find My zoom pair, compass and dimmed 3D button are not compared here.
