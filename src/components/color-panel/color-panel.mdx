AppKit calls this window the *color panel* (`NSColorPanel`); its title is "Colors". It is the
standard place to choose a colour on the Mac: a toolbar of pickers, an optional opacity slider,
the current colour and an eyedropper. Here it is a [`Window`](/components/window) of the panel
kind whose toolbar is a tab list built on
[Base UI's Tabs](https://base-ui.com/react/components/tabs), and each picker is a component of
its own that also works alone:

| Toolbar item | Picker | Component |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Color Wheel | Hue and saturation on a disc, with a brightness slider | [`ColorWheel`](/components/color-wheel) |
| Color Sliders | Grey scale, RGB or HSB sliders and a hex field | [`ColorSliders`](/components/color-sliders) |
| Color Palettes | The "Apple" list of named colours | [`ColorPalette`](/components/color-palette) |
| Image Palettes | The spectrum | [`ColorSpectrum`](/components/color-wheel) |
| Pencils | The 48 crayons | [`ColorPalette`](/components/color-palette), `variant="pencils"` |

**No reference screenshot shows the Colors panel.** Its layout follows Apple's documentation
of `NSColorPanel` and the Human Interface Guidelines; every measurement of its own is an
estimate and is tagged provisional. See [Visual comparison](#visual-comparison).

## Usage

```tsx
import { ColorPanel, ColorPanelWell } from "macalicious/color-panel";
import "macalicious/color-panel.css";

<ColorPanel value={color} onValueChange={setColor} onClose={() => setShown(false)} />

<ColorPanelWell aria-label="Text colour" value={color} onValueChange={setColor} />
```

The value is a colour as `#rrggbb`. With `showsAlpha` the panel has an opacity slider, and a
colour that is not opaque is reported as `#rrggbbaa`. Or copy the source into your project:
`npx macalicious add color-panel`. See [Installation](/installation).

### Opened by a colour well

`ColorPanelWell` is a [colour well](/components/color-well) that opens the panel under itself
in place of the browser's own colour chooser. The well is drawn active while its panel is
open; Escape, a click outside the panel or its close button closes it.

<Example name="well" />

In AppKit one shared panel floats above every window and edits whichever well is active. On a
page, a panel per well in a popup is the closer fit; for one shared panel, render a
`ColorPanel` yourself (in a [managed window](/components/managed-window), for example) and
point it at the active well's state.

## Guidelines

From Apple's documentation of
[NSColorPanel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nscolorpanel) and the macOS
Human Interface Guidelines for
[color wells](https://web.archive.org/web/20200504154558/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/selectors/color-wells/):

- The colour panel is the system's standard interface for selecting a colour. Open it from a
  colour well, which darkens to show that it is the one being edited, rather than building a
  chooser of your own.
- Show the opacity slider (`showsAlpha`) only where a colour may be translucent. Without it,
  treat every colour as opaque.
- The panel is continuous by default: it reports the colour while a slider or the wheel's
  marker is being dragged. Apply the colour as it changes; there is no OK button.
- AppKit's panel also lets people keep frequently used colours as swatches, by dragging the
  current colour into a grid at the bottom. This component does not implement the swatch grid
  or dragging colours; keep favourite colours in a `ColorPalette` of your own.

## Variants and states

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chosen picker | a click on its toolbar item, or the arrow keys and Return | A darker rounded rectangle behind the item's glyph |
| Pressed toolbar item | `:active` | A darker rectangle still |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` on a toolbar item or the eyedropper | Focus ring in the accent colour |
| Eyedropper unavailable | the browser has no `EyeDropper` (anything but Chromium on a desktop) | The eyedropper is disabled and dimmed, and a line under it says why, which makes the panel that line taller |
| Translucent colour | `showsAlpha` and an opacity under 100% | The current colour is drawn over grey and white tiles |
| Window not key | `state="main"` or `"inactive"`, or such an ancestor | As any window: flat title bar when inactive, no focus rings, unemphasised selection in the palette |

The pickers' own states are on their pages.

## Sizes

The panel is 228 pt wide by default (`width`) and as tall as its content: the pickers share
one height, so the panel does not change size with the picker. It has no control sizes; the
controls inside are small ones, as in AppKit's panel.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-window-state`, `data-variant="panel"` | as on `Window` | Set by the window. |

The transient states (a pressed toolbar item, keyboard focus) are on elements inside the
panel, so there is no `data-force` preview for them; the visual checks reach them with real
input. The panel has no CSS variables of its own for a consumer to set.

## Accessibility

- The panel is a labelled region named by its title, "Colors"; in a `ColorPanelWell` it is a
  `dialog` that takes the focus when it opens and returns it to the well when it closes.
- The toolbar is a `tablist` named "Color pickers" and is one tab stop. Left and Right Arrow,
  Home and End move between its items; Return or Space chooses one. Each item is named after
  its picker and shows the name as a tooltip, since it has no visible text.
- Every control inside is reachable with Tab and has a name: the wheel's and spectrum's areas
  and the palettes follow the arrow keys (see their pages), every slider has a number field
  beside it, and the hex field takes a typed colour.
- The current colour is an image whose name is its hex value, and its opacity when it has one.
- The eyedropper is a button named "Pick a color from the screen". Where the browser cannot
  do that it is disabled, not hidden, and a line of text under it says why.
- A `ColorPanelWell` needs an accessible name (`aria-label`, `aria-labelledby` or a
  `<label>`), like any colour well. It stays a colour input and says that it opens a dialog
  (`aria-haspopup`); Return, Space or a click opens the panel and closes it again. It cannot
  say whether the panel is open: `aria-expanded` is not allowed on a colour input, so the open
  panel announces itself by taking the focus.
- In forced-colours mode the chosen toolbar item is outlined and the colours of the pickers
  and of the current colour are kept.

## Visual comparison

No screenshot in the reference set shows the Colors panel, so there is no crop to compare with
and `npm run visual -- color-panel` reports no score. Instead its checks drive the panel in a
real browser, at 1x and 2x in both appearances, and hold chosen pixels to values that do not
come from the panel's own output (`color-panel.visual.json`):

| What is checked | How it is brought about | Source of the values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The body's fill, the wheel's colours, the opacity track and the current colour, in both appearances | the panel as it opens | Window background and slider colours measured on other screenshots; the HSB model; the colour given |
| Each of the four other pickers | a click on its toolbar item | The colour given, the HSB model, the crayons' values |
| The toolbar from the keyboard | focus, Right Arrow, Return | The slider's measured colours |
| A colour chosen in a palette becomes the current colour | clicks on the palette | The colour chosen |
| The chosen row in a page that is not key | the same, with the page inactive | Unemphasised selection, measured in Contacts |
| Opacity 0 shows the tiles | the opacity field emptied with Backspace | The tiles' tokens (inferred) |
| A well opens the panel, with the pointer and with Tab and Space; Escape closes it; a colour chosen in the panel is the well's | real input on a `ColorPanelWell` | The well's active bezel (inferred by its spec), its measured bezel at rest, the colours given |

Everything about the panel's own geometry is provisional: its width, the 50 pt of title bar
and toolbar, the toolbar's glyphs (drawn for this library, not Apple's), the 12 pt margins and
the size of the current colour. Not implemented: the swatch grid for favourite colours,
dragging a colour to or from a well, the CMYK sliders, image palettes other than the
spectrum, colour lists other than "Apple", and choosing a colour space. Not checked anywhere:
Firefox and Safari, and the eyedropper itself, which needs a person to click on the screen;
its wiring is covered by a unit test with a stand-in for the browser's `EyeDropper`.
