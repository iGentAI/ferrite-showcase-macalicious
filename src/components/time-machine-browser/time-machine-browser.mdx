Entering Time Machine takes over the screen: the current window moves to the middle in front of
a stack of the same window at earlier backups, over a blurred desktop picture. The arrows and
the timeline move through the backups, the date of the one shown stands between the arrows,
Restore copies the selected items back and Cancel leaves.

The component draws that screen around a window you give it. It reads no backups and restores
nothing: the backups arrive through `backups`, and the choices leave through `onValueChange`,
`onRestore` and `onCancel`. Its buttons are built on
[Base UI's Button](https://base-ui.com/react/components/button).

## Usage

```tsx
import { TimeMachineBrowser } from "macalicious/time-machine-browser";
import { Window } from "macalicious/window";
import "macalicious/time-machine-browser.css";

<TimeMachineBrowser
  backups={[
    { value: "2019-10-06T10:17", label: "Yesterday at 10:17 AM", mark: "Yesterday" },
    { value: "2019-10-07T15:34", label: "Today at 3:34 PM", mark: "Today" },
    { value: "now", label: "Now", caption: "Now" },
  ]}
  value={backup}
  onValueChange={setBackup}
  title="Downloads"
  restorable={selection.length > 0}
  onRestore={(backup) => restore(backup, selection)}
  onCancel={leave}
  style={{ height: "100vh" }}
>
  <Window width={770} height={436}>…the folder as it was in that backup…</Window>
</TimeMachineBrowser>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add time-machine-browser`. The front
window is a [window](/components/window) of your own, with whatever is in it.

- **`backups`** are in the order of the timeline, oldest first; the last one is normally the
  present, with `caption: "Now"`. `label` is the date shown between the arrows. `mark` puts a
  day's name on a long tick above a backup: give it to the first backup of each day.
- **The stack** shows `stack` windows. One stands for each backup older than the one shown and
  carries a title bar with `title`; the windows beyond the oldest backup are blank. So the
  stack shrinks to blank windows as you go back.
- **The desktop picture** is yours: the browser paints a blue gradient of the measured colours.
  Set `--mac-time-machine-browser-backdrop` to a picture, blurred beforehand; see
  [Sizes](#sizes) for why not `background` and `filter`.

## Guidelines

Paraphrased from the measured spec and from Apple's support article on restoring items with
Time Machine; not checked against a published Human Interface Guidelines page.

- Show the same window the person was looking at, at the same size, so that what changes
  between backups is its content only.
- Dim the items that do not exist in the backup shown rather than removing them, and enable
  Restore only when something restorable is selected.
- Start at the present ("Now"), and let the arrows and the timeline move one backup at a time.
- Leave with Cancel or Escape without changing anything.

## Variants and states

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backup shown | `value`, or the arrows, the timeline and the keyboard | Its tick is red; its date is between the arrows; the stack has one titled window per older backup |
| Oldest backup | the first of `backups` is shown | The up arrow is disabled; every window of the stack is blank |
| Newest backup | the last of `backups` is shown | The down arrow is disabled |
| Restore disabled | default (`restorable` is `false`) | Face at 60% and a dim title |
| Restore enabled | `restorable` | As Cancel |
| Pressed | `:active` on a button | The face brightens |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring round the button; inside the edge of the timeline |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | No focus ring |

The buttons, the ticks and the timeline labels are not painted over the picture but added to
it (`mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter`), as the captures measure: a button is the picture plus 60
levels on every channel, over four different backdrops in the two appearances. They therefore
need no values of their own for Dark Aqua and follow any picture you put behind them.

Measured, in both appearances: the perspective of the stack, the blank window, the buttons
with their disabled look, the arrows, the date, the ticks, the day marks and the red tick.
Measured in Aqua only: the titled windows of the stack with their fading (the Dark Aqua capture
shows the oldest backup, so its stack is blank; the dark title bars follow the same rule and
are provisional). Provisional: the enabled Restore, the pressed face, and the placeholder
backdrop. Not drawn: the magnified tick and its date under the pointer, which macOS shows on
the timeline.

## Sizes

The browser fills the element it is in: give that element, or the browser itself, a height.
The front window keeps the size you give it and is centred, 57 pt below the middle; the arrows
stand 21 pt beside it and the buttons 23 pt under it, so leave about 150 pt free on each side
of the window and 120 pt above it for the stack. The timeline takes the trailing 140 pt, its
ticks the last 36 of them, and has room for one tick every 16 pt; more backups than fit
scroll in it, without a scroller, and the backup shown is kept in view.

Nothing between the browser's root and its buttons may open a stacking context, or the
buttons are added to nothing: that is why the picture is the root's own `background`, set
through `--mac-time-machine-browser-backdrop`, and why a `filter`, `opacity` or `transform` on
the root is to be avoided.

## API

<PropsTable name="TimeMachineBrowserProps" />

`className`, `style`, `ref` and other attributes go to the root element. A backup is
`{ value: string; label: string; mark?: string; caption?: string }` (the type
`TimeMachineBackup`).

| Attribute | On | Values | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `aria-selected` | `.mac-time-machine-browser-tick` | `true`, `false` | The backup shown. |
| `data-mark` | `.mac-time-machine-browser-tick` | present | The backup has a day mark above it. |
| `data-step` | `.mac-time-machine-browser-button` | `older`, `newer` | The two arrows. |
| `data-disabled` | `.mac-time-machine-browser-button` | present | A disabled arrow, or Restore. |
| `data-empty` | `.mac-time-machine-browser-layer` | present | A window of the stack with no backup behind it. |

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-time-machine-browser-backdrop` | a gradient of the measured blues | The background of the screen: any CSS background, for example a blurred picture. |

## Accessibility

- The root is a `dialog` named "Time Machine"; name it otherwise with `aria-label`. It does
  not trap the keyboard or make the rest of the page inert: do that where you open it.
- The timeline is a `listbox` named by `timelineLabel`, with one `option` per backup, named by
  its `label`. It is one stop in the tab order. Up and Down move to the backup before and
  after, Home and End to the oldest and the newest; the selection follows at once. A click on
  a tick selects its backup.
- The arrows are buttons named by `olderLabel` and `newerLabel`. At either end the arrow is
  disabled but keeps the keyboard focus, so stepping with Return or Space does not lose it.
- The date between the arrows is an `output`: a change of backup is announced.
- Escape anywhere in the browser calls `onCancel`, unless a control inside kept the key.
  Restore is a button; make Return press it from your own content if you want the key
  equivalent.
- The stack, the day marks and the captions are decoration and hidden from assistive
  technology.
- Nothing in the browser moves on its own, so there is nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion` to
  switch off. In forced-colours mode the buttons and the windows of the stack get a border and
  the selected tick the highlight colour.

The stack and the masked comparisons count a pixel as different at a colour distance of 0.05,
half the default. What remains in the stack is the ring round each window control, which the
stack does not draw, and one device pixel along some leading edges; in the others, corner and
chevron antialiasing. The titles, the date and the labels are set in the fallback font. In the
Aqua capture the 603 pt window starts on a whole point where centring gives a half point, so
the Aqua examples add half a point at the top. The arrows of the Aqua capture also start on a
half point, which a 1x display cannot show; their 1x figure is the highest of the masked ones.

The examples beside and under the window draw the window without its shadow, because the
colours measured there already hold the shadow of the window in the capture. Keyboard focus on
Cancel and the pressed Cancel are checked by pixel (`cancel-focus-light`,
`cancel-pressed-dark`), and so is a timeline with more backups than fit, at its newest and,
after Home, at its oldest backup (`timeline-long-newest`, `timeline-long-oldest`), since no
screenshot shows them. Not compared: the front window, which
is the [window](/components/window) component's, and the titles on the stack's title bars,
which the capture shows with a folder icon.
