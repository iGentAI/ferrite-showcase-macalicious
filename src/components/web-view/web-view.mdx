title: Web View
description: The content area of a browser window. It frames a page you supply, as children or in a sandboxed frame, and draws Safari's own pages - the Favorites grid, Reader, the Tab Overview, a loading line and an error page.

<Example name="basic" both />

AppKit's `WKWebView`, as Safari 13 uses it under its toolbar and tab bar. A web view has no
frame of its own: it is a surface that starts under the window's chrome and scrolls what does
not fit. This component frames content that you supply. It has no browsing engine: it does not
load, navigate or keep a history, and a site that forbids framing stays blank.

## Usage

```tsx
import { WebView, WebViewProgress } from "macalicious/web-view";
import "macalicious/web-view.css";

// The children are the page
<WebView title="Example page" style={{ height: 400 }}>
  <article>…</article>
</WebView>

// A document in a sandboxed frame; the title is required
<WebView src="https://example.com/" title="Example Domain" style={{ height: 400 }}>
  {loading && <WebViewProgress value={progress} aria-label="Loading example.com" />}
</WebView>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add web-view`.

Give the view a height (or put it in a layout that does): it scrolls its children with the
browser's own scroll bars. For Catalina's overlay scroll bars, make a
[scroll view](/components/scroll-view) of the view's full height its only child.

<Example name="frame" both />

With `src` the page is an `<iframe>` that fills the view (the example loads its frame when
asked). Its `sandbox` defaults to
`allow-scripts allow-forms`: the page runs its scripts in an origin of its own, and cannot read
the embedding page, open windows or navigate the top frame. Its referrer policy defaults to
`no-referrer`. Both are props; other frame attributes go in `frameProps`. Do not add
`allow-same-origin` to a sandbox that has `allow-scripts` for a page from your own origin: such
a page can remove its own sandbox.

### Safari's own pages

<Example name="favorites" both />

`WebViewFavorites` is the Favorites grid of the start page, on `surface="start"`. A
`WebViewFavorite` is a link; `render={<button type="button" />}` makes it a button, for a
folder. The tile shows its children: a letter, a drawing or an image of your own. The library
ships no site artwork and no Apple artwork. A tile takes a colour from
`--mac-web-view-tile-fill`, and an image that fills the tile covers it edge to edge.

<Example name="reader" both />

`WebViewReader` is the Reader sheet, on `surface="reader"`: a sheet of at most 786 pt, 22 pt
under the view's top edge, set in 20 pt text on 29 pt lines, with a 36 pt bold
`WebViewReaderTitle`. Paragraphs are yours; Safari sets them 18 pt apart.

<Example name="tab-overview" both />

`WebViewTabOverview` is the grid of the Tab Overview, on `surface="overview"`. A
`WebViewTabThumbnail` is a button with the page's title over a miniature, its children: a
picture or a drawing that you supply. `variant="new-tab"` is the grey tile with the +.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[web views](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503225736/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/web-views/):

- A web view loads and displays rich web content, such as embedded HTML and websites, directly
  within an app.
- Enable forward and back navigation when people will visit more than one page, and give them
  the controls for it. A web view has none of its own.
- Do not use a web view to build a web browser.

The parts on this page that reproduce Safari's start page, Reader and Tab Overview exist for a
demonstration of Safari itself.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page | default | The page background: white in Aqua, the text background colour in Dark Aqua |
| Start page | `surface="start"` | Safari's grey start-page canvas, behind `WebViewFavorites` |
| Tab Overview | `surface="overview"` | The same canvas, one level apart in Aqua, behind `WebViewTabOverview` |
| Reader | `surface="reader"` | Grey canvas around the `WebViewReader` sheet and its shadow |
| Framed | `src` | A sandboxed `<iframe>` fills the view |
| Loading | a `WebViewProgress` child | A 2 pt line in the accent colour along the top edge (provisional) |
| Cannot open the page | a `WebViewError` child | Centred grey headline and explanation (provisional) |
| Pressed tile or thumbnail | `:active` | Darkened (provisional) |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring, in the key window only: inside the view's edge, around a tile, around a thumbnail (provisional) |
| Window not key | `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` on an ancestor | The loading line turns grey and no focus ring is drawn (provisional) |

A page with a background of its own sets `--mac-web-view-background` on the view: Safari's
canvas shows wherever the page does not cover it.

Not covered: a browsing engine, navigation and history; the Frequently Visited section of the
start page and the close buttons of Tab Overview thumbnails, which no screenshot shows; and
the bookmarks sidebar, which is a [sidebar](/components/sidebar) beside the view.

## Sizes

A web view has no `size` prop. It is as wide as its container and needs a height. The
Favorites grid has a fixed width, 115 pt for each of its `columns`, and is centred; the Tab
Overview shares the view's width among its `columns`, and each thumbnail keeps the proportion
in `--mac-web-view-thumbnail-aspect`, as Safari's keep the window's.

## Accessibility

- Without `src` the view is a scrolling `<div>` in the tab order, so that the arrow keys, Page
  Up and Page Down scroll it. With a `title` it is a `region` of that name. Pass
  `tabIndex={-1}` when the page inside has focusable content and the extra stop is not wanted.
- With `src` the view is an `<iframe>` and needs a `title`, which names the frame; the view
  around it is then neither focusable nor a region.
- `WebViewProgress` is a `progressbar` (Base UI's Progress). Name it with `aria-label`, and
  remove it when the page has loaded.
- `WebViewFavorites` is a section named by its heading, an `<h2>`. A `WebViewFavorite` is
  named by its `label` and `detail`; the tile's content is hidden from assistive technology,
  so a tile must not be the only place a site is named.
- `WebViewReader` is an `<article>` and `WebViewReaderTitle` its `<h1>`; `WebViewError` has an
  `<h2>`. Give the error `role="alert"` when it replaces a page the person was waiting for.
- `WebViewTabOverview` is a `group`: name it with `aria-label`. A `WebViewTabThumbnail` is a
  button named by its `title`; the miniature is hidden from assistive technology. The new-tab
  tile has no text and needs an `aria-label`.
- A disabled thumbnail, and a favourite with `aria-disabled="true"`, ignore a click on
  whatever element they are rendered as: a link is not followed. A thumbnail rendered as a
  link gets `aria-disabled` from `disabled`.
- The focus ring is drawn in the key window only. In forced-colours mode tiles, the Reader
  sheet and thumbnails keep a border and the loading line takes the highlight colour.

difference out of 255.

What is in the crops, and what is left out of the score:

- **Reader**: the top 80 pt of the page across the whole sheet, above the article's title.
  No text or site content is in the crop, and nothing is masked.
- **Favorites**: the heading and the first two columns of the grid. The heading and the tile
  titles are masked because they are text, set in the fallback font off Apple platforms; the
  middle of every tile is masked because the capture has site artwork there (Apple's logo, a
  folder's miniatures), which the library does not reproduce. The tiles' corners, edges and
  shadows, their pitch and the canvas are scored.
- **Tab Overview**: the new-tab tile whole, with its +, and two corners of the first
  thumbnail: border, title band and shadow. The crops avoid the title, and the miniature of
  the third-party page is masked in the bottom corner.

The shadow comparisons count a pixel as different at a colour distance of 0.02, a fifth of the
default. A fitted shadow differs from the capture by a level or two at most, so their mismatch
stays at zero and it is the mean Δ, with a budget just above the score, that fails when a
shadow or a fill changes.

Measured (image pixels in the light capture; the dark one is 70 px narrower):

- Canvases: start page `#e7e9ea` (Favorites @ 1096,152), Tab Overview `#e7e8e9` (@ 220,152),
  Reader `#e6e6e6` (@ 600,300); dark `#2c2d30`, `#2c2d30` and `#323233`. The page surface in
  Aqua is white (Safari-v13 @ 1400,204).
- Favorites: heading 20 pt bold with its baseline 51 pt under the page's top edge; tiles
  80 x 80 pt with 8 pt corners, first row 75 pt under the top edge, at a pitch of 115 x 148 pt;
  titles 13 pt semibold with the baseline 19 pt under the tile, and a 12 pt second line. The
  tile with no artwork is `#f3f3f4` (`#6a6b6c` in Dark Aqua) with a 1 pt edge (row 730).
- Reader: sheet 786 pt wide, 22 pt under the top edge, 70 pt of padding; title 36 pt bold with
  its baseline 87 pt under the sheet's top edge; text 20 pt on 29 pt lines, `#1b1b1b`
  (`#d7d7d8`).
- Tab Overview: thumbnails 18 pt apart, 49 pt from the sides and 62 pt under the top edge, with
  a square 1 pt border and a 25 pt title band (`#ececec`, `#303134`) holding a 13 pt title;
  the new-tab tile `#d0d1d2` (`#3f4143`) with a + of 49 pt in a 220 pt tile.

Computed from measurements: the three shadows (tile, sheet and thumbnail), fitted to the
capture's rows and columns, and the alphas of the tile's edge and the thumbnail's border.

These readings differ from the written spec in three places, where the pixels were followed:
the tile titles measure 13 pt and their second line 12 pt (the spec has 12 and 11), the Tab
Overview titles measure 13 pt (the spec has 11), and the tiles have a shadow, which the spec
does not mention.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows it: the loading line (the spec only says that Safari
draws a thin accent-coloured bar while a page loads), the error page, the pressed and focused
looks, every look in a window that is not key, the colour of a tile's letter, and the page
surface in Dark Aqua, where a real page defines its own. `web-view.visual.json` checks the
loading line, the focus ring and the inactive window with real input against these inferred
values. The framed form has no comparison: `web-view.browser.test.tsx` tests in a real
browser that the frame fills the view, that the loading line is drawn over it and that the
framed document runs in an origin of its own. Text is San Francisco only on Apple devices.
