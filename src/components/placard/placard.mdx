A placard is a view at the bottom edge of a window, in the row of the horizontal scroll bar. It
held controls for quick changes to the view of the content: buttons to switch pages, a pop-up
to adjust the magnification. AppKit has no class for it; applications placed a small control in
the scroll view's scroller area themselves.

**No visual reference.** None of the 294 reference screenshots shows a placard, and Apple
discourages them. The purpose follows the Human Interface Guidelines; every measurement and
colour on this page is inferred from the always-visible scroll bar the placard sits beside,
and is provisional.

The buttons are built on [Base UI's Button](https://base-ui.com/react/components/button); the
strip itself is a plain group.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Placard, PlacardButton, PlacardLabel } from "macalicious/placard";
import "macalicious/placard.css";

<Placard aria-label="Pages">
  <PlacardButton aria-label="Previous Page" onClick={previous}>…</PlacardButton>
  <PlacardLabel>Page 3 of 12</PlacardLabel>
  <PlacardButton aria-label="Next Page" onClick={next}>…</PlacardButton>
</Placard>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add placard`.

The placard is placed by the caller. The [scroll view](/components/scroll-view) has no slot
for one: put the placard in a row under the scroll view, as the example does, or lay it over
the bottom left corner of a view with overlay scroll bars. It is as wide as its content; give
a button a `width` to keep a changing title from moving its neighbours.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[placards](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503140902/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/placards/):

- **Avoid using placards.** They are seldom used in modern applications and their use is
  discouraged. Toolbars and menus are the preferred ways to let people manipulate the view of
  content.
- Historically a placard provided controls for quick modifications to the view of content
  within a window: for example, buttons to switch pages or a pop-up button to adjust the
  magnification.

Use this component for a period interface, not for a new one.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | – | The scroll bar track's fill, a hairline at the top and at the trailing edge, hairlines between neighbours |
| Pop-up button | `popUp` | Two arrowheads after the title |
| Pressed, menu open | `:active`, or `data-popup-open` from a menu trigger | Its part of the strip darkens (Aqua) or lightens (Dark Aqua) by 10% |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring inside the button; none in a window that is not key |
| Disabled | `disabled` | Title at 25% |

All of it is provisional.

## Sizes

| Size | Height | Font | Beside |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `regular` | 15 pt | 10 pt | a regular scroll bar |
| `small`, `mini` | 11 pt | 9 pt | a small scroll bar |

The heights are those of the [scroll view](/components/scroll-view)'s always-visible scroll
bars, which are themselves inferred.

## Accessibility

- The strip is a `group`; name it with `aria-label`. Pass `role="toolbar"` only if you also
  give it a toolbar's arrow-key navigation.
- `PlacardButton` is a native button: Space and Return activate it, Tab reaches each one, and
  a disabled one leaves the tab order. A button that holds only a glyph needs an `aria-label`.
- The pop-up arrowheads are decoration. A button that opens a menu gets `aria-haspopup` and
  `aria-expanded` from the menu trigger it is rendered by.
- Text in a placard is 10 pt, or 9 pt when small: small for reading. Keep it to a value or a
  few words, and do not put anything there that is not available elsewhere.
- A placard button is 15 pt high, less than the 24 pt target size WCAG 2.2 asks for. That is
  the historical control; one more reason to prefer a toolbar or a menu.
- In forced-colours mode the hairlines use the text colour.

## Visual comparison

No comparison with a screenshot is possible: no placard is in the reference set, which
`placard.visual.json` records as pending. `npm run visual -- placard` instead renders a regular
and a small placard in a real browser and checks pixels against the values the spec infers.
The checks pass in both appearances at 1x and 2x.

| State | What is checked | Source of the values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At rest | Fill `#fafafa` (Dark Aqua `#2b2b2b`); hairlines `#e8e8e8` (`#3b3b3b`) at the top, at the trailing edge and between two buttons; heights of 15 and 11 pt | Inferred from the scroll bar track |
| Pressed | The pressed button's fill, 10% darker or lighter; the other button unchanged | Inferred |
| Keyboard focus | Ring colour in the 3 pt band inside the button, fill inside it | Focus ring token; placement inferred |
| Window not key | No ring on the focused button | Inferred |

Provisional: everything, including the height, the fill, the hairlines, the text size, the
padding of 6 pt, the pop-up arrowheads and the pressed look.
