The floating panel Siri opens at the top right of the screen. The component is the window and
its result card only: it is no assistant. The prompt, the results and the level arrive through
props, and the orb is the library's own drawing, not Siri's.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Siri, SiriCard } from "macalicious/siri";
import "macalicious/siri.css";

<Siri prompt="What’s the weather today?" listening={listening} onListen={toggle} onClose={close}>
  <SiriCard title="Weather">…</SiriCard>
</Siri>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add siri`.

The children are the results, normally one `SiriCard` each; the region they are in scrolls
under the fixed strips. A card is light in both appearances: when it holds other controls of
the library, put `data-appearance="light"` on it. Text in a card takes the card's colour;
`var(--mac-siri-card-secondary)` is the colour of its secondary lines.

## Guidelines

From the macOS User Guide on Siri (the Human Interface Guidelines treat Siri as a technology,
not as a macOS control):

- Open the window at the top right of the screen, under the menu bar, and close it with its
  close button or Escape.
- Show what was understood at the top, then the answer as cards that can be scrolled.
- The orb listens again when it is clicked; while listening it is a waveform that follows the
  voice.

## Variants and states

<Example name="listening" both hideCode />

| Variant or state | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Result shown | children | Cards 320 pt wide, 12 pt from the window's edges, under the 28 pt top strip |
| Prompt | `prompt` | Text above the results |
| Listening | `listening` | A waveform of five bars replaces the orb; `level` sets its height |
| Waveform still | `animated={false}`, or reduced motion | Each bar at the height `level` gives it |
| Help button | `onHelp` | A 12 pt disc with a question mark at the left of the bottom strip |
| Button pressed | pointer or keyboard press | The discs lighten; the orb dims |
| Keyboard focus | Tab | Focus ring on the button, or inside the results |
| Not the key window | `data-window-state` of `main` or `inactive` on an ancestor | No focus ring; the window itself does not change |

Measured: the window, its strips, lines, edge and shadow, the close and help buttons, the card
with its header band, and the text colours on the card. Provisional: the prompt, the listening
state and the pressed looks, which no screenshot shows; the orb and the waveform, which are
original drawings in place of Siri's artwork; and the chrome's material, the shared
`--mac-material-hud`, whose alpha is inferred because the window was captured with nothing
behind it.

## Sizes

One size: 344 x 600 pt. `--mac-siri-width` and `--mac-siri-height` change it, for a page with
less room; the strips keep their heights and the results take the rest.

## API

`className`, `style`, `ref` and other attributes go to the root element. `aria-label` defaults
to "Siri".

<PropsTable name="SiriCardProps" />

| CSS variable | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-siri-width` | `344px` | Width of the window. |
| `--mac-siri-height` | `600px` | Height of the window. |

| `--mac-siri-card-secondary` | black at 55% | Read it for secondary text in a card. |

## Accessibility

- The root is a `dialog` named "Siri". It is not modal and moves no focus: focus the window,
  or its orb, when it opens, and return the focus when it closes.
- The close button, the help button and the orb have no text, so each has an accessible name:
  `closeLabel`, `helpLabel` and `listenLabel`. The orb's button is announced as pressed while
  listening.
- The results are a `group` named by `resultsLabel` and a polite live region, so a new answer
  is announced. The region is in the tab order, so it can be scrolled with the arrow keys. A
  card is an `article` named by its title; its icon is decorative.
- Tab moves through close, results, help and orb; Return and Space activate a button; Escape
  calls `onClose` while the keyboard focus is in the window.
- The waveform animates transforms only and stands still with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- In forced-colours mode the window, the cards and the buttons get a border, and the orb keeps
  its colours.

## Visual comparison

`visual-light` and `visual-dark` reproduce the two Siri captures whole, shadow included, with
the Weather result. The window is the same in both: the captures differ in the result and in
the desktop picture, which Catalina blended into the materials before capturing the window
with nothing behind it. Two things in the examples stand in for the picture: an element behind
the window, grey (#757575, which the HUD material turns into the measured #383838) with the
casts the chrome has in each capture, and `--mac-siri-card-glow` for the tints in the card.
Results in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Comparison | 2x | 1x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window, Aqua capture | 2.68% of pixels differ, mean Δ 3.86 | 3.22%, mean Δ 3.98 |
| Window, Aqua capture, text, glyphs and orb masked, threshold 0.05 | 0.03%, mean Δ 1.01 | 0.03%, mean Δ 1.02 |
| Window, Dark Aqua capture | 2.56%, mean Δ 4.77 | 3.12%, mean Δ 4.90 |
| Window, Dark Aqua capture, text, glyphs and orb masked, threshold 0.05 | 0.02%, mean Δ 2.06 | 0.03%, mean Δ 2.08 |

In the unmasked comparisons what differs is the text, set in the fallback font instead of San
Francisco, the weather glyphs and the header icon, which are plain shapes, and the orb. With
those masked the remainder is the tint of the desktop picture, which the gradients follow only
roughly, more so in the Dark Aqua capture, whose casts are stronger.

What no screenshot shows is checked on the same flat grey (`visual-states`), in both
appearances: the chrome, rim and lines, the close button's disc at rest and held down with the
mouse, the card's fill and header band, the focus ring on the orb after four Tabs, and the
waveform at full level. The pressed looks and the listening state are inferred; the checks
only hold the component to what is inferred.
