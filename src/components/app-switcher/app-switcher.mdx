<Example name="basic" both />

Holding Command and pressing Tab shows the running applications, most recently used first;
Tab moves along the row and letting go switches to the selected one. `AppSwitcher` is that
panel: it shows the items you give it, keeps the selection, and tells you which application
was chosen. Base UI has no primitive for it, so it follows the listbox pattern of the WAI-ARIA
Authoring Practices.

## Usage

```tsx
import { AppSwitcher, AppSwitcherItem } from "macalicious/app-switcher";
import "macalicious/app-switcher.css";

<div style={{ position: "relative", height: "100vh" }}>
  <AppSwitcher open={open} onOpenChange={setOpen} onChoose={(app) => activate(app)}>
    <AppSwitcherItem value="terminal" icon={<TerminalIcon />}>Terminal</AppSwitcherItem>
    <AppSwitcherItem value="mail" icon={<MailIcon />}>Mail</AppSwitcherItem>
    <AppSwitcherItem value="notes" icon={<NotesIcon />}>Notes</AppSwitcherItem>
  </AppSwitcher>
</div>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add app-switcher`.

- The panel is centred in the nearest positioned ancestor: that element is the screen. Its
  material blurs what is behind it in the same page, and nothing else
  ([Fidelity limits](/fidelity)).
- An item's `value` identifies the application, its `icon` is any image or drawing of your
  own, and its children are the name. The library ships no icons.
- **A page cannot have Command-Tab**: the operating system keeps it. Open the switcher from
  a button or from a key of your own (`open`, `onOpenChange`), as the examples do.
- It opens with the second item selected, the application used before the present one, so
  list the applications most recently used first. `defaultValue` names another item to open
  on; `value` and `onValueChange` hand the selection to you.
- Return, Space or a click chooses: `onChoose` is called with the item's `value` and the
  switcher closes. Escape, and keyboard focus going elsewhere, close it without choosing.
  Catalina chooses when Command is let go. A page has no held key to wait for; if you open
  the switcher from a key chord of your own, hold `value` yourself and choose from
  `onKeyUp`.
- A closed switcher is not rendered.

### With the window manager

Inside a [Window Manager](/components/window-manager) the switcher is a component of its
own that reads the windows with `useWindowManager()`. The manager lists windows in the order
they were last key, the key window last, so the distinct `app` values in reverse are the
switcher's order, and choosing one calls `focus(id)` for that application's front window.
The manager stores no icons: map the `app` id to yours.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines,
[Keyboard](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503161456/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/user-interaction/keyboard/):

- Respect the standard keyboard shortcuts and, in general, do not override them: people are
  confused when a shortcut they know works differently.
- Make the core of an application usable with the keyboard alone.

## Variants and states

| State | How it is set | Look | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open | `open`, `defaultOpen` | The panel, with keyboard focus | Measured in the Command-Tab captures |
| Selected item | `value`, `defaultValue`; Tab, the arrow keys, the pointer | A tile 4 points larger than the icon on every side, black at 15% in Aqua and 34% in Dark Aqua, and the name under the icon | Measured on the first item; any other item follows from it |
| Other items | | The icon alone; the name is not shown | Measured |
| More items than fit | | The icons shrink together so that the panel stays 20 points inside the screen | That they shrink is catalog; by how much, and the 20 points, are provisional |
| No backdrop blur, or reduced transparency | browser support, `prefers-reduced-transparency` | The opaque panel, `#e5e5e3` or `#44484b` | Measured as the panel's typical fill |

The switcher has no disabled, pressed or inactive look, and no sizes of its own. Catalina's
badges on icons, and its Q and H keys that quit and hide the selected application, are not
implemented; handle a key in `onKeyDown` to add them.

## Sizes

Icons are 128 points, 5 points apart, with 23 points beside the row and 29 above and under
it: three applications make a panel of 440 by 186 points, as in the captures.
`--mac-app-switcher-icon-size` sets another icon size; padding, tile and name keep theirs.
There is no `size` prop.

## Accessibility

- The panel is a `listbox` with `aria-orientation="horizontal"`, named "Applications"; pass
  `aria-label` to change the name. Each item is an `option` named by its children, so give
  every item its application's name, also where an icon would say it to the eye. The icon is
  hidden from assistive technology.
- The panel takes keyboard focus when it opens and keeps it: the selected option is
  announced through `aria-activedescendant`, and the selection tile is what shows where the
  keyboard is. When it closes, focus goes back to the element that had it, unless it has
  moved on by then, for example into the window `onChoose` brought to the front.
- Keys: Tab or Right Arrow selects the next application and Shift-Tab or Left Arrow the
  previous one, round the ends; Home and End select the first and the last; Return or Space
  chooses; Escape closes. In a right-to-left page the arrows are mirrored.
- Tab moves the selection, as it does in Catalina, so it does not leave the panel. The panel
  is nevertheless no trap: Escape closes it, and it closes by itself as soon as focus is
  anywhere else, so it is never open without the keyboard being in it. It makes nothing else
  on the page inert.
- Moving the pointer over an item selects it and a click chooses it. Resting over an item
  is not enough: the pointer has to move.
- The panel does not move or fade, so there is nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion` to switch
  off. With `prefers-reduced-transparency` it is opaque.
- In forced-colours mode the panel has an edge and the selection tile an outline in the
  system's highlight colour.
- All key handling is on the panel's own element; nothing listens on `window` or `document`.

## Visual comparison

`app-switcher.visual.json` compares the whole panel with the Command-Tab screenshots in both
appearances: three applications, the first selected. The crop is the panel exactly, since it
has neither border nor shadow.

The three application icons are Apple's artwork. The example has neutral tiles in their
place and the comparison masks them (60% of the crop). The `-drawing` comparisons also mask
the name, which is set in the fallback font off Apple platforms, and use the stricter
threshold of 0.05.

The panel's colour is its material over what is behind it, which in the screenshots is the
System Preferences window. The System Preferences screenshots show the same window
uncovered, so the backdrop in the example is measured: the band the panes are on and, for
each pane under the panel, a flat tile of its icon's mean colour and a bar for its label.
They are stand-ins, not drawings of the icons, and are seen only through the blur.

Scores from `npm run visual -- app-switcher/` in Chromium on Linux: the share of pixels that
differ visibly, and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Reference (crop x, y, width, height) | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `panel-light` | Light-Command-Tab (1240, 864, 880, 372) | 0.84% | 2.05 | 0.59% | 1.90 |
| `panel-light-drawing` (name masked) | Light-Command-Tab (1240, 864, 880, 372) | 0.33% | 1.50 | 0.35% | 1.48 |
| `panel-dark` | Dark-Command-Tab (1240, 864, 880, 372) | 1.12% | 2.70 | 0.86% | 2.60 |
| `panel-dark-drawing` (name masked) | Dark-Command-Tab (1240, 864, 880, 372) | 0.26% | 2.12 | 0.27% | 2.17 |

What is left in the `-drawing` rows is outside the panel's corners, where the screenshots
show the window behind it: a pane's icon and label, and the edge of the Terminal window.

State checks cover what no screenshot shows, in a real browser over a flat backdrop, where
the panel's and the tile's colours follow from the tokens: the second item selected when the
switcher opens, the selection after Right Arrow, after two Tabs (round the end), after
Shift-Tab and under the pointer, the switcher gone after Escape and after a click, and five
applications on a screen too narrow for them, shrunk to fit with 20 points clear at each side.

Measured: the panel's size and padding, the icon size and pitch, the selection tile and its
colour, the name's colour and place, the absence of border and shadow, and the tint of the
material, which is a least-squares fit to the screenshots over the System Preferences window
(within 1.5 of 255 in Aqua and 2.0 in Dark Aqua). Two values depart from the measured spec
because the pixels do: the panel's corners are drawn with a radius of 24 points, not 20, and
the tile's with 9, not 8. Provisional: the blur radius and saturation, which are the shared
material filter's; the icons shrinking when the row does not fit; and everything about the
selection away from the first item.
