Notification Center shows a notification in one of two styles, chosen per application in
System Preferences. A *banner* leaves after a few seconds; an *alert* stays on screen until it
is dismissed and carries its buttons in a column at the trailing edge. This component is the
alert: the example is Finder's “Disk Not Ejected Properly” warning, the one notification in the
reference screenshots.

It is a surface, not a manager. It draws one notification and reports its dismissal; where it
appears, when, and how several stack is the caller's business. Its buttons are built on
[Base UI's Button](https://base-ui.com/react/components/button). Base UI's Toast is not used:
it is a queue with its own viewport, timers and swipe gestures, and an alert-style notification
has no timer and is placed by the desktop that shows it.

## Usage

```tsx
import { NotificationAlert, NotificationAlertClose } from "macalicious/notification-alert";
import "macalicious/notification-alert.css";

<NotificationAlert
  icon={<img src={finderIcon} alt="Finder" width={32} height={32} />}
  title="Disk Not Ejected Properly"
  actions={<NotificationAlertClose>Close</NotificationAlertClose>}
  onDismiss={() => setShown(false)}
>
  Eject “USB Key” before disconnecting or turning it off.
</NotificationAlert>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add notification-alert`.

The alert holds no open state: render it while the notification is showing and remove it in
`onDismiss`. A `NotificationAlertClose` button calls `onDismiss`; a `NotificationAlertAction`
does only what its own `onClick` does. With two buttons the column is split between them:

<Example name="actions" both />

On a desktop, put the alert 8 pt below the menu bar and 8 pt from the trailing edge of the
screen, and stack further ones below it 8 pt apart (inferred: the captures show the alert
alone). The alert is 344 pt wide; set another `width` with `style` if you must.

No icon is included: Apple's application icons are not part of this library. Pass your own
32 pt image.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[notifications](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/system-capabilities/notifications/):

- An alert-style notification stays until it is dismissed manually and includes one or two
  buttons for dismissing it and taking action.
- Provide useful, informative notifications. Write the message in complete sentences with
  sentence-style capitalisation and proper punctuation, and do not truncate it yourself.
- Do not tell people to open the application, go to a screen or click a button: such
  instructions are hard to remember once the notification is gone.
- Do not use a notification for an error message. People expect notifications to be
  informational and deferrable; an alert (`NSAlert`) has greater impact.

## Variants and states

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One button | one child in `actions` | The button takes the full height of the column (measured) |
| Two buttons | two children in `actions` | The column is split by a 1 pt line (provisional: no screenshot shows it) |
| No buttons | no `actions` | The text column takes the full width (provisional) |
| Subtitle | `subtitle` | A bold 11 pt line under the title (provisional) |
| Opaque | `material="opaque"` | The fill the captures record, `#ededed` or `#383838`, instead of the material (measured) |
| Button pressed | `:active` | Its part of the column darkens by black at 10% (provisional) |
| Button focused | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring inside the button; none while the page is not key (provisional) |
| Button disabled | `disabled` | Title at 25% (provisional) |
| Arriving, leaving | `data-starting-style`, `data-ending-style` | Off screen at the trailing side; the alert slides over 0.3 s, or fades under reduced motion (provisional) |

A notification is a window of its own, so it follows only the window state of the page: with
`data-window-state="inactive"` on `<html>` its buttons show no focus ring. Nothing else
changes.

## Sizes

One size: 344 pt wide, and 63 pt high with a title and two lines of message. Each further line
adds 14 pt, up to four lines; a shorter text is centred in the 63 pt. The title is 12 pt bold,
the message 11 pt on a 14 pt pitch, a button title 12 pt, and the button column is 70 pt wide
after a 1 pt divider. The corners have a radius of 8 pt.

## Accessibility

- The root has `role="alert"`, is named by the title and described by the message. A screen
  reader announces an alert when it is added to the page, so render it when the notification
  arrives rather than hiding and showing it with CSS. For a notification that should not
  interrupt, pass `role="status"`; for one that is already on the page when it loads,
  `role="group"`.
- It is not modal and never takes the focus. Its buttons are native buttons in the tab order:
  Space and Return activate them, and a disabled one leaves the tab order.
- Escape dismisses the alert while the focus is on one of its buttons (it calls `onDismiss`).
  A `keydown` handler that prevents the default keeps the key. macOS itself has no key for
  this; the pointer is not required here.
- Clicking the body of a macOS notification opens the application. The body here is not a
  control; offer that as a button (“Show”), which the keyboard can reach.
- Give the icon a text alternative that names the application, or an empty one when the title
  already says where the notification comes from.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the alert fades instead of sliding; under
  `prefers-reduced-transparency` the material is the opaque fill; in forced-colours mode the
  panel, the divider and the button line are drawn with system colours.

Measured: the size, the 8 pt corner, the fill as captured, the divider and the 70 pt button
column, the dark alert's inner rim and outer line, the shadow (fitted to the alpha of the
captures' margins), the text colours, and the position of every line of text: in the fallback
font the title, both message lines and the button title land on the same pixel rows as in the
captures. What remains in the whole-crop scores is the shape of the glyphs.

Three measurements differ from the spec sheet and from the shared tokens, and are kept in this
folder. The fonts are 12 pt bold, 11 pt and 12 pt (the sheet computes 13, 12 and 13): the cap
height of the title is 17 px and of the message under 16 px, where the 13 pt bold message of
the Empty Trash alert measures about 18.5 px, and the “C” of Close is 18 px high and 15 px wide
where the 13 pt “Cancel” of a dialog button is 19 and 16. The corner radius is 8 pt, not the
6 pt of `--mac-radius-notification`. The Aqua shadow is lighter below the alert than
`--mac-shadow-notification`.

In the dark capture the disk is named “USB KEY” and the first line breaks before “or”. The
fallback font sets that line 1.5 pt shorter than San Francisco, so the dark example forces the
break the capture has.

No screenshot shows two buttons, a pressed or focused button, or the alert over a desktop.
Those are checked in a real browser against computed values (the `expect` comparisons): the
example is pressed with the mouse or focused with Tab, and chosen pixels must have the
expected colour. They pass in both appearances at 1x and 2x.
