The sidebar of Stocks lists the tickers you follow. Each row has the symbol over the company's
name at the leading edge, a small chart of the session in the middle, and the last price over
a coloured badge at the trailing edge: green when the ticker is up on the previous close, red
when it is down. Stocks is a Mac Catalyst app, so the row is an iOS table cell at 77% and
AppKit has no class for it; Base UI has no primitive either. `StockRow` draws it as a list
item with an inline SVG sparkline, and `StockList` is the list.

## Usage

```tsx
import { StockList, StockRow } from "macalicious/stock-row";
import "macalicious/stock-row.css";

<StockList aria-label="Watchlist">
  <StockRow
    symbol="AAPL"
    name="Apple Inc."
    price="220.82"
    change="+1.86"
    direction="gain"
    data={session}
    previousClose={218.96}
    selected={shown === "AAPL"}
    onSelect={() => setShown("AAPL")}
    onBadgeClick={() => setBadge(next)}
  />
</StockList>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add stock-row`.

The row formats nothing: pass the price and the badge's text as they should read, with the
sign on the change. `data` is the session's prices in order, spaced evenly; `progress` says
how much of the session they cover, so that the line stops part of the way across while the
dashed previous close runs on. The sparkline's height spans the lowest to the highest price,
the previous close included; pass `domain` to span another range, for instance the same one
for every row.

A row is plain text until it is given something to do. With `onSelect` its symbol, name,
sparkline and price become one button; with `onBadgeClick` the badge becomes a second button,
which in Stocks cycles between the change in price, the change in percent and the market
capitalisation:

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines have no section for the row; it is a cell of a single-column
[table view](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503153039/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/windows-and-views/table-views/)
in a
[Mac Catalyst](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ios/overview/mac-catalyst/)
app. From those and from what Stocks does:

- Keep the sign on the change. The badge's colour repeats it and must not be the only thing
  that says whether a ticker is up or down.
- Show the same kind of value in every badge of a list, so that the column can be compared
  down the rows.
- Select the row whose ticker the pane beside the list shows, and only that one.
- Use the symbol the market uses, and the full name under it; let a long name be cut rather
  than shrinking the text.

There is no disabled look: a row that cannot be chosen is a row without `onSelect`.

## Sizes

One size. A row is 48.2 pt tall and as wide as its list, 330 pt in Stocks; a hairline along
its bottom starts 12.3 pt from the leading edge. The symbol is 14 pt medium with its baseline
21 pt below the row's top, the name 12.5 pt regular with its baseline at 37.5 pt. The
sparkline's box is 54.67 x 26 pt, 13 pt below the top and 136 pt before the trailing edge; its
line is 1.5 pt with round joins, and the dashed line 0.85 pt, 2.33 pt on and 1.52 pt off. The
price is 13 pt regular on the symbol's baseline and ends 12 pt before the trailing edge. The
badge is 58.5 x 17.65 pt with 3.08 pt corners, 25.85 pt below the top, and holds 11.5 pt
semibold text aligned to its right. In a wider list only the name's column grows.

Three of these depart from the spec's nominal values, each to match the pixels: the pitch is
48.2 pt and not 48.25 pt (ten hairlines lie 867.65 px apart), the badge's corner is 3.08 pt
and not 2.5 pt, and the sparkline's box is 54.67 x 26 pt from 13 pt and not 55 x 25 pt from
14 pt (the dashed line ends 54.67 pt along, and it is drawn as high as 13.3 pt below the top).

## Accessibility

- The list is a `list` and each row a list item, not a `listbox` with options: an option
  cannot hold a second control, and the badge is one. Name the list with `aria-label`. Both
  are `<div>`s with the list roles and not `<ul>` and `<li>`, so that a page's own list styles
  do not reach the rows.
- With `onSelect` a row's content is one native button, named by what it shows: symbol, name,
  the sparkline's label, price and, unless the badge is a button too, the change. The selected
  row's button has `aria-current`.
- Every button is in the tab order. With the focus on a row's button, Up and Down move it to
  the row above or below, and Home and End to the first and last row; Return and Space choose
  the row. On a badge the arrow keys do nothing.
- With `onBadgeClick` the badge is a button of its own, named by its text. Say what a click
  does with `badgeLabel`, such as “+1.86, show percent change”.
- The sparkline is an image named “Trending up”, “Trending down” or, for a ticker without a
  quote, “No change”; pass `chartLabel` to say more, or in another language. Its numbers are
  in the row as text.
- The sign in the badge carries the direction, so the row does not rely on red and green
  alone. White on the green badge has a contrast of about 2.2:1, as in Stocks.
- The focus ring is drawn only in the key window. In forced-colours mode the badge has an
  outline, the sparkline is drawn in the system's text colour and the selected row is
  outlined in the highlight colour.

## Visual comparison

`npm run visual -- stock-row` compares a gain and a loss row with the Stocks screenshots in
both appearances: Dow Jones in each, DIS in Aqua and GE in Dark Aqua. Each crop is 328 x 48 pt
of the sidebar, without the window's edge and the sidebar's divider. Share of pixels that
differ at 2x, in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Crop (`114,y,656,96`) | Gain, Aqua (`y` 328) | Gain, Dark Aqua (328) | Loss, Aqua (810) | Loss, Dark Aqua (906) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Row with its text | 10.07% | 10.40% | 5.31% | 4.08% |
| Badge, hairline and sparkline, text masked | 1.05% | 0.85% | 1.39% | 0.87% |

The same comparisons at 1x score 12.56%, 13.31%, 7.84% and 6.34% with the text and 3.65%,
3.27%, 4.64% and 2.15% without; at 1x the half-point hairline and the 1.5 pt line cannot match
their averaged counterparts. The masks cover a little over half of each crop, so the second
row counts differing pixels against the other half only.

The sparkline is not masked. Its prices cannot be known, so each fixture's series is the
screenshot's own line, traced column by column and corrected against the rendering, with
`domain` mapping a value straight to a height. About half of what is left is the tracing's
error along the line (0.4 to 0.9 points of the second row). The rest is the badge's top edge,
0.4 to 0.5 points in every crop: it lies 25.86 pt below the row's top in the screenshot,
between two device pixels, and a browser draws it on one.

Measured: the row's pitch and hairline, the positions, sizes and colours of the three texts,
the badge's box, corner and colours, the sparkline's box, line, fill and dashed line, and the
share of the box a session under way covers.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows them: the selected and pressed row, the pressed
badge, the focus ring, the look in a window that is not key, a row without data, the grey of a
ticker without a quote (the spec infers a grey badge and gives no value; it is the system
grey, checked with an `expect` comparison), and how far a
long name may run before it is cut (4 pt before the sparkline; the longest name shown ends
12.5 pt before it, uncut). The selected fill, the pressed badge, the focus ring and its
absence in a window that is not key are checked in a real browser against the tokens (`expect`
comparisons), as is the forced-colours outline, and that the text of a row that is a button
stands where a plain row's does (`button-alignment`). The rule that scales prices to the box, lowest
to highest with the previous close included, is the spec's inference: in the screenshots the
lines do not fill the box, so Stocks scales them some other way, which is why the fixtures set
`domain`.
