---

<Example name="basic" both />

Stickies puts small coloured notes on the desktop. A note is a borderless window: square
corners, no standard title bar and no traffic lights. The frontmost note of the active app
shows a 12 pt strip in a stronger shade of its colour with three 8 pt boxes: close at the left,
zoom and collapse at the right. Every other note shows neither strip nor boxes, and its text
does not move. AppKit has no public class for it. `StickyNote` draws the window, the strip and
the widgets in CSS; the widgets are Base UI buttons and the text is edited by the browser.

## Usage

```tsx
import { StickyNote, StickyNoteText, StickyNoteTitleBar } from "macalicious/sticky-note";
import "macalicious/sticky-note.css";

<StickyNote color="yellow" aria-label="Make a note of it!" style={{ width: 352, height: 260 }}>
  <StickyNoteTitleBar onClose={remove}>Make a note of it!</StickyNoteTitleBar>
  <StickyNoteText onInput={save}>
    <b>Make a note of it!</b>
    {"\n\nStickies lets you keep notes (like these) on your desktop."}
  </StickyNoteText>
</StickyNote>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add sticky-note`.

Give the note its size with `style` or a class; Stickies opens a note at 352 x 260 pt. The
note is a window of its own: it writes its `state` to `data-window-state`, and whoever manages
the notes decides which one is key, as a window manager does for windows. A note inside an
ancestor that is not key (a page whose browser window lost focus, with
`trackWindowActivation()`) loses its strip as well.

The title bar's children are the note's title, which Stickies takes from the first line of
the text; it is shown only while the note is collapsed. The text is uncontrolled: its children
are the initial content, and the result is read from the element (`ref`, `onInput`). Children passed later are ignored, so a render of the owner never overwrites an edit; give the
text another `key` to replace it. Pass text and plain markup only (`<b>`, `<i>`): a component
with state of its own inside the text would keep rendering into what the browser has edited.
Line
breaks in a string are kept.

The close and zoom boxes report to `onClose` and `onZoom` and do nothing themselves: removing
or resizing a note is the application's job. The collapse box and a double click on the strip
collapse the note to its title bar; `collapsed` and `onCollapsedChange` control that.

## Guidelines

The Human Interface Guidelines have no entry for this note: it is specific to Stickies. What
follows is how Stickies itself uses it, read from its menus and the two screenshots (not
verified against a published guideline):

- A note holds a short piece of text. Its first line is its title, in bold by habit.
- Offer the six colours of the Color menu and no others; yellow is the default.
- Only the frontmost note shows its strip. Do not show the widgets of several notes at once.
- New notes cascade about 10 pt right and down from the previous one.

Float on Top, the third command of Stickies' Window menu, has no prop: it changes where the
note stands among the other windows and nothing about how it is drawn (the spec records it as
not distinguishable in the screenshots). Keep a floating note above its neighbours with the
stacking order of whatever holds the notes, such as a window manager's level or a `z-index`.

## Sizes

A note has any size; there is one size of chrome. The title strip is 12 pt tall. Each widget
is an 8 pt box with a 1 pt border, 2 pt below the top edge: the close box 12 pt from the left
edge, the collapse box 12 pt from the right edge and the zoom box 4 pt before it. The text is
Helvetica 12 on a 14 pt line, 11 pt from the left and right edges, with its first baseline
29 pt below the note's top edge.

## Accessibility

- The note is a `group`; name it with `aria-label`, usually the first line of its text. Its
  text is a multi-line `textbox` named "Note"; give it another name with `aria-label` when a
  page holds several notes.
- The three widgets are buttons named "Close", "Zoom" and "Collapse" (`closeLabel`,
  `zoomLabel`, `collapseLabel` translate them). The collapse box is a toggle button whose
  `aria-pressed` says whether the note is collapsed. Tab reaches them in order, then the text;
  Return or Space activates one.
- In a note that is not key the widgets are hidden and leave the Tab order, as in Stickies.
  Make the note key (on focus or on a click) to bring them back.
- Focus on text that can be edited shows as its insertion point, as in Stickies. Read-only
  text stays in the Tab order so that it can be scrolled and copied, and shows a focus ring.
  In forced-colours mode both are outlined.
- A double click on the strip is a shortcut for the collapse box, never the only way.
- The text is black on the note colour in both appearances: at least 12:1 on every body
  colour, and at least 7:1 for the title on a collapsed note's strip.
- In forced-colours mode the note is the system's canvas with a border, and the widgets keep
  their shapes in the system's button text colour.
