<Example name="basic" />

An assistant walks the user through a task one step at a time: a heading names the step, a box
of text or a group of controls fills the body, and the navigation buttons sit at the bottom
right. Artwork fills a fixed column at the left. In AppKit it is a plain `NSWindow` that can be
closed and minimized and not resized, with a custom view for each step. `AssistantWindow`
assembles the library's [Window](/components/window), [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) and
[Window Controls](/components/window-controls) that way, and its parts lay out a step.

Base UI has no primitive for a window or for a sequence of steps, so the parts are elements of
their own; the buttons you put in the row are the library's, which are built on Base UI's
Button.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  AssistantWindow,
  AssistantWindowActions,
  AssistantWindowHeading,
  AssistantWindowText,
} from "macalicious/assistant-window";
import { PushButton } from "macalicious/push-button";
import "macalicious/styles.css";

<AssistantWindow
  title="Boot Camp Assistant"
  artwork={<img src={diamonds} alt="" />}
  onClose={quit}
  onCancel={goBack}
>
  <AssistantWindowHeading id="step">Introduction</AssistantWindowHeading>
  <AssistantWindowText aria-labelledby="step">
    <p>This assistant helps you install another operating system.</p>
    <p>Back up your disk before you continue.</p>
  </AssistantWindowText>
  <AssistantWindowActions leading={<PushButton onClick={help}>Open Help</PushButton>}>
    <PushButton onClick={next}>Continue</PushButton>
  </AssistantWindowActions>
</AssistantWindow>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add assistant-window`.

- The assistant keeps no step of its own. Hold the step in your state, render its heading and
  content, and change it from the buttons: Continue, and Go Back from the second step on.
- `AssistantWindowText` is the bordered box of text, for steps that explain. Put paragraphs
  (`<p>`) in it; it scrolls when they are longer than the box. `AssistantWindowContent` takes
  the same room without a border, for steps that ask or show progress: put
  [Checkboxes](/components/checkbox), a [Progress Bar](/components/progress-bar) or a form in
  it.
- `AssistantWindowActions` is the button row: its children are the navigation buttons, at the
  trailing end with Continue last; `leading` takes a button that opens the help.
- `artwork` is the picture of the left column. The library ships no artwork: supply an `<img>`
  with an empty `alt`, or an inline SVG. It may be wider than its column, as Boot Camp's
  diamonds are, and then reaches over the step; the window clips it at its own edge.
- The window does not open, move or close itself: a window manager gives it `state` and a
  position through `style`, moves it from `onDragRegionPointerDown` (a press on the title bar,
  not on a window control), and unmounts it on `onClose`.

## Guidelines

From the Human Interface Guidelines for macOS,
[Onboarding](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503145009/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/app-architecture/onboarding/),
as the measured spec summarises them; the page was not re-read for this one (not verified):

- Keep the steps few, and say in each what is about to happen.
- Let people leave at any point, and do not change anything before they have confirmed it.
- Do not ask for setup information the task can do without or can find out by itself.

## Sizes

An assistant is not resizable. `width` and `height` are 783 and 548 pt unless given, the frame
of Boot Camp Assistant. Measured from the window's top-left corner: title bar 22 pt; the
artwork column ends and the step begins at 174.5 pt (`--mac-assistant-window-artwork-width`),
and the step ends 16 pt from the right edge; baseline of the heading at 53.5 pt, its text
3.5 pt after the box's edge; text box 592.5 x 423 pt at 174.5, 65, with a 1 pt border; its text
7.5 pt inside the edge, the first baseline 20.5 pt below the box's top, on a 16 pt line, with
a blank line between paragraphs; buttons 20 pt high, 20.5 pt below the box and 19.5 pt above
the bottom edge, the leading one flush with the box's left edge and the trailing one ending
4.5 pt inside its right edge. The heading is 13 pt bold and the text 13 pt.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title.
- `AssistantWindowHeading` is an `<h1>`, the heading of the window, and a polite live region:
  keep the same heading mounted and change its text, and a screen reader says the new step.
- `AssistantWindowText` is a `group` and a tab stop, so that the arrow keys, Page Up and Page
  Down scroll it from the keyboard. Name it with `aria-labelledby` and the heading's `id`.
  Its text can be selected and copied.
- Tab moves from the window controls to the text box, the help button and the navigation
  buttons. Return activates Continue when it is a
  [Default Button](/components/default-button). Escape pressed anywhere inside calls
  `onCancel`, unless a control inside has used the key: go back a step, or leave.
- The artwork is decoration and is hidden from assistive technology.
- The assistant does not move the focus when the step changes. If Continue disappears on the
  last step, move the focus to what replaces it.
- Command-W and Command-period are a window manager's keys; the window only reports its close
  button through `onClose`.
- In forced-colours mode the text box keeps a border in the text colour.

## Visual comparison

`assistant-window.visual.json` compares the whole window with the Boot Camp Assistant capture
in both appearances (crop 112, 76, 1566 x 1096). The capture is a full screen, so the window is
cropped at its frame: the shadow is not compared, and the four corners, where the desktop
picture shows, are masked. The diamonds are Apple's artwork: plain diamonds stand in for them
and every comparison masks their area, which reaches over the left edge of the text box. The
wording of the text is a stand-in. The `-layout` twins also mask the title, the heading, the
text and the button titles, and score the frame, the title bar, the text box and the buttons
at the tighter threshold (0.05).

Scores on Linux with the fallback font (Chromium, mismatch and mean difference of 255):

| Comparison | 1x | 2x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `boot-camp-light` | 5.26%, 6.77 | 4.38%, 6.87 |
| `boot-camp-light-layout` | 0.55%, 0.22 | 0.03%, 0.19 |
| `boot-camp-dark` | 5.45%, 6.67 | 4.60%, 6.58 |
| `boot-camp-dark-layout` | 0.29%, 0.50 | 0.02%, 0.36 |

The unmasked scores are the text: its wording is a stand-in and it is set in the fallback
font. The `-layout` twins are the gate for the drawing: they mask the lines of text and leave
the lower half of the text box exposed, so its measured fill is compared in both appearances.
