AppKit calls this control a *checkbox* (`NSButton` of the switch type). It is built on
[Base UI's Checkbox](https://base-ui.com/react/components/checkbox), which supplies the
`checkbox` role, the hidden form input, the keyboard handling and the `checked`,
`indeterminate`, `disabled`, `readOnly` and `render` props. The box, its checkmark and its
dash are drawn here.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Checkbox } from "macalicious/checkbox";
import "macalicious/checkbox.css";

<Checkbox defaultChecked>Show Bluetooth in menu bar</Checkbox>
<Checkbox checked={on} onCheckedChange={setOn}>Ask to keep changes when closing documents</Checkbox>
```

The children are the title. It is part of the control: clicking it toggles the checkbox, and it
is the accessible name. Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add checkbox`.
See [Installation](/installation).

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines for
[checkboxes](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503161552/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/buttons/checkboxes/):

- Use a checkbox for a choice between two opposite states. For a set of mutually exclusive
  choices use [radio buttons](/components/radio-button).
- Give every checkbox a title, in sentence case and without ending punctuation, unless it is a
  cell in a checklist.
- Checkboxes belong in a view, not in a toolbar or a status bar.
- Stack related checkboxes vertically with their left edges aligned, 22 pt from one box to the
  next: `gap: 8px` between 14 pt boxes. Indent a checkbox that depends on another by 26 pt, and
  disable it while it does not apply.
- A checkbox that stands for a group whose members differ shows the mixed state.

## Variants and states

Pressed and keyboard-focused are transient, so this gallery pins them. In an application they
appear on their own.

<Example name="states" both hideCode />

| State | How it is triggered | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Off | – | White well with an inner shadow (a translucent white gradient in Dark Aqua) |
| On | `checked`, `defaultChecked` | Accent fill, white checkmark (white at 85% in Dark Aqua) |
| Mixed | `indeterminate` | Accent fill, 8 x 2 pt dash |
| Pressed | `:active` | The well darkens (lightens in Dark Aqua); an accent fill deepens |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring around the box |
| Disabled | `disabled` | Box at half opacity, title at 25% |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | No accent: on and mixed are drawn on the off well with a dark (light in Dark Aqua) glyph; no focus ring |

### Mixed state

`indeterminate` is AppKit's mixed state. It is a prop of its own, as in Base UI: the parent
decides when its children differ.

The element's height is the box's, AppKit's alignment rectangle; the title's line extends
above and below it. A title that does not fit wraps, hanging to the right of the box.

## Accent colour

The fill of an on or mixed checkbox follows the accent colour. Set `data-accent` on any
ancestor. See [Theming](/theming). With the graphite accent in Dark Aqua the glyph is dark, as
in Catalina.

## Accessibility

- The root has `role="checkbox"` and `aria-checked` (`true`, `false` or `mixed`); Base UI
  renders a hidden native input beside it, so the checkbox takes part in forms with `name` and
  `value`.
- Space toggles it; Tab reaches it. A disabled checkbox leaves the tab order.
- The title is the accessible name. A checkbox without a title needs `aria-label` or
  `aria-labelledby`.
- The glyph is hidden from assistive technology; the state is carried by `aria-checked`.
- In forced-colours mode the box is drawn with system colours.

The pixels that still differ are on the box's four corners and along the edge of the
checkmark, where the browser's antialiasing and Catalina's differ by a few levels. With its

full pixel cannot match its averaged counterpart ([Fidelity limits](/fidelity)).

No screenshot shows a pressed checkbox, keyboard focus on one, a checkbox in a window that is
not key, or the small and mini sizes. Those are checked in a real browser against values from
the spec (the `expect` comparisons): the example is pressed with the mouse or focused with Tab,
and chosen pixels must have the expected colour.

sizes (it is the regular glyph scaled) and the title's position at those sizes. All eight
accents are compared with the General pane's screenshot of that accent, in both appearances.
