---
title: Picture in Picture
description: A small borderless video window that floats in a corner of the screen. Under the pointer it shows close, return-to-window and play or pause buttons; it can be dragged to another corner and resized.
---

<Example name="basic" both />

The window Safari and other players send a video to, so that it keeps playing above
everything else. `PictureInPicture` draws the window and its controls and keeps where it is,
how wide it is and whether it plays; the video is whatever you put inside. Base UI has no
such primitive; the three controls are Base UI buttons.

## Usage

```tsx
import { PictureInPicture } from "macalicious/picture-in-picture";
import "macalicious/picture-in-picture.css";

<div style={{ position: "relative", height: "100vh" }}>
  <PictureInPicture
    open={floating}
    onOpenChange={setFloating}
    onReturn={showInPage}
    playing={playing}
    onPlayingChange={setPlaying}
  >
    <video src="movie.mp4" />
  </PictureInPicture>
</div>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add picture-in-picture`.

- The window is placed in a corner of the nearest positioned ancestor, 20 points from its
  edges: that element is the screen. Inside a
  [Window Manager](/components/window-manager) it is the desktop.
- The child fills the window and is clipped to its corners. The component does not play
  anything: `playing` and `onPlayingChange` only say which glyph the middle button shows and
  what it asks for; call `play()` and `pause()` on your video from there.
- The close button and Escape close the window; the return button calls `onReturn` first.
  A closed window is not rendered. While it is open, the player it came from shows a
  placeholder, which is yours to draw.
- Dragging the window and dropping it moves it to the corner of the quarter it was dropped
  in. The corner of the window that points to the middle of the screen is its resize handle.
  A press becomes a drag once the pointer has moved 4 points, so a click on the video still
  reaches it, and a press on a link, a form control or a `<video controls>` inside never
  moves the window.
  Nothing renders while either goes on: `onCornerChange` and `onWidthChange` are called
  once, on release.
- A page cannot float a window above other applications. For a real `<video>`,
  `video.requestPictureInPicture()` opens the system's own window where the browser has
  one; this component is the window inside a page.

## Guidelines

The macOS Human Interface Guidelines of Catalina's time have no page for Picture in
Picture. What follows is the system's behaviour as the measured spec records it from the
macOS User Guide, not checked here against Apple's text:

- The window snaps to one of the four corners of the screen and stays above other windows.
- It keeps the video's aspect ratio when it is resized.
- Its controls appear only under the pointer, so that nothing covers the video otherwise.
- The player the video came from says that the video is playing in Picture in Picture, and
  the return button brings it back there.

## Variants and states

| State | How it is set | Look | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Idle | default | The video alone: 4 point corners, no border, the window's shadow | Measured in the Safari Picture in Picture captures |
| Under the pointer, or focus inside | `:hover`, `:focus-within`, `data-force="hover"` | A black scrim at 50% with a close button top left, a return button top right and play or pause in the middle, white glyphs | Provisional: no screenshot shows it; the spec infers the layout from AVKit |
| Button under the pointer, pressed | `:hover`, `:active` | A white disc at 20%, at 35% | Provisional |
| Button focused | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring; none while the page is not key | Provisional |
| Corner | `corner`, `defaultCorner`; drag; arrow keys | `top-right` (default), `top-left`, `bottom-right`, `bottom-left` | Top right is measured; the other three are catalog |
| Playing | `playing`, `defaultPlaying` | The middle button shows pause, otherwise play | Provisional glyphs, drawn for this library |

Catalina also places the window freely when it is dragged with Command held. That is not
implemented: the window always snaps to a corner.

## Sizes

The window is `width` points wide and as high as `aspectRatio` makes it (16:9 by default).
It opens 420 points wide, about a quarter of a 1680 point screen, and resizing keeps it
between `minWidth` and `maxWidth` (210 and 840). The plus and minus keys change the width by
32 points. The window in the captures is 528 by 297. There is no `size` prop.

## API

<PropsTable name="PictureInPictureProps" />

| Attribute, class or variable | On | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.mac-picture-in-picture` | the root | The window; `position: absolute` in its corner. |
| `data-corner` | the root | `top-left`, `top-right`, `bottom-left` or `bottom-right`. |
| `data-playing` | the root | The video is playing. |
| `data-dragging` | the root | While the pointer moves or resizes the window. |
| `.mac-picture-in-picture-video` | the video's wrapper | Clips the child to the window's corners. |
| `.mac-picture-in-picture-controls` | the scrim | Holds the three buttons. |
| `.mac-picture-in-picture-button` | a button | `data-kind` is `close`, `return` or `play`. |
| `.mac-picture-in-picture-resize` | the resize handle | The 10 point corner opposite the screen's corner. |
| `--mac-picture-in-picture-inset` | set on the root or above | Distance from the edges of the screen; default `20px`. |
| `--mac-picture-in-picture-shadow` | token | The window's shadow. |
| `--mac-picture-in-picture-scrim`, `-glyph`, `-button-hover`, `-button-pressed`, `-backdrop` | tokens | The controls' colours, and the fill behind a video that does not cover the window. |

`ref`, `className`, `style` and every other prop go to the root.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `group` named "Picture in Picture"; pass `aria-label` to name the video.
- The controls are buttons named "Close", "Return to Window", and "Pause" or "Play". They are
  in the tab order whether or not they are showing, and focus inside the window shows them.
- With focus on a button, the arrow keys move the window to the corner in that direction,
  plus and minus resize it, and Escape closes it. Catalina's window takes no keyboard focus
  at all: these keys are this library's addition, so that everything the pointer does can be
  done without one.
- Hidden controls take no click: on a touch screen the first touch shows them and the
  second one acts.
- A touch on the window moves it and does not pan the page (`touch-action: none`).
- Give the video itself a text alternative or captions as you would in the page.
- In forced-colours mode the window has an edge instead of its shadow, and the buttons the
  system's button colours.

## Visual comparison

`picture-in-picture.visual.json` compares the window's top-right and bottom-right corners
with the Safari Picture in Picture screenshots in both appearances: the 4 point radius, the
borderless edge and the shadow on the desktop. The video frame is the content of someone's
video and is masked (30% of the top crop, where the 4 point square at the corner stays in,
and 16% of the bottom one). The desktop picture behind the window is replaced by a colour
ramp fitted to the same rows of the Desktop screenshots, so the picture's grain stays in the
scores.

Scores from `npm run visual -- picture-in-picture/` in Chromium on Linux: the share of pixels
that differ visibly, and the mean colour difference out of 255. The top corner is over the
smooth sky and is scored at threshold 0.05; the bottom corner is over the sea, whose grain
alone exceeds that threshold on 3% of the pixels, and is scored at the default 0.1.

State checks cover what no screenshot shows, in a real browser: the idle window without
controls, the scrim and the three controls under the pointer, the focus ring after Tab, the
window in another corner after an arrow key, narrower after the minus key, and gone after
Escape.

Measured: the corner radius, the absence of a border, the 20 point distance from the screen's
edges, the shadow (a fit to the captures against the Desktop screenshots). Provisional: the
scrim, the buttons' sizes, places, glyphs and fills, the keyboard commands, and the default,
smallest and largest widths.
