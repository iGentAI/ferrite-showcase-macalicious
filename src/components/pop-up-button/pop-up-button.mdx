AppKit's `NSPopUpButton` (with `pullsDown` off). It is built on
[Base UI's Select](https://base-ui.com/react/components/select), which supplies the roles,
focus management, arrow-key navigation, type-select, dismissal, the hidden form input and the
placement of the menu over the button. The open menu is the library's [menu](/components/menu)
and its rows are [menu items](/components/menu-item): the same surface, rim, row height,
highlight and checkmark, drawn by those components' stylesheets. For a menu of commands under a
button with a fixed title, use a [pull-down button](/components/pull-down-button).

## Usage

```tsx
import { PopUpButton, PopUpButtonItem, PopUpButtonSeparator } from "macalicious/pop-up-button";
import "macalicious/menu.css";
import "macalicious/menu-item.css";
import "macalicious/pop-up-button.css";

<PopUpButton defaultValue="medium" onValueChange={setSize} aria-label="Sidebar icon size">
  <PopUpButtonItem value="small">Small</PopUpButtonItem>
  <PopUpButtonItem value="medium">Medium</PopUpButtonItem>
  <PopUpButtonSeparator />
  <PopUpButtonItem value="large">Large</PopUpButtonItem>
</PopUpButton>
```

Or copy the source into your project: `npx macalicious add pop-up-button`.

The children are the menu. Each `PopUpButtonItem` has a `value`, which identifies it, and
children, which are its title in the menu and in the button. The button shows the selected
item's title from the first render, on the server too, because it reads the titles from its
children: keep the items direct children, or children of fragments and plain elements, rather
than hiding them inside components of your own. `value` and `onValueChange` control the
selection, `defaultValue` sets it once; `placeholder` is shown while the value is `null`.

A pop-up button is as wide as its layout makes it and does not follow its title: give it a
`width`, as Interface Builder does. A title too long for it is cut with an ellipsis before the
cap.

`PopUpButtonBezel` is the bezel alone, as a plain `<button>` that opens nothing: the trigger
for a popup of your own. The pull-down button is made of it.

## Guidelines

From the macOS Human Interface Guidelines,
[Pop-Up Buttons](https://web.archive.org/web/20200503110755/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/buttons/pop-up-buttons/):

- Use a pop-up button for a list of mutually exclusive choices or states, not for commands;
  commands belong in a pull-down button.
- Introduce it with a label in sentence case that ends with a colon, to its left, and give the
  items titles in title case.
- Prefer it to a group of radio buttons when there are more than about five choices or little
  room; prefer checkboxes when several choices can be on at once.
- Keep every item visible in the menu; dim one that is unavailable instead of removing it.

In the screenshots a label ends 8 pt before the button's box, and stacked pop-up buttons are 31
or 32 pt apart from top to top.

## Variants and states

Pressed and keyboard-focused are transient, so this gallery pins them. In an application they
appear on their own.

| State | How it is triggered | Look | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | – | Push button bezel; the trailing 16 pt are a cap in the accent gradient with a white double arrow, no line between the two | Measured |
| Open | click, Space, Up or Down Arrow | The menu lies over the button, the selected item's title over the button's title, and that item has the checkmark | Measured (Print sheet) |
| Pressed | `:active`, or while the menu is open | The cap deepens to 88% | Provisional |
| Keyboard focus | `:focus-visible` | Focus ring around the bezel | Ring measured on a text field |
| Disabled | `disabled` | Bezel at half opacity, no accent on the cap, title at 25%, arrows black at 40% (white at 25% in Dark Aqua) | Measured |
| No bezel | `bordered={false}` | The title and the double arrow in the title's colour, for a table cell or a sentence | Provisional: the screenshots show it only in Contacts, in that app's own colours (see below) |
| Window not key | an ancestor has `data-window-state="main"` or `"inactive"` | No accent on the cap, arrows in the title's colour, no focus ring | Provisional, from the disabled rendering |

The window state is not a prop: see [Building a window](/building-a-window). The open menu is
a window of its own and keeps its accent highlight.

### A tinted button without a bezel

An application can draw its pop-up buttons without a bezel in colours of its own: the labels of
a Contacts card in edit mode are blue with lighter arrows (`#4188f2` and about `#77adf9` on
white in the light Contacts screenshot). Two custom properties, set on the button or on any
ancestor, give an enabled button without a bezel those colours:

| Custom property | Colours | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--mac-pop-up-button-borderless-color` | the title | the control text colour |
| `--mac-pop-up-button-borderless-arrow-color` | the arrows | the title's colour |

A disabled button keeps the disabled colours, and a button with a bezel ignores both.

`.mac-menu`. The menu is at least 4.5 pt wider than the button, as in Catalina, and otherwise
as wide as its widest item.

## Accessibility

- The button has `role="combobox"` with `aria-haspopup="listbox"` and `aria-expanded`, the
  select-only combobox pattern; the menu is a `listbox` of `option`s and the selected one has
  `aria-selected`.
- The button has no label of its own. Name it with `aria-label`, or with `aria-labelledby`
  pointing at the label beside it.
- Tab reaches the button. Space, Return, Up Arrow or Down Arrow opens the menu with the
  current item highlighted; the arrow keys move, typing the start of a title selects it,
  Return or Space chooses, Escape closes without changing the value, and focus returns to the
  button.
- A disabled button uses the real `disabled` attribute and leaves the tab order. A disabled
  item has `aria-disabled` and cannot be chosen. The arrow keys stop on it without
  highlighting it, which is Base UI's Select; a Catalina menu passes over it.
- With `name` the value is submitted with a form through a hidden input; `required` takes part
  in the form's validation.
- The arrows are decoration and hidden from assistive technology. In forced-colours mode the
  bezel and the arrows are drawn with system colours.

## Visual comparison

`npm run visual -- pop-up-button/` compares the examples with the reference screenshots in both
appearances (`pop-up-button.visual.json`): Sidebar icon size in the General pane, the disabled
Change picture button of Desktop & Screen Saver, the small Open with button of Finder's Get
Info window, the Highlight color button in each of the eight accents (Theme screenshots), and
the open Presets menu of the Print sheet, opened by the pop-up button itself, and the tinted
label of a Contacts card (Aqua only: no dark screenshot shows a card being edited). Share of
pixels that differ at 2x, in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:

| Crop | Aqua, whole crop | Aqua, drawing only | Dark Aqua, whole crop | Dark Aqua, drawing only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General pane, regular | 5.86% | 0.22% | 5.45% | 0.24% |
| Disabled | 6.35% | 0.13% | 5.75% | 0.04% |
| Small | not scored | 0.24% | not scored | 0.24% |
| Eight accents | not scored | 0.21% to 0.29% | not scored | 0.29% to 0.35% |
| Open menu (Print) | 9.36% | 0.40% | 9.81% | 0.20% |
| Contacts label, no bezel, tinted | not scored | 4.67% | no screenshot | no screenshot |

"Drawing only" masks the titles and counts a pixel as differing at a colour distance of 0.05,
half the default; each of those comparisons has a budget 0.05 percentage points above its
score. What remains in them is the antialiasing of the arrows and, in the menu, the material.
The whole-crop scores are the title text, set in the fallback font instead of San Francisco.
The small and accent crops are scored with the title masked only: the Get Info title has the
application's icon, and the accent titles name the accent beside a chip in the highlight colour.

States no screenshot shows are checked in a real browser against values from the spec (the
`expect` comparisons): the cap's fill and its three hairlines, that no line separates the cap
from the title part, the focus ring after Tab, the cap without accent and no ring in a main and
in an inactive window, the box and cap of the mini size, and the two colour hooks of the button
without a bezel: an enabled one takes them, a disabled one and one with a bezel do not.

What is not exact, and what is provisional:

- **The arrows** are drawn for this library on the measured boxes; their stroke is 1.3 pt at
  the regular size and 1.2 pt at the small size, fitted to the coverage masks in the spec.
- **The Contacts label** shares its place, its size and its two colours with the small button
  without a bezel, but Contacts draws heavier arrows, 6 pt wide with their tips 9.5 pt apart,
  and sets the title at 12 pt where the small size has 11 pt (the example sets that font
  size). The arrows' weight is what its score is.
- **Pressed, window not key and the mini size** are inferred: no screenshot shows them. The
  untinted look without a bezel is inferred too.
- **The menu's place** is Base UI's: the selected item's text is laid exactly over the
  button's text. In the Print screenshot the menu starts 13 pt before the button's edge; here
  it starts 12 pt before it.
- A title too long for the button ends with an ellipsis; AppKit truncates the same way.
- The blink of the chosen item and the scroll arrows of a menu taller than the screen are not
  reproduced (see [Menu](/components/menu)).
