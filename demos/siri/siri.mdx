<Example name="basic" />

Click the orb: it listens for a moment and then answers. Click the question mark for requests
to choose from, or type one in the field. The window opens on the Weather result of the
reference screenshots. It is a demo, not an assistant: nothing is listened to, no microphone
is asked for, and nothing is looked up. The requests and their answers are data, and the orb
"hears" the next request of its list when its time is up.

## What it shows

- The library's [Siri](/components/siri) window: the dark translucent panel with its close
  button, the scrolling results, the help button and the orb, which gives way to a waveform
  while it listens. The demo supplies the prompt, the results and the listening state.
- The result as a `SiriCard`: the Weather snippet of the captures, with its summary block (a
  glyph, the city, three lines and the large temperature) and one row for each hour, and
  smaller cards for a calculation and the time.
- A [Borderless Button](/components/borderless-button) for each example request, in the text
  colour of the window.
- With `typeToSiri`, a [Text Field](/components/text-field) under the prompt, drawn in Dark
  Aqua because the window is dark in both appearances.

| Request | How it is asked | What answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “What’s the weather like today?” | typed text with "weather" or "forecast" | The Weather card: Memphis, as captured. |
| “What’s 18 percent of 240?” | typed text with "18" and "240" | A Calculator card: 43.2. |
| “What time is it in Cupertino?” | typed text with "time" | A Clock card: 9:41 AM, a fixed time. |
| “Open Calculator” | typed text with "open" and "calculator" | Words only; a host hears of it through `onAnswer` and opens the application. Nothing opens by itself. |
| anything else typed | | “I’m not sure I understand.” and the examples. |

Every request can also be chosen among the examples, and the orb "hears" them in this order,
starting after the one answered last.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The orb | Starts listening: the prompt is Siri's own, “What can I help you with?”, and the orb is a waveform. After `listenDuration` (2.4 seconds) the next request is answered. A second click stops listening without an answer. |
| The question mark | Lists the example requests under “Some things you can ask me:”; a second click puts them away. A chosen example is answered at once. |
| The field (`typeToSiri`) | Return asks for what was typed. |
| The close button, Escape | Close the window; Escape while the keyboard is in the window. A window that is shown again starts over, listening, and takes the keyboard. |

The request that was understood stands at the top of the results, Siri's reply under it and the
card under that. An answer taller than the results is scrolled until its card is at the top,
which is where both captures show the Weather card; the request is then above it, a scroll
away. The card is placed again when the window or the card changes size, and results without
a card (the examples, listening, an answer in words) go back to the top. With `typeToSiri` the
results are not scrolled, so the field stays in view.

## Using the window

`SiriAssistant` renders the library's `Siri` as its root and passes every prop it does not
use itself on to it, so `style`, `className`, `ref`, `level`, `animated` and the accessible
names work as they do on `Siri`. It is 344 by 600 pt; `--mac-siri-width` and
`--mac-siri-height`, or a size in `style`, change that.

```tsx
import { SiriAssistant } from "./siri";

<SiriAssistant capture="dark" typeToSiri onAnswer={(request) => open(request.opens)} />;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `capture` | `"light" \| "dark"` | Which reference capture's forecast the Weather card shows: 95° and the hours from 4 PM, or 73° and the hours from 12 PM. `"light"` by default. The window is the same in both. |
| `requests` | `SiriRequest[]` | The requests the window can answer, in the order the orb "hears" them; the four above by default. |
| `defaultRequest` | `string \| null` | The request answered when the window is first shown; `"weather"`, as captured. `null` shows Siri's own prompt. |
| `typeToSiri` | `boolean` | Shows the field to type a request in. `false` by default, as the setting of macOS is. |
| `listenDuration` | `number` | How long the orb listens, in milliseconds; 2400 by default. |
| `open`, `defaultOpen`, `onOpenChange` | `boolean`, `boolean`, `(open) => void` | Whether the window is shown, controlled or not. A window that is not shown renders nothing. |
| `onAnswer` | `(request) => void` | Called when a request is answered. |
| `onClose` | `() => void` | Called when the close button or Escape closes the window, after `onOpenChange`. |
| the rest | `SiriProps` | Without `prompt`, `listening`, `onListen`, `onHelp` and `children`, which the demo supplies. |

A request is `{ id, phrase, reply, keywords, card?, opens? }`. The rules are plain functions in
`siri-data.ts`: `understand()` gives the request typed text asks for, `nextRequest()` the one
the orb hears next, and `answerScrollTop()` where an answer is scrolled to.

## On the desktop

The demo has no `app.ts`, so the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) does not list it. The desktop hosts
a component whose root is a window of the library that takes what a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window) hands it, and the library's `Siri` cannot take
two of those things:

- **The window state.** The manager gives `state`, which a window writes to its own root as
  `data-window-state`; the desktop's test of every hosted demo looks for that attribute on the
  root. `Window`, `HudWindow`, `QuickLookPanel` and `Drawer` have a `state` prop for it.
  `Siri` has none: it reads the state from an ancestor. The demo could only write the
  attribute through `Siri`'s pass-through of unknown props, and the attribute is a window
  component's to set, not a demo's.
- **A drag region.** The manager moves a window from a press on its title bar, through
  `onDragRegionPointerDown`. `Siri` has no title bar and no part or callback for it; its top
  strip is padding of the root.

The rest fits as it is: the root takes `style`, `ref` and `tabIndex`, fills a frame it is
given the size of, and `onClose` closes it from its close button and from Escape.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `dialog` named "Siri". It is not modal. The window that is there when the
  page loads leaves the keyboard focus where it is; one that is shown again takes it, so that
  Escape closes it. When it closes it renders nothing, and the host puts the focus back, as the
  example above does with its button.
- The results are a `group` named "Results" and a polite live region, so the request, the
  reply and the card are announced when an answer arrives. The region is a tab stop and scrolls
  with the arrow keys.
- A card is an `article` named by its title. The placeholder in its header and the large
  placeholder of the summary are decorative; the summary's first line names the condition.
- The hourly rows are a list named "Hourly forecast". In a row the condition is shown by a
  shape only, so the shape is an image named after the condition ("Partly cloudy", "Clear
  night").
- The example requests are a list named "Example requests" of buttons.
- The field is a text box named "Type to Siri"; Return submits it.
- The orb is a button named "Listen" that is announced as pressed while it listens; the close
  and help buttons are named "Close" and "Help". These are the library's.
- The waveform stands still with `prefers-reduced-motion`; that is the library's too.

## Fidelity

`siri.visual.json` compares the whole window, shadow included, with the two Siri screenshots:
`10-15-Catalina-Light-Siri.png` with `capture="light"` and `10-15-Catalina-Dark-Siri.png` with
`capture="dark"`, each twice. The window was captured with the desktop picture blended into its
materials and nothing behind it, so each example puts a grey element with the capture's casts
behind the window and sets `--mac-siri-card-glow` for the tints in the card, as the library's
own comparison of the window does. Scores from `npm run visual -- siri/answer` in Chromium on
Linux with the fallback font: the share of pixels that differ and the mean colour difference
out of 255.

What is left in the two comparisons that keep the text is the text itself, set in the fallback
font off Apple devices. What is left in the two layout comparisons is the desktop picture's
tint, which the gradients follow only roughly, more so in the Dark Aqua capture, whose casts
are stronger.

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced by the demo's own layout: the 109.5 pt summary block
of the Aqua capture and the 94.5 pt one of the Dark Aqua capture; the city 87 pt inside the
card with its three lines under it, a label that breaks continuing after 15 pt and the next
label starting 15.75 pt after a label's last line; the temperature's digits centred 4 pt under
the middle of the block; the 39 pt hourly rows with their label, glyph column and degrees; and
the card standing at the top of the results, where an answer taller than the results is
scrolled to. The window, the card, its header band and the two strips are the library's.
The demo's sizes are tokens in `siri.tokens.css` (`--mac-siri-demo-*`), each tagged with where
it comes from: the summary, the city, the lines, the temperature and the hourly rows are
measured; the value cards, the example requests and the header placeholder are provisional.
The large numerals use the shared thin weight, `--mac-font-weight-thin`.

### What is not compared with a screenshot

The two captures show one state: the Weather answer. Nothing else on this page can be compared
with a screenshot, and it is provisional:

- **Listening.** `answer-listening-light` is not a comparison with a capture. It clicks the orb
  in a real browser and checks three pixels, which shows that the click starts listening and
  nothing about how Siri looks while it listens; the waveform is the library's own drawing.
- **The prompt, the reply and the examples**, their wording, type sizes and places, and the
  Calculator and Clock cards: the demo's own, after the way Siri answers.
- **The Type to Siri field**: the library's rounded text field in Dark Aqua, not a
  reproduction of the field macOS shows.
- **The scrolling to the card** is inferred from the card's place in both captures; the request
  and the reply above the card are not in either.
- **The last two hourly rows** of each forecast are invented. The captured card runs on under
  the bottom strip, so the demo's does too; the first ten rows are the captures'.
- **The window when it is not key**, its pressed buttons and its focus rings are the library's
  and are checked on its own page.

What differs from the captures, and why:

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the two scores
  that keep the text. The second line of the Aqua capture, “Chance of Rain: 20%”, breaks after
  “of” there. That break is in the data, because where a line breaks by itself depends on the
  font: “Chance of Rain: 0%” is 109 pt wide in the Dark Aqua capture and about 121 pt in the
  fallback font, so no one width breaks the same lines in both.
- **The artwork**: the header icon, the weather glyphs and the orb, as listed above.
- **The desktop picture's tint** in the chrome and the card, for which gradients stand in.
- **Three values of the blueprint** (`reference/spec/apps/siri.md`) were measured again and
  differ. The sun is 42 pt across, not 48 (x=110..193 of the 2x capture), and centred 57 pt
  from the window's left edge; 75 pt is its place in the capture, whose window starts at 19.
  The temperature's ink ends 312 pt from the window's left edge (x=662 of the capture), not at
  290.5. In the Aqua capture the hourly rows start 181.5 pt under the capture's top (y=363;
  the first row's text is at y=393..411 and a row is 78 px), where the blueprint says about
  y=372.
