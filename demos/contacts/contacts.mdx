<Example name="basic" />

Choose a group and a name, type in the search field, press Edit to change the card and Done
to keep it, press the plus to add a card, and click a field's label on a finished card for its
menu. Nothing is stored: the cards last as long as the page, and nothing is sent, called or
opened. Six of the eight cards are invented, their email addresses are on reserved domains,
and the glyphs of a card's actions are original stand-in drawings.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) without a title bar: the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) float over the sidebar, and the strip they
  sit in and the one around the search field are the window's drag regions.
- A [Split View](/components/split-view) of three panes with the measured divider colour.
  The groups are 223 pt wide and the names 188 pt; both dividers can be dragged.
- The groups are a [Sidebar](/components/sidebar) without icons: All Contacts, then a section
  for each account. A group lists the cards of its account. The sidebar runs from the
  window's top: the room for the window controls is a spacer of the demo's before the first
  row, not padding on the sidebar.
- The names are a [List View](/components/list-view) of 24 pt rows under a
  [Search Field](/components/search-field) set to the captured 21 pt. A card is listed by the
  name it is sorted by, in semibold: a person's last name, a company's own. A list with
  nothing in it shows an [Empty State](/components/empty-state).
- The card is a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view) over a bar of three
  [Push Buttons](/components/push-button): the plus, Edit or Done, and the share button, which
  is the trigger of a [Share Menu](/components/share-menu). Done is the emphasised Push
  Button. The plus, the share arrow, the mark of the user's own card and the silhouette of a
  picture are the library's [System Images](/components/system-image) `add`, `share` and
  `user`. The remove button of a field being edited is the destructive
  [Round Button](/components/round-button) at its mini size.
- A finished card has four [Round Buttons](/components/round-button) under the name: message,
  call, video and mail. Each is available when its service is set up (the `services` prop) and
  the card has a number or an address for it. A field's label is the trigger of a
  [Menu](/components/menu) of [Menu Items](/components/menu-item): Send Email, Send Message,
  Search with Spotlight. While the pointer is over a row its label sits in a capsule and a
  round button for the field's first command appears at the row's end.
- A card being edited has a [Text Field](/components/text-field) without a bezel for every
  value, as wide as its text; a small [Pop Up Button](/components/pop-up-button) without a
  bezel for every label, whose last item, Custom…, takes a label of the user's own; pop-up
  buttons for the ringtone, the text tone and the messaging service; a round button that
  removes a field; a small [Checkbox](/components/checkbox) that makes the card a company's;
  and a [Segmented Control](/components/segmented-control) that switches between the fields
  and the picture, where two [Radio Buttons](/components/radio-button) choose between the
  monogram and a silhouette.
- Every control is the library's as the library draws it. The demo gives a control a `size`
  from its own props, a place, at most a `width`, the name's 18 pt to the two
  [Text Fields](/components/text-field) a name is edited in, and the three custom properties their
  components document as hooks: `--mac-sidebar-section-gap`, `--mac-split-view-divider-color`
  and `--mac-search-field-height`. Where Contacts draws a control its own way, the demo keeps
  the library's look; [Fidelity](#fidelity) lists each difference. The two columns of a card
  are the demo's own grid and not the library's [Form](/components/form): a form's label is
  text that names the control beside it, and here the label is itself a control.

## How it behaves

| Action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Press a group | Lists that account's cards. The card that was chosen stays shown while the list holds it; otherwise the first of the list is shown. A group without cards shows "No Cards" and "No Card Selected". |
| Press a name, or move through the list with the arrow keys | Shows that card and makes it the chosen one, so it stays when the list changes again. An edit of the card that was shown ends, as with Done. |
| Type in the search field | Lists the cards whose names, company, note or any field's value contain the text, whatever the case and the accents; the text is taken as typed, so a `.` or a `*` is that character. Clearing the field brings the list back. |
| Press Edit | Makes every value a field. Each kind of field is followed by an empty one with the next free label; typing in it adds it to the card. The card stays listed while it is edited, also when a search no longer finds it. |
| Press Done | Keeps what was typed and drops the fields left empty. A search that no longer finds the card is cleared, so the card stays shown. |
| Choose in a label's pop-up menu | Changes the label; Custom… opens a field for a label of one's own, which Return keeps and Escape gives up. |
| Press a remove button | Removes that field. |
| Press the plus | Adds a card to the account that is shown (to iCloud from All Contacts), clears a search and opens the card with the first name focused. |
| Click a label of a finished card | Opens its menu of commands. The demo performs none of them: it calls `onAction` with the command and the value. |
| Double-click a name, or choose Card > Open in New Window (⌘I) | On a desktop, which gives the window `DesktopWindow`, opens each card selected in a window of its own. It is the same card: what is edited there shows in the list, and a card already open comes forward. Elsewhere neither does anything and the menu item is absent. |

## How it is built

The window is `Contacts` in `contacts.tsx`; the card pane is `ContactCard` in
`contacts-card.tsx`; the cards and the pure functions over them (`sortContacts`, `inGroup`,
`matches`, `tidy`, `nextLabel`, `labelOf`) are in `contacts-data.ts`, the six glyphs in
`contacts-artwork.tsx`, the layout in `contacts.css` and the measured colours in
`contacts.tokens.css`. What only the Ferrite desktop has is in `contacts-desktop.tsx` (its
commands, sheets and picture choosers), `contacts-server.ts` (its requests),
`contacts-vcard.ts` and `contacts-picture.ts`.

**State, selection and filtering.** The window keeps the cards, the group, the search text,
the chosen card's `id` and whether it is being edited. The list is derived on each render:
the cards of the group that the search finds, in order, with the card being edited kept in
whatever is typed. The card shown is the chosen one while the list holds it, and else the
list's first; the chosen card starts as the card shown, so a window that opens in edit mode
edits the card it shows.

```tsx
const [contacts, setContacts] = useState(defaultContacts);
const [group, setGroup] = useState(defaultGroup);
const [query, setQuery] = useState("");
const [selected, setSelected] = useState<string | null>(/* the card shown at first */);
const [editing, setEditing] = useState(defaultMode === "edit");

const visible = useMemo(
  () =>
    sortContacts(
      contacts.filter(
        (contact) =>
          inGroup(contact, group) &&
          (matches(contact, query) || (editing && contact.id === selected)),
      ),
    ),
  [contacts, group, query, editing, selected],
);
const current = visible.find((contact) => contact.id === selected) ?? visible[0] ?? null;
```

The List View is controlled by `current`, and a press on a name calls `choose`, which ends an
edit of another card and makes the pressed card the chosen one:

```tsx
<ListView
  count={visible.length}
  rowHeight={24}
  renderRow={renderRow}
  getRowId={(index) => visible[index]?.id ?? index}
  value={currentId === null ? [] : [currentId]}
  onValueChange={(next) => {
    if (typeof next[0] === "string") choose(next[0]);
  }}
/>
```

**Keeping edits.** While a card is edited every keystroke goes straight into the list of
cards (`change` replaces the card by `id`), so the list's row follows the name as it is
typed. Ending the edit only tidies the card: `tidy` trims its texts and drops the fields
left empty. Choosing another name, another group, a search or the plus all go through
`finish`; Done also clears a search that no longer finds the card:

```tsx
const finish = () => {
  if (!editing) return;
  setEditing(false);
  if (currentId !== null)
    setContacts((now) => now.map((contact) => (contact.id === currentId ? tidy(contact) : contact)));
};

const done = () => {
  finish();
  if (editing && current !== null && !matches(tidy(current), query)) setQuery("");
};

// Edit: the card shown becomes the chosen one, so it stays listed while it is edited.
const edit = () => {
  if (currentId === null) return;
  setSelected(currentId);
  setEditing(true);
};
```

The card pane is keyed by the card's `id` (`<ContactCard key={currentId} … />`), so the
state of one card's editor, such as which pane is shown, never carries over to another.

**The field rows.** A card's phone numbers, addresses, web addresses and messaging names
are four lists of `{ label, value }`. `FIELD_KINDS` says for each kind its name, its labels,
its placeholder and its commands, and the card renders one group per kind from it. In a
card being edited `EditGroup` shows the fields and, after the last one that holds
something, an empty row with the next free label; typing in that row is what adds a field,
and rows are keyed by position so the field keeps the focus when it does:

```tsx
const rows =
  last?.value !== "" ? [...fields, { label: nextLabel(kind, fields), value: "" }] : fields;
const set = (index: number, patch: Partial<ContactField>) => {
  const row = rows[index];
  if (!row) return;
  if (index < fields.length)
    onChange(fields.map((field, at) => (at === index ? { ...field, ...patch } : field)));
  else onChange([...fields, { ...row, ...patch }]);
};
```

Each row is a Round Button that removes the field, the label's Pop Up Button and a Text
Field without a bezel that is as wide as its text.

**The label menus.** A label is a control in both modes. In a card being edited it is a
small Pop Up Button without a bezel whose value is the label; its last item, Custom…, swaps
the button for a Text Field that takes a label of the user's own:

```tsx
<PopUpButton
  size="small"
  bordered={false}
  aria-label={`${spec.name} label`}
  value={label}
  onValueChange={(next) => {
    if (next === CUSTOM) setNaming(index);
    else if (next != null) set(index, { label: next });
  }}
>
  {labels.map((option) => (
    <PopUpButtonItem key={option} value={option}>{option}</PopUpButtonItem>
  ))}
  <PopUpButtonSeparator />
  <PopUpButtonItem value={CUSTOM}>Custom…</PopUpButtonItem>
</PopUpButton>
```

On a finished card `ViewGroup` makes the label the trigger of a Menu with the commands of
the field's kind. The demo performs none of them; each item reports the command and the
value through `onAction`:

```tsx
<Menu>
  <MenuTrigger className="mac-contacts-label">{labelOf(field)}</MenuTrigger>
  <MenuPopup side="right" align="start">
    {spec.actions.map(({ action, title }) => (
      <MenuItem key={action} onClick={() => onAction?.(action, { contact, value: field.value })}>
        {title}
      </MenuItem>
    ))}
  </MenuPopup>
</Menu>
```

## Hosting the window

`Contacts` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it,
so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component takes out before the rest goes to
the `Window`. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { contactsApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={contactsApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...contactsApp.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <contactsApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

Contacts has no title bar, so its drag regions are the strip over the groups and the strip
around the search field: a press there that is not on a control calls
`onDragRegionPointerDown`, and a double click calls `onDragRegionDoubleClick`.

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `defaultContacts` | `Contact[]` | The cards at first, each with its own `id`; eight by default, the two of the screenshots among them. An empty list shows "No Cards". |
| `defaultGroup` | `"all" \| "icloud" \| "mac"` | The group shown at first; `"all"` by default. |
| `defaultContact` | `string` | The card shown at first; `"stephen-hackett"` by default, and the first of the starting group when that group does not hold it, which is then the chosen card. |
| `defaultMode` | `"view" \| "edit"` | Whether the card is shown or being edited at first; `"view"` by default. |
| `services` | `ContactService[]` | The services set up on the Mac, which the four buttons under a name need; all four by default, and none dims all four. |
| `onContactChange` | `(id: string) => void` | Called with the card the user chooses in the list, not for a card the window shows by itself. |
| `onAction` | `(action, { contact, value }) => void` | Called for a button under the name, a field's badge, a command of a label's menu and a service of the share menu. |
| `material` | `"translucent" \| "opaque"` | The sidebar's surface: vibrant over a desktop (the default), or opaque on a plain page. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `controlsProps` | `WindowControlsProps` | Further props for the `WindowControls`; a `className` is added to the class that places them. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Called for the drag regions. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 938 by 438 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `contactsApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (938 by 438), `minSize` (780 by 320), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu.

A card (`Contact`) needs an `id`, an account and its two names, `first` and `last`, which are
strings that may be blank (`""`); everything else may be missing. A card
without names is listed and titled "No Name", or by its company when it has one; names that
are only spaces count as none. A company's card names the person under the company. A field
(`ContactField`) needs a `label` and a `value`, both strings: a blank label is shown and kept
as "other", a field with a blank value is dropped when the card is done, and a ringtone, a
text tone or a messaging
service that the pop-up menu does not offer is added to that menu for the card.

## On the desktop's Ferrite application

On the desktop served by the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application
(`ferrite-apps/desktop`, module `src/apps/contacts`), and only there, Contacts is the signed-in
person's own address book. Everything in this section is switched on by
`servedByDesktopApp()`; the window on this page, its examples and the visual comparisons have
none of it.

- **Groups, views and added fields.** Groups of the person's own (File > New Group, a drag of
  names onto a group, Edit > Remove from Group, Rename Group and Delete Group, and the contextual
  menus of a group and a name) are kept at `/api/contacts/groups`, as lists of card ids. The View
  menu's Hide Groups, Card Only and List and Card are kept with the person's settings. Card > Add
  Field adds a phonetic name, nickname, job title, department, address, related name, date or
  social profile; these are kept with the card and written to its vCard. Show on Map searches for the
  address in Maps.
- **My Card.** A person who has saved no cards starts with only their own card, made from
  their account: the first word of the full name as the first name, the rest as the last, and
  the account name as the card's `user`. Saved cards without the person's own card get it. A
  Guest User's card names no account. Before anyone signs in the window keeps its own cards.
- **The desktop's people.** The pull-down button with the `action` image in the bar, after the
  plus, holds the desktop's commands. Add Desktop User… asks for an account name in a
  [Sheet](/components/sheet) and looks it up on the desktop (`GET
  /api/contacts/users/<name>`); the new card takes the account's full name and its `user`. A
  name no account has is said so in the sheet, and an account that already has a card shows
  that card. A card with a `user` shows it as its "account" row, `martin@macalicious`, and
  its message and mail buttons are available: a message goes to the account name and mail to
  that address. `GET /api/contacts/people` answers the person's desktop users, without their
  own card, sorted by full name: the address book Messages and Mail read.
- **vCard files.** Import vCard… reads one or more `.vcf` files from the computer, and Import
  vCard from Drive… one from the person's drive in the drive's Open panel; a file dropped on the
  list of names is imported too. Export Card as vCard and Export All Cards as vCard download
  the shown card or every card; Save Card to Drive… and Save All Cards to Drive… save them with
  the drive's Save panel in a sheet. The files are vCard 3.0, or 2.1 when Preferences > vCard says so, in UTF-8 or the Windows Latin 1 Outlook reads, read and written in the browser
  (`contacts-vcard.ts`): names, company, phones, email addresses, web addresses, messaging
  names, the birthday, the note and the desktop account as
  `X-MACALICIOUS-USER`. A label vCard has no type for is written in Apple's way, with an
  `X-ABLabel`. Importing does not add a second card for a desktop user who already has one, nor
  the person's own card. A `PHOTO` that is a JPEG or PNG comes in as the card's picture, and an
  export writes each card's picture, unless Preferences > vCard says otherwise; a picture in any
  other format is left out, and Contacts says so. Preferences > vCard also says whether notes are
  written. Copy, Paste and a drag of names carry no pictures.
- **Contacts archive.** File > Export > Contacts Archive… downloads every card, group, smart group
  and picture as one file, and File > Import reads it back in place of what is there, after an
  alert. The file is named `.abbu`, as a Mac's is, but it is one JSON document of this desktop's
  own: a Mac's Contacts cannot read it, nor can this window read a Mac's archive.
- **Preferences.** General also sets the address format, which lays out a card's addresses.
  Template names the fields a new card starts with, and Phone formats ten-digit numbers where a
  finished card shows them. All of these are kept with the person's settings.
- **Cards in other applications.** The share menu and Card > Share My Card send a card as a `.vcf`
  file to Mail, Messages or Notes. AirDrop is dimmed: it needs another machine. A name dragged out
  of the list carries the card as vCard text and, in Chromium, as a `.vcf` for the computer.
- **Cut, Copy and Paste.** Edit > Copy puts the card, or the names selected, on the clipboard as
  vCard text, and Edit > Paste adds the cards it finds there as new ones. Edit > Cut copies and then
  deletes the cards, and Undo Cut brings them back. A card with a picture still asks first, as
  Delete Card does, since neither the vCard text nor Undo brings its picture back.
- **Pictures.** In the Picture pane of a card being edited, Choose Picture… takes an image
  from the computer and Choose from Drive… one from the drive, opening in Pictures, where Photo
  Booth keeps its pictures. The browser crops the middle square, or the square a person framed
  with Card > Edit Custom Image… or a double click on the picture, and draws it at most 256 by 256
  pixels as a JPEG of at most 64 KiB (`contacts-picture.ts`). The server keeps it beside the
  cards, one key per card, and checks that it is a JPEG or PNG `data:` URL under that size; the
  card names its version in `image` and shows it in place of the monogram. Remove Picture
  gives the monogram back.
- **Smart groups.** New Smart Group… makes a group of the cards its rules choose: all or any of
  up to 20 rules, each a field and a test, or whether the card is a desktop user. The groups are
  listed under Smart Groups in the sidebar, kept in a document of their own
  (`/api/contacts/smart-groups`). Edit Smart Group changes the rules, and Delete Smart Group
  removes the one shown, after an alert.
- **What the window remembers.** The group shown and the card chosen are kept with the person's
  settings and come back when the window opens.
- **While a card is edited.** Card > Discard Changes puts the card back as it was when the edit
  began, or takes away a card just made; a picture chosen during the edit stays, since it is on
  the server already.

The cards are one document per person (`/api/contacts/cards`), saved after every change; the
pictures are not in it, since the whole list is sent with every change under the gateway's
256 KiB request limit. A save the desktop does not keep is said in an alert: when the cards were
changed in another window or browser first, the change is made again on that version where it
can be, and otherwise that version is kept and shown; any other failure is sent again whole with
the next change. `ferrite-apps/desktop/e2e/contacts-journey.mjs` drives two people
through all of this in a real browser.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named "Contacts". The groups are a `tree` named "Groups" and the
  names a `listbox` named by the group; the arrow keys move the selection and typing selects
  by name. The user's own card says so in its name ("Stephen Hackett, my card").
- A finished card's name is a level 2 heading. The four buttons under it are a group named
  "Actions"; each is named by its caption, and one that is not available is disabled.
- A label that opens a menu is a button named by its text. Return, Space or Down Arrow opens
  the menu, the arrow keys move in it and Escape closes it. The round button at the end of a
  row is hidden until the row is under the pointer or holds the keyboard focus, so Tab shows
  it; it is named by its command and the value ("Send Email stephen@example.com").
- In a card being edited every field is named by its label and its kind ("work email"), every
  label's pop-up button by the kind ("email label") and every remove button by what it
  removes ("Remove work email"). The fields of one kind are a group named by the kind.
- The plus is named "New Contact" and the share button "Share". An empty search is announced
  through a `status` element.
- On the Ferrite desktop the pull-down of the desktop's commands is named "Contacts commands",
  the sheets are dialogs named by their titles ("Add a Desktop User", "New Smart Group"), a name
  no account has is announced through an `alert`, and an import's result is an alert dialog. A
  card's own picture is decorative, as the monogram is: the card's name is its heading.

`contacts.visual.json` compares the window with the Contacts screenshots in both appearances,
each as captured and again with the text and the stand-in drawings masked. The Aqua capture
shows the card being edited with the label menu of the email field open, the Dark Aqua one
the finished card with the action menu of the "work" label open; the comparisons open both
menus with real clicks. Scores from `npm run visual -- contacts/` in Chromium on Linux with
the fallback font: the share of pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

### Where the library's controls are not Contacts' own

The demo does not restyle a library control: no padding, height, colour, radius or
background of a control is set from outside, and no type but one: the two fields of a name
being edited are given the name's 18 pt, as a [Text Field](/components/text-field) takes the
font it is given (its `large-text` example is this name). Where Contacts draws a control its
own way, the
window therefore shows the library's. Each row is a departure from the captures.

| Control | Contacts draws | The library's control draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Label of a field being edited | 12 pt text in the link blue, ending 107.5 pt from the card's edge, with blue arrows 6 pt wide 8 pt after it; a menu 95 pt wide. | A small Pop Up Button without a bezel: 11 pt text, in Contacts' blue through the button's two colour hooks, arrows too, the title ending 4 pt nearer the arrows. The demo sets the button at the end of the label column, so the arrows are where the capture has them. Its menu is as wide as its titles, 96 pt in the fallback font, and stands about 5 pt right of the captured one. |
| The four buttons under the name | 24 pt discs. | Regular Round Buttons, 20 pt discs, centred where the captured ones are. The badge at the end of a row is the same button, and the captured badge is 20 pt. |
| Done | A 59 pt bezel with a blue, semibold title. | The emphasised Push Button: the blue, semibold title, in a bezel as wide as the title and the button's 14 pt of padding on each side make it. |
| Plus | A 34 pt bezel (33 pt in Dark Aqua). | A Push Button with the `add` System Image, 38 pt: the 11 pt image and the same padding. |
| Names of the groups | Text 20 pt from the sidebar's edge. | A Sidebar row without an icon: text 18 pt from the edge. |
| Info and Picture | A flat capsule with a grey selected half. | A Segmented Control: the push button's bezel, and the accent colour on the selected segment. |

### Other differences

- The captures are regions of a desktop, and the sidebar in them is the vibrant material
  over the desktop picture, which is not reproduced. The examples use the opaque sidebar. In
  Aqua the picture tints the captured sidebar from beige to blue, so both Aqua rows leave out
  the sidebar, a quarter of the window, and the line beside it: unmasked, the Aqua crop
  scores 22.8% at 2x. In Dark Aqua the two surfaces are a few levels apart, which nothing
  counts as a differing pixel; it is most of that row's mean difference. The window controls,
  the groups and their selection are therefore compared in Dark Aqua only.
- Most of the pixels that differ in `window-light-layout` at 2x are the Info and Picture
  control and the open label menu (its place, its width and its rim); the rest are the
  search field's outline, lighter than the captured one, the arrows of the pop-up
  buttons beside the values, and the bar, with its wider plus button.
- The capture's window has a lighter line along its top edge than along the other three
  (`#707171` over `#5d5e5e` in Dark Aqua, where the sides are `#4b4c4d`): the two highlight
  rows a title bar draws. The window has no title bar, so it draws them itself through the
  Window's `topHighlight`. Of what still differs in `window-dark-layout` at 2x, most is the
  four 20 pt discs, the action menu's width and rim and the bar.
- In the Aqua capture the first phone field holds a selection and has the focus ring while
  the menu is open. In a browser the focus is in the open menu, so the field has neither; the
  masked comparison leaves that field out.
- The menus are as wide as their titles in the font in use: with the fallback font the
  action menu is 182 pt where the capture has 179.
- The email address of the captured card is replaced by one on a reserved domain.
- The name of a card being edited is two fields, first and last; the name's prefix, suffix,
  nickname and the other fields of the Card menu are not included, and a card has no address,
  related names or dates other than the birthday. A note is edited in a field of one line: a
  finished card shows a note's lines, and editing the note joins them.
- A finished card, a label's capsule and the badges in Aqua, and a card being edited in Dark
  Aqua, have no reference screenshot; neither have the Picture pane, the Custom… field, the
  share menu or the empty states. They are composed to Catalina's conventions and are
  provisional, as the tags in `contacts.tokens.css` say.
- Cards cannot be deleted, and the window here has no groups of its own and no pictures but
  the monogram and the silhouette: the demo has no files. On the desktop served by Ferrite a
  card can have a picture and the sidebar smart groups ([On the desktop's Ferrite
  application](#on-the-desktops-ferrite-application)).
