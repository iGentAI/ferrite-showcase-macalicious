<Example name="basic" />

Click a heading to sort, pick a view in the toolbar, type in the search field, select a row
and quit it. The processes are invented: nothing is read from the machine the page runs on. A
timer moves their numbers every two seconds, the rows re-sort as they change, and the footer
and its graph follow. The process icons are original stand-in drawings; the toolbar glyphs are
the library's [System Images](/components/system-image).

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with a unified [Title Bar](/components/title-bar): the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), the title and a
  [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) on one surface.
- The toolbar: two [Toolbar Buttons](/components/toolbar-button), Quit Process and Inspect,
  which are unavailable while no process is selected or a search hides the selected one; the
  Action pull-down, a toolbar button
  that opens a [Menu](/components/menu) of [Menu Items](/components/menu-item); the five views
  as a [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) centred on the
  window; and a [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field) that filters the list
  by process name. Quit Process asks in a [Sheet](/components/sheet) that hangs from the
  title bar, with a [Default Button](/components/default-button) for Quit. The three buttons'
  glyphs are [System Images](/components/system-image): `stop-progress`, `info`, and `action`
  beside `chevron-down`.
- The process list: a [Table View](/components/table-view) with 18 pt alternating rows at the
  small size, a [Table Header](/components/table-header) whose every heading sorts and is
  centred over right-aligned numbers and left-aligned names (`headerAlign`), single
  selection that is emphasised while the table has the keyboard focus in the key window, and
  type-select by process name. Rows are selected by process identifier, so the selection stays
  on its process while the rows re-sort and when the view changes.
- The footer: a [Box](/components/box) in its `well` variant, of three cells. The outer cells
  hold up to three statistics each, set in the [Label](/components/label)'s 12 pt `callout`
  style, and the middle one a history graph, drawn as SVG, with the newest point at
  the trailing edge.

| View | Columns | Footer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU | % CPU, CPU Time, Threads, Idle Wake Ups, % GPU, GPU Time, PID, User | System, User and Idle shares, the CPU load graph, thread and process counts |
| Memory | Memory, Threads, Ports, PID, User | Physical memory, memory used, swap; memory pressure; app, wired and compressed memory |
| Energy | Energy Impact, Avg Energy Impact, App Nap, Preventing Sleep, User | Energy impact, graphics card, charge; energy impact graph; time on AC |
| Disk | Bytes Written, Bytes Read, Kind, PID, User | Data read and written per second; data graph; totals |
| Network | Sent Bytes, Rcvd Bytes, Sent Packets, Rcvd Packets, PID, User | Packets in and out per second; packets graph; totals |

Only the CPU view has a reference screenshot. The columns of the other four follow Activity
Monitor's defaults at plausible widths, and their footers use the CPU view's three-cell layout.

The Action menu holds Quit Process, Inspect Process and the update frequency: every second,
every two seconds (the default), every five seconds, or paused. Quit Process asks first, in a
sheet on the window: Cancel (or Escape) keeps the process, and Quit (or Return) and Force Quit
remove it. While the sheet waits the window is main, not key, its toolbar and content are
inert and its close button is disabled. Inspect, a double click on a row and Return call `onInspect` with
the process; the line under the example stands for the inspector window a host would open.

## Hosting the window

`ActivityMonitor` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to
it, so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
the title bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { activityMonitorApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={activityMonitorApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...activityMonitorApp.defaultSize }}
  minWidth={activityMonitorApp.minSize.width}
  minHeight={activityMonitorApp.minSize.height}
  render={(props) => <activityMonitorApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `processes` | `ActivityMonitorProcess[]` | The processes the window opens with; the default is the list of the light screenshot and eight more. |
| `totals` | `ActivityMonitorTotals` | The footer's figures for the whole machine when the window opens. |
| `history` | `ActivityMonitorSample[]` | Points the graphs start with; without it they fill as the numbers move. |
| `defaultUpdateInterval` | `number` | Milliseconds between updates at first; 2000 by default, and 0 opens the window paused. |
| `tab`, `defaultTab`, `onTabChange` | `"cpu" \| "memory" \| "energy" \| "disk" \| "network"` and a function | The view, controlled or not. |
| `defaultSelectedPid` | `number` | The process selected at first. |
| `onInspect` | `(process: ActivityMonitorProcess) => void` | Called for Inspect, a double click on a row and Return. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 839 by 634 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `activityMonitorApp` and as `app`:
`id`, `name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (839 by 634), `minSize` (640 by 300), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu.

The simulation is a pure function of its state (`advance` in `activity-monitor-data.ts`): the
random numbers come from a seed the state carries, so every run of the demo, and of its tests,
shows the same sequence.

## On the desktop's Ferrite application

When the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves the desktop (`ferrite-apps/desktop`),
`DesktopActivityMonitor` (`activity-monitor-desktop.tsx`) shows three views:

- **Applications**: the desktop's running applications with their windows; Quit Process quits
  one, as Force Quit does.
- **Ferrite**: how fast the application answers, per app, over the last five minutes: requests
  per second, the median and 95th-percentile server time, the store calls each request made
  (every call the app's code makes, counted by the router) and failed requests, with the
  deployment generation and instance that answered. The application's
  router measures every request a module answers on the platform clock and keeps the last five
  minutes in the module's request counter, in the write that counts the request
  (`ferrite-apps/desktop/src/timing.rs`), so measuring costs no store call; the percentiles are
  estimated within their latency bucket. The graph plots the median and the 95th percentile.
  The figures are for everyone together, by app, never by person. The window reads nothing
  while the page is hidden.
- **Network**: the round trips the browser itself timed to the application (`/health`) and to
  Activity Monitor's own read, every two seconds.

Ferrite shows an application no processes, CPU, memory, energy or disk figures, so none is
shown.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title. The toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys
  move between its buttons, the five views and the search field.
- Quit Process, Inspect and Action show only a glyph and are named with `aria-label`. The
  first two are marked unavailable (`aria-disabled`) while no listed process is selected and
  stay reachable with the arrow keys.
- The views are toggle buttons in a group named "View"; the selected one has
  `aria-pressed="true"` and cannot be switched off.
- The table is a `grid` named "Processes" and one tab stop: the arrow keys, Page Up, Page
  Down, Home and End move the selection, typing selects by name, and Return inspects. Each
  heading is a button, and its column reports the order with `aria-sort`.
- The footer box is a `group` named after the view ("CPU summary"); its statistics are
  description lists, and the graph is an image named "CPU history". The System and User
  values are also told apart by their labels, not by colour alone.
- The quit confirmation is a modal `dialog` named by its question and described by the line
  that names the process. The keyboard stays inside it; Escape cancels and Return quits.
- The numbers change without an announcement: a live region that spoke every two seconds
  would be noise. Pause Updates in the Action menu stops the movement.

## Fidelity

`activity-monitor.visual.json` compares the whole window with the Activity Monitor
screenshots in both appearances, each as captured and again with the text and the stand-in
drawings masked. The dark screenshot lists other processes than the light one and has its
seventh row selected, so it has an example of its own. Scores from
`npm run visual -- activity-monitor/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font: the share
of pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 6.98% | 6.95 | 5.95% | 7.70 |
| `window-light-layout` (masked) | 0.61% | 0.59 | 0.09% | 0.48 |
| `window-dark` | 6.84% | 6.44 | 5.83% | 7.01 |
| `window-dark-layout` (masked) | 0.63% | 0.70 | 0.06% | 0.50 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 839 by 634 pt frame, the 56 pt chrome with
its controls where the capture has them, the nine column widths, the 18 pt rows with the name
46 pt from the window's edge, the 116 pt footer under its line, the 580 by 90 pt summary box
with its three cells, the 20 pt pitch and the rules of the statistics, and the red and blue of
the System and User values.

What differs, and why:

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the unmasked
  scores. The table's and the footer's text is 12 pt; the title, the toolbar and the headings
  follow the library's controls.
- **Icons and glyphs.** Process icons are application artwork and are replaced by original
  drawings. The toolbar glyphs are the library's System Images in place of Apple's, placed
  where the captures have them: `stop-progress`, a disc where Activity Monitor draws an
  octagon; `info` at 14 of its 15 pt; and `action` with `chevron-down` at 7.5 pt wide.
- **The graph.** The capture shows one small rise at the trailing edge of the CPU load graph;
  the comparison draws a rise of the same size there. Its shape while the demo runs is the
  simulation's.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the Memory, Energy, Disk and Network views, the
  Action menu's items, the quit confirmation sheet with its wording, width and button order,
  the window when it is not key, and every size other than the captured
  one.
