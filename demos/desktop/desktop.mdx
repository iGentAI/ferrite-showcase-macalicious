<Example name="basic" />

Click a tile in the Dock to open an application. Drag a window by its title bar, resize it at
its edges and corners, and use its three buttons: red closes it, yellow sends it to the Dock,
where a click on its tile brings it back, and green enters full screen (hold Option to zoom
instead). The menu bar shows the menus of the application whose window is key, and Finder's
while no window is open. Open **System Preferences** from the Apple menu or its tile in the
Dock and choose General to switch the appearance and the accent colour of the whole page.

## The applications

Every demo in `demos/` that exports a descriptor is in the Dock: the desktop finds them when
the page is built, so a demo that is added later appears here without a change to this page.

## What it shows

- A [Window Manager](/components/window-manager) as the screen. Each application is a
  [Managed Window](/components/managed-window) in its `render` form: the manager places,
  sizes, orders and focuses the window, and the application draws it.
- A [Menu Bar](/components/menu-bar) fixed to the top edge: the Apple menu with the rows of
  the Apple-Menu screenshots, the front application's [Menus](/components/menu) of
  [Menu Items](/components/menu-item) and [Submenus](/components/submenu), and the
  [Menu Bar Extras](/components/menu-bar-extra) of the Desktop screenshots with a clock that
  keeps the time.
- A [Dock](/components/dock): Finder, Launchpad, one tile per application with a dot under
  the ones that are running, a tile for every minimized window after the separator, a
  Downloads [Dock Stack](/components/dock-stack) that opens as a fan, and the Trash. The tiles
  shrink from 64 pt towards a least size of 12 pt to fit the desktop's width.
- [Launchpad](/components/launchpad), [Spotlight](/components/spotlight) and
  [Siri](/components/siri) over the windows, one at a time. Launchpad lists the desktop's
  applications and Spotlight searches their names; choosing one opens it. Siri answers nothing:
  its window shows what a request would look like.
- A [Desktop](/components/desktop) behind the windows with two volumes as
  [Desktop Icons](/components/desktop-icon) and two [Desktop Stacks](/components/desktop-stack).
- The appearance and the accent colour, switched for the whole page. The General pane of the
  [System Preferences](/demos/system-preferences) demo sets `data-appearance` and
  `data-accent` on the page, so every window, menu and popup follows. A desktop whose `apps`
  have no System Preferences brings a General window of its own: the appearance as three
  thumbnails and the accent colour as eight swatches, both a
  [Color Swatch Picker](/components/color-swatch-picker), set with the helpers of
  `macalicious/lib/appearance`; the page gets back what it had when that desktop is removed.

| Command | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Open an application | Its Dock tile; Launchpad; Spotlight, Return on a result or a double click; Apple menu, Recent Items; Finder's Go menu, Applications |
| Launchpad | Its Dock tile shows and hides it; Escape or a click on its background closes it |
| Spotlight, Siri | Their extras in the menu bar show and hide them; Escape in an empty field, or a press beside the window, closes Spotlight, and Siri has a close button |
| Bring a window forward | A press anywhere in it; its application's Dock tile; the Window menu |
| Move, resize | The title bar; the edges and corners. Control-Option with the arrow keys moves, and with Shift resizes |
| Minimize, zoom, full screen | The window's buttons; the Window and View menus; Control-Option-M, Z and F |
| Hide, hide others, show all | The application menu |
| Preferences | The application menu, Preferences…; Command-comma, or Control-comma off Apple platforms, while the application is in front |
| Close, quit | The red button; File, Close Window; the application menu, Quit |

The menus of an application come from the titles its descriptor names. The desktop fills each
from its title with the commands it can carry out on the application's windows, and dims the
rest; a title it knows nothing about holds one dimmed row. In the Apple menu and the status
menus, the rows that open preferences open System Preferences, About This Mac and Force Quit…
open the [About This Mac](/demos/about-this-mac) and [Force Quit](/demos/force-quit) windows,
the applications listed under Recent Items open, and the other rows do nothing.

## Hosting an application

The desktop reads `app` from every `demos/<app>/app.ts`:

```ts
export const app = {
  id: "notes", // the folder's name; the window's and the application's id
  name: "Notes", // the Dock label and the title of the application menu
  component: Notes, // its root is the library's Window
  defaultSize: { width: 1000, height: 666 },
  minSize: { width: 1000, height: 300 }, // optional
  resizable: true, // optional; false disables zoom and removes the resize handles
  zoomButton: "zoom", // optional; for a window without full screen
  sizesItself: true, // optional; the component takes onFrameSizeChange and is given that frame
  preferences: "own", // optional; or a second window of the desktop, see Preferences below
  windows: true, // optional; the component opens further windows, see Further windows below
  menus: ["Notes", "File", "Edit", "Format", "View", "Window", "Help"],
};
```

`component` is rendered by a managed window's `render` form, so it takes the props of a
`Window` and eight more, which it takes out before the rest goes to its `Window`: `onClose`,
`onMinimize`, `onZoom`, `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled` and `zoomDisabled` for its
`WindowControls`, and `onDragRegionPointerDown` and `onDragRegionDoubleClick` for its
`TitleBar`. A window opens at `defaultSize`, or at what the desktop has room for when it is
resizable, centred, a third of the way down the space between the menu bar and the Dock.

Two demos say the last two things in the prose of their descriptors and not as fields, so the
desktop adds them by the demo's id: a Stickies note zooms, and System Preferences is resized
to the frame of each pane, which it reports through `onFrameSizeChange`. Stickies opens as one
note of its default size.

### Preferences

An application whose descriptor names its `preferences` has a Preferences… command in its
application menu, and Command-comma (Control-comma off Apple platforms) gives it while the
application is in front. A descriptor without `preferences` leaves the command dimmed. There
are two forms:

```ts
// A second window that the desktop hosts beside the main one (Calendar).
preferences: {
  component: CalendarPreferences, // its root is the library's PreferencesWindow
  defaultSize: { width: 537, height: 542 },
  title: "Calendar Preferences", // optional; this is what it is when left out
  sharesSettings: true, // optional
},

// The application renders its Preferences window itself (Mail, Music, Podcasts).
preferences: "own",
```

| Form | What `component` is given | What the desktop does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a window | nothing for it: the desktop takes Command-comma before the application's windows see the key | Opens `preferences.component` as a second [Managed Window](/components/managed-window) of the same application, or brings it forward: it is moved by its title bar, minimized into the Dock, listed in the Window menu and closed by itself. The application runs while either window is open. |
| `"own"` | `preferencesOpen` and `onPreferencesOpenChange`, which it calls with `true` on its own Command-comma and with `false` when its Preferences close | Sets `preferencesOpen`, and while they are open brings them forward: the desktop raises a `DesktopWindow` that says `isPreferences`, and any other form listens with `useOwnPreferencesRaised`. The window is the application's: where it stands and how it is moved are its own, and it goes with the main window. |

`preferences.component` has the library's [Preferences Window](/components/preferences-window)
as its root and hands it every prop it is given (`state`, `width`, `style`, `onClose`,
`controls`, `onPointerDown`, `ref`). The window opens at `defaultSize`, cannot be resized, and
keeps its width; its height is that of the pane it shows, as in Catalina, and the frame
follows it. With `sharesSettings: true`, `preferences.component` is also given `settings` and
`onSettingsChange` (always both, so it may declare them required), and the main `component`
is given `settings`: what `onSettingsChange` was last called with, and `undefined` before
that, when each window shows its own default.

### Further windows

An application that opens more windows than its main one (Mail's New Message window, the
Finder's Info window and Quick Look) says `windows: true` in its descriptor. Its `component`
is then given `DesktopWindow`, a component that renders one of them as a window of the
desktop. The application renders a `DesktopWindow` where it would otherwise render the window
itself, for as long as the window is open, and keeps what the window shows; the desktop
places it, moves it, orders it with the other windows, focuses it, lists it in the Window
menu and minimizes it into the Dock, as a window of the same application.

```tsx
function Mail({ DesktopWindow, ...props }: MailProps) {
  const [draft, setDraft] = useState<MailDraft | null>(null);
  return (
    <>
      <Window {...props}>…</Window>
      {DesktopWindow && draft ? (
        <DesktopWindow
          id="compose"
          title="New Message"
          defaultSize={{ width: 886, height: 585 }}
          onClose={() => setDraft(null)}
          render={(windowProps) => <MailCompose {...windowProps} defaultDraft={draft} />}
        />
      ) : null}
    </>
  );
}
```

| Prop of `DesktopWindow` | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | Names the window among the application's own; on the desktop it is `<app>:<id>`. Not `"preferences"`, which is the id of hosted Preferences: that id is refused. |
| `title` | Its name in the Window menu and on its Dock tile while it is minimized. |
| `defaultSize` | The frame it opens at, in points. |
| `minSize`, `resizable`, `zoomButton` | As in the descriptor, for this window; resizable when left out. |
| `panel` | A utility panel: it floats above the ordinary windows, and the front one stays main while it is key. |
| `fitsContent` | The window is as tall as what it shows (an Info window whose sections open and close): `render` is given `style` with an automatic height and no `height`, and the frame follows the window. Such a window is not resizable, whatever `resizable` says. |
| `dragRegion` | A selector for the part that moves a window whose root takes no drag region callbacks (an [Inspector Panel](/components/inspector-panel), a [Quick Look Panel](/components/quick-look-panel)): `render` is given an `onPointerDown` for the window's root. |
| `onClose` | Called when the window is closed from the desktop; the application then stops rendering it. |
| `render` | The window: a component whose root is a window of the library, given the props a managed window's `render` form supplies, as `component` is. |

The windows are the main window's to render. When the main window closes while a further window that is no panel is
open, the main window stays mounted out of sight (`desktop-hidden.ts`) until the last of them closes, so the further
windows stay, as on a Mac. A panel goes when the last other window of its application has gone, and all of them go with the
application's Quit command. Without `windows` in the descriptor no `DesktopWindow` is given,
and an application's further windows are its own to place, as on its demo page.

`CatalinaDesktop` takes the applications as `apps`, for a desktop with others than the demos:

```tsx
import { CatalinaDesktop } from "./desktop";

<CatalinaDesktop apps={[notesApp, calculatorApp]} defaultOpen={["notes"]} style={{ height: 720 }} />;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `apps` | descriptors | The applications. By default every demo found in `demos/`. |
| `defaultOpen` | ids | The applications whose windows are open at first. |
| `clock` | `string` | Pins the text of the menu bar clock. Without it the clock shows the time. |
| `userName` | `string` | The user in the menu bar and the Apple menu; `"Stephen"` as captured. |
| `stacks` | `boolean` | Whether the desktop shows its two stacks; `true` by default. |
| the rest | `WindowManagerProps` | `style`, `className`, `ref` and so on. Give the desktop a size. |

## Accessibility

- The menu bar is a `menubar`, and the status menus are a second one named "Status menus".
  Left and Right Arrow move between titles, Down Arrow opens a menu, and Escape closes it.
- The desktop is a group named "Desktop"; its icons are buttons that the arrow keys move
  between, and Return opens one.
- Each window is a `region` named by its title. A window opened or brought forward by a
  command takes the keyboard focus; the window manager's key commands (Control-Option with
  the arrow keys, M, W, Z, F and the backquote) act on the key window.
- The Dock is a `toolbar` named "Dock" with one tab stop. Its tiles are buttons named after
  their applications; a running application is described as "running", and a minimized
  window's tile is named with its title and "minimized window".
- While a full-screen window is key it covers the menu bar and the Dock, which are then
  `inert`.
- The pictures are decorative and hidden from assistive technology.

What differs, and why:

- **The desktop picture.** Catalina's picture is Apple's and is not shipped. The stand-in is
  two gradients that follow the picture's colours behind the menu bar, the Apple menu and the
  Dock, so the whole screen is not compared: only the menu bar, the Apple menu and the Dock
  are. The backdrop is a plain gradient standing in for the wallpaper, not a drawing of it.
  The demo shows an original generated island photograph over that gradient; the visual
  examples leave the photograph out (`ReferenceDesktop`), so the translucent bar, menu and Dock
  are compared over the gradient and no pixel of the generated picture is compared.
- **Artwork.** The Dock's icons, the Apple mark and the status images are stand-in drawings
  of the measured sizes. The Dock comparison masks the tiles.
- **Text.** Off Apple devices the titles are set in the fallback font.
- **Not compared here:** Spotlight, Siri and Launchpad are on the desktop, drawn with the
  library's components, which are compared with the Spotlight, Siri and Launchpad screenshots
  on their own pages: [Spotlight](/components/spotlight), [Siri](/components/siri) and
  [Launchpad](/components/launchpad). The desktop's own versions hold its applications and
  stand-in content, not what those screenshots show.
- **Notification Center** opens from its extra in the menu bar, drawn with the library's
  component, which is compared with the Notification-Center screenshots on
  [its own page](/components/notification-center). When the desktop is served by its Ferrite
  application, it lists what every app delivered to the signed-in person, live, grouped by app,
  and its Today view adds the Do Not Disturb switch and widgets with the person's own Calendar
  events, Reminders due and Stocks watchlist, and the weather at the places they choose from
  Open-Meteo; notifications also come in as banners and alerts
  at the top trailing corner (the library's [Notification Alert](/components/notification-alert)),
  and an Option-click on the extra turns Do Not Disturb on or off. Every other build shows the
  date and no notifications. No reference screenshot shows a banner on the desktop or the
  widgets' content, so their placement and rows are not compared.
- **Mission Control and the application switcher** use the live windows, scaled apart. F3 or
  Control-Up shows every window, Control-Down the front application's, and Show All Windows in a
  Dock tile's menu that application's. Control-Option-D shows the desktop. Option-Tab or Option-`
  brings up the switcher: keep Option held, Tab steps, Q quits, H hides, the Down Arrow shows that
  application's windows and letting go chooses. A page cannot have Command-Tab or a swipe.
- **Spaces** are in Mission Control's bar: add, remove, drag to reorder, and drag a window's thumbnail
  onto one. Control-Left and Control-Right move between them, Control-Option-1 to 9 go to one, and a
  window brought forward from the Dock takes you to its Space. Each window is on one Space; the icons,
  the Dock and the menu bar are shared. Off a Mac those keys come to the page, and a list, table or icon
  view inside the desktop leaves Control with an arrow alone (`data-system-arrows`), so that it reaches
  the application in front and then the desktop, as on a Mac.
- **Compared elsewhere:** the open Downloads fan of the Stacks screenshots, on the
  [Dock Stack](/components/dock-stack) page, and the battery menu of the Menu-Bar-Battery
  screenshots, on the [Menu Bar Extra](/components/menu-bar-extra) page.
- **Key equivalents:** the keys shown in the menus run their commands while the focus is on the
  desktop, and on the page's body when the desktop fills the page, so they work with no window
  open. A desktop embedded in a page leaves a key pressed outside it to the page. A browser keeps
  some of them for itself, such as Command-Q and Command-W.
- **Not built:** each
  application has one main window on the desktop unless its descriptor names `newWindowTitle`.
