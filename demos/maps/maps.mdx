<Example name="basic" />

Click the pin to open Apple Park's place card, as in the reference screenshots. Switch the
map type, zoom, open or close a tab, or search: type "theater", "visitor" or "park" and
press Return. The map is an abstract placeholder and the demo knows five places; nothing is
fetched anywhere.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) whose [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) hides the title
  (`variant="title-hidden"`): the window controls and the [Toolbar](/components/toolbar)
  share one 38 pt row, and `divider="bar"` gives it the lighter bottom edge of a bar that is
  followed by another.
- The toolbar: a [Toolbar Button](/components/toolbar-button) that is a toggle, Directions;
  one with a glyph, the location button; a [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field)
  with its clear button; a [Share Menu](/components/share-menu); and a
  [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) that selects one of
  Map, Transit and Satellite.
- A [Window Tab Bar](/components/window-tab-bar) of two tabs with close buttons and the
  new-tab cell. Every tab keeps its own part of the map, zoom, map type, search and place.
- The map, held by an [Image View](/components/image-view) that names it, with the
  [Map Controls](/components/map-controls) over it: the Show pull-down, a `MapButton` that is
  the trigger of a [Menu](/components/menu) of checkbox items, at the leading corner, and the
  compass with its 3D button over the zoom pair at the trailing one.
- The place card: a [Popover](/components/popover) at the left of the pin, whose arrow
  points at it. Inside, two [Push Buttons](/components/push-button) with glyphs (the second
  is the trigger of another Share Menu), a full-width Directions button, three image views,
  a disabled Call button, [Labels](/components/label) as links, and the popover's
  separators, in a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view) that scrolls when the card has
  less room than it needs.

| Command | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search | the search field, then Return | Puts the pin on the best match, moves the map to it and opens its place card. A search that finds nothing says so on the map. The clear button or Escape empties the field and takes the pin away. |
| Map, Transit, Satellite | the toolbar | The tab's map type: the transit view adds two lines to the placeholder, the satellite view darkens it. |
| Directions | the toolbar and the card | Draws a route from the current location to the place; the toolbar button takes it away again. |
| Current location | the toolbar | Moves the map so that the current location's dot is in the middle. |
| Share | the toolbar and the card | A menu of three services; choosing one is reported to `onShare`. |
| Open a card | the pin | A click on the pin opens its card; Escape, or a press anywhere else, closes it. |
| Favorite | the card | Marks the place; the heart fills. |
| Tabs | the tab bar | A click selects a tab, its close button or Delete closes it, the + opens a tab on the same part of the map. The last tab cannot be closed. |
| Show | the map | Traffic, 3D Map and Scale, each on or off. |
| Zoom, 3D | the map | Nine zoom levels, from 25% to 400%; the middle of the compass switches 3D. |

## Source walkthrough

The demo is six files in `demos/maps/`.

**`maps-data.ts`** is what the demo knows: five places, each a name, the two halves of a
card's second line and a point of the abstract map, and for Apple Park the address, the
phone number and the links of the captures' card; the two regions of the tabs; the weather
of the Aqua capture; and `searchPlaces(places, query)`, which ranks a name that starts with
the text before one with a word that does, and that before one that only contains it.

**`maps-artwork.tsx`** holds the drawings, all of them original and all placeholders: the
map, the pin, the weather's mark, the three photographs, the glyphs of the location,
Favorite and Share buttons and the icons of the Share menus. The map is one tile repeated
in every direction and placed by the view's middle and scale, so it needs no measuring:

```tsx
<pattern id={id} patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse" width={480} height={360}
         patternTransform={`scale(${scale}) translate(${-center.x} ${-center.y})`}>
  {/* two wide and two narrow bands, a disc, a block and a stripe */}
</pattern>
```

**`maps.tsx`** is the window. Its state is the list of tabs, each a view of the map, and
whether the card is open; everything shown is derived from the tab in front:

```tsx
const view = tabs.views.find((item) => item.id === tabs.active);
const place = places.find((item) => item.id === view.placeId) ?? null;
const scale = scaleOf(view.zoom);
```

The chrome is the title bar with its toolbar, then the tab bar:

```tsx
<TitleBar variant="title-hidden" divider="bar" controls={<WindowControls />}>
  <Toolbar aria-label="Toolbar">
    <ToolbarButton pressed={view.route} onPressedChange={(route) => change({ route })}>
      Directions
    </ToolbarButton>
    <ToolbarSpace />
    <ToolbarButton aria-label="Show Your Current Location"><LocationGlyph /></ToolbarButton>
    <ToolbarSearchField placeholder="Search for a place or address" value={view.query}
                        onValueChange={(query) => change({ query })} onClear={() => showPlace(null)} />
    <ShareMenu>{/* ShareMenuItems */}</ShareMenu>
    <ToolbarSegmentedControl aria-label="Map type" value={[view.mapType]}
                             onValueChange={(value) => change({ mapType: value[0] })}>
      <ToolbarSegment value="map">Map</ToolbarSegment>
      {/* Transit, Satellite */}
    </ToolbarSegmentedControl>
  </Toolbar>
</TitleBar>
<WindowTabBar aria-label="Tabs" value={String(view.id)} onValueChange={selectTab} onNewTab={newTab}>
  {tabs.views.map((item) => (
    <WindowTab key={item.id} value={String(item.id)} onClose={() => closeTab(item.id)}>
      {item.title}
    </WindowTab>
  ))}
</WindowTabBar>
```

The pin is the popover's trigger and its ball is what the card points at. The card's fill
is the popover's material without its translucency, and its padding is taken away, both
through the popover's own hooks:

```tsx
<Popover open={cardOpen} onOpenChange={setCardOpen}>
  <PopoverTrigger render={<button type="button" className="mac-maps-pin" style={where} />}>
    <Pin />
    <span ref={setPinHead} className="mac-maps-pin-head" />
    <span className="mac-maps-pin-label">{place.name}</span>
  </PopoverTrigger>
  <PopoverPopup side="left" sideOffset={17} anchor={pinHead} className="mac-maps-card"
                style={{ "--mac-popover-padding": "0px",
                         "--mac-material-popover": "var(--mac-material-popover-opaque)" }}>
    <PlaceCard place={place} />
  </PopoverPopup>
</Popover>
```

The map controls are the library's, in two groups that the demo places itself:

```tsx
<MapControls placement="none" aria-label="Map options" className="mac-maps-options">
  <Menu>
    <MenuTrigger render={<MapButton chevron>Show</MapButton>} />
    <MenuPopup align="start">
      <MenuCheckboxItem checked={shown.traffic} onCheckedChange={setTraffic}>Traffic</MenuCheckboxItem>
      {/* 3D Map, Scale */}
    </MenuPopup>
  </Menu>
</MapControls>
<MapControls placement="none" orientation="vertical" aria-label="Map controls" className="mac-maps-controls">
  <MapCompass pitched={pitched} onPitchedChange={setPitched} />
  <MapZoomControl onZoomIn={zoomIn} onZoomOut={zoomOut}
                  zoomInDisabled={view.zoom >= MAX_ZOOM} zoomOutDisabled={view.zoom <= MIN_ZOOM} />
</MapControls>
```

**`maps.css`** gives the toolbar's items the capture's widths, lays the map out with what
lies over it, and sets the card's rows where the captures have them. **`maps.tokens.css`**
holds the colours no shared token has: the measured ones (the land, the card's headings and
links, the temperature, the air quality capsule and the weather's plate) and the demo's own
for the placeholders; and one length, the point by which each map type is wider in Dark Aqua.

**`app.ts`** is the descriptor the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) reads.

## Hosting the window

`Maps` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and the title
bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

<Example name="hosted" />

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `places` | `MapsPlace[]` | The places the search finds; the demo's five by default. |
| `defaultQuery` | `string` | The text in the search field at first; `"Apple Park"`, as in the Aqua capture. |
| `defaultPlaceId` | `string \| null` | The place with the pin at first; `"apple-park"`. |
| `defaultCardOpen` | `boolean` | Whether that place's card is open at first; `false`, because the card takes the keyboard and lies over the page. |
| `defaultMapType` | `"map" \| "transit" \| "satellite"` | The map type selected at first; `"map"`. |
| `defaultCenter` | `{ x, y }` | The point of the abstract map at the middle of the first tab; the capture's. |
| `weather` | `{ temperature, airQuality } \| null` | The weather in the map's corner; 72° and AQI 49, as in the Aqua capture. `null` shows none, as in the Dark Aqua one. |
| `onPlaceChange` | `(place: MapsPlace \| null) => void` | Called with the place that has the pin when it changes: another place, none, or the same place given other details through `places`. |
| `onShare` | `(service: string, place: MapsPlace \| null) => void` | Called when a service of a Share menu is chosen. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1000 by 690 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `mapsApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (1000 by 690), `minSize` (640 by 320), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. Its `component` is `DesktopMaps`
(`maps-desktop.tsx`), which renders `Maps` in every build but one, described next.

## On the desktop's Ferrite application

When the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves the desktop
(`ferrite-apps/desktop`), `DesktopMaps` shows a working Maps instead of the demo. Nothing of
it runs on this documentation site, which asks no server anything.

- **The map** is OpenStreetMap's standard map (`maps-tiles.tsx`, `maps-geo.ts`): the
  browser loads the tiles in view and no others, drag moves it, the wheel, a double-click,
  the keyboard and the zoom pair zoom it from level 2 to 19, the context menu drops a pin,
  and "© OpenStreetMap contributors" links to the licence, as the tiles' usage policy asks.
- **Search** runs on Return, never as you type. The `maps` module asks OpenStreetMap's
  Nominatim, at most once a second for the whole application, and keeps each answer for a
  day. One place found gets its pin and its card, with the address, the phone, the website,
  Wikipedia and the coordinates OpenStreetMap knows. Several get a pin each, framed on the map,
  and a list under the field whose rows open their cards. While you type, the field suggests
  the person's own Favorites, Guides and Recents; nothing typed leaves the window for it.
- **Directions** run from the toolbar, ⌘R, a card's button and the context menus: a panel with
  the two ends, Drive or Walk (Transit stays dimmed: the routers have none), the time, the
  length and the steps, and the route's line over the map. The module asks the FOSSGIS OSRM
  routers (`https://routing.openstreetmap.de/`) behind a declared egress, at most once a second
  for the whole application, and keeps each 200 answer for a day. Only the router's `NoRoute`
  and `NoSegment` codes read as "no journey" (the routers send those under HTTP 400; the module
  answers that with its own error and keeps nothing); an `Ok` answer with no routes fails rather
  than reads as one; an answer under any other status is never a route and is never cached; a
  route of more than 1,000 steps fails rather than loses some; and a change
  to the journey forgets any question on its way. The panel shows the attribution and the
  "fix the map" link the routers' usage policy asks for.
- **Places** opens Favorites, Recents and Guides, which the module keeps for the person under
  the revision rule of the desktop's documents. The card's heart, its Add to Guide menu and
  the sidebar's New Guide, Remove and Clear Recents change them; a row's context menu shows it,
  routes to it, copies its link, renames a guide or removes it.
- **Share** hands the place's link on openstreetmap.org to Mail, Messages, Notes or Reminders,
  or copies it.
- **Context menus:** the map's drops a pin, routes to or from the point, and reports an issue on
  OpenStreetMap; a pin's shows its card, routes, adds it to Favorites and copies its link.
- **The menu bar** carries the commands (`maps-menus.tsx`): File > New Window, Share, Export as
  PDF and Print (a picture of the map as the window shows it, with its pins and route); Edit >
  Find, Drop Pin and Remove Pin; View > Map, Show Scale, Zoom In and Out, Go to Current Location
  and Directions, with their keys. What the served window does not have stays in the menus,
  dimmed, saying why.
- **Kept for the person:** the last map view, the way the map is turned, the open panel and the
  scale, for the next window. Each window has its own map; all of them show the person's one set
  of places.
- **The compass:** dragged round, it turns the map, pins and route with it; the arrow keys turn it
  by 15° while it has the keyboard; its ring faces north. The tiles turn with the map, so their
  labels do too.

The served window has no tabs (New Window instead), no Transit or Satellite map
(OpenStreetMap has neither) and no 3D. Its plate in the map's top corner shows the temperature at
the map's middle once the map is zoomed in far enough for one temperature to mean something, from
Open-Meteo through the desktop's server, with the condition, source and time in its tooltip; `--`
and the reason when the source is down. It has no air quality capsule, which Open-Meteo serves
from a host this application does not declare. The free services are for light use: a busy deployment
needs a tile, geocoding and routing provider of its own, such as MapTiler or Stadia Maps, with a
key.

## Accessibility

- The window has no title, so it is a `region` named "Maps" by `aria-label`.
- The toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys move between its items. Directions is a toggle
  button whose `aria-pressed` says whether the route is drawn; it is marked unavailable
  (`aria-disabled`) and stays reachable while no place has the pin. The location button
  shows a glyph only and is named "Show Your Current Location"; Share is named "Share".
- The search field is a `searchbox` named by its placeholder, "Search for a place or
  address"; its clear button is named "Clear".
- The map types are a group named "Map type" of three toggle buttons.
- The tabs are a `tablist` named "Tabs". A tab's close button is named after it ("Close
  Manhattan — Jersey City"), and Delete closes the tab that has the keyboard.
- The map is an image whose text alternative says that it is a placeholder, and names the
  map type, the part of the map and the zoom, so a change of any of them can be read.
- The pin is a button named after its place. The card is a `dialog` named by its title; it
  takes the keyboard when it opens and returns it to the pin when it closes. Its headings
  are headings, its links are links that open a new tab, and Call is a disabled button.
- The note of a search ("No Results Found") is a `status`, so it is announced.
- The weather is a group named "Weather"; the two groups of map controls are named "Map
  options" and "Map controls", and their buttons "Show", "Face north", "3D", "Zoom out" and
  "Zoom in". A zoom button at its limit stays reachable.
- The placeholder drawings are decorative; the elements around them name what they stand for.

### Placeholders and masks

reproduced or imitated: the owner of the project decided that these are abstract
placeholders, flat fills and simple shapes that are not fitted to the pictures in the
screenshots. Every one of them is masked in every comparison that contains it, so what is
scored is the chrome and the layout around them.

### Known differences

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices: most of the two whole-window scores.
- **The card's arrow.** Maps' card is a MapKit callout whose arrow is 22 pt wide and 11 pt
  deep; the library's popover has the arrow of an `NSPopover`, 29 by 13 pt. The card's body
  is where the captures have it, so the demo's arrow reaches 2 pt further.
- **The bars' colours.** The library's title bar and toolbar bezels are those measured in
  Finder and Preview; Maps' Dark Aqua toolbar is a few levels lighter, which is the
  mean difference of `toolbar-dark`. The side edges of the Aqua bezels and the cross of the
  search field's clear button differ by a hairline.
- **The Show pull-down's chevron** stands about a point further left than in the captures.
- **The last row of the card** is greyer in the captures than its other headings, as if it
  were fading out at the card's edge; here it has the colour of the others.
- **The tab bar's layout.** Maps divides the whole bar among its tabs and takes the new-tab
  cell from the last one; the library divides what the cell leaves. The demo gives every tab
  but the last 25 pt more to start from, which reproduces the capture.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the first tab's title, which the card hides; every
  place but Apple Park and the cards of those places; the transit and satellite views,
  traffic, the 3D map, the scale, the route and the current location; the Show and Share
  menus; a search that finds nothing; the weather in Dark Aqua; the zoom levels; the minimum
  size; and the window when it is not key.
