<Example name="basic" />

Click **Launchpad**, the second tile of the Dock. Type to search, turn the page with the dots
or the arrow keys, open the folder Other, and choose an application: Launchpad closes and the
desktop says which one was chosen. Escape, a click on the background, or the Launchpad tile
again closes it without choosing. Nothing is launched: the applications are names.

Every application icon and the desktop picture are Apple's artwork, so the demo shows
placeholders: a plain rounded square in the tint of the application's category with its
initial, and an abstract gradient for the picture. Neither is a drawing of what it replaces.

## What it shows

- The stage: a `div` that stands for the screen. It is as wide as the page allows and keeps
  the 1680 by 1050 proportion of the captured screen down to 672 pt wide; the grid, the
  icons and the Dock's tiles shrink with it. Narrower than that the stage stops shrinking in
  height and stays 420 pt tall, so that the grid and the Dock keep room to be used. Launchpad
  is rendered into the stage and covers it, not the page.
- A [Desktop](/components/desktop) with the abstract picture, and on it the line that names
  the application that was chosen.
- A [Dock](/components/dock) with the 20 tiles of the captures as placeholders: Finder with
  its running dot, Launchpad, sixteen applications, the separator, Downloads and the Trash.
  It stays in front of Launchpad, as in Catalina, and a click on one of its applications
  while Launchpad shows closes Launchpad and reports that application.
- [Launchpad](/components/launchpad): the overlay with its scrim and blur, the search field,
  the 7 by 5 grid of `LaunchpadItem`s and the page dots, a
  [Page Control](/components/page-control). Page 1 holds the 35 entries of the captures in
  their order; the second page holds six more names of the demo's choosing, because the
  captures show that a second page exists and not what is on it.
- A [Launchpad Folder](/components/launchpad-folder): Other, the 28th entry, with the 17
  applications of the Launchpad-Folder captures. Its tile shows the first nine placeholders,
  small. While it is open the search field, the grid, the page dots and the Dock are gone,
  as in the captures.

| Action | Where | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show or hide | the Dock's Launchpad tile | Launchpad fades in over the stage, or out. |
| Close | Escape, or a click between the icons | Launchpad goes; nothing is chosen. |
| Search | type anywhere, or click the field | Every application whose name holds the text is shown on one page, those in the folder too; the dots go. Return chooses the first. The field is empty the next time Launchpad shows. |
| Turn the page | a page dot; Left and Right Arrow | The arrows move among the applications first and turn the page from the last or first one of a page, and from the overlay itself. An arrow held with Command, Option, Control or Shift does not turn the page. Launchpad remembers the page. |
| Open the folder | click Other, or Return on it | The panel of its applications; Escape or a click beside it closes the folder and leaves Launchpad. A `defaultFolder` that is not a folder on the page shown opens nothing. |
| Choose | click an application, or Return on it | Launchpad (and its folder) closes; `onLaunch` is called and the desktop names the application. |

## Source walkthrough

**`launchpad-data.ts`** is the content: `pageOne`, `otherFolder`, `pageTwo` and
`defaultEntries`, each entry an application `{ id, name, category }` or a folder
`{ id, name, apps }`, and the Dock's sixteen applications. The category only chooses the
placeholder's tint.

**`launchpad.tsx`** is `CatalinaLaunchpad`. The stage is state, because Launchpad is rendered
into it, and the entries become the library's elements directly, so that Launchpad's own
search and paging work on them:

```tsx
<div ref={setStage} className="mac-launchpad-demo">
  <Desktop picture={picture}>…</Desktop>
  <Dock>…</Dock>
  <Launchpad
    open={open}
    onOpenChange={onOverlayOpenChange}
    container={stage}
    page={page}
    onPageChange={setPage}
    search={search}
    onSearchChange={setSearch}
    onKeyDown={turnPage}
  >
    {shown.map((entry) =>
      isGroup(entry) ? (
        <LaunchpadFolder
          key={entry.id}
          label={entry.name}
          open={folderOpen && folder === entry.id}
          onOpenChange={(next) => setFolder(next ? entry.id : null)}
        >
          {entry.apps.map(item)}
        </LaunchpadFolder>
      ) : (
        item(entry)
      ),
    )}
  </Launchpad>
</div>
```

Three things are the demo's own. A search is given every application, the folders' included
(`shown` is `applicationsOf(entries)` while the field holds text), so that the library's
filter finds an application inside a folder, as Catalina does. `turnPage` is Launchpad's
`onKeyDown`: the library's items leave an arrow key that leads nowhere to the grid's owner,
and the demo turns the page with it. And the folder is controlled, so that the stage knows
when to take the Dock away; `folderOpen` holds only while the folder's id is a folder on the
page shown. `onOverlayOpenChange` notes when the press that closed Launchpad was on the
Dock's Launchpad tile, so that the click that follows hides Launchpad and does not show it
again: a browser closes the overlay on that press before the tile hears of it.

**`launchpad.css`** sizes the stage, keeps the Dock above Launchpad's layer and draws the
placeholders; **`launchpad.tokens.css`** holds the colours of the status line and of a
placeholder's initial. Nothing in them restyles a library component.

## Using the demo

`CatalinaLaunchpad` renders the stage as its root and passes every other prop to that `div`.

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entries` | `LaunchpadEntry[]` | What Launchpad holds; the captured page and the second page by default. |
| `open`, `defaultOpen`, `onOpenChange` | `boolean`, `boolean`, `(open) => void` | Whether Launchpad shows, controlled or not; hidden at first by default. |
| `defaultPage` | `number` | The page shown at first, from 0. |
| `defaultSearch` | `string` | The text in the search field at first. |
| `defaultFolder` | `string` | The id of the folder that is open at first, while Launchpad shows; only a folder on the page shown opens. |
| `onLaunch` | `(app: LaunchpadApp) => void` | Called with the application chosen in the grid, in a folder or in the Dock. |
| `picture` | `ReactNode` | The desktop picture, in place of the abstract one. |
| `overlayProps` | `{ className?, style? }` | For the library's Launchpad overlay; the comparison fixtures use it. |
| the rest | `ComponentProps<"div">` | `style`, `className`, `ref` and so on, on the stage. |

## On the desktop

The demo has no `app.ts`, so the [Desktop demo](/demos/desktop) does not list it, and that is
deliberate. The desktop's contract for a hosted application is a component whose root is a
window of the library, placed and sized by the window manager; its test of every descriptor
asks the root for `data-window-state`. Launchpad is not a window. It is an overlay that
covers the whole desktop under the Dock and hides the menu bar, and wrapping it in a window to
satisfy the contract would show something Catalina does not have.

The Desktop demo has a Launchpad of its own instead: the Launchpad tile of its Dock shows the
library's [Launchpad](/components/launchpad) over its windows, filled with the applications
of that desktop, and choosing one opens it there. This demo stays the page that reproduces
the Launchpad captures: their grid, their folder and their Dock.

## Accessibility

- The stage's desktop is a group named "Desktop" and the Dock a `toolbar` named "Dock" with
  one tab stop; its tiles are buttons named after what they stand for, and Finder is
  described as running. The line that names the chosen application is a `status`.
- Launchpad is a `dialog` named "Launchpad" that holds the keyboard focus while it shows and
  returns it to where it was when it goes. The search field is a `searchbox`
  named "Search", each application a `button` named by its name, and the dots a group named
  "Pages" whose buttons are named "Page 1 of 2" and "Page 2 of 2".
- Down Arrow goes from the overlay or the field into the grid, the arrow keys move among the
  applications, Return or Space chooses, Escape closes. Left and Right Arrow turn the page
  where no application takes them. In the search field they move the insertion point.
- The folder is a button that says it opens a dialog; the open folder is a `dialog` named
  "Other" with that name as its heading. Escape closes the folder first and Launchpad second.
  Opened from the keyboard, or open at first, its first application has the focus and shows
  the library's focus ring.
- While Launchpad shows, the library hides everything outside it from assistive technology
  and keeps the Tab key inside it, so the Dock, which Catalina leaves usable, can then be
  reached with the pointer only. While a folder is open the Dock is `inert` and invisible.
- The placeholders are decorative and hidden from assistive technology.

## Fidelity

Launchpad draws is translucent over it. The demo's picture is an abstract gradient, so the
whole screen cannot be compared in colour: a score of it would measure the picture.

### What is and is not compared

Compared with the captures: the search field's place, size, fill and border; the page dots;
the folder's tile where the grid puts its 28th entry; where the names of the first and last
entries stand; two opposite corners of the open folder's panel; and the folder's name above
the panel's top edge. Checked by computed colours: the rest of the grid's geometry, the
Dock's place, the folder's panel and its grid.

Not compared, and why:

- **The application icons and the desktop picture** are Apple's artwork. The placeholders
  and the abstract picture are not meant to look like them.
- **The backdrop** in every crop is painted by the fixture from the capture's own pixels, so
  no score says anything about how the demo's picture looks through Launchpad. The library's
  scrim over a known backdrop is checked by `check-grid-*` (and by the library's own
  `scrim-*`).
- **The Dock over Launchpad** is checked for its place only. Its tiles are placeholders, and
  its material over the blurred picture is the library's (compared on the Dock's own page
  with the Desktop captures).
- **The second page**, a search, keyboard focus and the stage at any other size are in no
  screenshot: provisional.

What differs, and why:

- **The names stand about 1.5 pt too high.** In the captures a name's capitals start at
  193.5 pt in the first row (858 pt in the fifth); the library's `LaunchpadItem` sets them
  from 192 pt (856 pt). This is the library's distance between icon and name, which the demo
  does not restyle, and it is nearly all of the `grid-label-*` scores together with the
  fallback font.
- **Text** is set in the fallback font off Apple devices: the names, the placeholder and the
  folder's title.
- **The folder's title in Aqua** carries a mean Δ of about 4 with the name masked: the
  capture's backdrop turns from blue to brown along the crop's top, and the folder's blur,
  applied to the fixture's measured colours a second time, flattens the turn.
- **The folder's first application looks focused** when the folder is open at first or opened
  from the keyboard: the library focuses it. The captures were opened with the pointer and
  show no focus. It lies outside every crop.
- **Pages are not swiped.** Catalina turns pages with a swipe and with Command-Arrow; the demo
  uses the dots and the plain arrow keys, since a browser keeps Command-Arrow for history.
