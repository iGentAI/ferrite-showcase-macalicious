<Example name="basic" />

Press Play, or the space bar with the window focused: the time code runs and the playhead
moves. Leave the pointer still for three seconds and the title bar and the controller fade
until it moves again. Drag the timeline, step the speed with the rewind and fast-forward
buttons, change the volume, open the Share menu, or float the picture. No media is included:
the transport is a model driven by a timer, nothing is heard, and the picture is one original
generated still of a river valley.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with `data-appearance="dark"`: a movie window is dark in Aqua
  and in Dark Aqua alike, and the attribute gives everything inside its Dark Aqua look. The
  picture fills the frame; the bars lie over it.
- A [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) in its plain 22 pt form and its translucent material
  (`material="translucent"`), with the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), a
  stand-in proxy icon in its `icon` slot and the movie's name. The demo's stylesheet only lays
  it over the top of the picture and fades it.
- The controller: a [HUD Window](/components/hud-window) in its `controller` variant, 440 by
  75 pt, holding the library's `HudWindowButton`s (mute, full volume, rewind, play or pause,
  fast-forward, AirPlay, Share, Picture in Picture), two `HudWindowSlider`s (the `knob` for the
  volume, the `scrubber` for the timeline) and two `HudWindowText`s for the time codes. The
  demo only places them where the captures have them and draws the glyphs; the Share glyph is
  the library's `share` [System Image](/components/system-image).
- Share is a [Share Menu](/components/share-menu) whose trigger is the controller's button;
  AirPlay opens a [Menu](/components/menu) of two radio [Menu Items](/components/menu-item),
  this computer and an invented device. Both menus follow the page's appearance, as
  Catalina's do.
- Picture in Picture moves the picture into the library's
  [Picture in Picture](/components/picture-in-picture) window, whose play button is the
  movie's. It floats inside the QuickTime window, because the demo has no screen to put it on.
- The placeholder picture is held by an [Image View](/components/image-view), which names it for
  assistive technology; a small HUD panel shows the speed while the movie scans.

The transport is a pure model, `quicktime-player-clock.ts`: a position in frames, a length, a
frame rate, and a rate. Play runs at normal speed and stops at the end; Play at the end starts
over. Fast-forward and rewind go to 2x and with every further press to 5x, 10x, 30x and 60x,
then round to 2x again; a rewind stops at the start. While the movie scans, the middle button
is Play and returns to normal speed. The component's timer tells the model how much real time
has passed, so playback keeps time when a page's timers are slowed down.

| Key | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Space | anywhere in the window, except on a button | Play or pause |
| Left, Right | the window itself focused | Step one frame back or forward, and pause |
| Left, Right, Page Up, Page Down, Home, End | the timeline | One frame, ten seconds, the start and the end |
| Left, Right, Page Up, Page Down, Home, End | the volume slider | One and ten hundredths, silent and full |

## Playing a movie of your own

Give `src` the address of a video the browser can play (the `blob:` address of a file, for
one) and the window plays it in a `<video>` of its own: Play and Pause, the timeline, the frame
steps, the scan speeds (fast-forward as the movie's playback rate, rewind by moving it back),
the volume and Mute act on the movie, and its own length replaces `duration`. Picture in
Picture floats the movie in the browser's own window where the browser has one (Chromium,
Safari), and otherwise in the floating window inside this one, which shows a live copy of the
picture. AirPlay opens the browser's own picker of screens (the Remote Playback API in
Chromium, the AirPlay picker in Safari) instead of the invented device, and is disabled where
the browser has neither; while the movie plays on another screen the window says so. A double
click on the picture, or Control-Command-F, fills the screen. A movie the
browser cannot decode is reported in the window. Another `src` given while a movie plays starts
at its beginning and, once it has loaded, goes on as the one it replaced did: playing, at the
same forward speed (normal speed after a rewind); a paused movie's replacement waits for Play.
`onTimeChange` hears the place, the length and
whether it plays.

The [Desktop](/demos/desktop) opens QuickTime Player as `QuickTimePlayerApp`
(`quicktime-player-app.tsx`): an Open window, a titled window around the browser of the
[Open Panel](/components/open-panel) (`OpenPanelBrowser`), that lists the person's
Recents and the folders of their drive, as a list, icons, columns or a gallery, with "From This Computer…", which uploads the
chosen movie into Movies; then the movie window, which plays the file as read back from the
drive. Several movies chosen open in a window each, and View > Play All Movies plays them all. The
window's volume and the movie it shows are kept with the person's settings, so a reload brings them
back. The title opens a menu of the file's commands and the proxy icon drags the file; the controller
moves with a drag on its background; the Movie Inspector works out the format, data rate and frame rate
from the file and the playing picture, and Window > Show Export Progress lists exports with a Stop. Command-O in the movie window returns to the Open window. File > New Movie Recording, New
Audio Recording and New Screen Recording (`quicktime-player-recording.tsx`) record the camera, the
microphone or a screen the browser's picker gives with `MediaRecorder`; a take goes into Movies with
a dated name when it stops, or when its window closes, then opens. The Edit menu's Trim, Rotate, Flip
and Remove commands, and Export As, make a new movie (`quicktime-player-replay.ts`). Where the browser
has WebCodecs encoders for WebM (`quicktime-player-encode.ts`), each frame is taken from its exact place
and the sound from the decoded file, at 30 frames a second, into a WebM written here
(`quicktime-player-webm.ts`) that knows its length and can seek. Elsewhere the movie replays through a
canvas and `MediaRecorder` in real time, in the browser's recording format. Either way the result is a
generation worse than its source. Undo and Redo go between the replayed movies,
and Save writes the result into the movie's file, whose earlier bytes the drive keeps as a version for File > Revert To; a file whose extension says another format than the recording stays as it is, and Save As writes a new one. View > Show Clips (`quicktime-player-clips.tsx`)
shows the movie's clips with a still of each, and Edit > Split Clip splits one at the playhead; a split
changes no picture, so it is no replay, and the window does not call the movie edited or keep the split
once it closes. Edit > Cut, Copy, Paste and Delete act on the clips chosen in the strip, by a click, Command-click or Shift-click; Select All chooses every clip and opens the strip, and Copy then takes the whole movie; in the Open panel, Undo to Select All act on the search field; a drag in the strip
puts a clip in another place, a drag out of it gives the clip as a file to what takes it, a movie dropped on it joins it, and Edit > Add Clip to End… and Insert Clip After Selection… join another movie: each replays the clips in
their new order, so Play All Movies, Save and Export As all have them. A clip of another shape shows whole on
black. A copied clip stays in the page, since a browser takes no movie onto the system's clipboard. View >
Float on Top keeps the window over other windows; File > Move To… puts its file in another folder of the
drive. In full screen, where the screen has its own shape, `scaling` lays the picture out: View >
Fit to Screen keeps it whole, Fill Screen crops it to cover the screen, and Panoramic draws it on a canvas,
its centre in true shape and stretched more towards the ends (`panoramicStrips`). On the desktop that the Ferrite
application serves, the drive is the person's own (`demos/desktop/desktop-files.ts`) and Recents
and the place in each movie are kept by the application (`/api/quicktime-player/recents`), so a
movie opens where it was left. Where a saved movie is split into clips is kept with it
(`/api/quicktime-player/clips/<file>`) and comes back when it opens: a
split marks the movie edited, and rotating, flipping and trimming keep the splits. Elsewhere there is no drive: the chosen file plays in the page
only. A host can pass its own `drive` (the `QuickTimeDrive` interface of
`quicktime-player-drive.ts`).

## Hosting the window

`QuickTimePlayer` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to
it, so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
the title bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { quicktimePlayerApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={quicktimePlayerApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...quicktimePlayerApp.defaultSize }}
  minWidth={quicktimePlayerApp.minSize.width}
  minHeight={quicktimePlayerApp.minSize.height}
  render={(props) => <quicktimePlayerApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `title` | `string` | The movie's name in the title bar; it names the window. Default: "Untitled Movie"; the capture's title is a third party's and is not repeated. |
| `duration` | `number \| string` | The length, in frames or as a time code; `"00:12:49:22"` by default. Read when the window opens. |
| `defaultPosition` | `number \| string` | Where the playhead starts, in frames or as a time code; 0 by default. |
| `defaultPlaying` | `boolean` | Play as soon as the window opens. |
| `defaultVolume` | `number` | The volume at first, 0 to 1; 1 by default. |
| `frameRate` | `number` | Frames in a second, a whole number: the timebase of the time codes; 30 by default. |
| `poster` | `ReactNode` | A still shown instead of the placeholder picture. |
| `children` | `ReactNode` | The movie itself, shown instead of the placeholder and the poster: your own `<video>`, for example. The demo does not drive it. |
| `src` | `string` | A movie the window plays itself, in its own `<video>` (above). |
| `onTimeChange` | `(seconds, duration, playing) => void` | With `src`: the movie's place and length in seconds, and whether it plays. |
| `onPlayingChange`, `onSeek`, `onVolumeLevelChange` | functions | Called with the new state, the frame sought and the volume, so that a movie of your own can follow the controller. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1470 by 827 by default, as in the light capture. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer and key events and so on. |

Two custom properties, set on the window, move the controller, which QuickTime Player lets
its user drag: `--mac-quicktime-player-controller-bottom`, its distance from the window's
bottom edge (15.3% of the window's height by default), and
`--mac-quicktime-player-controller-shift`, its distance to the right of the centre (0).

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `quicktimePlayerApp` and as `app`:
`id`, `name`, `component` (`QuickTimePlayerApp`, above), `defaultSize` (1470 by 827),
`minSize` (480 by 270, the 440 pt controller with 20 pt beside it), `resizable`,
`sizesItself` (the window asks for the Open panel's frame, 760 by 460, then a movie's) and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by the movie's name. It can take the focus from a click on
  the picture or from a script, without being a tab stop, so that the space bar and the arrow
  keys work on the window as they do in QuickTime Player.
- The controller is a `group` named "Playback controls". Every control in it is a tab stop,
  in reading order: Mute, Volume, Full Volume, Rewind, Play or Pause, Fast Forward, AirPlay,
  Share, Picture in Picture, Timeline. The buttons show only a glyph and are named with
  `aria-label`; Mute and Picture in Picture are toggles and say so with `aria-pressed`. With a
  movie of your own, an AirPlay button the browser cannot serve stays a tab stop and is
  announced as disabled.
- The middle button is named for what it will do: "Play" while the movie is paused or
  scanning, "Pause" while it plays at normal speed.
- Both sliders hold a native range input. The timeline's value is read as time codes,
  "00:03:16:12 of 00:12:49:22". The running time is not announced as it changes: a live
  region that spoke every second would be noise.
- The speed of a rewind or fast-forward is announced by a polite `status` ("Fast forward
  5×"); the badge that shows it is hidden from assistive technology.
- The bars fade only from a playing movie that nothing has touched for three seconds. They
  stay while one of their controls shows the keyboard focus, they come back with any key or
  pointer movement, and while hidden they take no press, so a first tap shows them instead of
  activating a control nobody can see. The fade is an opacity change of a quarter of a
  second, and there is none with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The placeholder picture is an image whose text alternative says what it is: an abstract
  placeholder for a movie frame. While the picture floats or plays elsewhere, a line of text
  says where it went.
- The controller's glyphs add light to what is behind them, so their contrast depends on the
  picture, as noted on the [HUD Window](/components/hud-window) page; with
  `prefers-reduced-transparency` the controller and the title bar are opaque.

## Fidelity

**The picture is an original generated still.** The two QuickTime captures show a third
party's movie. The toolkit reproduces Catalina's chrome, not that movie, so the demo does not
ship it and does not imitate it: the picture is a misty river valley at golden hour, and no
outline and no colour of it comes from the captures. For the same reason the
picture is left out of the comparisons with the captures, which are about the chrome only.

`quicktime-player.visual.json` has an example for each capture, since they show the same dark
window at two sizes and at two moments: `visual-window` (Aqua, 1470 by 827 pt, 00:03:16:12)
and `visual-window-dark` (Dark Aqua, 1346 by 757 pt, 00:04:40:02). What is compared with a
capture, what is masked, and what is held another way:

- **Not compared: the window as a whole.** Nearly all of the window is the picture, and the
  harness lets a comparison leave at most 75% of its crop out of the score, so the picture
  cannot be masked out of a crop of the whole window. Crops of the chrome are compared
  instead.
- **Compared, with the bar's fill masked: the window controls.** `window-controls-*` is the
  left 68 by 22 pt of the title bar: the three window controls at x 8, 28 and 48 pt, y 5 pt,
  with the bar's two highlight rows above them and its two bottom rows below. The bar is
  translucent, so its fill is partly the picture: everything between the edge rows is masked
  except the three 12 pt squares that hold the controls (62% of the crop). The corners of
  those squares, which are fill, and the highlight rows, which let about a seventh of the
  picture through, stay in the score.
- **Compared, nothing masked: the bar's bottom rows.** `title-bar-bottom-*` is the last point
  of the bar across the whole window. Both rows are opaque in the demo, so this score does
  not depend on the picture.
- **Held by expected pixels, not compared: the bar's fill and highlight rows, and the
  controller.** They are translucent over the picture, so in a capture they are partly the
  movie. With a picture behind them the title bar's strip mismatches the Aqua capture
  by 17% at 2x, its highlight rows alone by 13%, and the crop of the controller by 96%:
  scores of the placeholder against a movie, which say nothing of the chrome, so those
  comparisons are not kept. Instead `title-bar-material-*` and `controller-geometry-*` check
  pixels against colours computed from the tokens. That needs known colours behind them, so
  the two visual examples pass a flat backdrop as the `poster` instead of the generated still:
  a plain gradient (`#30353d` to `#5d6672`) behind the bar and one flat grey, `#707070`, behind
  the controller. No pixel of the generated still is compared.
- **In no comparison:** the title, its proxy icon and the two time codes, which are text in
  the fallback font and artwork of the demo's own.

Scores from `npm run visual -- quicktime-player/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:
the share of pixels that differ by more than the threshold, 0.05 in all four, and the mean
colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Scores | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-controls-light` | the window controls and the bar's edge rows beside them, fill masked | 10.39% | 4.73 | 2.73% | 3.93 |
| `title-bar-bottom-light` | the bar's two bottom rows, the whole width | 0.00% | 3.52 | 7.47% | 3.37 |
| `window-controls-dark` | as `window-controls-light`, Dark Aqua capture | 5.46% | 3.39 | 1.50% | 2.62 |
| `title-bar-bottom-dark` | as `title-bar-bottom-light`, Dark Aqua capture | 0.00% | 2.31 | 5.74% | 2.25 |

In `title-bar-bottom-*` the separator matches, `#000000`. The row above it is the library's
opaque `#2a2a2a`, where the capture's row lets the movie through (`#2a2a2b` to `#404243` in
the Aqua capture): the mismatch at 2x is that row where the movie behind it is light.

| Check | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `title-bar-material-light`, `title-bar-material-dark` | The bar's 22 pt across the window; its fill near the top and near the bottom, `#43474b` and `#3a3d41`, which is the measured tint over what the material's filter makes of the placeholder's gradient; at 2x its two highlight rows, `#888a8c` and `#797b7e`, its shadow row and its separator. |
| `controller-geometry-light`, `controller-geometry-dark` | The controller's 440 by 75 pt at the place measured in each capture (x 515.5 to 955.5 and y 624.5 to 699.5 pt in the Aqua one, x 453 to 893 and y 566.5 to 641.5 pt in the Dark Aqua one), to half a point at 2x and to a point at 1x: the panel's computed colour inside each of its four edges and the band's outside. Then seven glyphs (all but Share, a thin outline), the volume knob at its maximum, and the timeline's played part, its bar and the rest, each at its measured place in its computed colour. |

These checks hold the layout and the demo's use of the tokens. They do not show that the
materials look like Catalina's over a movie, and no comparison in the library does: the
[HUD Window](/components/hud-window) page checks the controller over a flat grey too, for the
same reason, and compares only its opaque volume knob with the captures.

Measured in the captures and reproduced: the two frames; the 22 pt title bar over the
picture, whose two highlight rows and last row are the Dark Aqua title bar's; its fill, which
follows the picture with a slope of 0.21 at the top and 0.18 at the bottom over a grey of
`#4b4d4e` falling to `#3b3c3e`; the window controls and the title block with its 11 by
14.5 pt icon; the controller's 440 by 75 pt, its place (centred, its bottom edge 127.5 of
827 pt and 115.5 of 757 pt above the window's, 15.3% by default), and the places of its nine
glyphs, two sliders and two time codes, which are the [HUD Window](/components/hud-window)
page's measurements.

What differs, and why:

- **The title bar's fill** is the library's translucent title bar material
  ([Title Bar](/components/title-bar), `material="translucent"`): one value for both captures,
  the mean of the tints measured in the two, which are 7 levels apart; the bar's row above the
  last is the library's opaque `#2a2a2a`, where the captures' row lets the movie through.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices; its digits are wider than San
  Francisco's, so the elapsed time reaches the start of the timeline.
- **Glyphs and the proxy icon** are original drawings at the measured sizes.
- **The timeline's bar** stands where the time puts it, 25.5% and 36.4% along its travel,
  which is 0.4 and 0.7 pt left of the captures' and between two device pixels.
- **Vibrancy.** The controller's material is the library's: a tint over a blur of the page
  behind it, not AppKit's blend of the movie, and provisional as on the HUD Window page.
- **The fade.** A HUD cannot fade without losing its material: while the wrapper around it
  is less than opaque, the browser gives the panel nothing to blur and its glyphs nothing to
  add their light to, so for the quarter of a second of the fade the panel is a plain tint
  with dimmer glyphs. The title bar fades as it is.

Not in a screenshot (provisional): the order and the wrap of the scanning speeds and the
middle button's return to normal speed, the three seconds before the bars fade, the speed
badge, the bars of a window that is not key, the contents of the Share and AirPlay menus and
the line shown while the picture is elsewhere.

Not built: sound and real media (the volume is a number, and `children` are shown, not
driven), dragging the controller (two custom properties place it), full screen, the menu
bar's commands (trim, export, recording), and QuickTime Player's J, K and L keys.
