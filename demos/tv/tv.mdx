<Example name="basic" />

Choose a section in the bar, move the pointer over a shelf and turn its page with the
chevrons, open a title and add it to the Library, or search for "comedy". The catalogue is a
dozen titles invented for the demo, and every picture is original generated key art for its
invented title, with no title text on it. Nothing is played or streamed.

## YouTube on the desktop

The [Desktop](/demos/desktop) opens TV as `TvYouTube` (`tv-youtube.tsx`), the same window
built on the same frame (`TvFrame`), which plays YouTube videos for real:

- Paste a YouTube link (`youtube.com/watch?v=`, `youtu.be/`, Shorts, embeds) into the search
  field: its card appears, and Return plays it. On the desktop that the Ferrite application
  serves, the card shows YouTube's own title and channel, which the application asks of
  YouTube's oEmbed endpoint.
- Movies and Kids hold open films that the Blender Foundation publishes on YouTube under
  Creative Commons licences; Watch Now has Up Next, Continue Watching, those films as its hero,
  and a hint about pasting links; TV Shows holds open series (Caminandes, Pepper & Carrot,
  Morevna) by shelf in episode order, then what the person added, and an episode that ends goes
  on to the next one; Library holds Watch Later and History, with Clear History.
- A video plays in YouTube's official embedded player (the IFrame Player API, from
  `youtube-nocookie.com`) with YouTube's own controls off and TV's controller under it: a
  [HUD Window](/components/hud-window) with play and pause, back and forward ten seconds, a
  timeline, mute, volume, Watch Later and full screen. Nothing of TV's lies over YouTube's
  player, so its own screens (consent, sign-in, its links) stay usable. With the player
  focused, Space plays and pauses and the arrows skip ten seconds. Done returns to the video's
  page, where Resume starts where it was left.
- When YouTube will not play a video (its owner does not allow it in other apps, it is
  private, or YouTube refuses the network it is asked from), the window says so in its own
  words and offers Watch on YouTube.
- Searching words searches the videos the window knows; YouTube itself is searched only when
  the desktop has a YouTube Data API key, and otherwise the window says so.
- Home Videos are files of the person's drive. File > Import… chooses one, a drag from a Finder
  window adds one, and Finder's Open With plays one (QuickTime Player stays a movie's default). A
  home video is read from the drive and played in the page under the same controller, and it can
  float in Picture in Picture. The Library keeps the file's id and the place; the bytes stay in
  the drive, and a file moved to the Trash leaves the shelf.
- Get Info, in a card's menu and in the Edit menu, sets a video's or a home video's Genre, typed
  or chosen from the iTunes Store's list. Once one has a genre the Library shows a sidebar of
  its Genres, and a row shows that genre's videos; the desktop's application keeps them.

Apart from home videos, nothing is downloaded or kept but video ids, titles, channel names and
times; the pictures are
YouTube's own thumbnails, which the browser loads from YouTube. On the desktop that the
Ferrite application serves, History, Up Next, Watch Later and the place in every started video
are the person's own, kept by the application (`/api/tv/library`); elsewhere they last as long
as the page. Picture in Picture is not offered for a YouTube video: a browser cannot float
another site's player.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) whose [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) is the
  `title-hidden` layout: the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) and a
  [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) on one row, without a title.
- The sections: a [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) of five
  equal segments that selects one, placed against the middle of the window, and a
  [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field) at the trailing edge.
- The page: [Card Grids](/components/card-grid) in a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view).
  A shelf is a grid of one row of `landscape` cards with a `See All` action; the sections
  after the first draw the line that separates them.
- The hero: an [Editorial Card](/components/editorial-card) in its `hero` variant, `raised`
  as TV draws it.
- The paging chevrons: a [Shelf Pager](/components/shelf-pager) around every shelf.
- A title's page with a [Push Button](/components/push-button), and an
  [Empty State](/components/empty-state) for a search that finds nothing and for an empty
  Library.
- The Preferences: a [Preferences Window](/components/preferences-window) of the modal kind
  with a centred [Preferences Toolbar](/components/preferences-toolbar),
  [Checkboxes](/components/checkbox), [Labels](/components/label), a
  [Pop-Up Button](/components/pop-up-button), [Separators](/components/separator), a
  [Help Button](/components/help-button), a Push Button and a
  [Default Button](/components/default-button).

| Command | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a section | the bar | Watch Now is the shelves; Movies, TV Shows and Kids are grids of the catalogue; Library lists what was added. |
| Turn a page | a shelf's chevrons | What to Watch has two pages of four, the hero three titles, the trailers two pages of three. A chevron is dimmed at its end. The chevrons are drawn while the pointer or the keyboard is in the shelf. |
| See All | What to Watch | All eight titles of the shelf as a grid; Done goes back. |
| Open a title | a card or the hero | The title's page: what it is, a sentence about it, and Add to Library or Remove from Library. Back returns to where the title was opened: the section, all of What to Watch, or the results when the title was found by a search. |
| Search | the search field | Lists the titles whose name or genre contains the text, in every section. Escape or the clear button empties the field. |
| Preferences | Command-comma | Reported to the host through `onOpenPreferences`; on the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) it opens the Preferences window. |

<Example name="preferences" />

The Preferences window edits a copy of the settings. OK reports the copy and closes, Cancel
and Escape close without reporting. Two of the settings change the main window: "Checkboxes
in library" gives every title of the Library a checkbox, and List Size sets how wide the
Library's cards are. The other panes hold one checkbox each, which changes nothing.

## Placeholders and masks

The screenshots show third parties' films and series: four cards, a hero and three trailers,
two thirds of the window. The demo does not reproduce them and does not imitate their
colours or composition. In their place it shows original key art for its invented titles (`tv-artwork.tsx`), and
the comparisons mask these boxes, so that no score depends on a picture:

around them: the bar, the sections, the search field, the titles, the cards' shadows, the
lines between the shelves and the chevrons. The corners of the cards and of the hero lie
inside the masks; the [Card Grid](/components/card-grid) and the
[Editorial Card](/components/editorial-card) compare them with the same screenshots on their
own pages. The five icons of the Preferences toolbar are original drawings and are masked in
the layout comparisons of that window.

## Source walkthrough

The demo is seven files in `demos/tv/`, and four more for YouTube.

**`tv-data.ts`** is the catalogue, the lists of the three shelves and the settings. A title
has a name, a kind, a year, a genre and a sentence; `inSection` filters the catalogue for a
section and `titleMatches` is the search.

**`tv.tsx`** is the window. Its bar is the title bar without a title. The window controls are
the demo's own first item, because TV has them 10 pt from the edge, and the sections are
placed against the window in `tv.css`:

```tsx
<TitleBar variant="title-hidden" divider="bar" style={{ gridTemplateRows: "37px" }}>
  <div className="mac-tv-bar">
    <div className="mac-tv-controls">
      <WindowControls onClose={onClose} onMinimize={onMinimize} onZoom={onZoom} />
    </div>
    <Toolbar aria-label="Toolbar" style={{ "--mac-toolbar-padding-top": "6.5px" }}>
      <ToolbarSegmentedControl aria-label="Sections" value={[section]} onValueChange={choose}>
        {SECTIONS.map((item) => (
          <ToolbarSegment key={item.id} value={item.id}>{item.name}</ToolbarSegment>
        ))}
      </ToolbarSegmentedControl>
      <ToolbarSearchField value={query} onValueChange={setQuery} />
    </Toolbar>
  </div>
</TitleBar>
```

A shelf shows one page of its titles, and its chevrons turn the page. The Shelf Pager does
not scroll anything itself, so the page is state:

```tsx
<Pager label="What to Watch" pages={2} page={shelf.watch} onPageChange={setWatchPage}>
  <CardGrid
    title="What to Watch"
    action={<CardGridAction render={<button type="button" />}>See All</CardGridAction>}
    columns={4}
  >
    {titlesOfThePage.map((title) => (
      <CardGridCard
        key={title.id}
        render={<button type="button" />}
        aria-label={title.title}
        artwork={<Artwork title={title} />}
        onClick={() => open(title)}
      />
    ))}
  </CardGrid>
</Pager>
```

`Pager` is the demo's wrapper of the Shelf Pager. TV draws a shelf's chevrons only under the
pointer, so the wrapper sets the pager's two colour properties to `transparent` while neither
the pointer nor the keyboard focus is in the shelf; the chevrons stay in the tab order, and
tabbing to one draws them. `--mac-shelf-pager-offset` moves them level with the cards.

The hero is a grid of one column whose only card is the Editorial Card:

```tsx
<CardGrid title="Originals" separator columns={1}>
  <EditorialCard
    variant="hero"
    raised
    render={<button type="button" />}
    aria-label={hero.title}
    artwork={<Artwork title={hero} />}
  />
</CardGrid>
```

**`tv-preferences.tsx`** is the Preferences window: the modal kind of the Preferences Window,
whose form is laid out against the left edge with a 172 pt label column, each row carrying
the height the capture gives it. It keeps a draft of the settings in state and hands it to
`onSettingsChange` when OK is chosen.

**`tv-artwork.tsx`** draws the placeholders and the five toolbar icons. **`tv.css`** places
the bar's items and lays out a title's page and the YouTube player; **`tv.tokens.css`** holds
TV's own colours and the player's sizes. **`app.ts`** is the descriptor the
[Desktop](/demos/desktop) reads.

**`tv-youtube.tsx`** is the YouTube window, **`tv-youtube-player.tsx`** its player (it loads
YouTube's IFrame API once per page and gives YouTube an element of its own to replace with its
frame), **`tv-youtube-data.ts`** the person's library as pure functions, and
**`tv-youtube-server.ts`** its calls to the desktop's Ferrite application.

## Hosting the window

`Tv` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and the bar.
These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form supplies,
so the component can be spread with them as they come.

<Example name="hosted" />

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `titles` | `TvTitle[]` | The catalogue; the demo's own by default. |
| `defaultSection` | `"watch-now" \| "movies" \| "tv-shows" \| "kids" \| "library"` | The section selected at first; `"watch-now"`, as captured. |
| `onSectionChange` | `(section) => void` | Called with the section the user selects. |
| `defaultLibrary` | `string[]` | The `id`s of the titles in the Library at first; none by default. |
| `settings` | `TvSettings` | The settings the Library follows; those of the screenshots by default. |
| `onOpenPreferences` | `() => void` | Called on Command-comma or Control-comma in the window. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1294 by 876 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`TvPreferences` renders the library's `PreferencesWindow` as its root and passes it every
prop it does not set itself. Its own are `settings`, the settings it opens with, and
`onSettingsChange`, called with the edited settings when OK is chosen; `onClose` is called by
OK, Cancel and Escape.

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `tvApp` and as `app`: `id`, `name`,
`component` (`TvYouTube`, above), `defaultSize` (1294 by 876), `minSize` (900 by 420),
`resizable`, `menus`, and
`preferences`, which names `TvPreferences` as a second window of 640 by 360 that shares the
settings with the first. `TvYouTube` applies List Size to its Library's cards, as `Tv` does. On the
desktop it also carries out "Checkboxes in library", where a cleared box keeps Up Next from playing
the video, and "Use the screen saver while paused", which starts the desktop's screen saver after a
minute paused with no input. The downloads, the media folder and restrictions have no counterpart
in a window of YouTube videos and change nothing there.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named "TV".
- The bar's toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys move between the five sections and the
  search field. The sections are a group named "Sections" of toggle buttons, of which one is
  pressed.
- The search field is a `searchbox` named "Search"; its clear button is named "Clear".
- Every shelf and grid is a `region` named by its title, which is a heading, and its cards
  are a list. A card is a button: a card of a shelf is named after its title, a card of a
  grid by the name and the line under it.
- The chevrons are buttons named after their shelf ("More in What to Watch"). One that has
  nothing further is marked unavailable (`aria-disabled`) and stays reachable. They are in
  the tab order whether or not they are drawn, and are drawn when one has the focus.
- "No Results" and "Your library is empty" are a `status`, so they are announced.
- The Preferences window is a `dialog` named by its pane. It takes the focus when it appears
  and Escape closes it. It does not make the page behind it inert: a host that wants it
  modal presents it so. Its panes are `tabpanel`s of a `tablist` named "TV preferences".
- The two unavailable checkboxes are disabled; the reason, that downloads need an account, is
  not in the window, as in TV.

the TV Preferences screenshots, in both appearances, each as captured and again with the text
masked. The window's comparisons mask the pictures as listed above and move the pointer over
the shelf whose chevrons the capture shows: the hero in Aqua, What to Watch in Dark Aqua.

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 1294 by 876 pt frame, the 37 pt bar, the

What differs, and why:

- **Pictures.** Abstract placeholders, masked: see above.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the unmasked
  scores.
- **The bar's bottom rows.** The capture has two device-pixel rows of one colour under the
  bar, `#b8b8b8` in Aqua and `#4a4b4c` in Dark Aqua. The title bar's `bar` divider is the
  nearest pair the library has: `#c4c4c4` over `#aeaeae`, and `#434445` over `#505152`.
- **The search field.** The library's toolbar search field is half a point taller on each
  side than TV's, which is 22 pt with its border.
- **The first line.** The line under What to Watch is half a point lower than in the capture:
  the cards are 16:9 of their 288.5 pt width, a quarter point taller than captured.
- **The chevrons at rest.** In TV a shelf's chevrons are not there until the pointer is over
  it. Here they are there, invisible, so that the keyboard reaches them.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the Movies, TV Shows, Kids and Library sections, See
  All, a title's page, searching, the pages after the first of every shelf, the four other
  panes of the Preferences window, the Library's checkboxes and List Size, the minimum size,
  and both windows when they are not key.
