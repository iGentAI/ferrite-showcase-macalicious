<Example name="basic" />

Choose a conversation, type a message and press Return, search, start a new conversation with
the compose button. Messages is simulated: nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is stored, the
people are invented, and nothing of Apple's artwork is reproduced: the glyphs of the buttons
are original line drawings, and a contact's picture, the icons of the Preferences panes and
the folder in a pop-up button are abstract placeholders, a plain disc and plain squares in
one grey, which the visual comparisons mask (see Fidelity).
The first conversation holds the two messages of the reference screenshots.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) without a title bar: the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) float over the conversation list, and the
  strip over the list and the bar over the transcript are the window's drag regions.
- A [Split View](/components/split-view) of two panes with a divider that can be dragged. The
  left pane is on the sidebar material and holds a [Search Field](/components/search-field)
  in its sidebar variant, set to Messages' 21 pt through the field's height hook, and the
  compose button, a compact [Push Button](/components/push-button) around a glyph, at
  Messages' 22 pt height.
- The conversations are a [List View](/components/list-view) of 64 pt rows: a placeholder
  disc for the picture, the
  name with the time, and up to two lines of the last message. The list is always
  emphasised (`emphasis="always"`), because Messages keeps its selected conversation
  emphasised whether or not the list has the keyboard, and in a window that is not key; a
  row's time, its Do Not Disturb mark and its last message are in the list view's
  `ListViewRowDetail`, which draws them in the detail colour of a selected row there. The
  list's own
  surface is transparent, so that it stands on the pane's material. Search narrows the list
  to the conversations whose name or messages hold the text, without regard to case or
  accents and with every character taken as itself; an empty result is an
  [Empty State](/components/empty-state).
- The bar over the transcript has two [Labels](/components/label), "To:" and the name, and
  Details: a [Borderless Button](/components/borderless-button) in its link colour. It opens a
  [Popover](/components/popover) with a [Checkbox](/components/checkbox) for Do Not Disturb,
  which marks the conversation's row.
- The transcript is a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view) around the parts of the
  [Message Bubble](/components/message-bubble): the transcript, a header with a time over
  each run of messages that follows more than an hour of silence, the bubbles, with the tail
  on the last one of each writer's turn, and the status under the last message sent. A
  conversation that goes by text message has green bubbles.
- The message field is a [Text Field](/components/text-field) with the capsule bezel, with an
  [Image Button](/components/image-button) inside it, which adds an emoji, and another beside
  it, the audio button, disabled in this demo; on the served desktop it records an audio message in a
  conversation between accounts. Return sends what is typed: the
  message becomes the last bubble of the selected conversation, Delivered moves under it,
  the conversation's row shows it with its time and goes to the top of the list, the field is
  emptied and the transcript scrolls to its end. A message of nothing but spaces is not sent.
  What is typed and not sent stays with its conversation. The message goes to the
  conversation the transcript shows, also while a search leaves that conversation out of the
  list.
- The compose button starts a conversation: its recipient is typed in a Text Field without a
  bezel in the bar, Return there goes on to the message, and the conversation is listed at
  once, at the top, as New Message until its recipient has a name. One that is never written
  to is given up when another is chosen. Nothing
  is sent to a conversation without a recipient: Return in the message goes to the
  recipient's field.
- Times are written against the `now` the window is given: the hour on the same day,
  "Yesterday", the weekday up to six days back, otherwise the date. By default that moment is
  fixed at the minute of the last default message, so the page shows the same text whenever
  it is rendered, and a message that is sent carries it.
- Every library control is as the library draws it. The window sets a control's width, its
  margins and its place, and the custom properties that the control's own page documents as
  hooks: the divider's colour, the search field's height, the text
  field's fill and the image buttons' padding. `messages.tsx` names each with the page that
  documents it.

## Preferences

<Example name="preferences" />

Messages' preferences are a [Preferences Window](/components/preferences-window) with a
[Preferences Toolbar](/components/preferences-toolbar) of two panes. General is the pane of
the screenshots, laid out by a [Form](/components/form) with the label column's width set
through its `labelWidth`: three [Pop Up Buttons](/components/pop-up-button), one with an icon
before its title, a group of four [Checkboxes](/components/checkbox), a
[Slider](/components/slider) with seven tick marks and a
[Help Button](/components/help-button). Changes take effect at once. On a page by itself, one
setting does something:

| Setting | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| General, Text size | The size of the text in the transcript's bubbles: 10 to 16 pt, 13 pt at the captured step. The bubble is the library's and grows around its text. |
| General, Play sound effects and Message received sound | A short tone when a message goes out and when one comes from someone else, in a conversation without Do Not Disturb. Each choice is a tone of its own, standing in for Apple's sounds. |

Served by the desktop, the Help buttons open Help Viewer on Messages, the iMessage pane names the
person's account, and the settings with nothing to act on there are dimmed and say why
(`SERVED_UNAVAILABLE`).

The other settings keep their state and change nothing. Only the General pane has a reference
screenshot; the iMessage pane is a plausible stand-in.

The app opens its preferences with Command-comma (Control-comma off Apple platforms) while
the keyboard focus is in one of its windows, and through the `preferencesOpen` prop, which is
how a menu bar's Preferences command reaches it; the button under the first example stands
for that command.

## How it is built

The window is one component, `Messages` in `messages.tsx`, over the data and the pure
functions of `messages-data.ts` (the default conversations, `byRecency`, `matches`,
`listTime`, `transcriptTime`, `minutesBetween`). `messages-preferences.tsx` is the
Preferences window, `messages-artwork.tsx` the glyphs and the placeholders, `messages.css`
the layout and `messages.tokens.css` the measured colours.

**State.** Everything the window shows is derived from a few pieces of state. The
conversations are kept in the order of the list, the one last heard from first; the
selection is an `id`; and what is typed and not sent is kept per conversation, in a `Map`,
so that an `id` may be any text:

```tsx
const [conversations, setConversations] = useState(() => byRecency(defaultConversations));
const [selectedId, setSelectedId] = useState<string | null>(/* the first, or the one asked for */);
const [query, setQuery] = useState("");
const [texts, setTexts] = useState<ReadonlyMap<string, string>>(() => new Map());

const selected = conversations.find((conversation) => conversation.id === selectedId) ?? null;
const visible = useMemo(
  () => conversations.filter((conversation) => matches(conversation, query)),
  [conversations, query],
);
```

**Selecting a conversation.** The List View is controlled: its `value` is the selected `id`
and its `onValueChange` calls `select`, which also gives up a conversation that the compose
button started and that was never written to, and closes the Details popover. The
transcript, the name in the bar and the message field all read `selected`, so nothing else
has to be told:

```tsx
<ListView
  count={visible.length}
  rowHeight={64}
  separator="inset"
  emptyRows="separator"
  renderRow={renderRow}
  getRowId={(index) => visible[index]?.id ?? index}
  value={selectedId === null ? [] : [selectedId]}
  onValueChange={(next) => select(typeof next[0] === "string" ? next[0] : null)}
  onRowAction={() => field.current?.focus()}
/>
```

**Sending.** Return in the message field calls `send`. It takes the selected conversation's
draft, refuses an empty one and a conversation without a recipient, appends the message
with the window's present as its time, moves the conversation to the top of the list and
empties the draft. A layout effect keyed on the selection and the number of messages then
scrolls the transcript to its end:

```tsx
const send = () => {
  if (!selected) return false;
  const text = (texts.get(selected.id) ?? "").trim();
  if (text === "" || selected.name.trim() === "") return false;
  const message = { id: /* an id no message of it has */, from: "me", text, at: present };
  const id = selected.id;
  setConversations((all) => {
    const target = all.find((conversation) => conversation.id === id);
    if (!target) return all;
    return [
      { ...target, messages: [...target.messages, message] },
      ...all.filter((conversation) => conversation.id !== id),
    ];
  });
  setText(id, () => "");
  onSend?.(id, message);
  return true;
};
```

**The transcript.** The bubbles are not stored: each render walks the selected
conversation's messages and composes the parts of the Message Bubble component. A message
that follows more than an hour of silence (`RUN_GAP`) gets a `MessageTranscriptHeader` with
its time; a bubble has its tail when it ends a run or its writer's turn; and the one
`MessageStatus` stands under the last message sent, found by its place and not by its `id`:

```tsx
const delivered = all.findLastIndex((message) => message.from === "me");
all.forEach((message, index) => {
  const before = all[index - 1];
  const after = all[index + 1];
  const starts = !before || minutesBetween(before.at, message.at) > RUN_GAP;
  const ends = !after || minutesBetween(message.at, after.at) > RUN_GAP;
  items.push(
    <Fragment key={/* the id with its count among equal ids */}>
      {starts ? (
        <MessageTranscriptHeader title={index === 0 ? title : undefined}>
          {transcriptTime(message.at, present)}
        </MessageTranscriptHeader>
      ) : null}
      <MessageBubble
        direction={message.from === "me" ? "sent" : "received"}
        service={selected.service}
        sender={message.from === "me" ? "You" : name}
        tail={ends || after?.from !== message.from || index === delivered}
      >
        {message.text}
      </MessageBubble>
      {index === delivered ? <MessageStatus>Delivered</MessageStatus> : null}
    </Fragment>,
  );
});
```

The items go into a `MessageTranscript` (a `log`, so a sent message is announced) inside a
Scroll View whose viewport the window holds a ref to.

**Settings and the Preferences window.** The settings are one object, held by the window or
by its owner (`settings`, `defaultSettings`, `onSettingsChange`), and the Preferences window
takes the same three props. The window renders the Preferences beside itself, through a
portal into the element that positions it, with the settings it has; the one setting that
acts, Text size, is read where the bubbles are composed:

```tsx
const settings = givenSettings ?? ownSettings;
const step = textSizeStep(settings.textSize); // a step of the slider, 0 to 6
const size = TEXT_SIZES[step] ?? 13; // 10 to 16 pt

<MessagesPreferences
  state={stateOf("preferences")}
  settings={settings}
  onSettingsChange={setSettings}
  onClose={() => setPreferences(false)}
/>
```

At the captured step a bubble's text is the bubble's own. At another step the text is set in
an element of the demo's own inside the bubble, in that size, and the library's bubble grows
around it. Which of the two windows is in front is state as well (`front`): a press on
either brings it forward, the front one has the `state` the owner gave the app and the other
is inactive, and their `z-index` follows (see Hosting the window).

## Hosting the window

`Messages` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so
it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component takes out before the rest goes to the
`Window`. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { messagesApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={messagesApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...messagesApp.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <messagesApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

The Preferences window is not inside the window's element: it is rendered into the window's
offset parent, or into the element given as `windowsContainer`, and positioned over the
window, 95 pt right of its corner and 70 pt below it unless `preferencesOffset` says
otherwise; it follows the window when its owner moves it. The app decides which of its two
windows is in front: that one has the `state` the manager gave the app, and the other is
inactive. A press on either brings that one to the front, and it is painted over the other:
while its Preferences are open the window takes the `z-index` its owner gave it in `style`
(a number, a number as text, or an expression such as a custom property), or 1, and the
Preferences are one above or one below what the browser computes that level to for the
window. A keyword such as `auto` or `inherit` counts as none given. Here the level is a
custom property set on the window itself:

When the Messages window covers the
Preferences, Command-comma brings them back over it.

A desktop that manages every window hosts the Preferences itself, from the descriptor's
`preferences`: the component, the frame it opens at (`defaultSize`) and `sharesSettings`,
which says that both windows take the `settings` the Preferences report. The desktop demo
does this for every app that names its Preferences. The Preferences window takes what a
managed window's `render` form supplies as well. It sizes
itself to its pane, so it is hosted as a window that is not resizable, at the descriptor's
width and with `height="auto"` in place of the manager's height (a managed window stretches
what it hosts to its frame), and the manager's frame is given the height it has. The
example below does the same by hand, with the library's window manager: the
Messages window is then told that its own Preferences stay closed
(`preferencesOpen={false}`), asks for the hosted one through `onPreferencesOpenChange` on
Command-comma, and shares its `settings` with it:

The selected conversation's row is the List View's emphasised selection, in the library's
selection colour, `--mac-color-selected-content-background`, which follows the accent. The
window has no colour of its own for it; a page that wants another colour sets that token on
the window or an ancestor, as the visual examples do.

The list draws a 1 pt line in the last point of every row and, with the List View's
`emptyRows="separator"`, of every empty row below the last conversation, as both captures
do. It starts 20 pt in (the List View's `--mac-list-view-separator-inset`, where the text
starts at 77) and is `--mac-messages-list-separator`, black at 6%: measured over the five
empty rows of the Dark Aqua capture and the one the Aqua capture shows below the
Preferences window.

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `messagesApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (730 by 520), `minSize` (560 by 320), `resizable`, `menus`,
the menu titles after the Apple menu, and `preferences`: the `MessagesPreferences` component,
the frame it opens at as `defaultSize` (540 by 309) and `sharesSettings`. It also exports
`MessagesPreferences`, whose root is the `PreferencesWindow`, and the types of the
conversations and the settings.

On the desktop served by its Ferrite application (`ferrite-apps/desktop`), the app's
component is `MessagesLive` (`messages-live.tsx`), which adds a shared conversation, Ferrite
Room, first in the list. What is sent to it goes to the application with an idempotency key
and is resent with the same key for up to ten minutes; every browser on the desktop hears the
room through a live feed. A message still unanswered then, or refused, says Not Delivered with
Try Again. Try Again first reads the room: a message found there by its key is taken as
delivered, one that is not is sent again with its first key, and while the room cannot be read
the message stays Not Delivered. The application keeps a room's newest 200 messages and, for
15 minutes, a receipt of each keyed message, so a resend is recognised while either still holds
it. **Known limit:** a Try Again more than 15 minutes after a message that was in fact stored,
in a room where more than 200 messages have been written since, sends it a second time, since
nothing the room keeps says any longer that it arrived. Only the desktop build made for that
application turns this on (`VITE_MACALICIOUS_API=1`); on this site and anywhere else the window
is the plain `Messages` and nothing is fetched.

The same window also holds the person's conversations with the desktop's other accounts
(`messages-accounts.tsx`). The compose button starts one by account name, one person or
several (a group), and the To field offers the person's contacts who are desktop users; the
first message makes the conversation on the server, and writing to the same person again finds
the same one. A contact whose name holds a comma is offered quoted (`"Lee, Ann" (ann)`), so
the To field reads it as one person. Each conversation is listed with an unread dot; the one that is open shows what
the others type, Read once they have read it (when they send read receipts, Messages >
Preferences > iMessage), a group member's name over their messages, tapbacks and replies from
the menu after each message, and Load Earlier Messages for history older than the newest
hundred messages. A message is sent with a fresh idempotency key and resent with the same key
until the server answers, so it is never stored twice; every other member is notified.

**Known limits:** the list and the open conversation are read again when the desktop's
personal live stream says the person's Messages changed, when the window gains focus, and every
15 seconds while the page is visible, one read of each at a time (a change heard during a read
is read after it; a tick then is skipped). The list follows changes at most every 5 seconds,
since typing and read receipts move them too. While that stream is not connected, another person's message, typing or read receipt
can take up to those 15 seconds to show. A person starts at most 100 conversations and writes at
most 2,000 messages a day, and a Guest User starts none: nobody can address a guest, and its
data ends with its time. A message the server has not taken yet is kept in this browser, in
`localStorage` under the account's name, until it is: reloading or closing the window while it is
being sent loses nothing, and it is sent again under its own key when Messages next opens there. It
stays there across a sign-out, and only one the server refused is dropped, after 30 days. Where the
browser cannot store it, the transcript says so. Messages
to the shared room are not kept, so a reload while one is being sent loses it. A conversation of
accounts takes files, from the Attach button or dragged from Finder: each is uploaded and goes as a
message of its own, with a picture shown, Save to Downloads and Download; the shared room takes
none. A tapback can be added or taken away only on a message among the newest
hundred; one loaded with Load Earlier Messages offers no tapback commands (reactions kept
apart from the history pages are a follow-up). The list of conversations is read from the person's
activity index, the most recently active first. A conversation of two people has
no name; a group's name is given when it is started or by renaming it.

## Accessibility

- Each window is a `region`: the main one is named "Messages", the preferences by the
  selected pane.
- The conversations are a `listbox`: the arrow keys move the selection, typing selects by
  name, and Return goes to the message field. A row's name is its content: the person, the
  time and the last message; a conversation set to Do Not Disturb says so in an image's name.
- The transcript is a `log`, named after the person, so a message that is sent is announced
  without moving the focus; each bubble says who wrote it before its text. The scrolling
  element is a tab stop, so the transcript can be scrolled from the keyboard.
- The message field is named "Message" and the recipient's field "To"; the search field, the
  compose button, the emoji button and the audio button are named too. In this demo the audio button is
  disabled: without a server the demo keeps no recording.
- Starting a conversation moves the keyboard focus to its recipient's field, and Return in
  the message field without a recipient moves it back there. A conversation that is given
  without a recipient shows the field and leaves the focus where it is.
- Details is a button, named by its text, that opens a popover named after the person;
  Escape closes it.
- In the Preferences window each pop-up button is named by its label, the four checkboxes
  are a group named "Application:", and the slider is named "Text size" and says its value
  in points.
- The windows do not contain the focus: Tab moves on through the page, as between any two
  windows of one page.

Where Messages draws a control in a way the library's control has no option or hook for, the
demo keeps the library's control as it is:

- The message field is the library's capsule Text Field. While it has the keyboard in a key
  window it shows the library's focus ring, which Messages does not draw; the recipient's
  field does too.
- The compose button is the library's compact Push Button, which has the capture's width and
  height; the capture's bezel has rounder corners.
- The Details link. Messages draws it in `#3091ff` in both appearances. The library's
  Borderless Button in its link colour is `#0168da` in Aqua and `#429bff` in Dark Aqua.

The other differences:

- The captures are of a region of the screen, so the desktop picture is in them: around the
  windows, and through the conversation list, whose material is vibrant even while the
  window is not key. The picture is not reproduced. The scene paints a flat stand-in colour
  where the desktop shows (masked), and gives the list the opaque surface in the tint
  measured over the first conversation (`#cfcbc0`, Dark Aqua `#2c2e32`), the selected row its
  measured mean (`#186de1`, `#0866d2`) and the divider its measured colour: the list and the
  divider through the two custom properties above, the selected row through the library's
  selection colour token, set for the scene. In the Aqua capture the picture shades the list from beige at the
  top to blue-grey at the bottom and brightens the selected row towards the right; the part
  of the list under the Preferences window, where a flat colour is furthest off, is masked.
  On a plain page (`material="opaque"` without those settings) the list is the library's
  opaque sidebar surface and the selection the accent's.
- The glyphs of the compose, emoji and audio buttons are original line drawings. A
  contact's picture, the two Preferences toolbar icons and the folder in a pop-up button are
  not drawn at all: each is an abstract placeholder, a plain disc or a plain rounded square
  in one neutral grey that is not taken from the captures, and the masked comparisons leave
  them out. Apple's artwork is not reproduced or approximated. In Dark Aqua the capture's
  emoji face is filled; the stand-in is a ring in
  both appearances.
- The person is invented: the capture's name is blurred out, in the row, in the bar and in
  the transcript's header. The text is set in the fallback font off Apple platforms, which
  also makes the received bubble 2.5 pt wider than the capture's.
- The list's lines are measured only under empty rows: in both captures the one
  conversation is selected, and the selection covers its line. That the line under a
  conversation that is not selected is the same one is inferred.
- The window controls of the inactive window are the library's `#dedede` (`#4b4b4e`); over
  the tinted list the Aqua capture's are darker, `#cecece` with a ring.
- The top edge of both windows is the library's rim; the captures have a brighter line there

- The audio button is disabled, which draws its glyph at half strength: close to the
  capture's in Aqua, dimmer than it in Dark Aqua.
- In Dark Aqua the Preferences pane is the library's window background, `#323232`, where the
  capture has `#303133`.
- The colours of the two bars, the transcript and the field are measured in the captures,
  where the window is inactive, and are used in a key window too; no screenshot shows
  Messages as the key window.
- Sending, searching, the compose flow, the Details popover (one checkbox, where Messages

  character, and a
  message is marked Delivered at once: nobody answers.
