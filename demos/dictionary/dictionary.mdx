Type in the search field, choose a source in the bar under the toolbar, and pick a word in
the list. The window opens on the search of the reference screenshots, "computer", whose 32
headwords and first entry are the captures'. Everything else is a vocabulary of a few dozen
words written for the demo: try "d", "menu" or "aqua". Nothing is looked up anywhere here;
on the desktop served by the Macalicious Ferrite application the same window looks words up
in public dictionaries (see [On the Ferrite desktop](#on-the-ferrite-desktop)).

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with a unified [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) whose
  title counts what was found, and a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) on the same surface.
- The toolbar: two [Toolbar Segmented Controls](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) in
  the `navigation` variant with momentary segments, Back and Forward and the two text size
  buttons, and a [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field) at the trailing
  edge with its focus ring and clear button.
- A [Scope Bar](/components/scope-bar) on its `toolbar` surface in the `small` size, without
  a label: the sources are its single-selection group, and the selected one is the filled
  pill.
- A [Split View](/components/split-view) of the word list and the entry, whose divider
  resizes the list.
- The word list: a [List View](/components/list-view) of 28 pt rows on the sidebar
  material, with the grey selection of a list that does not have the keyboard, the arrow
  keys and type-select.
- The entry: a [Web View](/components/web-view) whose children are the page, as in
  Dictionary, where the entry is a web view too. The demo sets the page's typography; the
  text size buttons scale it.
- An [Empty State](/components/empty-state) when nothing is found.

| Command | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search | the search field | Lists the headwords that start with the text, in the selected source, and shows the first one's entry. The title counts them. Escape or the clear button empties the field. |
| Choose a source | the scope bar | All, Dictionary, Thesaurus, Apple or Wikipedia. Under All an entry has a section for every source that knows the word. |
| Choose a word | the list | A click, the arrow keys, Home and End, or typing the start of a word. |
| Back, Forward | the toolbar | Steps through what was shown. Choosing a word adds a step; typing changes the step it began. |
| Smaller, larger | the toolbar | Six sizes of the entry's text, from 80% to 150% of the captured size. |
| Back, Forward | Go, Command-[ and Command-] | The same steps as the toolbar's, on the desktop. |
| Make Text Bigger, Make Text Smaller | View, Command-+ and Command-- | The same sizes as the toolbar's, on the desktop. |
| Print | File, Command-P | Prints a copy of the entry from a frame of its own, without its Listen button. |
| Undo, Redo, Cut, Paste, Delete | Edit | Act on the search field. Dimmed for a selection in the entry, which cannot change. Paste is dimmed, with the reason, once the browser refuses to let a menu read the clipboard. |
| Copy, Select All | Edit | Act on whichever of the search field and the entry last held the selection. Command-A in the entry selects the entry alone. |
| Look Up | Edit | Names the word selected in the field or the entry and searches for it as a new step. Dimmed when what is selected cannot be looked up. |

## Source walkthrough

The demo is five files in `demos/dictionary/`.

**`dictionary-data.ts`** is the vocabulary and the search. An entry is a headword with what
each source says about it, and `search(entries, query, scope)` returns the entries whose
headword starts with the query and that the scope can show, in the list's order: letter by
letter, with a space or a hyphen before every letter, so that "computer virus" comes before
"computerese" as in the capture.

**`dictionary.tsx`** is the window. Its state is the scope, the text size and a history of
visits, each a query and the headword that was shown; the list and the entry are derived
from the current visit, so Back and Forward need nothing but an index:

```tsx
const visit = history.visits[history.at];
const results = useMemo(() => search(entries, visit.query, scope), [entries, visit.query, scope]);
// The word of the visit while the scope lists it, and else the first one found.
const shown = results.find((entry) => entry.word === visit.word) ?? results[0];
```

The chrome is the title bar with its toolbar, then the scope bar as the next bar of the
window. `divider="line"` gives the title bar the single line that Dictionary draws between
its title bar and its scope bar, both rows of it one colour:

```tsx
<TitleBar variant="unified" divider="line" title={title} controls={<WindowControls />}>
  <Toolbar aria-label="Toolbar">
    <ToolbarSegmentedControl variant="navigation" tracking="momentary" aria-label="History">
      <ToolbarSegment aria-label="Back" disabled={history.at === 0} onClick={() => go(-1)}>
        <Chevron />
      </ToolbarSegment>
      {/* Forward, then the two text size segments */}
    </ToolbarSegmentedControl>
    <ToolbarSearchField value={visit.query} onValueChange={type} />
  </Toolbar>
</TitleBar>
<ScopeBar surface="toolbar" size="small">
  <ScopeBarGroup aria-label="Source" value={scope} onValueChange={setScope}>
    {SCOPES.map((item) => (
      <ScopeBarButton key={item.id} value={item.id}>{item.title}</ScopeBarButton>
    ))}
  </ScopeBarGroup>
</ScopeBar>
```

The body is the split view. The list's selection is controlled: it is the shown word, and a
change of it is a choice:

```tsx
<SplitView>
  <SplitViewPane defaultSize={183} minSize={120} maxSize={320}>
    <ListView
      aria-label="Words"
      count={results.length}
      rowHeight={28}
      separatorInset={24}
      renderRow={renderRow}
      getRowId={(index) => results[index].word}
      getRowLabel={(index) => results[index].word}
      value={shown ? [shown.word] : []}
      onValueChange={(value) => choose(value[0])}
    />
  </SplitViewPane>
  <SplitViewDivider aria-label="Resize the word list" />
  <SplitViewPane>
    <WebView title={`Entry for ${shown.word}`}>
      <Article entry={shown} scope={scope} />
    </WebView>
  </SplitViewPane>
</SplitView>
```

**`dictionary.css`** places the toolbar's controls where the capture has them, makes the
small scope bar 25 pt with 12 pt titles, puts the list on the sidebar material 14 pt under the
bar, and sets the entry's type: every size is multiplied by `--mac-dictionary-text-scale`,
which the window sets from the text size buttons. **`dictionary.tokens.css`** holds the
three colours measured in the captures that no shared token has (the list's selection, the
divider, and the grey of the pronunciation) and every length and font size of
`dictionary.css` (see [Styling hooks](#styling-hooks)).

**`app.ts`** is the descriptor the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) reads.

## Hosting the window

`Dictionary` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it,
so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
the title bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

<Example name="hosted" />

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entries` | `DictionaryEntry[]` | The vocabulary; the demo's own by default. |
| `defaultQuery` | `string` | The text in the search field at first; `"computer"`, as captured. |
| `defaultScope` | `"all" \| "dictionary" \| "thesaurus" \| "apple" \| "wikipedia"` | The source selected at first; `"dictionary"`, as captured. |
| `onLookUp` | `(word: string \| null, chosen: boolean) => void` | Called with the headword whose entry is shown when it changes, and with `null` when none is; also when the word already shown is chosen in the list. `chosen` says the word was chosen in the list rather than shown by what was typed. |
| `onQueryChange` | `(query: string) => void` | Called with the search field's text whenever it changes. |
| `recent` | `string[]` | The words the list shows while the search field is empty, newest first; none by default. |
| `lookUpRequest` | `{ word: string; n: number }` | A word Look Up names while the window is open; each new `n` starts a search for `word` as a new history step, keeping the scope and text size. |
| `notice` | `string \| null` | A line above the entry about the window's own state, such as a look-up the server could not save; none by default. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 690 by 650 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `dictionaryApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (690 by 650), `minSize` (480 by 240), `resizable`,
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu, and `onDesktopStart`, which starts Look Up.

### On the Ferrite desktop

`component` is `DesktopDictionary` (`dictionary-desktop.tsx`). On the desktop that the
`macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves, it asks the application's Dictionary module
(`ferrite-apps/desktop/src/apps/dictionary`), which looks words up through the application's
declared egress and keeps each answer for everyone for a week:

- the word list is Datamuse's suggestions for what is typed, with the typed word itself first;
- an entry's senses, examples and further parts of speech come from Wiktionary, its
  pronunciation and the recording its Listen button plays from the Free Dictionary API, the
  thesaurus from Datamuse and the encyclopedia from Wikipedia, each credited under the entry
  (Wiktionary and Wikipedia under CC BY-SA 4.0);
- while the search field is empty the list shows the person's recent look-ups, kept on the
  server;
- Look Up: Control-Command-D (or Control-Option-D) with text selected in any window of the
  desktop shows it in Dictionary, opening the window if it is closed. It works on the static
  desktop too, against the demo's vocabulary.

The Apple source has no public counterpart, so it stays empty there. On every other page the
window is the demo described above and requests nothing.

## Styling hooks

The demo's measured lengths and font sizes are custom properties in `dictionary.tokens.css`,
declared on `:root`; set one on the window with `style` to change it. The entry multiplies
its font sizes, line heights and the top margins of the part of speech, a sense and a
narrower sense by `--mac-dictionary-text-scale`, so those tokens hold their values at scale
1; the entry's padding and a source's spacing are not scaled. That file tags each with where
it comes from: the search field's least width, the
space before the pronunciation, the gap between two sources and the spacing of a source's
title are provisional, the rest is measured in the two captures. Left as literals in
`dictionary.css`, because they are structure rather than measurements: zeros, the 100% sizes
of the entry, the flex factors, the 1 pt rule under a source's title, and the letter spacing
of 0.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title, so the number found is part of its name.
- The toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys move between its four buttons and the search
  field. The buttons show a glyph only and are named "Back", "Forward", "Decrease text size"
  and "Increase text size"; one that has nothing to do is marked unavailable
  (`aria-disabled`) and stays reachable.
- The search field is a `searchbox` named "Search"; its clear button is named "Clear".
- The sources are a `radiogroup` named "Source" and one tab stop; the arrow keys move the
  selection.
- The word list is a `listbox` named "Words": the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and
  End move the selection, and typing selects by the start of a word.
- The divider is a `separator` named "Resize the word list" that the arrow keys move.
- The entry is a `region` named after its headword ("Entry for computer") that can take the
  keyboard, so a long entry scrolls with the arrow keys. Its headword is a heading, under a
  heading for the source when all sources are shown; senses are a list.
- "No entries found" is a `status`, so it is announced when a search finds nothing.

## Fidelity

`dictionary.visual.json` compares the whole window with the Dictionary screenshots in both
appearances, each as captured and again with the text and the stand-in drawings masked. The
comparison clicks in the search field, which has the keyboard in the captures. Scores from
`npm run visual -- dictionary/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font: the share of
pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 5.46% | 6.40 | 4.29% | 6.26 |
| `window-light-layout` (masked) | 0.37% | 0.27 | 0.06% | 0.16 |
| `window-dark` | 5.48% | 6.43 | 4.34% | 6.23 |
| `window-dark-layout` (masked) | 0.33% | 0.86 | 0.04% | 0.74 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 690 by 650 pt frame, the two pairs of
toolbar buttons and the 318 pt search field with its focus ring, the 25 pt scope bar and its
pill, the 183 pt word list with its 28 pt rows from 14 pt under the bar and its selection
colour, the divider, and the entry's headword, part of speech and senses where the captures
have them.

What differs, and why:

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the unmasked
  scores. The scope titles are wider in it, so the pill, 72 pt in the capture, is about
  74 pt there, and the scopes after it stand that much further right.
- **The bars' fill.** The title bar's bottom line is the library's `divider="line"`,
  measured in these captures (`#bdbdbd` in Aqua, `#5d5e5f` in Dark Aqua), and the scope bar
  is the library's small bar. In Dictionary the two bars share one gradient; `divider="line"`
  fills the title bar with its upper part (`--mac-title-bar-line-fill`, measured here), so the
  title bar ends at `#d4d4d4` / `#3a3b3c`, as in the captures.
- **Glyphs.** The chevrons of Back and Forward are original drawings.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the Thesaurus, Apple and Wikipedia entries and the
  sections of All, the numbering of several senses, the text sizes, an empty search and one
  that finds nothing, the minimum size, and the window when it is not key.
