Select a disk, a container or a volume in the sidebar: the detail pane shows its size, how
much of it is used and what is known about it, and the toolbar offers what can be done with
it. The disks are data. Nothing is read from the machine the page runs on and no command
changes anything but the window's own copy, so a reload brings the disks of the reference
screenshots back. The sidebar icons, the large disk icon and five of the toolbar glyphs are
original stand-in drawings.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with a unified [Title Bar](/components/title-bar): the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), the title and a
  [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) in its `icon-and-label` display mode on one surface, 70 pt
  high.
- The toolbar: eight [Toolbar Labelled Items](/components/toolbar-labelled-item). View is a
  pull-down from [Toolbar Pop-Up](/components/toolbar-pop-up) whose menu holds two radio
  [Menu Items](/components/menu-item); Volume is a momentary
  [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) of two segments; the
  other six are [Toolbar Buttons](/components/toolbar-button). Two flexible spaces of the
  toolbar keep the group of five where the capture has it as the window is resized.
- The outline: a [Sidebar](/components/sidebar) on its opaque material, three levels deep,
  with [disclosure triangles](/components/disclosure-triangle), single selection that takes
  the accent colour while the list has the keyboard focus, arrow-key navigation and
  type-select. It sits in the leading pane of a [Split View](/components/split-view), whose
  divider resizes it.
- The detail pane: the capacity bar is a [Level Indicator](/components/level-indicator) in
  its `storage` variant, the two rules are [Separators](/components/separator), and the
  information table is a [Box](/components/box) in its `well` variant with two description
  lists set in the [Label](/components/label)'s 12 pt `callout` style. The name, the capacity
  box and the legend are the demo's own text, laid out in `disk-utility.css`.
- The commands ask in a [Sheet](/components/sheet) that hangs from the title bar, with
  [Push Buttons](/components/push-button), a [Default Button](/components/default-button),
  a [Form](/components/form) with a [Text Field](/components/text-field) for a name and a
  [Pop-Up Button](/components/pop-up-button) for a format or a source volume, and a
  [Progress Bar](/components/progress-bar) for First Aid.
- The standard glyphs are the library's [System Images](/components/system-image): the
  sidebar mark of View, the plus and minus of Volume and the i of Info. The other five
  toolbar glyphs are Disk Utility's own and are drawn for the demo.

| Toolbar item | What it does | Available for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| View | Show All Devices, or Show Only Volumes: the volumes without their disks and containers. A selected disk or container then hands the selection to its first volume | always |
| Volume + | Asks for a name and a format and adds an empty volume to the row's container | a container or a volume |
| Volume − | Asks, then deletes the volume and returns its space to the container | a volume the machine did not start from |
| First Aid | Asks, then reports line by line under a progress bar | every row |
| Partition | Explains that APFS volumes share their container's space and offers Add Volume | every row |
| Erase | Asks for a name and a format; the volume is empty afterwards. A container or a disk has all its volumes erased | a row that holds no startup volume |
| Restore | Asks which other volume to copy onto this one; only volumes that fit are offered | a volume the machine did not start from |
| Unmount, Mount | Unmounts the volume, which dims its name and takes its eject mark away, or mounts it again | a volume the machine did not start from |
| Info | Lists the row's facts in a sheet | every row |

Macintosh SSD and Macintosh SSD - Data are the volumes the machine of the screenshots started
from (the first is mounted at `/`), which is why the capture shows Erase, Restore and Unmount
dimmed. Select Time Machine or a volume of the internal disk to try them. While a sheet waits
the window is main, not key, its toolbar and content are inert and its close button is
disabled.

The state is in three small pieces: the disks (a tree of plain objects), the selected row's
identifier and the sheet that is open. Everything the commands do is a pure function in
`disk-utility-data.ts` (`addVolume`, `deleteVolume`, `eraseVolume`, `restoreVolume`,
`setMounted`), as is what the detail pane shows (`summarize`) and what the toolbar offers
(`abilities`), so the window only maps data to components.

## Hosting the window

`DiskUtility` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it,
so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
the title bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { diskUtilityApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={diskUtilityApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...diskUtilityApp.defaultSize }}
  minWidth={diskUtilityApp.minSize.width}
  minHeight={diskUtilityApp.minSize.height}
  render={(props) => <diskUtilityApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `disks` | `DiskUtilityDisk[]` | The disks the window opens with; the default is the two disks of the screenshots. Read once. |
| `selection`, `defaultSelection`, `onSelectionChange` | `string \| null` and a function | The selected row's identifier, controlled or not; `"macintosh-ssd"` at first. The function is called with `null` when a command leaves nothing selected. |
| `defaultShowAllDevices` | `boolean` | Whether the sidebar opens with disks and containers above their volumes; `true` by default. |
| `firstAidStepDuration` | `number` | Milliseconds First Aid spends on each line of its report; 350 by default. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 926 by 571 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

A disk is `{ id, name, device, location, connection, capacity, containers }`, a container
`{ id, device, store, capacity, free, hiddenVolumes, volumes }` and a volume
`{ id, name, device, used, purgeable, mountPoint, owners }` with the optional `system`,
`startup`, `format` and `reserved`; sizes are numbers of gigabytes. `disk-utility-data.ts`
documents each field.

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `diskUtilityApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (926 by 571), `minSize` (780 by 400), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. The [desktop demo](/demos/desktop) finds it
there and lists Disk Utility among its applications.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title. The toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys
  move between its items. Each item's label is its control's accessible name; the two
  segments of Volume show only a sign and are named "Add Volume" and "Delete Volume".
- A toolbar item that does not apply to the selected row is disabled, and its label dims
  with it.
- The sidebar is a `tree` named "Devices" and one tab stop: the arrow keys move the
  selection and open and close outline rows, and typing selects by name. The two headers
  name their groups. The eject mark at the end of a row is decoration: Unmount in the
  toolbar is the command.
- The detail pane is a `region` named by the selected row's name, which is a heading. The
  capacity bar is a `meter` named "Used space" whose value is spoken as a share of the
  capacity. The legend and the two columns of the information box are description lists;
  the legend's entries are told apart by their labels, the squares add colour only.
- A sheet is a modal `dialog` named by its question. The keyboard stays inside it, Escape
  cancels and Return presses the default button. First Aid's progress is a `progressbar`,
  and its report is a list named "Details".
- A name that is not mounted is dimmed; the Mount Point row says "Not Mounted" in words.
- The values under the legend's labels keep the captures' grey (`#717171`), which on the
  Dark Aqua pane is a contrast of about 2.7 to 1, below what small text should have. That is
  the price of matching the screenshot; with `prefers-contrast: more` the values take the
  label colour, and the same figures are in the information table at full contrast.

## Fidelity

`disk-utility.visual.json` compares the whole window with the two Disk Utility screenshots,
each as captured and again with the text and the stand-in drawings masked. The dark capture
shows less on the selected volume than the light one (10.9 GB used against 16.36 GB), so it
has an example of its own with those figures. Scores from `npm run visual -- disk-utility/`
in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font: the share of pixels that differ and the mean
colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 4.20% | 4.47 | 3.59% | 4.57 |
| `window-light-layout` (masked) | 0.49% | 0.52 | 0.03% | 0.34 |
| `window-dark` | 4.23% | 4.54 | 3.54% | 4.58 |
| `window-dark-layout` (masked) | 0.43% | 0.66 | 0.04% | 0.49 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 926 by 571 pt frame, the 70 pt chrome with
every toolbar item where the capture has it, the 253 pt sidebar and its divider, the 32 pt
margins of the detail pane, the 120 by 39 pt capacity box and its colour, the two rules, the
600 by 24 pt capacity bar and the width of its used part in both captures (46 and 32 pt), the
legend's 10 pt squares at a 204 pt pitch with their colours in both appearances, and the
information box with its line, its rules at 32, 63 and 94 pt and its divider.

What differs, and why:

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the unmasked
  scores. The sizes are read from the captures: 24 pt bold for the name, 13 and 11 pt for
  its two lines, 19 pt in the capacity box, 10 pt for its caption and 12 pt in the legend and
  the table.
- **Icons and glyphs.** The disk icon with the Finder face, the sidebar's drive icons and
  the toolbar glyphs are Apple's artwork; all are replaced by original drawings.
- **The bar's used part.** The captures draw it wider than the Used figure alone would be
  (46 pt where 16.36 GB of 249.85 GB is 39 pt). The demo's data carries that difference as
  the volume's `reserved` space so that both captures are matched; what Disk Utility really
  counts there is not known.
- **Other Volumes.** The captures print two dashes for it, and so does the demo, for every
  volume.

Provisional, because no screenshot shows them: every row but Macintosh SSD (the detail pane
of a container and of a disk follow Disk Utility's layout with invented figures), the Show
Only Volumes list, an unmounted volume's dimmed name, the Mount item, and all the sheets,
whose wording follows Disk Utility's where it is remembered and is not checked against a
running Catalina.

Not built:

- Partitioning. The Partition item explains APFS space sharing and offers Add Volume, as
  Disk Utility does first; the partition pie that its Partition button opens is not built.
- The Info window. Disk Utility opens a separate window with a longer table; the demo lists
  the detail pane's facts in a sheet.
- Clicking an eject mark in the sidebar, ejecting a whole disk, disk images, RAID, the
  capacity bar's segments for the other volumes of a container, and reserve and quota sizes
  when a volume is added.
- Key equivalents and the menu bar's commands: the descriptor lists the menu titles, and a
  page cannot take Command-key shortcuts from the browser.
