<Example name="basic" />

Press play, drag the volume, pick a row in the sidebar, choose an album, type in the search
field. Nothing is played: a clock advances the position one second per second, and the
display, the transport buttons and the Up Next list follow it. The albums, artists and songs
are invented for the demo, and every cover and icon is an original stand-in drawing.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) without a title bar, laid out by
  [Media App Chrome](/components/media-app-chrome): the sidebar column runs the height of the
  window and holds the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) and a sidebar
  [Search Field](/components/search-field); the 55 pt playback bar sits over the pane.
- The playback bar: previous, play or pause and next (previous and next are disabled while
  nothing is loaded, as in the screenshots), a [Now Playing Lcd](/components/now-playing-lcd)
  whose progress line can be dragged, the volume slider, and the Lyrics and Up Next buttons,
  which open a panel at the trailing edge of the pane.
- A [Sidebar](/components/sidebar) of eight places in three groups. Albums and Recently Added
  show a [Card Grid](/components/card-grid) of square album cells in a
  [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view); Artists shows one titled grid per artist; Songs shows
  a sortable [Table View](/components/table-view); the Apple Music and Store rows show an
  [Empty State](/components/empty-state), because those services are not part of the demo.
- Choosing an album opens its details under the grid: the cover, a
  [Push Button](/components/push-button) that plays it and its songs in a table. A double
  click on a cell plays the album; a double click or Return on a song plays from that song.
- Search finds albums by title or artist and songs by title, artist or album, and shows both
  lists; a search without matches shows "No Results".
- Up Next is a [List View](/components/list-view) of the queue after the song that is loaded;
  a double click on a row plays from there.

## Preferences

<Example name="preferences" />

Music's preferences are the modal kind of the
[Preferences Window](/components/preferences-window): a dialog without window controls, with a
centred [Preferences Toolbar](/components/preferences-toolbar) of five panes, and a
[Help Button](/components/help-button), Cancel and a [Default Button](/components/default-button)
under each. Changes take effect when OK is chosen (or Return is pressed); Cancel and Escape
drop them. In the demo four settings do something:

| Setting | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| General, Show: iTunes Store | Shows or hides the Store group of the sidebar. |
| General, Show: Songs list checkboxes | Adds a [Checkbox](/components/checkbox) column to the Songs list. |
| General, List Size | A [Pop-Up Button](/components/pop-up-button): the rows of the song lists are 20, 24 or 28 pt high. |
| General, Notifications: When song changes | Announces each new song through a `status` element, for screen readers. |

The other checkboxes keep their state and change nothing. Only the General pane has a
reference screenshot; the contents of the other four panes are plausible stand-ins.

The Music window opens its preferences with Command-comma (Control-comma off Apple platforms)
while the keyboard focus is in the window, and through the `preferencesOpen` prop, which is
how a menu bar's Preferences command reaches it; the button under the first example stands for
that command. While they are open the Music window is the main window (`data-window-state`
is `main`, so its window controls are grey), its content is inert, and the preferences are
rendered outside the window's element, over its middle, into its offset parent or the element
given as `preferencesContainer`. For a host they still belong to the Music window: they take
the `z-index` of its `style`, where they are in front of it because they come after it in the
document, and the `onPointerDown` and `onPointerDownCapture` handlers given to the window hear
presses on them too, so a window manager that raises a window on a press raises Music.

## On the Ferrite desktop

Served by the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application (`ferrite-apps/desktop`), the Dock's
Music is `DesktopMusic` (`music-desktop.tsx`), a real player built from the same components.
Every other build, this page included, shows the demo above.

- **Your own songs.** Add to Library (Command-O, the button of an empty library, or a drop of
  files on the window) takes MP3, AAC and M4A, FLAC, Ogg and Opus, and WAV files, whichever
  the browser can play. The title, artist, album, genre, year, track number and cover are read
  from the file's tags in the browser (`music-tags.ts`: ID3v1 and ID3v2, MP4 `ilst`, Vorbis
  comments, WAV `INFO`), and its length from the browser's own decoder. The file goes to the
  Music folder of the person's drive in pieces under the gateway's 256 KiB request limit; the
  cover, made small in a canvas, is kept by the Music module under its SHA-256.
- **Openly licensed music.** Internet Archive, Browse lists the most played netlabel releases
  and searches the Archive, only among items under a Creative Commons licence or in the public
  domain. An item shows its licence and lists its tracks, which play straight from archive.org
  (MP3 where the browser plays it, else Ogg Vorbis, else FLAC) and can be added to the library.
  The server checks the licence again before it lists an item's tracks or keeps one.
- **Internet radio.** Radio lists stations from the free Radio Browser directory, most listened
  first, by name or genre, only MP3 at https addresses. A station plays straight from its own
  server, and Add keeps it in the library as a stream. These are not Apple's stations, so the row
  sits under Internet Radio.
- **Playback.** Play, pause, previous and next, Shuffle, Repeat (off, all, one), the volume,
  seeking on the display's progress line, Up Next with Play Next and Play Later, and Space,
  Command-Left and Command-Right in the window. The keyboard's and the system's media keys work
  through the Media Session API, which also shows the song, artist, album and cover to the
  operating system. Playback goes on while the views change and while the MiniPlayer (the
  button after Up Next, or Shift-Command-M) is open; it stops when the window closes, as
  quitting Music does.
- **The library.** Albums, Artists, Songs, Genres, Composers and Recently Added; Loved Songs (the heart on each
  row, or the row's menu), Recently Played and Top 25 Most Played; playlists, made from a
  row's contextual menu (Add to Playlist, New Playlist), renamed and deleted in the playlist's
  heading; Delete from Library; search of the library, with a button that searches the
  Internet Archive for the same words.
- **Song-change notifications.** With General, Notifications: When song changes, a
  [Notification Banner](/components/notification-banner) appears at the top right of the
  desktop for five seconds when a new song starts while Music is not the front application.
- **The Dock's Music menu.** While its window is open, the tile's menu names the song loaded and
  offers Play or Pause, Next and Previous.
- **The Help menu.** Music Help opens Help Viewer on a search for Music, and Keyboard Shortcuts
  lists the menus' key equivalents, read from the same commands they run.
- **The Visualizer.** View > Visualizer chooses between two original drawings of the sound, and
  the choice is kept with the person's settings.
- **Playback preferences.** Preferences, Playback: Crossfade songs runs each song into the next
  over 1 to 12 seconds on a second media element; Sound Enhancer brightens the highs, adds some
  bass and widens the stereo image, from Low to High, through Web Audio; Sound Check measures each
  song from its decoded sound once a page and brings it to the same level, within 12 dB and
  without clipping. A file over 64 MB plays at its own level. They are kept on the server.
- **The Audio output button.** Its menu lists the sound outputs the browser names, which it does
  once the desktop may use the microphone, and the choice is kept by name with the person's
  settings. In
  a browser without `setSinkId` the button is dimmed, saying so.
- **Share Song.** Copy Link and the desktop's Share rows give an Internet Archive song's page. A
  song on the drive has no web address, so its rows are dimmed, saying so.

What is kept on the server, per person: the library (songs, playlists, Loved) at
`/api/music/library`, the playback (Up Next, the place in the song, Shuffle, Repeat, volume,
Recently Played and play counts) at `/api/music/playback`, and the covers. The place in the song
is saved at a pause, a seek and a change of song, and every 10 seconds while it plays; Music
opened again shows the selected song at that place and resumes it from there once its sound has
loaded, or from 0:00 when it had finished. The song files are the drive's. How seeking works: an uploaded song is read back from the drive in pieces and
joined into one `Blob` before it plays, so a seek is answered from memory; an Internet Archive
song is played from archive.org, which answers range requests, so the browser fetches only the
part it seeks to. Limits: at most 500 songs and 100 playlists; an uploaded song plays once all
of it has been read from the drive; there is no now-playing
item in the menu bar or in Notification Center, because Catalina has neither.

## Hosting the window

`Music` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component takes out before the rest goes to the
`Window`. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { musicApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={musicApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...musicApp.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <musicApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

Music has no title bar, so its drag region is the playback bar and the top of the sidebar: a
press there that is not on a control calls `onDragRegionPointerDown`, and a double click calls
`onDragRegionDoubleClick`.

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `albums` | `MusicAlbum[]` | The library; the default is eight invented albums. |
| `defaultSection` | `MusicSection` | The sidebar row selected at first; `"albums"` by default. |
| `onSectionChange` | `(section: MusicSection) => void` | Called with the row the user selects. |
| `defaultVolume` | `number` | The volume at first, 0 to 100; 100 by default. |
| `material` | `"translucent" \| "opaque"` | The sidebar's surface: vibrant over a desktop (the default), or opaque on a plain page. |
| `defaultSettings` | `MusicSettings` | The settings the Preferences window starts with. |
| `preferencesOpen`, `defaultPreferencesOpen`, `onPreferencesOpenChange` | `boolean`, `boolean`, `(open: boolean) => void` | The Preferences window, controlled or not. |
| `preferencesContainer` | `HTMLElement` | The element the Preferences window is rendered into. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `controlsProps` | `WindowControlsProps` | Further props for the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Called for the drag region. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1220 by 720 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `musicApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (1220 by 720), `minSize` (860 by 400), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. It also exports `MusicPreferences`, whose root
is the `PreferencesWindow`.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named "Music". The playback bar is a `toolbar` with one tab stop;
  the volume and the progress line are sliders with tab stops of their own.
- The play button's name changes with its glyph, "Play" or "Pause". Lyrics and Up Next are
  toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`), and so is an album cell, whose details open below the grid.
- The sidebar is a `tree`; the arrow keys move the selection and typing selects by name.
- An empty search result is announced through a `status` element, and so is each new song
  while the notifications preference is on.
- Opening the preferences moves the keyboard focus to their toolbar, and closing them gives it
  back. The Music window's content is inert meanwhile. As the Preferences Window page says,
  the dialog does not contain the focus by itself: Tab can leave it for the rest of the page.
- The demo does not bind Space to play and pause; that belongs to the application's menu
  commands, which a menu bar supplies.

The masked rows count a pixel as differing at a colour distance of 0.05, half the default.
All four window rows mask the whole 168 pt album cover (at 260, 95 in the window): the cover
is generated artwork, so none of its pixels are compared, only the cell's edge and shadow.
What remains in the masked window comparisons is the library's own glyph drawings in the
playback bar and, in Dark Aqua, the frame's top rim.

- The album covers, the eight sidebar icons, the five Preferences toolbar icons and the
  audio-output glyph are original stand-in drawings; Apple's and the record labels' artwork is
  not reproduced. The now-playing display shows no idle mark where Music shows Apple's logo.
- The albums, artists and songs are invented, so the album cell's text differs from the
  capture's.
- Music's source list is set with the [Sidebar](/components/sidebar)'s spacing properties:
  rows that touch (a 24 pt pitch), 10 pt between sections, the label 5 pt after the icon and
  the headers 2 pt lower. Music's list also begins 1.5 pt higher than Podcasts' in the same
  [Media App Chrome](/components/media-app-chrome) (its first header's cap top at 103.5 pt
  and its selected Albums row at 267 pt, where the chrome's list puts them at 105 and 268.5),
  so the demo raises its first section by 1.5 pt.
  The row labels keep the sidebar's measured colour, which is a little lighter than Music's.
- An album cell that is chosen has no look of its own: the measured card has no selected
  state, and Catalina shows none in the screenshots.
- The Lyrics and Up Next panel, the album's details, the Artists and Songs views, the search
  results and four of the five Preferences panes have no reference screenshot; they are
  composed from library components to Catalina's conventions.
- Playback is simulated and silent; the volume slider keeps its value and changes nothing.
