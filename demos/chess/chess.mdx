<Example name="basic" />

Click a piece of the side to move, then one of the squares it marks; or move through the
board with the arrow keys and pick up and put down with Return or the space bar. The title
follows the game as the application's does: `(New Game)`, then `(White to Move)` or
`(Black to Move)`, and at the end who won, or `(Draw)`.

**There is no computer opponent.** Both sides are moved by whoever is at the window, whatever
the second name in the title says. And **the board here is flat**: Chess draws a wooden board
and turned pieces in three dimensions, and this window shows a flat board with flat pieces of
its own. With `boardView="3d"`, which is what the desktop shows on graphics hardware unless Preferences say Flat, the
same squares lie in perspective and a canvas draws turned pieces of original shapes, with no
artwork of the application's. A drag on the frame or the backdrop moves the camera. A
preference takes moves and commands by voice, through the browser's speech recognizer.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with a plain 22 pt [Title Bar](/components/title-bar): the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) and a centred title, no proxy icon and no
  toolbar. This is all of Catalina's chrome that the window has, and it is what the visual
  comparison scores.
- The view under the bar is the application's own content, so it is the demo's own markup:
  a labelled grid of 64 squares in `chess.css`, with the pieces drawn in `chess-artwork.tsx`.
  It is dark in Aqua and in Dark Aqua alike, as the application's view is, so it carries
  `data-appearance="dark"` and the label and the two buttons on it are drawn in Dark Aqua.
- Under the board, a [Label](/components/label) says how the game stands ("Check. Black to
  move."), a [Push Button](/components/push-button) claims a draw when the side to move may,
  and another starts a new game. In the application New Game is a command of the Game menu in
  the menu bar; the window itself has no controls.
- A pawn that reaches the far side asks what it should become in a [Sheet](/components/sheet)
  that hangs from the title bar: four choices as the sheet's buttons, Queen the
  [Default Button](/components/default-button) that Return activates, and Cancel to take the
  move back. While the sheet waits the window is main, not key, its view is inert and its
  close button is disabled.

What a square shows, besides its piece:

| Mark | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Green | The piece on it is picked up. |
| A dot | The picked piece can move here. |
| A ring around a piece | The picked piece can take it. An empty square with a ring is a capture en passant. |
| Yellow | The last move left this square or arrived on it. |
| Red | The king on it is in check. |

| Key | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrow keys | on the board | Move to the next square; the edge of the board stops them |
| Home, End | on the board | The first and the last square of the rank |
| Return, Space | on a square | Pick up the piece, put it down on a marked square, or put it back |
| Escape | on the board | Put the picked piece back |
| Tab | anywhere | Between the board, which is one tab stop, Claim Draw while it is enabled, and New Game |

## Source walkthrough

The demo is six files in `demos/chess/`.

**`chess-game.ts`** is the rules, as pure functions over plain objects; it knows nothing of
React. A game is a position (the 64 squares, the side to move, the castling rights, the en
passant square and two move counters) with the moves that led to it. `legalMoves(game, from)`
lists what a piece may do, `play(game, from, to, promotion)` returns the game after a move or
`null` for an illegal one, and `gameStatus(game)` says whether the game goes on, is won or is
drawn. No function changes its argument.

A move is legal when the piece moves that way and the mover's king is not attacked
afterwards, which one filter says for every rule at once: a pinned piece, a king that would
step into check, a check that must be answered, and a capture en passant that would uncover
an attack along the rank.

```ts
for (const move of candidates(position, from)) {
  const next = apply(position, move);
  const king = kingOf(next.board, turn);
  if (king < 0 || !attacked(next.board, king, next.turn)) found.push(move);
}
```

The rules it implements: the moves and captures of all six pieces; the pawn's double step,
its capture en passant on the next move only, and its promotion to a queen, rook, bishop or
knight; castling on either side, which needs a king and a rook that have not moved, empty
squares between them, and a king that is not in check and neither passes over nor lands on
an attacked square; check, checkmate and stalemate; and the draws. Four end the game by
themselves: stalemate, a dead position (below), seventy-five moves by each side without a
capture or a pawn move, and the fifth occurrence of a position. Two are draws only when the
side to move claims them: the third occurrence of a position and fifty moves by each side
without a capture or a pawn move. `drawClaim(game)` names the claim the side to move has, if
any, and `claimDraw(game)` returns the game drawn by it; the window's Claim Draw button is
enabled while there is one, and the status line adds "White may claim a draw." A claim is made
in the position on the board, for either rule: claiming on a move that would bring a repetition
about, or that would complete the fifty moves, is not offered.

The dead positions recognised, and only these: the kings alone; a king and one bishop or
knight against a king; kings with bishops that all stand on squares of one colour; and a
board locked by pawns, where only kings and pawns remain, every pawn is blocked by an enemy
pawn straight in front of it, no capture is possible, and neither king can reach a pawn it
may take without stepping onto a square an enemy pawn attacks (for example
`4k3/8/8/p1p1p1p1/PpPpPpPp/1P1P1P1P/8/4K3 w - - 0 1`). Other dead positions, such as a
bishop shut in behind locked pawns, are not recognised, and such a game goes on until
seventy-five moves or a repetition ends it.

`parseFen` and `toFen` read and write a position in Forsyth-Edwards Notation, so a game can
open anywhere; `parseFen` refuses more than six fields and an en passant square the side to
move cannot take on (the sixth rank for White, the third for Black):

```tsx
import { Chess } from "./chess";
import { parseFen } from "./chess-game";

<Chess defaultGame={parseFen("7k/4P3/8/8/8/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1")} />;
```

The module's tests count the positions reachable from five standard test positions to three
half-moves and compare them with the published numbers (8,902 from the starting position,
97,862 from the position known as Kiwipete), which exercises castling, en passant, promotion
and pins together.

**`chess.tsx`** is the window. Its state is the game, the square of the piece that is picked
up, the one square that is the board's tab stop, and the sentence last announced; everything
drawn is derived from them. The title is one line:

```tsx
<TitleBar
  title={<span className="mac-chess-title">{`${white} - ${black}   (${gameTitle(game)})`}</span>}
  controls={<WindowControls onClose={onClose} onMinimize={onMinimize} onZoom={onZoom} />}
/>
```

The board is a `grid` of eight `row`s of eight `gridcell`s. A cell is named after its square
and what stands on it, and a click, Return and the space bar all end in the same function:

```tsx
<div
  role="gridcell"
  tabIndex={square === cursor ? 0 : -1}
  aria-label={describeSquare(game, square)} // "e2, white pawn"
  aria-selected={square === selected}
  data-square={square}
  onClick={() => activate(square)}
>
  {piece ? <ChessPieceShape kind={piece.kind} side={piece.side} /> : null}
</div>
```

`activate` picks the piece up, puts it back, moves it, opens the promotion sheet, or says
why nothing happens. Whatever it does it also says, in a polite `status` region: "White pawn
on e2 picked up. It can move to e3, e4.", then "White pawn e2 to e4. Black to move."

**`chess-artwork.tsx`** draws the six pieces as flat silhouettes on a 40 unit square, each
twice: a thick outline in one colour and the fill in the other, so that a white piece shows
on a light square and a black one on a dark square. **`chess.css`** lays the view out: the
board is the largest square that fits the room above the bar, measured with container units,
so it follows the window's size. **`chess.tokens.css`** holds the demo's colours; only the
backdrop is measured, the flat `#404040` of the scene's floor. **`app.ts`** is the
descriptor the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) reads.

## Hosting the window

`Chess` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so
it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
the title bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render`
form supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { chessApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={chessApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...chessApp.defaultSize }}
  minWidth={chessApp.minSize.width}
  minHeight={chessApp.minSize.height}
  render={(props) => <chessApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `white`, `black` | `string` | The two names in the title; "Player One" and "Player Two" by default. The capture's first name is a person's and is not repeated. |
| `defaultGame` | `ChessGame` | The game the window opens with; the starting position by default. Read when the window opens. New Game always starts from the starting position. |
| `onMove` | function | Called after every move with the move, which carries the state of the game after it, and the game. |
| `onNewGame` | function | Called when the New Game button starts a game. |
| `onClaimDraw` | function | Called when the Claim Draw button draws the game, with the game after it. |
| `game` | `ChessGame` | The game, when the owner holds it: the window shows it, and a move, New Game and Claim Draw are only reported for the owner to apply. |
| `notice` | `string` | A sentence the status bar shows instead of how the game stands. |
| `refuse` | function | Asked before a move, a draw or a new game: a sentence saying why it is refused, or `undefined`. |
| `actions` | `ReactNode` | Further buttons for the bar, before Claim Draw. |
| `newGameLabel`, `newGameDisabled` | `string`, `boolean` | The New Game button's title ("New Game" by default) and whether it is disabled. |
| `onNewGameRequest` | function | Called instead of starting a game when New Game is clicked, for an owner that asks first. |
| `sheets` | function | Further sheets, given the title bar they hang from. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 971 by 702 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer and key events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `chessApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (971 by 702), `minSize` (420 by 480), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu: Chess, Game, Edit, Moves, View, Window, Help.

On the desktop served by its Ferrite application (`ferrite-apps/desktop`), the app's
component is `ChessLive` (`chess-live.tsx`). With `?game=<id>` in the page's address it plays
that open board, which any browser naming it can join. Otherwise it plays the signed-in
person's own games (`chess-account.tsx`): New Game… asks whether to play the computer, at one
of four levels, or another account by its account name, who gets an invitation with Accept
and Decline; Games… lists the person's games and the invitations waiting for them; Back and
Forward step through the moves; Resign gives a game up. The server is the only record of
every game: a move is shown once the server has it, only the two players can read or move in
a game, and the other player's moves reach the window through the person's own live stream.
The window opens on the newest game still being played, or on a fresh board. Moving a white
piece starts a standard game against the computer at the selected strength. Regular games use Reckless 0.9.0 compiled to WebAssembly. Opening Chess loads and caches
the engine; its worker stays ready between moves and searches for up to half a second at the
strongest setting. In the opening it can choose among closely scored replies. The variants use their own rules and search. Moves > Show Hint gives you a clue about the position. Choose
Show Suggested Move to see the squares. Only the Ferrite desktop enables the computer
opponent (`VITE_MACALICIOUS_API=1`). On this site and anywhere else
the window is the plain `Chess` and nothing is fetched.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title, so the state of the game is part of its name.
- The board is a `grid` named "Chessboard" with eight rows of eight cells, the eighth rank
  first, as it is seen. Each cell is named for its square and its piece, "e2, white pawn",
  or for the square alone when it is empty; the picked piece's cell is `aria-selected`. The
  rank digits and file letters beside the board are decoration and are hidden, since every
  cell says its own name.
- The board is one tab stop. The arrow keys, Home and End move within it; Return and the
  space bar pick up and put down; Escape puts the piece back. Everything the pointer does the
  keys do: there is no dragging.
- Every move is announced by a polite `status` region in words, with the result: "White
  queen d1 to h5. Check. Black to move.", "Black castles queenside. White to move.",
  "Checkmate. Black wins." Picking a piece up announces where it can go, and a refused move
  says so ("White pawn on e2 cannot move to e5."). The visible line under the board says how
  the game stands and is not a live region, so nothing is read twice.
- The marks on the squares are not the only carrier of what they mean: the squares a piece
  can reach are in the announcement, a check is in the status line and the announcement, and
  the result is in the title too.
- The keyboard focus ring is the system ring over a dark band with a light line inside, so
  that it shows on a square of either shade; like every focus ring it is drawn only in a key
  window. In forced colours the squares keep an edge, the pieces take the system's text and
  background colours, and the marks become outlines.
- The promotion sheet is a modal `dialog` named by its question. Its buttons are in the tab
  order, Return makes a queen, Escape cancels, and the focus returns to the board.
- Each side's pieces have an outline in the other side's colour, so a piece is told from its
  square by shape and edge, not by one colour. Nothing moves or animates.

## Fidelity

**What is compared, and what is masked.** The two captures show the same window, 971 by
702 pt, in Aqua and in Dark Aqua. Everything under the title bar, the region at 0,22 of 971 by
680 pt, is the application's three-dimensional scene in the captures and the demo's own board
here. **That region is left out of every comparison**: scoring a flat board
against a rendered one would say nothing of the chrome, and no budget was set to let it pass.

The harness lets a comparison leave at most 75% of its crop out of the score, and the view is
97% of the window, so the view cannot be masked out of a crop of the whole window. The
comparisons therefore crop the chrome instead, which leaves the view out altogether:

- **`title-bar-light`, `title-bar-dark`**: the whole title bar, 971 by 22 pt, against each
  capture, nothing masked: the window's top corners, the bar's gradient with its two
  highlight rows and two bottom rows, the three window controls and the title.
- **`title-bar-drawing-light`, `title-bar-drawing-dark`**: the same crop with the title
  masked (232 by 16 pt at 370,4, which is 17% of the crop) at the stricter threshold of
  0.05, so that the bar's drawing is scored without the fallback font.
- **`window-chrome-light`, `window-chrome-dark`**: the whole window at the captured size,
  held by expected pixels instead of a score. They check that the window is 971 by 702 pt;
  the bar's two highlight rows, its gradient at the top, the middle and the bottom, its
  shadow row and its separator, each against the colour measured in that capture; the three
  window controls at x 8, 28 and 48 pt, by the bar's fill on either side of each and the
  control's colour at its centre; and that the view begins at 22 pt. The one pixel read in
  the view is the placeholder's own backdrop, to show where the bar ends; it is not compared
  with the capture.

### Known differences

- **The board and the pieces** are the demo's own, by decision: a flat board in two cool greys
  with flat silhouettes, seen from above, or the 3D view (`boardView="3d"`), where the squares
  stay plain colours, without wood, and the turned pieces are shapes made here. The 3D view
  takes the capture's camera angle and backdrop greys, and its horizon is straight where the
  capture's curves. The file letters are lower case beside the board, as squares are
  named; the application engraves capitals on its frame.
- **No computer opponent here.** The application plays the other side; on this site both are
  moved by hand, and the default names are "Player One" and "Player Two". On the desktop's
  Ferrite application the window can play the computer, with an engine of its own that is not
  the application's.
- **New Game is a button in the window.** In the application it is a command of the Game
  menu in the menu bar and opens a sheet that asks for the players and the variant; the
  window has no controls at all. The status line beside the button is the demo's too.
- **Moving.** A piece is moved with two clicks or two key presses and jumps to its square.
  The application's pieces are dragged and slide. The marks for the picked piece, its
  destinations, the last move and a check are the demo's own.
- **The Dark Aqua title bar** is the library's, measured in Finder: `#404142` to `#343435`,
  where this window's runs from `#434447` to `#37383a`, three to five levels lighter and a
  little bluer, and its first highlight row is `#838485` where the capture has `#86878a`.
  That is the mean difference of 3.66 in `title-bar-drawing-dark`; few pixels differ visibly
  (0.09%). The demo does not restyle the library's bar.
- **The title's text** is set in the fallback font off Apple devices and names invented
  players, which is nearly all of the unmasked scores.
- **No Image View.** The reference blueprint suggests the library's
  [Image View](/components/image-view) as a stand-in for the application's view. An image
  view is a picture, one element with the role `img`, and cannot hold a board that is played
  on, so the view is the demo's own grid.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** every title but `(New Game)`, the wording of the
  results among them; the promotion sheet; the Claim Draw button; and the window when it is
  not key.
- **Draws.** Threefold repetition and fifty moves are draws the side to move claims with
  Claim Draw, as the rules of chess have them; the game ends by itself only at stalemate, at a
  dead position the rules module recognises (listed in the walkthrough; not every dead
  position is), at seventy-five moves and at the fifth occurrence of a position. A claim is
  made only in the position on the board, for repetition and for fifty moves alike: the rules
  also let a player claim by naming the move that would bring either about, and the demo does
  not. Making that move does not give its player the claim: the position it leaves is the
  other player's to claim in. How the application treats claims is not shown in a capture.
- **Not built:** speech and offering a draw to the other player. Taking back a move, hints, saving
  and opening games, the game log, clocks and the variants need the desktop's Ferrite application,
  as does resigning.
- **Clocks.** New Game offers a clock of 1, 5, 10 or 30 minutes each, or none. The real application
  has none, so this is an addition: the server charges each move and ends a game whose side to move
  has run out of time.
- **Variants.** New Game offers Regular, Crazyhouse, Losers and Suicide, and the rules are the same in
  the page and on the server. In Suicide a pawn does not promote to a king. A variant is written in
  a saved game's `Variant` tag and read back from it.
