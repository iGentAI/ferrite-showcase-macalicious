<Example name="basic" />

Click an accessory to switch it and a scene to run it; the status lines under the title follow.
The toolbar changes the view and the home. Nothing is connected to a real home: the
accessories are invented, they live in the page for as long as it is open, and a reload brings
the screenshot's state back. The wallpaper is a drawn gradient and every glyph an original
stand-in drawing.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) that holds a [Catalyst Chrome](/components/catalyst-chrome)
  with `underToolbar`: the content starts at the window's top edge and the 38 pt toolbar row
  is the translucent material that shows the wallpaper through. The row holds the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) and a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar), and is
  the window's drag region. The window has no title; the `Window` is named with `aria-label`.
- The toolbar: the homes as a pull-down of the [Toolbar Pop Up](/components/toolbar-pop-up)
  family, with a house glyph and a chevron, whose [Menu Items](/components/menu-item) are a
  radio group; the three views as a
  [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control) that two flexible spaces
  keep centred on the window; and the add button, a
  [Toolbar Button](/components/toolbar-button) that opens a [Menu](/components/menu).
- The tiles: a [HomeKit Tile](/components/homekit-tile) for each accessory and each scene, in
  the tile grids of the same component, each grid named by the header above it. An accessory
  tile toggles. A scene tile does not: the demo marks a scene as active while every accessory
  it sets is in the state it sets, so running Recording also lights Studio On, and switching
  one of its lights by hand ends both.
- The Rooms tab: the rooms in a Catalyst sidebar, the parts `CatalystSidebar`,
  `CatalystSidebarGroup` and `CatalystSidebarItem` of the chrome; the room chosen there is the
  title and its accessories, favourites or not, are the tiles.
- The Automation tab: an [Empty State](/components/empty-state), in the Dark Aqua text colour
  because the wallpaper is dark in both appearances.
- Add Accessory… and Add Scene… open a [Sheet](/components/sheet) that hangs under the
  toolbar, with a [Text Field](/components/text-field), [Pop Up Buttons](/components/pop-up-button),
  [Labels](/components/label) and a [Default Button](/components/default-button). While it is
  open the window is main, not key, and its content is inert.
- The type of the content is the chrome's: its large title, title and subheadline styles
  (`--mac-catalyst-chrome-font-*`), which are the iPad's text styles at 77%.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Accessory tile | Switches the accessory. Its last line says the state in the accessory's own words: `On` and `Off` for a light or an outlet, a percentage for a dimmable light that is on, `Playing` and `Paused` for an Apple TV or a HomePod, `Open` and `Closed` for the garage door, and how far open blinds are. A sensor only reports and is not switched. Command-click adds a tile to a selection and Shift-click reaches from the last one chosen; a secondary click on the selection switches, moves or removes them together, and dragging one to a room takes them all. |
| Scene tile | Puts each accessory the scene names in the state the scene sets. |
| and 1 More | Shows the status lines beyond the third; Show Less hides them again. |
| Home, Rooms, Automation | The view. |
| A room in the sidebar | Shows that room. |
| Homes | A menu of the homes; each keeps its own state. Home Settings… changes its name and wallpaper and removes it; Room Settings… renames and removes the room. Sharing is dimmed with its reason. |
| Add | A menu: Add Accessory… adds a light with the name and in the room the sheet asks for, as a favourite; Add Scene… adds a scene that sets one room's lights and any other accessories chosen. |

The status lines are computed from the accessories (`statusLines` in `home-data.ts`): how many
lights are on, what the humidity sensor reads, the rooms whose every light is on, and what is
playing, open or switched on. With the screenshot's state they read as the screenshot does,
with a fourth line behind "and 1 More".

## Hosting the window

<Example name="hosted" />

`Home` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and to the
toolbar row, which is this window's drag region. These are the props a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form supplies, so the component can be
spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { app } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={app.id}
  title={app.name}
  resizable={app.resizable}
  minWidth={app.minSize.width}
  minHeight={app.minSize.height}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...app.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <app.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `CatalystChrome`: a press with the primary button, or a double click, on the toolbar row that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 914 by 768 by default, as captured. |
| `state` | `"key" \| "main" \| "inactive"` | The window's state. While the add sheet is open a key window shows itself as main. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, `aria-label`, pointer and focus events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `homeApp` and as `app`: `id`, `name`,
`component`, `defaultSize` (914 by 768), `minSize` (600 by 400, the width at which the
toolbar's items still fit), `resizable` and `menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu.

At other sizes the segmented control stays centred on the window, the tiles keep their size
and wrap, and the content scrolls over the wallpaper, which stays in place.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named "Home". The toolbar is one tab stop; the arrow keys move
  between the Homes button, the three views and the Add button.
- The Homes and Add buttons show only a glyph and are named with `aria-label`. The views are
  toggle buttons in a group named "View".
- The title is a heading and each section header a heading one level below it; a header names
  the `group` of tiles under it.
- An accessory tile is a toggle button named by its three lines, "Master Corner Light On":
  `aria-pressed` and the last line both say its state, and Space or Return switches it. The
  badge on the Studio HomePod's glyph is hidden from assistive technology, so its meaning is
  added to that tile's state line as text that is not shown: "Paused, microphone off".
- The sensor's tile is a toggle button too, because the library's tile has no other form for
  an accessory, and it stays unpressed when activated.
- A scene tile is a plain button. That a scene is in effect is shown by its white fill alone;
  it is not announced.
- "and 1 More" is a [Borderless Button](/components/borderless-button) with `aria-expanded`.
  The status lines are not a live region: the
  tile that was switched already announces its own state.
- The Rooms sidebar is a `navigation` landmark named "Rooms"; the room shown has
  `aria-current="page"`.
- The sheet is a modal `dialog` named by its title; its text field takes the focus, Return
  adds and Escape cancels.
- The content's text is white on the wallpaper in both appearances. The text of a tile that is
  off is tone on tone by the library's design and low in contrast in Aqua (see
  [HomeKit Tile](/components/homekit-tile)).

## Fidelity

`home.visual.json` compares the demo with the two Home screenshots, the whole capture with the
window's shadow in each appearance: once as it is (`window-light`, `window-dark`), and once
with the text and the stand-in glyphs masked (`window-layout-light`, `window-layout-dark`).
The masks cover the title, the status lines, the section headers, the glyph and the lines of
every tile, the three segment titles and the two toolbar glyphs; the wallpaper, the toolbar,
the bezels, the tiles' fills and the shadow are scored.

Scores from `npm run visual -- home/` (Chromium on Linux, fallback font), share of pixels that
differ and mean colour distance out of 255:

| Comparison | 1x | 2x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 2.4%, 7.3 | 2.4%, 7.6 |
| `window-layout-light` | 0.9%, 5.9 | 1.0%, 6.1 |
| `window-dark` | 3.3%, 7.6 | 3.1%, 7.9 |
| `window-layout-dark` | 1.1%, 5.8 | 1.1%, 6.0 |

The budgets in `home.visual.json` are set just above these. The mean distance is nearly all
wallpaper, which no mask covers.

Taken from the spec's measurements of the screenshots and laid out to them: the 914 by 768 pt
frame; the 38 pt toolbar row with the window controls, the 47 pt pull-down from 77.5 pt, the
290 pt segmented control centred on the window with its dividers 96.5 pt apart, and the 39 pt
add button 7.5 pt from the trailing edge; the title, the three status lines 22 pt apart, "and
1 More" and the two section headers at the capture's heights; the scene tiles from 326 pt and
the accessory tiles from 484 pt, both from 15.5 pt off the leading edge, four scenes and eight
accessories to a row. The colours that are the app's own are in `home.tokens.css`, each with
the place it was measured.

What differs, and why:

- **Wallpaper.** Home's is a blurred photograph, an image asset that is not reproduced. The
  demo draws gradients in its colours: lighter under the toolbar, darker towards the leading
  edge and the bottom. The photograph's streaks are not there. The backdrop is a plain
  gradient standing in for the wallpaper, not a drawing of it. It is not masked, so it is the
  largest term of every comparison.
- **Toolbar controls in Aqua.** Home's buttons and its segmented control let the wallpaper
  tint them (the selected segment measures `#596c70`). The library's toolbar controls are
  opaque in Aqua, so they stay neutral grey; in Dark Aqua they are translucent and take the
  tint. The demo does not restyle them.
- **Tile pitch.** The capture's accessory tiles are 109.4 pt apart. The library's tiles are
  101.5 pt with a 7.5 pt gap, 109 pt, so the eighth tile of a row stands 3 pt before the
  capture's. The demo does not change the grid's gap.
- **Glyphs.** Every glyph is an original drawing to the measured size: a bulb, an outlet, a
  box for the Apple TV (Home draws its logo), a garage door, a lamp, a sensor, a speaker, the
  two houses of the scenes and the toolbar's house. They are masked in the layout comparisons.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, so a long name is cut a letter or two
  from where the screenshot cuts it.
- **Badge.** The mark on the Studio HomePod's glyph is drawn as a microphone that is struck
  out; the capture's mark is too small to read, and the library's badge has no ring.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the Rooms and Automation tabs, both menus, the
  sheet, the second home, an accessory in any state other than the captured one (the glyph
  colours of a speaker, an outlet and a door that are on), the window when it is not key and
  every size other than the captured one. In Home the rooms are pages of the Rooms tab; the
  demo lists them in a sidebar, as the Catalyst guidelines ask of an iPad app on the Mac.
- **Not built:** a double click or a long press on a tile (a secondary click opens its menu),
  cameras, location triggers, and any connection to HomeKit: on the served desktop every accessory
  is a simulated record, and the window says so.
