<Example name="basic" />

Safari 13 as it opens in the Catalina captures: three tabs with the middle one in front, a
toolbar with Back and Forward, the sidebar button, the Smart Search field, Share and Tab
Overview, and a web view under them. Everything that draws is a library component; the demo
adds the browser's state (tabs, each with its own history, Reader and loading) and a small
invented web to browse.

Nothing is fetched. The pages are placeholder sites written for the demo under the reserved
`.example` domain, and every such address opens one: type `orchard.example` in the address
field, or a few words to search the demo's pages. Any other address shows Safari's error page.
No site, article, photograph or icon from the captures is reproduced.

## What to try

- Click a tab, close one with the button that appears under the pointer (or Delete while the
  tab has the keyboard), open one with the + at the end of the tab bar, or, while one tab is
  open, with the + at the end of the toolbar.
- Click the address field: it shows the whole address, selected. Return loads what was typed,
  Escape puts the address back. Reload turns into Stop while the page "loads"; each tab
  loads on its own, so a load goes on while another tab is in front.
- Follow a link on a page, then use Back and Forward: each tab keeps its own history.
- The Reader button at the leading end of the field shows the page's article on a sheet of
  paper; the button that replaces Reload there changes the text size.
- The Tab Overview button shows a thumbnail per tab and a tile that opens a new one.
- The sidebar button opens Bookmarks and the Reading List; a link there loads in the front tab.
- The Share button opens the Share menu on a page that loaded: Add to Reading List and Add
  Bookmark put the page in the sidebar's lists.
- Type `reel.example`: the page has a video, and the button under it moves the video to a
  Picture in Picture window.

## Start page and sidebar

A window with one tab has no tab bar, as in Safari, and its new-tab cell is the last item of
the toolbar: the library's `SafariNewTabButton`, against the window's edge. The start page is
the web view's Favorites grid; a folder of favourites opens the Bookmarks sidebar, whose width
the divider changes. The switch between Bookmarks and the Reading List is the `header` of the
library's [Sidebar](/components/sidebar), so it is drawn on the sidebar's own material. A
link's row stays selected while its page is in front; once the tab shows another page the row
can be chosen again.

## Share menu

The Share button is the library's [Share Menu](/components/share-menu) with Safari's rows:
Add to Reading List, Add Bookmark, five services and More…. The first two work: the page in
front joins the Reading List, or the end of the Bookmarks list, unless the list or one of its
folders already holds it. The services have
nowhere to send a page and More… has no Extensions preferences to open, so they close the
menu and do nothing; their icons are abstract placeholders in the demo's tints. The button is dimmed where
there is nothing to share: on the start page, on the error page and in the Tab Overview.

## Picture in Picture

A page with a video (the demo has one, `reel.example`) can send it to the library's
[Picture in Picture](/components/picture-in-picture) window. The window outlives a change of
tab; its return button brings the video's tab to the front, and it closes when that tab
closes or loads another page. By default it floats over the page, inside the Safari window.
Give `pictureInPictureContainer` the positioned element that stands for the screen and it
floats there, as in the capture, where Safari is behind another application's window:

## Preferences

`SafariPreferences` is a second window: the library's preferences window with Safari's ten
panes. Pass it the browser's current address and "Set to Current Page" copies it to the
Homepage field; without one the button is disabled, as in the Dark Aqua capture.

<Example name="preferences" />

The General pane has the capture's rows: eight pop-up buttons around the Homepage field, each
with a few of Safari's choices, at the capture's places. The other nine panes hold a few
checkboxes each; no capture shows them.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Safari } from "./safari";
import { SafariPreferences } from "./safari-preferences";

<Safari width={1254} height={903} />
<Safari defaultTabs={[""]} defaultSidebarOpen />            {/* start page, Bookmarks open */}
<Safari defaultActiveTab={2} defaultReader />               {/* third tab in Reader */}
<SafariPreferences currentPage="https://harbor.example/" />
```

The demo is built from these entry points and nothing else: `macalicious/window`,
`window-controls`, `title-bar`, `toolbar`, `toolbar-button`, `toolbar-segmented-control`,
`smart-search-field`, `safari-tab-bar`, `web-view`, `split-view`, `sidebar`,
`segmented-control`, `share-menu`, `picture-in-picture` and `push-button`, and for the
preferences window `preferences-window`, `preferences-toolbar`, `pop-up-button`,
`text-field`, `push-button`, `checkbox` and `help-button`. Its own stylesheet sizes and
places them and styles the placeholder pages; it changes no control.

## Hosting the window

The root of `Safari` is the library's [Window](/components/window), and it takes the window's
props, so a window manager can place, size and order it:

| Prop | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `state` | `key`, `main` or `inactive`; every control inside follows it. |
| `width`, `height`, `style`, `ref` | The frame's size and position. Without a size the window is 1254 x 903 pt, the Dark Aqua capture's. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom`, `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | The three window controls. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | The toolbar is the window's drag region: a press or double click on the bar itself, not on a control in it. |
| `defaultTabs`, `defaultActiveTab`, `defaultSidebarOpen`, `defaultReader`, `defaultTabOverview`, `defaultPictureInPicture` | What the window opens with. |
| `pictureInPictureContainer`, `pictureInPictureWidth` | Where the Picture in Picture window floats (the window's page when left out; `null` while the element is not there yet) and the width it opens with, 320 pt when left out. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) demo reads, as `app` like every
demo: `id`, `name`, `component`, `defaultSize`, `minSize`, `resizable` and the menu bar's
`menus`, so Safari is in that desktop's Dock and opens there as a window. The descriptor also
names `preferences` (the preferences window's component and the frame it opens with), which
the Desktop demo opens from Safari > Preferences… as a second window. Both components take what a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window) hands to its `render` function, so the
[Window Manager](/components/window-manager) can host them as they are:

## On the Ferrite desktop app

When the [Ferrite desktop application](https://github.com/iGentAI/ferrite-showcase-macalicious/tree/main/ferrite-apps/desktop)
serves the desktop, Safari opens real pages. A Ferrite application reaches the network only
through destinations it declares in advance, so it works in two ways:

- A page of a listed site (Wikipedia and its search, news, developer documentation, Marginalia
  search and a few more) is fetched by the server module and made safe there: no scripts, frames
  or handlers; its pictures and stylesheets fetched by the server too, each address checked, and
  put in the page (stylesheets reduced to a strict subset); and a Content-Security-Policy that
  allows Safari's own link relay and no network source at all, so the page makes no request of
  its own. It is shown in a sandboxed frame without `allow-same-origin`. Links and GET forms
  open in Safari; Command-click opens a new tab; Reader shows the article.
- Any other page is neither fetched nor framed. Safari says it opens outside Safari on Ferrite
  and offers **Open in New Browser Tab**, which opens it in a tab of the visitor's own browser
  and adds it to the history. Back and Forward step over it like any other page.

Addresses that are not `http` or `https`, use a user name, another port, or point at a loopback,
private, link-local or metadata address are refused with Safari's error page, as are pages over
the 1 MB a Ferrite application can fetch and servers that do not answer in time. The Favorites
(the Favorites bar and the start page), other bookmarks, the Reading List, the open tabs and the
history (searchable on the History page, and the start page's Frequently Visited) are kept on the
server for the session. The kept tabs are the ones Safari opens with; a window that is already
open keeps its own tabs, with their Back and Forward, when the desktop reloads Safari's data in
the background, which it does when another browser or tab changed it, never for the page's own
saves. Another app can open an address in an open Safari window with
`window.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent("macalicious:open-url", { detail: { url }, cancelable: true }))`;
the window calls `preventDefault()` when it takes it.

Every other build, the documentation included, keeps the demo's own placeholder pages.

A preferences window sizes itself to its pane, so it is hosted as a window that cannot be
resized, at its own width, and the example gives the manager's frame the height the window
has whenever that changes (`useWindowManagerStore().patch`). What is chosen in a pane is kept with
the person's desktop values (`safari-settings.ts`), where every Safari window reads and acts on it;
served by the Ferrite desktop app, a control nothing could carry out there is dimmed and its pane
says why. The [Preferences Window](/components/preferences-window) does not
pass on the drag-region callbacks of its title bar, so `SafariPreferences` watches its root
for a press on the bar instead and reports it through the same two callbacks.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named "Safari"; the web view inside it is a `region` named after
  the page ("Start Page", "Tab Overview", `Reader: ` and the page's title in Reader).
- The toolbar is one tab stop with the arrow keys between its items, the address field
  included; the tab bar is a `tablist` in which the arrow keys move focus and Space or Return
  selects, so passing over a tab does not load it. Delete closes the focused tab.
- The sidebar button and the Tab Overview button change their name with their state ("Show
  Sidebar", "Hide Sidebar"); the Reader button is a toggle with `aria-pressed`.
- In the Tab Overview each thumbnail is a button named by its page's title, the front tab's
  marked `aria-current`; the miniatures are hidden from assistive technology.
- A link on a placeholder page is a real link; activating it loads the page in the tab.
- The Share button opens a `menu` of `menuitem`s; while there is nothing to share it is
  dimmed and stays in the toolbar's arrow-key order.
- The floating video is a `group` named "Picture in Picture" with Close, Return to Window and
  Pause buttons; Escape closes it from inside. The drawing in it is an `img` named
  "Placeholder video", and the page says in text that its video is playing there.

### What is masked

Stand-in artwork is abstract: it is not drawn to resemble the pictures in the captures, and it
is left out of every comparison together with the text. The demo's generated artwork (touch
icons, page photographs and the video's still) is never compared: it lies under the masks
below, and where a check samples a picture, the floating video, the visual example sets
`artwork={false}` to show the placeholder drawing instead.

- **Text**: the address or placeholder in the Smart Search field, the band of the tab titles,
  the sidebar's rows, the Favorites heading and the tile titles, the thumbnails' titles. It is
  the demo's own and is set in the fallback font off Apple platforms.
- **Favorites tiles**: each 80 pt tile whole. The captures have site artwork there; the demo
  has a letter on a plain ground. The tiles' shadows are compared.
- **Reader**: the article on the sheet, from 15 pt under its top edge, and the sheet's whole
  width from the row where the capture's photograph can begin. The demo has placeholder text
  and a placeholder picture of two gradients.
- **Tab Overview**: the miniatures, pictures of third-party pages in the captures and
  schematic blocks in the demo.
- **Sidebar switch**: its two glyphs, the demo's own drawings.
- **Sharing**: the window's rounded corner, where the capture shows the desktop picture, and
  the top of the menu under the toolbar, whose material shows the page behind it.
- **Picture in Picture**: the video, a third-party frame in the capture and a placeholder of
  two gradients and a tinted band in the demo; the window's rounded corner; the Reader button
  the demo's page has and the captured site has not; the speaker the demo does not draw.
- **Preferences**: the Homepage field, which the captures caught focused.

Each comparison's description in `safari.visual.json` gives its masks in full.
