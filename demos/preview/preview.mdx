<Example name="basic" />

Choose a thumbnail to go to its page, scroll the document, zoom and rotate it, and type in the
search field to list the pages that hold a word (try “pilot”). On this page Preview is the
window of the reference captures: nothing is opened, saved or shared. The booklet is invented
and is set from its text; the picture and the banner of a page are abstract placeholders, and
each glyph and thumbnail is an original stand-in drawing. On the desktop that the Ferrite
application serves, Preview opens the person's own files (below).

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with a unified [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) that
  carries the document's proxy icon and, for a PDF, the page shown: “Island Ferry.pdf (page 9
  of 22)”.
- Its [Toolbar](/components/toolbar), in the order of the screenshots and, but for the View
  button, at the widths measured in them: the View button, a
  [Toolbar Pull Down](/components/toolbar-pop-up) with the sidebar
  [System Image](/components/system-image), whose menu
  of [Menu Radio Items](/components/menu-item) hides and shows the sidebar; zoom out and zoom
  in, the separated pair of a momentary
  [Toolbar Segmented Control](/components/toolbar-segmented-control); a
  [Share Menu](/components/share-menu); after the flexible space the Highlight button, a
  [Toolbar Split Pull Down](/components/toolbar-pop-up) whose main part switches the
  highlighting of the text found on and off and whose menu chooses its colour; Rotate Left, a
  [Toolbar Button](/components/toolbar-button); Show Markup Toolbar, a toolbar button that is
  a toggle; and a [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field).
- A [Split View](/components/split-view) of the sidebar and the document, with a divider that
  can be dragged and a sidebar that collapses.
- The sidebar: the file's name over a [List View](/components/list-view) of 82 pt rows, one
  thumbnail for every page. Choosing a row, with the pointer or the arrow keys, brings its
  page to the top of the document, the last page too: under it the canvas runs on for as much
  of the view as a page leaves. Scrolling the document moves the selection with it.
- The document, in a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view) that scrolls both ways. A page is
  98.5% of the view wide at first, as in the screenshots, times the zoom; the pane is a CSS
  size container, so the size of a page is not measured in script. A page is set from its
  text in em of its own width and scales as one picture. When the view changes width, as the
  divider is dragged or the window resized, the pages change size with it and the page shown
  is put back at the top of the view.
- Search. With text in the field the window gets Preview's find bar, a
  [Scope Bar](/components/scope-bar) on the toolbar surface: the scopes sort the pages found
  by page or by the number of matches, a joined pair of actions steps through them and Done
  ends the search. The sidebar then lists the pages found, each with its number of matches
  and the sentence of the first, and every occurrence is marked in the document.
- Show Markup Toolbar puts a [Markup Toolbar](/components/markup-toolbar) under the toolbar.
  Its drawing tools can be chosen; nothing is drawn on the document.

## An image

<Example name="image" />

Both Preview screenshots show a picture: it fills the window, the sidebar is hidden and the
Highlight button is unavailable. An image document is an [Image View](/components/image-view)
that fills the view, times the zoom, and turns with Rotate Left; its thumbnail is the one row
of the sidebar. The demo's image document, `desktopPicture`, is an original generated
photograph of a bay at sunrise, not drawn after the picture in the screenshots; an image
document without an address shows an abstract placeholder picture.

## Preview on the Ferrite desktop

On the desktop that the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves, `app.ts` hosts
`PreviewApp` (`preview-app.tsx`), which works on the person's own files in their drive
(`demos/desktop/desktop-files.ts`). Where no drive is given, as on this site, `PreviewApp` is
the `Preview` window above.

- **Open.** The window starts with the drive's Open dialog (the shared `DriveOpenPanel`) on
  Pictures; Open… in the toolbar and Command-O show it again. Pictures (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP,
  BMP, SVG, AVIF) and PDFs open; the file is read from the drive in pieces and marked opened,
  for Recents. On the desktop each file has a window of its own: with a document open, Open…, Open
  Recent, New from Clipboard and a file sent from Finder open another window, the window that
  already has a file comes to the front, and each window comes back with its file after a reload.
- **Pages and thumbnails.** A picture is drawn on a canvas; a PDF's pages are drawn by
  Mozilla's pdf.js (`pdfjs-dist`), loaded only when the first PDF is opened, each page when it
  comes near the view. The sidebar has a thumbnail of every page, with its markup, and choosing
  one goes to its page; the title says the page shown.
- **Zoom and turn.** Zoom In and Zoom Out (Command-plus and Command-minus), Actual Size
  (Command-0) and Zoom to Fit (Command-9); Rotate Left and Rotate Right (Command-L,
  Command-R). A PDF's turn is kept with its markup; a picture's is an edit until it is saved.
  Tools has Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical for a picture: they flip what the window shows,
  with its marks, and Save draws the flip in. A PDF's pages are not flipped, as on a Mac.
- **A PDF's pages.** In the sidebar, Command or Shift chooses several. Dragging moves them, with
  the marks on them; the Delete key, Edit's Delete and the thumbnail's menu take them out; Edit >
  Insert > Blank Page adds one after the page chosen. Undo goes back. The file itself stays as it
  is: the order is kept beside it with the markup, and Export writes a PDF of the pages as shown.
- **Search a PDF.** The search field reads each page's text with pdf.js, lists the pages found
  with their number of matches and the words around the first, goes to the first, and colours
  every place it is found on a page.
- **Mark a PDF's text.** Tools > Annotate > Highlight Text (Control-Command-H), Underline Text
  and Strikethrough Text mark the text selected; with nothing selected they, or the toolbar's
  Highlight button, mark what is dragged over next, until the button is pressed again. Its menu
  chooses a colour, Underline or Strikethrough, and changes a mark that is chosen. Note
  (Control-Command-N, or the markup bar) places a note with a click; pressing it opens it to
  read and change. View > Highlights and Notes lists them all: a row goes to its page and
  chooses the mark, which Delete takes off.
- **Markup.** Show Markup Toolbar (Shift-Command-A) gives Select, Rectangular Selection (a
  picture's crop), Sketch, Shapes (Line, Arrow, Rectangle, Oval), Text and Sign, with the line
  width, the border and fill colours and the text size. A mark can be chosen, moved and
  deleted (Delete); a text is edited with a double click; Undo (Command-Z) takes back the last
  change. Sign → Create Signature… opens a pad to sign on; the signature is kept for the person
  (at most ten) and placed on the page. Markup is saved beside the file on the server (the
  `preview` module: `GET`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/preview/markup/<file id>`, and
  `/api/preview/signatures`), in fractions of the page, so it stays editable and is drawn again
  when the file is opened later, in any browser of that person.
- **Crop and resize a picture.** Rectangular, Elliptical or Lasso Selection, then Crop
  (Command-K); Adjust Size fits it into a size, or takes a width, height and resolution in the
  units chosen, in proportion or not.
- **Save.** File → Save writes a picture back in its own format (PNG, JPEG, WebP) with its
  turn, crop, size and markup drawn in, as Preview flattens a picture it saves; the file it
  replaces goes to the drive's Trash with its markup still kept beside it, so putting it back
  from the Trash brings its markup back; the saved picture is a new file, with none. A GIF or SVG
  picture, which cannot be written back as itself, is exported instead. A PDF has nothing to
  save: its markup is kept as it is drawn.
- **Export.** File → Export… saves a copy to the drive as JPEG, PNG or PDF, through the shared
  Save dialog; Download as JPEG, PNG or PDF hands the same copy to the computer. A picture
  exports as itself or as a one-page PDF; a PDF exports as a PDF of every page, or the page
  shown as a picture. An exported PDF is written in the browser, each page a 150 dpi JPEG of the
  page as Preview shows it with its markup drawn in: the copy's text cannot be selected or
  searched.

Limits: the markup of at most 1,000 files is kept per person, at most 500 marks a file. A file
is at most the drive's 64 MiB. A PDF's links can be followed; its forms and annotations already in
the file are drawn as pdf.js draws them, not edited. Markup on
a PDF is not written into the PDF itself, only into an exported copy, where a note is its icon
alone: what it says stays beside the file.

## Hosting the window

`Preview` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so
it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and the props that the component takes out before the rest goes to the
`Window`. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { previewApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={previewApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...previewApp.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <previewApp.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `document` | `PreviewDocument` | A PDF (`{ kind: "pdf", name, pages }`, each page its headings and paragraphs; a section line belongs to a page with a title; without pages the window shows “No Pages”) or an image (`{ kind: "image", name, src?, alt, aspect }`); the booklet `islandFerry` by default. The window keeps the state of the document it was given first: give it a `key` to open another. |
| `defaultPage`, `onPageChange` | `number`, `(page: number) => void` | The page shown at first (1), and the page that comes to the top of the view. With a `defaultQuery`, the `defaultPage` is the page shown at first if the text is found on it, and also if the text is found nowhere; if it is found on other pages only, the window opens on the first of them. |
| `defaultQuery` | `string` | The text in the search field at first. The window opens on the `defaultPage` if that is one of the pages found, or else on the first page found in the `defaultSort` order, with its row selected in the sidebar; when nothing is found, on the `defaultPage`. |
| `defaultSort` | `"page" \| "rank"` | The order of the pages found at first (`"page"`). |
| `defaultSidebar` | `"thumbnails" \| "hidden"` | What the sidebar shows while nothing is searched for: thumbnails for a PDF and hidden for an image by default. |
| `defaultZoom` | `number` | The zoom at first, as a multiple of the size that fits the view (1). |
| `onShare` | `(service: "mail" \| "messages" \| "notes") => void` | Called with the service chosen in the Share menu. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `controlsProps` | `WindowControlsProps` | Further props for the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Called for the title bar outside its controls. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1147 by 793 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `previewApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (1147 by 793), `minSize` (600 by 360), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. It also exports the two documents,
`islandFerry` and `desktopPicture`, the document types and `searchDocument`, which finds the
pages of a PDF that hold a text.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title, which says the page shown.
- The toolbar is one tab stop with arrow keys between its items. Every item has a name: View,
  Zoom Out, Zoom In, Share, Highlight (or Stop Highlighting) and Highlight Color, Rotate Left,
  Show Markup Toolbar, which reports whether it is on, and Search. An item that is
  unavailable stays reachable and says so.
- The sidebar is a `listbox`, named Thumbnails or Pages found: the arrow keys move the
  selection, and with it the document. A row's name is its page, and for a page found the
  number of matches and the words around the first.
- The document is a focusable `document` named by the file, so it scrolls from the keyboard;
  every page in it is a `region` named “Page 9”, and an image has its description.
- The find bar's scopes are a radio group labelled by “Sort by:”. The number of pages found
  is a `status`, so it is announced as the search changes; Previous and Next are disabled at
  the ends of the list. Done empties the search field and returns the focus to it.
- Text found is a `mark` in the document, also while its highlight colour is switched off.

from `npm run visual -- preview/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font: the share of
pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

The masked rows count a pixel as differing at a colour distance of 0.05, half the default.
Every comparison masks the demo's artwork, which stands for pictures that are not
reproduced: the placeholder picture of an image document, the banner of a page that opens a
section, and the page miniatures of the sidebar. The other search rows measure mostly the
document's text: the pages are the demo's own where the captures show another booklet.

Four more checks run the window in the browser and read pixels after a click, in both
appearances: choosing the row of a page found, and choosing a thumbnail, select that row and
bring a page to the top of the document, 4 pt under the bar (`choose-found-page-*`,
`choose-thumbnail-*`). Which page it is cannot be read from pixels:
`preview.browser.test.tsx` drives the same window in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, whichever
are installed, and reads the title, the
selected row and the place of the page, after choosing a thumbnail, choosing the last page,
scrolling, searching and choosing a page found, dragging the divider and resizing the window.
Each of its cases loads the window anew. The window scrolls a page it brings to the top to a
whole point, since the engines treat a fraction of a scroll position differently, so the
page starts within half a point of those 4 pt in each of them.

- The captures' PDF is a published booklet and their picture a desktop picture of macOS;
  neither is reproduced. The demo's booklet is invented and set as HTML in the layout of the
  captured pages (a banner, a narrow and a wide column; two columns on the other pages), with
  an abstract placeholder where the capture has a photograph; the comparisons mask it. A PDF is not parsed or rendered: a
  document is the data it is given.
- The picture covers 92% of the Preview captures, more than a comparison may mask, so only
  the strip with the title bar and the toolbar is compared with them.
- Every toolbar glyph but the View button's and the Share button's, which are the library's
  [System Image](/components/system-image) and Share Menu glyph, and the proxy icon, the
  thumbnails and the Share menu's icons are original stand-in drawings; Apple's artwork is not
  reproduced.
- The Aqua capture's sidebar shows the pages found without their matches and words; the Dark
  Aqua one shows both. The demo shows both in either appearance.
- The captured PDF window is vibrant: in Dark Aqua the white paper behind its bars lightens
  them, from the sidebar's edge on (`#6c6d6f` beside `#38393b` at the top of the title bar).
  The demo's bars are the library's, the same from edge to edge, so the Dark Aqua layout
  comparison masks the bars from 125 pt on and their bezels are scored by the
  `desktop-dark-layout` comparison. In Aqua the captured title bar is up to nine levels
  lighter at its lower edge than the library's. In the Dark Aqua Preview capture the bars
  are darker than the library's by about four levels, over a dark picture.
- The View button is the library's toolbar pull-down with the library's sidebar image, 47 pt
  wide where Preview's is 36 pt, so the zoom pair and the Share button sit 11 pt further
  right than in the captures. That is most of what the `desktop-*-layout` rows measure.
- The find bar is the library's Scope Bar: its label and scopes are a little wider apart than
  Preview's, and Previous is disabled on the first page found, where the Aqua capture shows
  both arrows disabled and the Dark Aqua one both enabled.
- The etched line under a sidebar row is drawn by the demo's row, 14 pt from either edge as
  measured; the List View's own separator is a single line to the trailing edge.
- The overlay scroll bar is visible in the captures and shows here only while scrolling.
- The thumbnail sidebar outside a search, the markup bar, the menus, zooming, rotating and
  everything the demo does when a button is pressed have no reference screenshot; they are
  composed from library components to Catalina's conventions. No screenshot shows the end of
  a document either: the canvas that runs on under the last page, so that it comes to the top
  of the view, is the demo's own. Preview's table of contents,
  contact sheet, annotations, cropping and its other tools are not part of the demo.
