<Example name="basic" />

Press play, skip 15 seconds either way, or drag the waveform or the red handle of the
overview: the position, the ruler and the timer follow. Done shows the list of recordings;
choosing one there shows it in the player. On this page no sound is played or recorded: every
recording is an invented amplitude envelope, playing moves the position at one second a second,
and the recordings live in the page for as long as it is open. Every glyph is an original
drawing. On the desktop served by the Ferrite application the same window is a working app,
`VoiceMemosApp`: it records from the microphone, keeps each recording as an audio file on the
person's drive and plays it back (see [On the Ferrite desktop](#on-the-ferrite-desktop)).

The screenshots show a recording that is being made, 3 seconds in: the pill holds a pause
mark and Trim is unavailable. The demo opens on that moment and holds it, so a page that shows
it stays still: no time passes until something is pressed. Pause the take and resume it, and
the recording grows for as long as it runs, by more of the same invented envelope.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) that holds a [Catalyst Chrome](/components/catalyst-chrome)
  with `underToolbar`: the black content starts at the window's top edge and shows through
  the 38 pt toolbar row, which is why the same toolbar is a darker grey here than over white
  content. The row holds the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), the recording's
  title, which names the window, and a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) with one
  [Toolbar Button](/components/toolbar-button), and is the window's drag region.
- The [Waveform View](/components/waveform-view) with both of its parts: the detailed view,
  whose bars and ruler move under a playhead that stays at the middle, and the overview, a
  strip of the whole recording whose handle is the slider that seeks. The demo owns the
  position (`value`, `onValueChange`), so its transport buttons, its clock and the view's own
  dragging and keys all move one number. With `span` the strip stands for 20 seconds when the
  recording is shorter, as in the screenshots.
- What the library does not have and the blueprint lists as the app's own: the timer, and the
  bottom bar with the outlined record pill, the three transport buttons and the Done pill.
  They are plain buttons, drawn in `voice-memos.css` from the colours and sizes in
  `voice-memos.tokens.css`.
- Behind Done, the list of recordings: a `CatalystSidebar` of the chrome that holds a
  [Search Field](/components/search-field), a [List View](/components/list-view) with a title,
  a date and a length to a row, and a round button that starts a recording. Where a search
  finds nothing, and in a window without recordings, an
  [Empty State](/components/empty-state) says so.

<Example name="library" />

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Play | Moves the position at one second a second and becomes Pause; at the end of the recording it stops and becomes Play again. From the end, it starts over. |
| Skip back, skip forward | Move the position 15 seconds, no further than the start or the end. |
| The waveform | Dragging it moves the recording under the playhead: 77 pt are one second. |
| The overview's handle | Dragging it, or the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End while it has the focus, seek. |
| The record pill | Pauses the take, and shows Resume; Resume continues the take at the end of the recording, whatever the position was, and the recording grows until the pill is pressed again (or for an hour). Playing, skipping or seeking pauses a take too. |
| Done | Ends a take and shows the list of recordings beside the player. |
| A row of the list | Shows that recording from its start. Return, or a double click, plays it. |
| Search | Keeps the rows whose title contains the text. |
| The round red button | Starts a recording under the next free title, "New Recording 5", in the editing view. |
| Edit | Opens the recording shown over the whole window, paused. |
| Trim | Listed, as in the screenshots, and unavailable in the demo. On the Ferrite desktop it trims the recording shown (below). |

The timer writes minutes, seconds and hundredths, cut and not rounded (`formatTimer` in
`voice-memos-data.ts`): the two captured recordings are 3.095 s and 3.065 s long and read
`00:03.09` and `00:03.06`. The envelopes are computed from a seed with integer arithmetic
(`envelope`), so the server and every browser draw the same bars.

## Hosting the window

<Example name="hosted" />

`VoiceMemos` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it,
so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state`, the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and
to the toolbar row, which is this window's drag region. These are the props a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form supplies, so the component can be
spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { app } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={app.id}
  title={app.name}
  resizable={app.resizable}
  minWidth={app.minSize.width}
  minHeight={app.minSize.height}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...app.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <app.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `CatalystChrome`: a press with the primary button, or a double click, on the toolbar row that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 789 by 630 by default, as captured. |
| `state` | `"key" \| "main" \| "inactive"` | The window's state. |
| `defaultRecordings` | `VoiceMemosRecording[]` | The recordings the window opens with; the six of `voice-memos-data.ts` by default. |
| `defaultRecordingId` | `string` | The recording shown first: `"new-recording-3"`, the Aqua capture's, by default; the Dark Aqua capture shows `"new-recording-4"`. |
| `defaultView` | `"editor" \| "library"` | One recording over the whole window, as captured, or the list beside the player. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer and focus events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `voiceMemosApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (789 by 630), `minSize` (560 by 600, which holds the
waveform, the overview, the timer and the bar with its two pills), `resizable` and `menus`,
the menu titles after the Apple menu. Its `component` is `VoiceMemosApp`, which takes the same
props: where the page is served by the desktop's Ferrite application it is the working app
below, and everywhere else, as on this site, it renders `VoiceMemos` and requests nothing. Its
`drive` prop names where recordings are kept (`VoiceMemosDrive` in `voice-memos-drive.ts`); by
default the person's drive when the desktop app serves the page, otherwise none, and `null`
asks for the demo.

At other sizes the waveform keeps its scale and its playhead stays at the middle of the
player, the overview keeps 33 pt from either side, the bar stays at the bottom edge with the
transport centred, and the space between the timer and the bar takes up the difference.

`voice-memos.tsx` exports the parts both windows are built from: `VoiceMemosFrame` (the window,
the Catalyst chrome and the black player), `VoiceMemosLibrary` (the sidebar's search field,
list and record button), `VoiceMemosWaveform`, `VoiceMemosTimer`, `VoiceMemosTransport` and
`VoiceMemosTrimButton`.

### On the Ferrite desktop

`VoiceMemosApp` keeps the person's recordings as audio files in a `Voice Memos` folder inside
the Music folder of their drive (`demos/desktop/desktop-files.ts`), so each person, signed in
or a guest, has their own, and Finder shows the same files. The first recording makes the
folder.

- **Recording.** The round red button asks for the microphone (`getUserMedia`) and starts a
  take in the editing view: the timer runs, and the waveform draws the microphone's level
  (an `AnalyserNode`) fifty times a second. The pill pauses and resumes the take; Done ends it
  and uploads it. A take is named as on a Mac, "New Recording", then "New Recording 2", with the
  first number no recording here or in Recently Deleted has, and is saved under that name and no
  other: the drive refuses a name that is taken rather than choosing another, and answers the
  file of an earlier save of the same take (one whose answer was lost) rather than making a
  second, so a save tried again never makes a copy. When another window took the number
  meanwhile, with other sound, the take moves to the next. The take is recorded by
  `MediaRecorder` in the first of Ogg Opus
  (Firefox), WebM Opus
  (Chromium) and MP4 (Safari) the browser records, and saved with that extension (`.ogg`,
  `.webm`, `.m4a`). A browser without `MediaRecorder` (WebKit on Linux) records the
  microphone's samples through Web Audio and saves a 16-bit WAV file. A take stops by itself
  at an hour, or as a WAV file at the 11 or 12 minutes the drive holds of one, and says so. Replace and Resume write WAV, so a recording longer than 12 minutes has neither, and is trimmed to 12 minutes at most. The microphone is released when the take ends or the window closes; a take
  under way when the window closes is stopped and saved.
- **A take the drive refuses** stays in the window's list, first, as a "Not saved" row that
  plays from what was recorded, with Retry and Discard in the toolbar (Discard asks first),
  until it is saved or discarded. It is also held in this browser's IndexedDB, for the person
  signed in, from before its save until the drive has it: closed windows and reloaded pages
  bring it back as a "Not saved" row, tried again at once. Another browser does not have it, so
  a window with a take under way or not saved still asks before it closes, and the browser asks
  before the page is left.
- **The list.** The audio files of the folder that are not in the Trash, newest first, with
  the date each was made and its length, which the browser decodes (`decodeAudioData`), one
  file at a time, and keeps by file in this browser's IndexedDB, for the person signed in, so a
  reload decodes nothing again. A change made in
  Finder (a rename, a move to the Trash) changes the list at once.
- **Dragging out.** A row, or the chosen rows, drag out as their files: Finder and the desktop
  copy them, and Mail's new messages and Messages attach them. Notes refuses sound, saying what it
  takes.
- **Where the person left off.** The recording shown, its playhead and whether the list stands
  beside the player are kept with the person's desktop settings (`voice-memos.place`), and come
  back when the window opens again, in any browser.
- **Playback.** The chosen recording's file is read from the drive and played by an
  `<audio>` element; the waveform shows its decoded peaks and the playhead follows the audio.
  Play, Pause, the two skips and dragging the waveform or the overview seek in it. A pinch on the
  waveform zooms it, from 20 to 308 points a second. Once a recording has played, the keyboard's
  media keys and the browser's Now Playing name it and control it (the Media Session API), until
  the window closes. A WebM file
  from `MediaRecorder`, which names no length, is first sought past its end so that the browser
  finds its length and can seek.
- **Rename.** A double click on a row, or Return on it, makes its title a field: Return or leaving
  the field renames, Escape does not. Rename in the toolbar and the menus opens a sheet with the
  title, since the list can be hidden; the file keeps its extension. A name the folder already has is refused.
- **Delete.** Delete in the toolbar, or the Delete key in the list, moves the file to the
  drive's Trash, from where Finder puts it back.
- **Recently Deleted.** The row under the list shows the recordings of the folder that are in
  the drive's Trash, the last deleted first. One plays as before. Recover puts it back; Delete
  asks, then removes it for good. Finder's Trash is the same place.
- **Preferences.** The application menu's Preferences (Command-comma) opens a panel with Audio
  Quality, Compressed or Lossless, which writes the next takes as WAV, and Clear Deleted, when a
  recording in Recently Deleted leaves the drive: Immediately, After 1, 7 or 30 Days (the
  default), or Never. Location-based Naming asks the browser where you are and names new
  recordings after the place. They are kept for the person on the server.
- **Share.** File, the toolbar and a row's menu hand the file to the apps that take files, and
  say why AirDrop cannot.
- **Trim and Delete.** In the editing view, the Trim button puts two yellow handles over the
  overview's strip; moving one moves the playhead to it, so the waveform shows the cut. Trim keeps
  the sound between them and Delete keeps the sound around them, and the window then shows and
  plays the result. Edit's Undo and Redo step through those changes, and Revert goes back to the
  recording as it was. Save decodes the recording, cuts it, and uploads a 16-bit WAV file with its
  channels as they were, under the same title beside the original, which goes to the Trash only
  once the new file is kept. Handles moved but not pressed count at Save as a Trim. Cancel leaves
  the recording as it was.
- **Resume and Replace.** In the editing view, the pill says Resume while the playhead is at the
  end and Replace anywhere else; Edit has Replace too. Recording goes over what is there from the
  playhead, or adds to the end, and Done saves the two as one WAV file in the original's place,
  whose old file goes to the Trash. The new take is held in the browser until then. If the change
  cannot be saved, the recording stays as it was and the new take becomes a recording of its own.
- **Edit menu.** Undo to Select All act on the search field when it has the keyboard. Elsewhere
  Select All, and Command-A, choose every recording the list shows, and Cut, Copy and Paste are
  dimmed with the reason. While a recording is trimmed, Undo and Redo step through its changes,
  Select All moves the handles to its two ends, and Cut, Copy and Paste stay dimmed, since a browser
  takes no sound onto the clipboard.
- **Several recordings.** Command-click and Shift-click choose several rows. Delete, Duplicate and
  Share act on all of them, and Recover and Delete in Recently Deleted too; Rename and Show in
  Finder wait for one.
- **Messages.** A refused microphone, a browser that cannot record, and a drive that refuses
  a change are said in a banner at the top of the player, never only in the console; a save
  that fails offers to try again with the same recording.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title, which is the title of the recording shown.
  The toolbar is one tab stop. Trim shows only a glyph and is named with `aria-label`; it is
  unavailable through `aria-disabled` and keeps its place in the toolbar's arrow-key order.
- The waveform is a `group` named by the recording's title. The detailed view is an image,
  "Waveform of New Recording 3"; the overview is one `slider`, "Recording position", from 0
  to the length in seconds with the position as its value text, "00:03". Everything the
  pointer can do to the position, the slider's keys and the two skip buttons can do too.
- The timer has the `timer` role and the name "Position". It is not a live region that
  announces: it changes a hundred times a second while something runs.
- The transport is a `group` named "Playback" of three buttons named with `aria-label`:
  "Skip Back 15 Seconds", "Play" and "Skip Forward 15 Seconds". The middle one is named
  "Pause" while the recording plays; its name, not `aria-pressed`, says what a press does.
- The record pill is named "Pause Recording" while it shows the pause mark and "Resume
  Recording" while it shows "Resume", so its name contains its text.
- The list of recordings is a `navigation` landmark named "Recordings" that holds a
  `searchbox` named "Search recordings", a `listbox` named "Recordings" whose options read a
  title, a date and a length, and a button named "New Recording". The arrow keys choose a
  row, typing finds one by title and Return plays it. An empty search result is a `status`.
- The app's own buttons show the focus ring of the library, in the key window only.
- The content is dark in both appearances, as in Voice Memos. The transport's grey on the
  bar is about 4.5:1; the ruler's and the overview's labels are about 3:1, as measured, which
  is below what small text needs (see [Waveform View](/components/waveform-view)).
- Nothing moves by itself: the waveform scrolls only while the recording plays or a take runs,
  which the person has started.
- In forced-colours mode the bar, the pills and the transport take the system's button
  colours.
- On the Ferrite desktop, a problem is a banner with the `alert` role and work under way
  (saving, trimming) one with the `status` role; the window is `aria-busy` meanwhile. The
  trim handles are a `group` named "Trim" with two sliders, "Trim start" and "Trim end", whose
  value text is the time; the arrow keys move them a hundredth of a second, Page Up and Page
  Down a second, Home and End to either end. The buttons by the timer are named "Trim to
  Selection" and "Delete Selection". A row's title being renamed is a field named "Recording
  name". Rename from the toolbar is a sheet named
  "Rename Recording" whose field is "Recording name"; Return renames.

## Fidelity

`voice-memos.visual.json` compares the demo with the two Voice Memos screenshots, the whole
capture with the window's shadow in each appearance: once as it is (`window-light`,
`window-dark`), and once with the text, the stand-in glyphs and the bars masked
(`window-layout-light`, `window-layout-dark`). The masks cover the title, the Trim glyph, the
bars of the waveform and of the overview, the ruler's and the overview's labels, the timer,
the two skip glyphs and the label of Done, a tenth of the capture; the frame and its shadow, the
toolbar, the panel, the playhead, the ticks, the strip and its handle, the bar, the record
pill with its pause mark, the play triangle and the Done pill are scored. The two captures
show different recordings, so each appearance has its own example.

Scores from `npm run visual -- voice-memos/` (Chromium on Linux, fallback font), share of
pixels that differ and mean colour distance out of 255:

| Comparison | 1x | 2x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 2.1%, 1.9 | 1.7%, 2.0 |
| `window-layout-light` | 0.5%, 0.4 | 0.3%, 0.5 |
| `window-dark` | 1.8%, 1.7 | 1.6%, 1.9 |
| `window-layout-dark` | 0.5%, 0.4 | 0.2%, 0.5 |

The budgets in `voice-memos.visual.json` are set just above these. Two more checks press
the buttons in a real browser and read pixels, because no screenshot shows what follows:
`done-shows-library` (Done brings the sidebar with its record button, and the Done pill is
gone) and `play-shows-pause` (a row of the list is chosen, play is pressed, and the triangle
has become the two bars of the pause mark).

Taken from the spec's measurements of the screenshots and laid out to them: the 789 by 630 pt
frame; the 38 pt toolbar row with the centred title and the 39 pt Trim button 7.5 pt from the
trailing edge; the waveform panel from 74.5 pt and the ruler under it; the overview's strip
at 394 pt, 33 pt from either side; the timer's baseline; the bottom bar from 556.5 pt with
its two top rows and its gradient; the record pill, its outline and its pause mark; the
transport glyphs' places and sizes; and the Done pill. The colours and sizes that are the
app's own are in `voice-memos.tokens.css`, each with the place it was measured.

What differs, and why:

- **Bars.** The screenshots do not hold the recordings' samples. The demo's envelopes are
  invented and only follow the captured outlines roughly: quiet, a burst, quiet. They are
  masked in the layout comparisons, and are most of the whole-window score.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices: the timer's digits are wider and
  heavier than San Francisco's, so the timer is wider than in the screenshots.
  The label of Done is set in the semibold weight, which is nearer the capture than the bold
  the spec names.
- **Glyphs.** The crop mark of Trim, the two skip rings with their "15", the play triangle
  and the pause marks are original drawings to the measured sizes. The play triangle and the
  pill's pause mark are near enough to be scored; the others are masked.
- **Dark edges.** The captures have a row of darker pixels around bright shapes on the bar,
  on some sides and not on others, which looks like the trace of the 77% scaling of an iPad
  app. The demo draws a hairline around the record pill's outline, its pause mark and the
  Done pill, which matches most of them and puts one under Done where the capture has none.
- **Window rim in Aqua.** The library's window draws its inner rim as white at 16%; over this
  window's dark content the Aqua capture has it at about 10% (`#191919` over black,
  `#2c2c2c` over the panel). It is the shared window's value and is not changed here; it is
  about 0.16 points of the 0.39% of `window-layout-light`.
- **Title in Aqua.** The capture's title is `#202020`; the library's title bar draws its
  title in black at 62%, which is about `#434343` over this toolbar. It is masked in the
  layout comparisons.
- **Trim's bezel.** The library's toolbar button draws its outline half a point outside its
  box, so the demo gives the button 38 pt for the captured 39, half a point in from where the
  chrome puts it.
- **Overview handle.** The time the strip stands for is fitted (20 s): it puts the handle on
