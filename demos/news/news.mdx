<Example name="basic" />

Click a channel for its stories and a story to read it. Back returns from a story; the Share
button, which works while a story is open, copies its headline or saves it to Saved Stories;
History lists what was opened. Nothing is fetched and nothing is real: where News shows the
stories of the day, with their photographs and their publishers' logos, the demo shows
invented headlines from invented publishers, of the same lengths, and an abstract placeholder
for every picture.

## What it shows

The source is `demos/news/`: `news.tsx` is the app, `news-stories.tsx` a story and a list of
them, `news-data.ts` the channels, the two
editions and the stories, `news-artwork.tsx` the four drawings, `news.css` the feed's layout
and `news.tokens.css` the app's own colours.

- A [Window](/components/window) that holds a [Catalyst Chrome](/components/catalyst-chrome):
  the 38 pt toolbar row with the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), the centred
  title, which names the window, and a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar). The row is the window's
  drag region.
- The toolbar has three [Toolbar Buttons](/components/toolbar-button), 39 pt wide, whose
  glyphs are the library's [System Images](/components/system-image) `go-back`, `sidebar` and
  `share`, and a [Toolbar Search Field](/components/toolbar-search-field), 161 pt wide.
- The sidebar is the chrome's `CatalystSidebar` with three `CatalystSidebarGroup`s of
  `CatalystSidebarItem`s; `selected` marks the row that is shown. It is 227 pt wide in Aqua and
  234 pt in Dark Aqua, as measured, which `news.tsx` sets through the chrome's width variable.
- The stories are in [Card Grids](/components/card-grid): two columns for the leads, three for
  the smaller stories, two for a channel's list. The story itself is the app's own element
  (`Story` in `news-stories.tsx`): an `article` with a picture, the publisher, the headline and the
  story's age, in two layouts that one attribute chooses. The headline is a button, and its
  area is the whole story.
- The grids stand in the feed's column, so their own side margin is set to zero through the
  grid's `--mac-card-grid-margin`, and a list's row gap through `--mac-card-grid-row-gap`. A
  card grid keeps 32 pt of room under its cards, more than the 24 pt between Today's rows, so
  the block after each grid begins 8 pt inside that room (`news.css`).
- The feed's two lines are half a point thick, where the library's separator is a full point:
  each is the edge of one of the app's own elements, the masthead and the row of More Top
  Stories.
- The Share button opens a [Menu](/components/menu) of [Menu Items](/components/menu-item).
- An [Empty State](/components/empty-state) takes the list's place when a destination or a
  search has no stories.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| A row of the sidebar | Shows that channel beside the sidebar and ends a search. Today is the feed of the screenshots; News Top Stories lists all of the day's stories; a topic lists its two. |
| A story | Opens it: the publisher, the headline, the picture's placeholder and three paragraphs that say what the demo is. It is added to History. |
| Back | Returns from a story to where it was opened. Unavailable while no story is open, as captured. |
| The sidebar button | Hides the sidebar, and shows it again. |
| Share | A menu that copies the headline to the clipboard, and saves the story to Saved Stories or removes it from there. Unavailable while no story is open, as captured. |
| Search | Lists the stories whose headline or publisher contains the text, from every channel; Escape empties it. |
| More Top Stories | Under Today's stories: shows the News Top Stories channel. |

`News` takes an `edition`: the date under the masthead and the day's top stories.
`news-data.ts` exports the two editions of the reference screenshots, `lightEdition`, the
default, and `darkEdition`, a day later. Saved Stories and History are kept by the window for
as long as the page is open.

## On the desktop's Ferrite application

On the desktop served by the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application (`ferrite-apps/desktop`),
`News` is a real news reader (`news-live-window.tsx`, with its data in `news-live.ts` and its
requests in `news-live-hooks.ts`); everywhere else, this page included, it is the demo above.

- The stories are real: the server reads the RSS and Atom feeds of 22 publishers (BBC News,
  NPR, The Guardian, The New York Times, Ars Technica and others) and of their topic sections,
  through the application's declared egress, and keeps each feed for 15 minutes for every
  reader. Each story is what its publisher puts in its feed: the headline, a summary, the
  picture, loaded from the publisher, and the link.
- Today leads with the newest stories of the channels the person follows (BBC News, NPR, The
  Guardian and Ars Technica at first), two leads from different publishers. More Top Stories
  lists all of them. Each topic merges the topic feeds of two or three publishers.
- Discover Channels lists every publisher with Follow and Block; a channel's own page has the
  same two buttons. A blocked channel's stories leave Today, the topics and search.
- A story opens as its article when Safari's server route can read its page (`/api/safari/page`,
  the sites Safari lists; nothing is kept, and it stays out of Safari's History), and otherwise with
  its feed's summary and the reason. Read Full Story opens the article at its publisher:
  in Safari when a Safari window is open (the `macalicious:open-url` event), otherwise in a new
  browser tab. The Share menu opens it in Safari, copies its link, or saves it.
- The menu bar carries the commands: File has Follow Channel and Share Story; Edit has Search
  (Command-F); View has Show or Hide Sidebar (Option-Command-S), Go Back (Command-[), Go
  to Today and Reload (Command-R); Story has Save Story (Command-S), Open in Safari and Block
  Channel. Reload asks the server now; it answers the copy it keeps until that is 15 minutes old.
- A secondary click on a story opens, saves, shares or copies it, and in History removes it from
  there. On a channel's row it opens, follows or blocks the channel; on a topic's row it opens or
  reloads the topic.
- The channels followed and blocked, Saved Stories (at most 150) and History (the last 60
  stories) are kept for the person on the server (`/api/news/library`). A saved story keeps a
  short summary (300 characters), so it still says what it is about once its feed has moved on.
- While the window is open it reads its feeds again when they are older than the server keeps
  them, and a line under each title says when they were read or which could not be. A feed that
  could not be read is left alone for two minutes. When the list of channels cannot be read the
  window tries three times, then says News is not available and offers Try Again.

## Hosting the window

<Example name="hosted" />

`News` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and to the
toolbar row, which is this window's drag region. These are the props a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form supplies, so the component can be
spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { app } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={app.id}
  title={app.name}
  resizable={app.resizable}
  minWidth={app.minSize.width}
  minHeight={app.minSize.height}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...app.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <app.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `edition` | `NewsEdition` | The date and the top stories; `lightEdition` by default. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom`, `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Called for a press with the primary button, or a double click, on the toolbar row itself, not on a control in it. |
| `state` | `"key" \| "main" \| "inactive"` | The window's state. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `width` (1222 by default), `height` (842), `style`, `className`, `ref` and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `newsApp` and as `app`: `id`, `name`,
`component`, `defaultSize` (1222 by 842), `minSize` (760 by 420), `resizable` and `menus`, the
menu titles after the Apple menu. The [Desktop](/demos/desktop) finds it there, so News is in
its Dock.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title. The sidebar is a `navigation` landmark named
  Channels; each group of rows is a `group` named by its header, and the row that is shown has
  `aria-current="page"`.
- The pane's title is a level 2 heading: the masthead with the date, a channel's name, or the
  headline of the open story. "Top Stories" is a level 3 heading that names the grid of the
  leads, and every headline is a heading one level under the one that names its list.
- A story's headline is a button, so the keyboard opens a story; the button's area is the
  whole story for the pointer. Opening a story moves the focus to its headline in the reader,
  and Back returns it to the headline the story was opened with. More Top Stories moves it
  to the title of the list it leads to. The pictures' placeholders, the play mark of a video story and
  the rows' icons are decorative and hidden from assistive technology.
- Back and Share stay in the toolbar's arrow-key order while they are unavailable, and say so
  with `aria-disabled`. The sidebar button's name says what it will do: Hide Sidebar or Show
  Sidebar.
- The search field is a `searchbox` named Search.
- The focus ring of a headline and of More Top Stories shows in the key window only.

## Fidelity

`news.visual.json` compares the demo with the two News screenshots, whole, window and shadow,
in each appearance, twice: once with the demo's own content masked (`window-light`,
`window-dark`), and once with the remaining text masked as well (`window-layout-light`,
`window-layout-dark`).

Masked in all four, because the screenshots hold other people's work there and the demo its
own stand-ins, and the comparison is of Catalina's chrome and layout:

- the five photographs, where the demo has an abstract placeholder;
- the sixteen channel icons of the sidebar, where the demo has a tile of one colour with the
  channel's initial;
- the mark of the masthead, Apple's logo in the screenshots and a folded sheet in the demo;
- each story's publisher and headline: the publishers' logos and the news of the day in the
  screenshots, invented names and headlines in the demo;
- in Dark Aqua, the scroll indicator at the pane's edge.

The layout comparisons also mask the window's title, the three toolbar glyphs, the search
field's glass and placeholder, the sidebar's headers and labels, the masthead, "Top Stories",
the stories' ages and "MORE TOP STORIES".

Scores from `npm run visual -- news/` (Chromium on Linux, fallback font), share of pixels that
differ and mean colour distance out of 255:

| Comparison | 1x | 2x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 1.39%, 1.73 | 1.22%, 2.00 |
| `window-layout-light` | 0.04%, 0.43 | 0.01%, 0.63 |
| `window-dark` | 1.30%, 1.67 | 1.20%, 1.93 |
| `window-layout-dark` | 0.03%, 0.60 | 0.07%, 0.82 |

The budgets in `news.visual.json` are set just above these. What the whole-window scores hold
beyond the layout scores is the text that both have, in the fallback font.

Measured on the screenshots and reproduced: the window's frame (1222 by 842 pt in Aqua, 1257
by 842 pt in Dark Aqua) and shadow; the sidebar's width in each appearance; the 820 pt column
centred on the pane; the masthead's two lines and the line under them; "Top Stories" and its
red; the two 399 by 300 pt pictures 22 pt apart; the three smaller stories with their 88 pt
pictures; the line over "MORE TOP STORIES"; and the section's background, which deepens down
the pane and ends 25 pt above the window's foot.

What differs, and why:

- **Content.** No story, photograph, publisher or logo of the screenshots is reproduced: the
  headlines and the five publishers are invented, and the same placeholder stands for every
  picture. The areas are masked in every comparison.
- **Publishers.** A name in capitals where News shows a logo, at one height. The logos of the
  Dark Aqua screenshot are less tall than those of the Aqua one, so its smaller stories, the
  line under them and the section's foot sit 1.5 to 3 pt higher than in the demo, which
  follows the Aqua screenshot. That is most of what the Dark Aqua layout score holds.
- **Icons.** The sixteen channel icons are tiles with an initial; the toolbar's three glyphs
  are the library's System Images.
- **Typeface.** News sets its masthead and headlines in its own condensed face; the demo uses
  the system font at the measured sizes, so lines break at other words.
- **Scroll indicator.** The Dark Aqua screenshot shows the iOS scroll indicator; the demo's
  pane has the browser's thin scroll bar, which shows only when the content is taller than
  the pane.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** every channel but Today; the opened story; the search
  results; the Share menu; Saved Stories and History; the window with the sidebar hidden, and
  when it is not key.
- **Not built:** fetching news, News+ and its issues, following and blocking channels, the
  story's own layout and typography, notifications, and the menu bar's commands.
