<Example name="basic" />

Click a symbol for its chart and figures, a green or red badge to change what every badge
shows, and Business News or the Back button to return to the stories. The search field filters
the watchlist. Nothing is fetched: the symbols, names, prices and changes are the ones the
reference screenshot shows, and everything it does not hold (the course of each session, the
longer histories, the figures under the chart) is computed from the symbol, the same on every
load. Where Stocks shows news photographs the demo shows an abstract placeholder, and the
publishers are text where Stocks shows their logos. On the desktop served by the Macalicious
Ferrite application the quotes, charts and news are real and the watchlist is the person's own
(see [On the Ferrite desktop](#on-the-ferrite-desktop)).

## What it shows

The source is `demos/stocks/`: `stocks.tsx` is the app, `stocks-data.ts` the quotes, the
stories and what is computed from them, `stocks-artwork.tsx` the two drawings, `stocks.css` the
layout and `stocks.tokens.css` the app's own colours.

- A [Window](/components/window) that holds a [Catalyst Chrome](/components/catalyst-chrome):
  the 38 pt toolbar row with the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls), the centred
  title, which names the window, and a [Toolbar](/components/toolbar) with two
  [Toolbar Buttons](/components/toolbar-button), 39 pt wide, whose glyphs are the library's
  [System Images](/components/system-image) `go-back` and `share`. The row is the window's
  drag region.
- The sidebar is the chrome's `CatalystSidebar`, 330 pt wide, holding a custom column, as the
  component allows. In Stocks its divider is half a point of shade where News has a full point
  of grey: the sidebar's `divider="shade"`.
- The search field is the library's [Search Field](/components/search-field) in its `sidebar`
  variant, given the metrics and colours of the iOS field through the field's variables
  (`SEARCH` in `stocks.tsx`): 28 pt tall with 4 pt corners, a flat fill and no edge. It is
  controlled, and `matchesQuery` keeps the quotes whose symbol or name contains the text.
- The watchlist is a `StockList` of [Stock Rows](/components/stock-row): the row draws the
  symbol, the name, the sparkline from `data` and `previousClose`, the price and the badge.
  `onSelect` makes the row a button, and `selected` marks the symbol that is shown;
  `onBadgeClick` makes the badge a button of its own, with `badgeLabel` saying what a click
  will show. One piece of state, the badge mode, feeds every row.
- The stories are in a [Card Grid](/components/card-grid) with two columns, named by the
  heading above it. The story itself is the app's own element (`Story` in `stocks.tsx`): an
  `article` with a picture, the publisher, the headline as a heading and the story's age, in
  four layouts (`lead`, `beside`, `card`, `compact`) that one attribute chooses.
- A symbol's view has a [Segmented Control](/components/segmented-control) for the chart's
  range, the chart (an inline SVG drawn as the stock row draws its sparkline, in the stock
  row's colours), and the figures as a description list.
- The Share button opens a [Menu](/components/menu) of [Menu Items](/components/menu-item).
- An [Empty State](/components/empty-state) takes the list's place when the search matches
  nothing.
- The type of the content is the chrome's (`--mac-catalyst-chrome-font-*`), with the feed's
  own larger sizes in `stocks.css`.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| A row of the watchlist | Shows that symbol beside the sidebar: its price and change, the chart, eight figures and four stories, those about the symbol first. |
| A badge | Changes what every badge shows: the change in price, the change in percent, then the market capitalisation. An index has no capitalisation and keeps its percentage. |
| Business News | Shows the stories again. It is the lighter cell while they are shown. |
| Back | The same, from the toolbar. Unavailable while the stories are shown, as captured. |
| Share | A menu that copies the symbol, or the symbol and its price, to the clipboard. Unavailable while the stories are shown, as captured. |
| Search | Filters the watchlist as you type; Escape empties it. The symbol that is shown stays shown. |
| 1D to 2Y | The chart's range. The day is measured against the previous close, drawn dashed; a longer range against its first price. The range stays when another symbol is chosen. |

`Stocks` takes a `market`: the quotes, how far the session has run, and the feed.
`stocks-data.ts` exports the two markets of the reference screenshots. `lightMarket`, the
default, is a finished session with Disney down and a lead story across the column;
`darkMarket` is the next morning, less than half a session in, with General Electric down and
a lead story that has its text beside the picture.

## Hosting the window

<Example name="hosted" />

`Stocks` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so it
does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and to the
toolbar row, which is this window's drag region. These are the props a
[Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form supplies, so the component can be
spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { app } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={app.id}
  title={app.name}
  resizable={app.resizable}
  minWidth={app.minSize.width}
  minHeight={app.minSize.height}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...app.defaultSize }}
  render={(props) => <app.component {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `market` | `StocksMarket` | The quotes and the feed; `lightMarket` by default. |
| `history` | `(stock, range) => StockHistory \| undefined` | The prices and labels of a chart; computed by the demo when left out or `undefined`. |
| `onSymbolChange`, `onRangeChange`, `onQueryChange` | functions | Called with the symbol shown (`undefined` for the news), the chart's range and the search field's text. |
| `searchResults` | `StockSearchResult[] \| null` | Symbols found for the search, listed under the watchlist to be added. |
| `onAddSymbol`, `onRemoveSymbol` | `(symbol: string) => void` | Add a found symbol; remove one (the Share menu's Remove from Watchlist, Edit > Delete, a row's menu and Edit mode's remove buttons). With `onRemoveSymbol` or `onMoveSymbol` the footer offers Edit. |
| `onRefresh` | `() => void` | Asks for the quotes and the news again: View > Refresh. Dimmed without it. |
| `keptView` | `StocksKeptView` | The last symbol, range and badge and whether the sidebar was hidden, shown until the person chooses otherwise. May arrive after the window opens. |
| `onBadgeModeChange` | `(mode: BadgeMode) => void` | Called when a row's badge is clicked. |
| `onSidebarShownChange` | `(shown: boolean) => void` | Called when View > Show Sidebar or Hide Sidebar is chosen. |
| `alerts` | `StockAlert[]` | Price alerts, shown in their symbol's detail view; none by default. |
| `onAddAlert`, `onRemoveAlert` | functions | With `onAddAlert` the detail view offers the alert form. |
| `footer` | `ReactNode` | The line at the watchlist's foot; `"Sample data"` by default. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `CatalystChrome`: a press with the primary button, or a double click, on the toolbar row that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1148 by 803 by default, as captured. |
| `state` | `"key" \| "main" \| "inactive"` | The window's state. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer and focus events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `stocksApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (1148 by 803), `minSize` (700 by 420), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. The [Desktop](/demos/desktop) finds it there, so
Stocks is in its Dock.

### On the Ferrite desktop

`component` is `DesktopStocks` (`stocks-desktop.tsx`). On the desktop that the
`macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves, it asks the application's Stocks module
(`ferrite-apps/desktop/src/apps/stocks`), which fetches market data through the application's
declared egress from one provider behind a small interface (`provider.rs`). The provider is
CNBC's public quote, chart, symbol and RSS services, which need no key, so live quotes are on
and no secret is needed; a keyed provider (Finnhub or Polygon, say, with its key as a Ferrite
secret) can replace it without a change to the window. These services are not a licensed data
feed, promise no service and their terms were not checked for redistribution, so a deployment
with real traffic should switch. Every provider call is bounded: it gives up after at most 5
seconds, inside the 8-second API deadline, and refuses an answer over 1 MiB.

- The watchlist is the person's own, kept on the server; it starts as the Dow, the S&P 500,
  the Nasdaq Composite and Apple. Typing in the search field lists symbols to add, and the
  Share menu removes the one shown.
- Quotes refresh every minute. A page of quotes is one provider call for every symbol not
  already kept, and each quote is kept for everyone for a minute; only one quotes call is on
  its way at a time for the whole application, and a request that finds it on its way waits
  for its quotes. A symbol the provider does not know is kept as unknown for the minute too.
  So however many windows are open, a symbol costs the provider at most one call a minute. Charts are kept for two minutes (the day) or an hour, symbol searches for a
  day and the news for ten minutes, for everyone. The sparklines are each symbol's day,
  the detail chart is the chosen range (the week is five sessions; two years are cut from five),
  and the figures are the provider's: open, high, low, volume, P/E, market cap and the 52-week
  range.
- Business News is the provider's Business News and Earnings feeds; a headline opens the story
  in the window with what the feed says of it, and its button opens the whole story on its site,
  in Safari. A symbol's view leads with the stories that name it or its company.
- Price alerts: the detail view sets an alert above or below a price. The server checks it
  against every quote it fetches for the person, and when Notification Center or the window
  asks for the alerts, and it fires once.

When the provider cannot be reached the window says so and keeps what it last showed; the server
answers 502 and makes nothing up. On every other page the window is the demo described above
and requests nothing.

At other sizes the sidebar keeps its 330 pt, the 493 pt column stays centred in the pane and
narrows once the pane is narrower than it, and both the watchlist and the pane scroll; the
footer stays at the sidebar's foot.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title, "Stocks". The toolbar is one tab stop; the
  arrow keys move between Back and Share, which show only a glyph and are named with
  `aria-label`. While they are unavailable they stay focusable and say so with
  `aria-disabled`.
- The sidebar is a `navigation` landmark named "Watchlist". Business News is a button with
  `aria-current="page"` while the stories are shown; the mark before "News" is decorative.
- The watchlist is a list named "Symbols". Each row is a button named by its symbol, its name,
  the trend of its sparkline ("Trending up") and its price, with `aria-current` on the one
  that is shown; Up and Down choose the row above or below, Home and End move between the rows,
  and Return or Space chooses.
  The badge is a second button in the tab order, named by what it says and what a click will
  show: "+1.86, show percent change".
- The search field is a `searchbox` named "Search". The list is not a live region: the number
  of matches is not announced.
- The pane's title is a heading, and each story an `article` whose headline is a heading one
  level below. The pictures are hidden from assistive technology. Stories are not links: the
  demo has no article to open.
- The chart is an image named by what it shows: "AAPL, today: from 218.96 to 220.82, +0.85%".
  Its scale and the labels under it are hidden, since the name carries the range's ends. The
  figures are a description list.
- The ranges are toggle buttons in a group named "Range".
- A symbol that is down is red and one that is up green, in the rows and in the chart; the
  sign of the change says the same in text. The change beside the price uses a darker green
  and red than the badges, to keep its contrast as text on the pane.

## Fidelity

`stocks.visual.json` compares the demo with the two Stocks screenshots, the whole capture with
the window's shadow in each appearance: once with only the pictures masked (`window-light`,
`window-dark`), and once with the text and the stand-in glyphs masked as well
(`window-layout-light`, `window-layout-dark`). The pictures are masked in all four: the
captures have news photographs there and the demo a placeholder, and the comparison is of
Catalina's chrome and layout, not of third-party pictures. The masks of the layout comparisons
also cover the window's title and the two toolbar glyphs; the search field's glass and
placeholder; the two lines of the Business News cell; in each row the symbol and name, the
sparkline, the price and the text of the badge; the footer's text; the feed's title; and the
text of every story. The frame and its shadow, the toolbar and its bezels, the sidebar's fills
and lines, the badges and the divider are scored. The Dark Aqua comparisons render
`darkMarket`.

Scores from `npm run visual -- stocks/` (Chromium on Linux, fallback font), share of pixels
that differ and mean colour distance out of 255:

| Comparison | 1x | 2x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 3.9%, 4.9 | 3.4%, 4.9 |
| `window-layout-light` | 0.25%, 0.37 | 0.24%, 0.48 |
| `window-dark` | 4.0%, 4.5 | 3.6%, 4.7 |
| `window-layout-dark` | 0.24%, 0.47 | 0.13%, 0.61 |

The budgets in `stocks.visual.json` are set just above these. What the whole-window scores
hold beyond the layout scores is text: the fallback font, and the publishers' names where the
capture has logos.

Taken from the spec's measurements of the screenshots and laid out to them: the 1148 by 803 pt
frame; the two 39 pt toolbar buttons 77.5 pt from the leading and 7.5 pt from the trailing
edge; the 330 pt sidebar with the 305 by 28 pt search field 5 pt under the toolbar, the line
at 76 pt, the Business News cell under it and the ten rows from 125.5 pt; the footer's line
39 pt above the window's foot; the 493 pt column centred in the pane, its title's cap top at
61.5 pt, the lead picture at 111 pt (493 by 277), the two-up pictures at 502 pt (234.5 by
175.5, 24 pt apart) and the next row at 776 pt; and for the Dark Aqua feed the 299 by 224 pt
lead picture with its text beside it and the rows of pictures from 351 and 625 pt. The colours
that are the app's own are in `stocks.tokens.css`, each with the place it was measured.

What differs, and why:

- **Pictures.** The photographs belong to their publishers and are not reproduced, nor
  imitated: every story shows the same abstract placeholder, a flat panel with the usual mark
  for a picture, in greys that are the demo's own. The pictures' boxes are at the captures'
  places and sizes, and they are masked in every comparison, so their place is held by the
  text and the rows around them, not scored itself.
- **Publishers.** Stocks shows each publisher's logo; the demo writes its name in small
  capitals.
- **"From News".** The cell shows Apple's logo before "News"; the demo draws a folded sheet.
- **Footer.** The capture has the wordmark of the data's provider; the demo says "Sample
  data", which is what it is.
- **Sparklines and prices.** The prices and changes are the captures'. The sessions behind the
  sparklines are computed, so each line has the capture's direction and end points but not its
  course.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices. It is wider than San Francisco at the
  bold weights, so a headline can break a word earlier than in the capture, and the stock
  row cuts the longest company name with an ellipsis where the capture shows it whole.
- **Headline sizes.** The spec gives 15.5 pt for the two-up headlines from their cap height.
  At that size the capture's lines do not fit the column; 13 pt on the measured 16.5 pt pitch
  does (the iOS headline style at 77%), and that is what the demo sets. The lead headline is
  the spec's 20.5 pt on a 24 pt pitch.
- **Toolbar in Dark Aqua.** The bar matches the capture to within two levels down its height:
  the demo's column runs from `#3f4041` at the top to `#353637` at the bottom where the
  capture's runs from `#3e3f41` to `#343537` (sampled in the `window-dark` comparison at 1x).
  The six to eight levels of difference this page used to record are gone; the demo does not
  restyle the bar.
- **Row separators and the search field's edge.** The capture's hairlines fall across two
  device pixel rows; the stock row draws them on one. The capture's field has a soft edge row
  the flat fill does not have.
- **Cut-off stories.** The Aqua capture shows only the first words of its last two stories and
  the Dark Aqua capture only the pictures of its last two; the rest of those headlines, and
  those two stories' publishers and ages, are the demo's.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the whole view of a symbol (its head, the ranges, the
  chart, the figures and its stories), the menu of the Share button, a selected or pressed
  row, the list while it is filtered or empty, the window when it is not key and every size
  other than the captured one.
- **Not built in the demo:** a story's whole text (the reader holds what the feed gives), editing the watchlist, live or delayed quotes, the chart's
  scrubbing, and the layout Stocks takes in a narrow window.
