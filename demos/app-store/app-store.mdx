<Example name="basic" />

Choose a section in the sidebar, type in the search field, press an offer button and confirm
it, open a collection with See All, or sign in. The Discover page repeats the words of the
reference screenshot; the other seven pages, their stories and their apps are invented.
Nothing is bought, downloaded or sent: an offer button only runs through the store's sequence
on a timer. The app icons, the sidebar's glyphs and the avatar are original stand-in drawings,
and the hero's picture and the stories' round artworks are abstract placeholders: the toolkit
reproduces Catalina's chrome, not the store's artwork.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) without a title bar, as the store has it: a
  [Split View](/components/split-view) fills the frame, with a 220 pt sidebar pane, a fixed
  divider in the measured colour (through `--mac-split-view-divider-color`) and the content
  pane.
- Over the sidebar's rows, the [Window Controls](/components/window-controls) and a
  [Search Field](/components/search-field) in its `store` variant, the store's own 26 pt
  field. This strip is the window's drag region, through the library's `windowDragRegion`
  (`macalicious/lib/window-drag-region`).
- The eight sections in a [Sidebar](/components/sidebar): 42 pt rows that touch, a 26 pt
  glyph and an 18 pt label, set through the sidebar's documented variables
  (`--mac-sidebar-row-height`, `-row-spacing`, `-icon-size`, `-label-gap`). The account button
  under the rows is the sidebar's `footer`: an [Image Button](/components/image-button) in
  its `row` layout, whose image is the avatar with the name beside it.
- The page in a [Scroll View](/components/scroll-view). The hero of Discover and the story
  cards of every section are the [Editorial Card](/components/editorial-card): its `hero`
  variant with an abstract placeholder as its `artwork` and the text over it, and its
  `panel` variant with a round placeholder. Neither is restyled: the hero keeps the component's
  945 : 340 proportions and both keep its type and colours.
- The page's sections are the [Card Grid](/components/card-grid): one grid whose two cards
  are the story cards, then a grid per collection
  with the `lockup` cards, a `separator`, and the store's header type through the grid's
  variables. The columns are not fixed: `minCardWidth` gives three columns of lockups and two
  story cards at the captured width, and fewer in a narrower window.
- Every lockup's `accessory` is a [Store Pill Button](/components/store-pill-button): the
  offer (`Get`, a price, `Update`), then the filled confirmation (`Install` or `Buy App`),
  then the progress ring, then `Open`. The `In-App Purchases` line is its `caption`.
- `See All` is a `CardGridAction` rendered as a button; the categories are lockup cards
  rendered as buttons; an empty search is an [Empty State](/components/empty-state); Sign Out
  on the account page is a [Push Button](/components/push-button).

The drawings and the placeholders that stand for the artwork are the demo's own
(`app-store-artwork.tsx`).

| Section | Page |
| --- | --- |
| Discover | The hero, two story cards, and “Apps and Games We Love Right Now”: nine lockups, twelve after See All. As in the screenshot. |
| Arcade, Create, Work, Play, Develop | A large title, two story cards and one collection of six apps. Invented. |
| Categories | Thirteen categories, each a button that lists its apps by searching for its name. Invented. |
| Updates | Three apps that wait for an update, with Update All, and three updated lately. Invented. |

Typing in the search field replaces the page with the apps whose name, line or category
contains the text, whatever its case; no sidebar row is selected meanwhile. Escape, the
field's cancel button or a press on a row ends the search. An offer keeps its state wherever
its app is listed. A confirmation is withdrawn when the keyboard focus leaves it, and a press
on a download stops it. Sign In asks for nothing: it signs in to an invented account, whose
page lists the apps got in this window and has Sign Out.

## Hosting the window

`AppStore` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to it, so
it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it `state`,
the size, and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and to its drag
region. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

```tsx
import { ManagedWindow } from "macalicious/managed-window";
import { appStoreApp } from "./app";

<ManagedWindow
  id={appStoreApp.id}
  defaultFrame={{ x: 80, y: 60, ...appStoreApp.defaultSize }}
  minWidth={appStoreApp.minSize.width}
  minHeight={appStoreApp.minSize.height}
  render={(props) => <appStoreApp.component material="opaque" {...props} />}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `section`, `defaultSection`, `onSectionChange` | `"discover" \| "arcade" \| "create" \| "work" \| "play" \| "develop" \| "categories" \| "updates"` and a function | The section shown, controlled or not; Discover by default. |
| `defaultScrollTop` | `number` | Points the page is scrolled by when the window opens; 0 by default. The comparison uses 20, the space over the hero. |
| `material` | `"translucent" \| "opaque"` | The sidebar's surface: the vibrant material (the default), or the same surface as captured over a neutral window. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | A press or a double click on the strip that holds the window controls and the search field, not on a control in it. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 1246 by 857 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `appStoreApp` and as `app`: `id`,
`name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (1246 by 857), `minSize` (760 by 480), `resizable` and
`menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu (App Store, Edit, Store, Window, Help).

The purchase sequence is a pure function of its state (`reduceOffers` in
`app-store-data.ts`); the component adds one timer, which runs only while something downloads.

## On the desktop's Ferrite application

When the `macalicious-desktop` Ferrite application serves the desktop (`ferrite-apps/desktop`),
the store sells the Macalicious apps of the server's catalogue. Get puts an app in the person's
library (`/api/app-store/library`), and so in their Dock and Launchpad in every browser.

- **Uninstall**: on the account page each app got has an Uninstall button; in Launchpad,
  holding Option makes the apps got from the store jiggle with a delete badge, and pressing one
  asks “Delete ‘Books’?” while Launchpad stays open (`app-store-launchpad.tsx`). Both take the
  app out of the library, and so out of the Dock and Launchpad in every browser. When the server
  does not keep the change, the account page or an alert says so, and the change is sent again
  the next time the library is read.
  Its data stays as well: Storage Management counts it, and the app finds it again when it is got
  again. Apps that come with the desktop cannot be deleted, and an app that is open stays in the
  Dock until it is quit, as on macOS. The badge's colours are the tokens
  `--mac-app-store-delete-badge-*` (`app-store.tokens.css`); its size and the jiggle are in
  `app-store-launchpad.css`. All are provisional: no screenshot shows them.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named “App Store”. It has no title bar, so the name is an
  `aria-label`.
- The sections are a `tree` named “Sections” and one tab stop: the arrow keys, Home and End
  move the selection, typing selects by name, and the page follows the selection. The glyphs
  are decorative; each row is named by its label.
- The search field is a `searchbox` named “Search”. Escape clears it.
- The account button is named by its text, Sign In or the account's name; once signed in it
  is a toggle whose `aria-pressed` says whether the account page is shown.
- A page starts with a level 1 heading, which on Discover is hidden from view because the
  store shows none there. The titles of the hero and of the story cards are level 2
  headings (the Editorial Card's `headingLevel`); the two story cards are the items of a
  list in a section named “Stories”; each collection is a `region` named by its level 2
  header and holds a list.
- Every offer button is named with its app (“Get, Cozy Corners”), and `In-App Purchases` is its
  description. While an app downloads the button is named “Stop downloading” and the app; the
  ring is decorative, so a live region says when a download starts and when the app is
  installed.
- `See All` is a button with `aria-expanded`. A category is a button named by the category
  and its count. Update All is disabled while nothing waits for an update.
- The content pane is not a tab stop of its own: Tab moves through its buttons, and the
  browser scrolls the focused one into view.
- Focus rings and the accent selection of the sidebar are drawn only while the window is key.
- The artwork is hidden from assistive technology; the text beside it names what it shows.

## Fidelity

`app-store.visual.json` compares the whole window with the App Store screenshots in both
appearances, twice each. Both comparisons mask the artwork: the hero (945 by 340 pt at 261, 0,
its text included) and the round artwork of the two story cards (170 pt squares at 538, 390
and 1020, 390). The store's pictures there are replaced by abstract placeholders that take
nothing from them, so there is nothing to compare; how the library draws a hero and a story
card is compared on the [Editorial Card](/components/editorial-card) page. The `-layout`
comparison also masks the text and the stand-in icons and glyphs. The two
screenshots show the same content, so one example serves both. Scores from
`npm run visual -- app-store/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font: the share of
pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` (artwork masked) | 7.10% | 7.31 | 6.48% | 7.45 |
| `window-light-layout` (artwork, text, icons and glyphs masked) | 0.02% | 0.14 | 0.01% | 0.12 |
| `window-dark` (artwork masked) | 7.33% | 7.47 | 6.52% | 7.39 |
| `window-dark-layout` (artwork, text, icons and glyphs masked) | 0.32% | 0.30 | 0.05% | 0.05 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 1246 by 857 pt frame; the 220 pt sidebar with
its colour, the divider and the content pane's colour; the window controls and the 180 by
26 pt search field where the capture has them; the eight rows at a 42 pt pitch from 94 pt,
their selection, glyph box and label; the account button's 40 pt avatar and its label; the
945 pt content column and the place of the hero's bottom edge; the two 462 by 230 pt story
cards with their fill, corners and type (as the Editorial Card draws them); the collection's line, its 19.5 pt header and 15 pt `See All`; the three 301 pt columns of
lockups with their 50 pt icons, their lines and the 66 by 21 pt offer capsules; and the title
“Banners of the Nine Rivers” on two lines, as the Card Grid's lockup sets a long title.

What differs, and why:

- **Artwork.** The hero's photograph and the two round artworks are the store's; abstract
  placeholders stand in their place and their regions are masked in every comparison. The
  app icons, the sidebar glyphs and the avatar are Apple's or the apps' own, and are replaced
  by original drawings, which the unmasked comparisons score as they are.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices. The two story cards' titles and
  sentences are the Editorial Card's 20.5 and 12 pt, where the store sets them at 24 and
  13 pt (in San Francisco “Act Fast!” is 89 pt of ink in the capture and 76.5 pt here), so
  the first card's sentence breaks after “reaction time” where the store breaks it after
  “reaction”. The selected row's label is the
  sidebar's own darker colour, where the store keeps the grey of the other rows.
- **The collection's line** ends 2 pt before the content column does, with the list of
  lockups: the card grid draws the line as wide as its list.
- **The hero's top.** The capture shows the hero's lower 340 pt; its top is scrolled out of
  the window, so its full height is unknown. The Editorial Card's hero is 340 pt high at this
  width and places its text from its bottom edge, the edge the capture shows, so the
  comparison scrolls by the 20 pt over the
  hero: the bottom edge is where the capture has it, and the hero's top edge
  lies on the window's top edge with its two 6 pt corners, where the capture's photograph
  runs on above the window. The 20 pt over the hero are an estimate, and the hero's region is
  masked, so none of this is scored here.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** every page other than Discover with its large
  title, the search results, the account page, the confirmation, progress and `Open` steps of
  an offer, the accent selection while the sidebar has the keyboard focus, the window when it
  is not key, and every size other than the captured one.
- **Not built:** the pages of a story and of an app (the hero, the story cards and the
  lockups' titles are not links), the page See All opens in the store (here the collection
  grows in place), the sheet that asks for an Apple ID (Sign In signs in at once), and the
  store's menus beyond their titles.
