<Example name="basic" />

Choose an interface in the pop-up button to see its addresses and its transfer statistics.
Pick another tab, enter an address and press Return or the pane's button. Nothing is sent to
a network and nothing is read from the machine the page runs on: the three interfaces are
data, of which Wi-Fi is the one of the reference screenshots, and what a tool prints is made
up from what was entered, so the same input always prints the same text.

On the served desktop the window does what a web service can. Lookup and Whois ask a real DNS
resolver, of the two the Server pop-up offers, and real registries through the application's declared egress; Ping times labelled HTTPS
requests to hosts the application declares and says it is not ping; Netstat and Info list what the
application and the browser can truly say; Traceroute, Finger and Port Scan say why they cannot
run and send nothing.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with the plain 22 pt [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) and
  its [Window Controls](/components/window-controls). The layout is fixed, as in the
  application, so the window is not resizable.
- A [Tab View](/components/tab-view) used the way Network Utility uses it: its box runs from
  one window edge to the other and to the bottom edge, so only the top line of the box shows,
  with the strip of eight tabs centred on it. The demo makes the tab view larger than the
  window's body and lets the body clip it. The segments have the widths measured in the
  capture.
- The Info pane: a [Label](/components/label) for the prompt, a regular
  [Pop-Up Button](/components/pop-up-button) of the interfaces, and two titled
  [Boxes](/components/box). In each box a [Form](/components/form) of `value` rows sets the
  keys against the values: right-aligned keys that end in a colon, 24 pt from one row to the
  next, and a long value that wraps under itself.
- The seven tool panes, one component for all: a prompt, a
  [Text Field](/components/text-field) with an example beside it (Netstat has four
  [Radio Buttons](/components/radio-button) instead), a [Push Button](/components/push-button)
  that submits the pane's form, and the output in a read-only fixed-pitch
  [Text View](/components/text-view) inside a box in its `content` variant. The button is
  unavailable while the field is empty.

| Tab | Takes | Prints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Info | an interface | hardware and IP address, link speed and status, vendor and model; packets and errors |
| Netstat | one of four choices | a routing table, protocol statistics, multicast groups or socket connections |
| Ping | an address | ten replies and the round-trip statistics |
| Lookup | an address | the answer section of a name lookup |
| Traceroute | an address | five to nine hops |
| Whois | a domain | a registration record marked as made up |
| Finger | `user@host` | the user's entry |
| Port Scan | an address | the open ports among eight well-known ones |

A name stands for an address in 203.0.113.0/24, a range reserved for documentation, picked
by the name; an address stands for itself.

## Source walkthrough

The demo is four files in `demos/network-utility/`, and its examples.

**`network-utility-data.ts`** holds everything that is not interface: the eight tabs with
their measured widths (`TABS`), the interfaces (`defaultInterfaces`), the tools' prompts,
fields and button titles (`TOOLS`), and `runTool(tool, input, option)`, a pure function that
builds a tool's output from its input.

**`network-utility.tsx`** is the window. The body holds one tab view; every pane stays
mounted (`keepMounted`), so a tool keeps what was entered and printed while another tab is
shown:

```tsx
<Window width={644} height={385} {...windowProps}>
  <TitleBar title="Network Utility" controls={<WindowControls onClose={onClose} />} />
  <WindowBody className="mac-network-utility-body">
    <TabView className="mac-network-utility-tabs" value={tab} onValueChange={choose}>
      <TabViewList aria-label="Network Utility">
        {TABS.map((item) => (
          <TabViewTab key={item.id} value={item.id} style={{ width: item.width, padding: 0 }}>
            {item.title}
          </TabViewTab>
        ))}
      </TabViewList>
      <TabViewPanel value="info" keepMounted>
        <InfoPane interfaces={interfaces} />
      </TabViewPanel>
      {/* one ToolPane per remaining tab */}
    </TabView>
  </WindowBody>
</Window>
```

A box of the Info pane is a form of `value` rows. `labelWidth` puts the keys' right edge
where the capture has it:

```tsx
<Box title="Interface Information">
  <Form render={<div />} labelWidth={130.5}>
    <FormRow kind="value" label="Hardware Address:">{shown.hardwareAddress}</FormRow>
    <FormRow kind="value" label="IP Address:">{shown.ipAddress}</FormRow>
  </Form>
</Box>
```

A tool pane is a `Form` whose submission runs the tool, so Return in the field and a click
on the button do the same:

```tsx
<Form onSubmit={(event) => { event.preventDefault(); setOutput(runTool(tool, input, option)); }}>
  <Label>{definition.prompt}</Label>
  <TextField aria-label={definition.field} value={input} onChange={...} />
  <PushButton type="submit" disabled={!ready}>{definition.action}</PushButton>
  <Box variant="content">
    <TextView aria-label="Ping output" readOnly monospace value={output} />
  </Box>
</Form>
```

**`network-utility.css`** is layout only: the tab view overhangs the body by 28 pt on the
left, 29 pt on the right and 20 pt at the bottom, a pane is the part of the box inside the
window, and the Info pane is a grid of the two measured box widths. It sets no colour: the
tab view's box tints the window background and each group box tints that again, which gives
the fills of both captures.

**`app.ts`** is the descriptor the [Desktop](/demos/desktop) reads, and `examples/` holds
the examples of this page and the two the visual comparison renders.

## Hosting the window

`NetworkUtility` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every `Window` prop to
it, so it does not assume it fills the page. Place it with `style`; a window manager gives it
`state` and eight more props that the component hands to the window controls and the title
bar. These are the props a [Managed Window](/components/managed-window)'s `render` form
supplies, so the component can be spread with them as they come.

<Example name="hosted" />

| Prop | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `interfaces` | `NetworkUtilityInterface[]` | The interfaces of the pop-up button; Wi-Fi as captured and two invented ones by default. |
| `defaultInterface` | `string` | The `id` of the interface shown at first; the first one by default. |
| `tab`, `defaultTab`, `onTabChange` | `"info" \| "netstat" \| "ping" \| "lookup" \| "traceroute" \| "whois" \| "finger" \| "port-scan"` and a function | The pane, controlled or not. |
| `onRun` | `(tool, input, output) => void` | Called when a tool is run, with what was entered (the chosen option for Netstat) and what it printed. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 644 by 385 by default, as captured. The layout is made for that size. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `networkUtilityApp` and as `app`:
`id`, `name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (644 by 385), `resizable` (`false`) and `menus`, the
menu titles after the Apple menu.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title.
- The strip is a `tablist` named "Network Utility" and one tab stop. Left and Right Arrow
  move to the neighbouring tab and select it, wrapping round, as in AppKit; each pane is a
  `tabpanel` named by its tab.
- The pop-up button shows no label of its own and is named "Network interface". Each box is a
  `group` named by its title; a key is a `term` and its value the `definition` that follows.
- A tool's field is named for what it takes ("Network address"), the example beside it is
  plain text, and the output view is a read-only text box named after the tool ("Ping
  output"), so its text can be selected, copied and read line by line.
- The button of a pane is disabled while its field is empty, and says Stop while a slow run is
  under way. Return in the field runs the
  tool.

## Fidelity

`network-utility.visual.json` compares the whole window on its Info pane with the Network
Utility screenshots in both appearances, each as captured and again with the text masked.
The dark screenshot shows other transfer statistics than the light one, so it has an example
of its own. Scores from `npm run visual -- network-utility/` in Chromium on Linux with the
fallback font: the share of pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` | 5.16% | 5.50 | 4.38% | 6.18 |
| `window-light-layout` (masked) | 1.73% | 1.46 | 0.03% | 0.91 |
| `window-dark` | 4.99% | 6.25 | 4.33% | 6.67 |
| `window-dark-layout` (masked) | 1.34% | 2.87 | 0.22% | 2.25 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 644 by 385 pt frame, the strip of 523 pt
with its eight segment widths 12.5 pt under the title bar, the pane's line 44 pt from the
top, the 313 pt pop-up button 104 pt from the top, the two boxes of 313 and 262 pt with their
titles, the 24 pt pitch of the rows, and the columns of keys and values.

Two more comparisons, `ping-light` and `ping-dark`, drive the real window: a click on the
Ping tab, an address typed in the field, and Return. They check three pixels each (the Info
tab is no longer selected, the pane's fill, the output well's fill) against values of the
spec and the tokens, since no screenshot shows that pane; what the tool prints is checked by
the behaviour tests.

What differs, and why:

- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices, which is nearly all of the unmasked
  scores.
- **Dark Aqua greys.** The dark capture's surfaces are slightly blue (`#363638` for the
  pane); the library's window and group fills are neutral there, three levels away, which is
  the masked dark comparison's mean difference.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the seven tool panes with their wording, layout and
  output; the interfaces other than Wi-Fi; the open pop-up menu; the window when it is not
  key.
