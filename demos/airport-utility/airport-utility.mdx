AirPort Utility is not part of the Ferrite build: this page shows its interface, and the window changes nothing
outside itself.

<Example name="basic" />

Press the Internet for the connection's details. Other Wi-Fi Devices lists two devices that
wait to be set up: choose one, name the network and the base station, give a password twice,
and the device joins the graph under the Internet, where its own popover shows its details and
renames it. Nothing is scanned or configured: every device, address and serial number is
invented, and the pictures are abstract placeholders.

## What it shows

- A [Window](/components/window) with `data-appearance="dark"`: both captures show the same
  dark window, the one named Light too, so the demo is dark in Aqua and in Dark Aqua alike and
  the attribute gives everything inside its Dark Aqua look.
- A plain 22 pt [Title Bar](/components/title-bar) with the
  [Window Controls](/components/window-controls). It is the window's drag region.
- The graph is the window's body with the measured gradient as its background
  (`--mac-airport-utility-backdrop`). Its nodes are placed in `airport-utility.css`.
- Other Wi-Fi Devices is a [Push Button](/components/push-button) that opens a
  [Menu](/components/menu) of [Menu Items](/components/menu-item), one for each device. It is
  disabled when no device waits.
- A node is an [Image Button](/components/image-button): the picture, and under it a
  [Status Dot](/components/status-dot) and the name. The gap between the two is set through
  `--mac-image-button-gap`. The name's 17 pt bold text is the demo's own.
- A node opens a [Popover](/components/popover) beside it. Its keys and values are a
  [Form](/components/form) of `value` rows in the small size, rendered without a `<form>`. A
  base station's popover has an Edit button: the name becomes a
  [Text Field](/components/text-field), which takes the keyboard focus, and Cancel and Done
  are a push button and a [Default Button](/components/default-button). The base stations
  are one row that scrolls sideways when it is wider than the window; a line runs from the
  Internet to a branch over the row and down to each of them, and a long name ends with an
  ellipsis. The line from the Internet is not part of the row and stays under the Internet
  when the row is scrolled. The graph is never narrower than the widest base station with
  24 pt on either side (208 pt), so that in a narrower window the line still meets the
  branch; the window cuts off what it cannot show.
- Setting a device up asks in a [Sheet](/components/sheet) that hangs from the title bar: a
  form of two text fields and two [Secure Text Fields](/components/secure-text-field), a
  [Label](/components/label) that says what is still missing, and Cancel and Done. While the
  sheet is open the window is main, not key, and its content is inert.

### The source

| File | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| `airport-utility.tsx` | `AirPortUtility`: the window, the graph, the menu of nearby devices, the nodes with their popovers and the setup sheet. `Station` is one base station's node; it owns its popover and the draft of a new name. |
| `airport-utility-data.ts` | The types of a base station, a nearby device and a setup, the two invented devices, and the rules as pure functions: `proposeSetup`, `setupProblem`, `setUp`, `nextAddress`, `renameStation`, `statusWords`. The tests call them directly. |
| `airport-utility-artwork.tsx` | The two abstract placeholders: `NetworkMark` for the Internet and `BaseStationMark` for a base station. |
| `airport-utility.css`, `airport-utility.tokens.css` | Where the nodes lie, and the colours the demo adds, each tagged measured or provisional. |
| `app.ts` | The descriptor the desktop demo reads. |

The state is small: the base stations, the devices still waiting, the device the sheet is
about with the four fields of its setup, and the last announcement. `setupProblem` returns
the first thing that keeps a setup from being finished, in the order of the fields; the sheet
shows that sentence and disables Done while there is one, and `setUp` refuses the same setup,
so the rule is in one place.

## Hosting the window

`AirPortUtility` renders the library's `Window` as its root and passes every prop it does not
use to it, so a window manager can place, size and activate it.

| Prop | Type | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `defaultStations` | `AirPortBaseStation[]` | The base stations when the window opens. Default: none, as captured. |
| `defaultNearbyDevices` | `AirPortNearbyDevice[]` | The devices Other Wi-Fi Devices lists. Default: two invented devices. One that is a base station already is not listed. |
| `onStationsChange` | `(stations) => void` | Called with the base stations after one was set up or renamed. |
| `onClose`, `onMinimize`, `onZoom` | functions | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `zoomRole`, `minimizeDisabled`, `zoomDisabled` | as on `WindowControls` | Passed to the `WindowControls`. |
| `onDragRegionPointerDown`, `onDragRegionDoubleClick` | functions | Passed to the `TitleBar`: a press or a double click on the bar that is not on a control. |
| `width`, `height` | `number \| string` | The frame; 696 by 532 by default, as captured. |
| the rest | `WindowProps` | `state`, `fullScreen`, `style`, `className`, `ref`, pointer events and so on. |

A base station is `{ id, name, model, network, address, serial, version, status }` and a
nearby device `{ id, model, label, serial, version }`; `airport-utility-data.ts` documents
each field.

`app.ts` exports the descriptor a desktop page reads, as `airportUtilityApp` and as `app`:
`id`, `name`, `component`, `defaultSize` (696 by 532), `minSize` (560 by 440), `resizable`
and `menus`, the menu titles after the Apple menu. The [desktop demo](/demos/desktop) finds it
there and lists AirPort Utility among its applications.

## Accessibility

- The window is a `region` named by its title.
- A node is a button named by its name and its state in words, such as “Internet, Connected”:
  the status dot adds colour only. The base stations are a list named “Base stations”.
- A popover is a `dialog` named by its title. It takes the keyboard focus when it opens,
  Escape closes it and the focus returns to its node. Its keys and values are a description
  list.
- Other Wi-Fi Devices opens a `menu`; the arrow keys move in it and Return chooses.
- The sheet is a modal `dialog` named by its question. Each field is named by its label, the
  keyboard stays inside, Escape cancels and Return presses Done when nothing is missing. What
  is missing is said in a sentence under the fields, which is a polite live region.
- A device that joins the network and a base station that is renamed are announced in a
  `status` region.

## Fidelity

`airport-utility.visual.json` compares the whole window with the two AirPort Utility
screenshots. **Both show a dark window**: the file named Light has the same dark title bar and
gradient as its Dark twin (the two differ only by noise of up to 13 levels), so no Aqua
rendering of this application exists in the captures. The demo is therefore dark in both
appearances, and each capture is compared as it is with the demo in the appearance its file
name gives.

**Masked in every comparison:** the globe with its ground shadow, 128.5 by 138.5 pt from
(284.5, 82). It is Apple's artwork in the captures; the demo shows an abstract placeholder in
its place, a flat hexagon with three spokes, which takes nothing from that artwork, so there
is nothing to compare. No budget was raised for it.

Scores from `npm run visual -- airport-utility/` in Chromium on Linux with the fallback font:
the share of pixels that differ and the mean colour difference out of 255.

| Comparison | Mismatch 1x | Mean Δ 1x | Mismatch 2x | Mean Δ 2x |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `window-light` (globe masked) | 0.63% | 1.41 | 0.54% | 1.52 |
| `window-light-layout` (globe and text masked) | 0.15% | 0.96 | 0.13% | 1.05 |
| `window-dark` (globe masked) | 0.63% | 1.24 | 0.54% | 1.34 |
| `window-dark-layout` (globe and text masked) | 0.15% | 0.80 | 0.13% | 0.88 |

Measured in the screenshots and reproduced: the 696 by 532 pt frame and its 22 pt title bar;
the gradient with its five stops; the button's 134 pt width and its place, 24 pt from the left
edge and 20.5 pt under the title bar; the place of the picture's box, 60 pt under the title
bar and centred; the status dot and the name centred as one, 8 pt apart, with the name in
17 pt bold white.

What differs, and why:

- **Artwork.** The globe is Apple's; an abstract placeholder stands in its place and its
  region is masked in every comparison.
- **The button.** AirPort Utility draws its own dark pill, 19 pt high with a fill of
  `#646870`. The demo uses the library's push button in its Dark Aqua look as it is: 21 pt
  high with its own fill, at the captured width. It is most of the masked score.
- **The status dot.** The capture's dot is 12 pt across (`#4bd964`); the library's regular
  status dot is 10 pt with its own green.
- **The title bar.** The capture's bar is a little lighter (`#4d4f4f` falling to `#49494b`)
  than the library's Dark Aqua title bar.
- **Text.** Set in the fallback font off Apple devices.
- **Not in a screenshot (provisional):** the popovers, the menu of devices, the setup sheet,
  the base stations with their line to the Internet, the window when it is not key, and every
  size other than the captured one.
- **Not built:** scanning for devices, the pages of a base station's settings beyond its
  name, restarting or restoring a base station, and the menus beyond their titles.
